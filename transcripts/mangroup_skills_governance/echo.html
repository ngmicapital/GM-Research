Man Group: Skills Governance Is the Enterprise AI Moat

👤 Tashara Fernando · Head of Data & AI, Man Group 🎓 Code with Claude Conference · ~20 min 🏷 Enterprise AI / Agentic Systems / Quant Finance

First-party practitioner — live production deployment with real capital. High credibility on implementation lessons; trading signal performance figures are illustrative/anonymised.

⚡ The Bottom Line

  • AI-generated trading signals are in live production at Man Group, running real pension capital. Full pipeline: idea → data → backtest → proposal → productionisation — all AI-driven, human-reviewed.
  • The signal is easy. The governed workflows underneath it are the moat. If different teams use different backtest logic, results are incomparable — the signal work is wasted.
  • Man Group learned governance the hard way: power users built skills, not process owners. Viral distribution of locally-optimised tools created org-wide dysfunction.
  • Skills governance — not adoption — is the unlock for enterprise-scale AI. A skills library with ownership, testing, lifecycle, and visibility is the foundation everything else is built on.
  • Your organisational context is your AI moat. Frontier labs cannot give it to you. The work is exposing what you already know, not learning new things.

🔑 Key Claims

1
AI-driven trading signals are live in production at a regulated $200B AUM investment firm
Evidence: Fernando states this directly; describes the full pipeline. Humans reviewed output but AI was "at the centre of the process."
Caveat: Specific signal, returns, and asset class not disclosed ("that's our IP").
Consensus: Contrarian in degree of automation; AI-assisted quant research is mainstream but full-pipeline AI ownership is at the frontier.
2
The iceberg problem: workflows beneath the signal must be shared to make results comparable
Evidence: Two teams running different backtest workflow versions produce incomparable results — you can't tell if Signal A is better or just measured differently.
Caveat: None. This is structural logic, not an empirical claim.
3
Power users, not process owners, will build your first wave of skills — and that's a problem
Evidence: Expense report story. Cost centre hardcoded to one user; distributed org-wide; wrong approval routing across departments.
Caveat: Anecdotal but structurally typical. Fernando confirmed audience recognition ("show of hands").
4
750 of ~1,700 Man Group employees use Claude Code (44% of the firm)
Evidence: Fernando's direct figure. Includes developers, quants, people team, finance team.
Caveat: Unverified externally; plausible given Anthropic partnership depth.
5
100+ governed skills and 100+ community skills operational
Evidence: Fernando's direct figure. Described as "well governed" and "looked after in a library."
Caveat: Quality of governance not independently audited.
6
Credit card spend signal outperforms buy-and-hold: $1K in 2021 → ~$2,500 now
Evidence: Shown as a demo case study, not a live trading signal.
Caveat: Illustrative demo; in-sample backtest. Does not represent actual Man Group trading performance.
7
Treat skills like production code — they will run in automated pipelines
Evidence: Logical consequence of agent deployment; Fernando states as a lesson from experience.
Caveat: None.
