Kalshi's Licence Is Its Growth Channel — Not Its Compliance Cost

Source Bankless · Date n/a · 67 min
Domain Crypto & Markets — Regulated Derivatives
Reliability Mixed — a well-informed operator with direct visibility into Kalshi's perps and crypto business, but several volume and market-share numbers are self-reported and sit above what public trackers support.
Content Type Founder / Executive Interview
Full Mode

⚡ The Bottom Line

  • Kalshi's regulatory posture is an acquisition strategy, not a compliance burden. Being a CFTC-designated contract market is what buys Facebook and Instagram ads, billboards, App Store presence and broker integrations — the only channels that reach what Wang calls the "99% TAM."
  • Perpetual futures, not prediction markets, are now Kalshi's volume monster. Wang says perps did an order of magnitude more volume in their first month than any single prediction market has since launch.
  • A single CFTC order did the heavy lifting. The BTCPERP Order for Approval (29 May 2026, Reg 40.3) opened a template that lets Kalshi self-certify additional liquid digital-commodity perps — the regulatory queue, not the matching engine, is the moat.
  • Kalshi bans insider trading on market-structure grounds, not ethical ones: toxic informed flow makes market makers pull quotes, which destroys the liquidity that produces the signal. Hoffman argues the opposite case hard and neither concedes.
  • The stated ambition is "the largest exchange on the planet" — a CME or NYSE regulatory posture welded to consumer-growth acquisition, with the institutional climb running through bespoke market creation and dragging OTC structured products into lit markets.

🔑 Key Claims (Ranked)

1. The onshore path was chosen for distribution, not virtue — and it required suing the regulator ↳ Evidence: Kalshi sued the CFTC over election contracts; Judge Jia Cobb ruled for Kalshi in September 2024, the D.C. Circuit denied a stay in October 2024, and the CFTC voluntarily dismissed its appeal in May 2025. Wang: "For prediction markets, we actually had to sue the CFTC." 09:44 ↳ Caveat: The win was on a narrow statutory question — whether Congress authorised the CFTC's public-interest review — not a broad endorsement of event contracts. ↳ Consensus: Contrarian — crypto's dominant narrative treats regulatory engagement as a drag on speed, not a growth channel.

2. Perps are the growth engine, and the Bitcoin approval was the key that opened the asset class ↳ Evidence: CFTC Order for Approval for BTCPERP issued 29 May 2026 under CEA §5c(c)(4) and Reg 40.3. Kalshi now lists BTC, ETH, HYPE, SOL, ZEC, NEAR, SUI, DOGE and XRP, with gold and silver filed. 18:13–18:47 ↳ Caveat: The Order's own scope is narrower than Wang implies — it covers BTCPERP "and similarly structured perpetual contracts" on digital commodities with deep spot markets, and the CFTC expressly said the perpetual design "may not be suitable for all asset classes." ↳ Consensus: Aligned — regulated US perps are now an established competitive front.

3. "First regulated perpetual futures exchange in the US" is contestable ↳ Evidence: Coinbase Financial Markets launched CFTC-regulated perpetual-style futures (nano BTC and ETH, no monthly expiry, up to 10x leverage) on 21 July 2025 via Reg 40.2 self-certification; Cboe listed continuous futures. 02:28 ↳ Caveat: Kalshi's genuine first is CFTC approval of a true perpetual under a 40.3 Order — a stronger regulatory status, but a different claim. ↳ Consensus: Emerging — the "perpetual-style vs perpetual" distinction is only now being disambiguated in US markets.

4. Insider trading is policed because adverse selection kills liquidity, not because it is unfair ↳ Evidence: Wang argues market makers pull quotes under heavy toxic flow, so a permissive venue ends up with no liquidity and therefore no signal. "You just have to draw this line... if you want to have market integrity and healthy markets that thrive and scale." 34:06 ↳ Caveat: Enforcement detail is thinner than the principle — Wang is only "90% sure" whether the ban on politicians trading political markets is categorical 36:04. ↳ Consensus: Aligned among regulated venues; contested by prediction-market theorists including Robin Hanson, whom Hoffman invokes at 42:48.

5. Kalshi says it has pulled 4–5x ahead of Polymarket overall and 10x in crypto ↳ Evidence: Wang states both multiples directly 63:15 and says partners now default to Kalshi rather than being auctioned between the two. ↳ Caveat: Public data supports the direction but not the size. June 2026 volumes were $21.1B (Kalshi) vs $9.7B (Polymarket) — about 2.2x. DeFi Rate had Kalshi near 73% share in early July 2026. In March 2026 the two were roughly at parity near $12B each. The 10x crypto claim could not be independently verified. ↳ Consensus: Aligned on the lead; contrarian on the magnitude.

6. Hyperliquid's RWA shift is the opening Kalshi is aiming at ↳ Evidence: Wang says around half of Hyperliquid's volume now comes from real-world assets, and that the centre of RWA liquidity is US retail and institutions who have had no regulated route to perpetual exposure. 16:42–17:32 ↳ Caveat: None on the fact — this is the best-supported number in the interview. RWA share on Hyperliquid rose 1.8% → 20.7% → 32.2% across Q4 2025 to Q2 2026, hitting ~52% in mid-July 2026. ↳ Consensus: Aligned and well documented.

7. The institutional thesis is migrating OTC structured products into lit markets ↳ Evidence: Kalshi's first block trade was a Houston environmental hedge fund hedging California May carbon-allowance auction prices; institutions can commission bespoke markets that are then publicly priced. Wang claims this cuts barriers to entry by an order of magnitude. 58:17–60:00 ↳ Caveat: Margin on event contracts — the single most requested institutional feature — is still not shipped 60:16, which pushes capital-efficiency-sensitive institutions into perps instead. ↳ Consensus: Emerging — plausible but largely unproven at size.

8. Sports is falling as a share of Kalshi volume, but not as fast as Wang implies ↳ Evidence: Wang claims crypto prediction markets alone are 20–30% of exchange volume and projects sports falling to 20–30% as commodities scale. 51:13–51:44 ↳ Caveat: Third-party trackers put sports at 68–80%+ of Kalshi notional through 2026, above 90% in individual weeks. At least one aggregator puts crypto nearer 7%. The gap may partly be a definitional one — whether perps notional is counted inside "exchange volume" — but neither side states its convention. ↳ Consensus: Contrarian relative to public data.

9. International growth is broker-led and stablecoin-funded because nothing else works ↳ Evidence: Open in 140 countries with crypto as the only accepted non-US deposit rail 03:28. XP International partnership announced 9 March 2026 for Brazil; Wealthsimple Predict announced 18 June 2026 for Canada with CIRO authorisation and ~4,000 Kalshi contracts limited to climate, financial markets and economic indicators. ↳ Consensus: Aligned — mirrors how regulated brokerages have historically entered new jurisdictions.

10. Nobody has defined what insider trading means for a prediction market ↳ Evidence: Hoffman contrasts the 2024 Polymarket whale "Théo" — who commissioned polls asking respondents how their neighbours would vote, and made up to $85M — with a Google employee trading on an unreleased model date, arguing the first is shrewd research and the second is selling corporate IP. 38:16–39:59 ↳ Caveat: The Google-employee example is unverified; Hoffman himself cannot recall which platform it occurred on. ↳ Consensus: Emerging — the CFTC has issued no prediction-market-specific insider-trading framework, and Kalshi publicly supports one being written.
