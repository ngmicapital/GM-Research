MARKET UPDATE: Crypto Is Used To Buy Oil?! FED Meeting, And Economy Ripping

Source 1000x · 2026-09-16 · 65 min
Domain Crypto Markets & Macro
Reliability Mixed — two professional traders reacting live to an FOMC print they are positioned for, talking their own book throughout; positions in Zcash, Intel, copper and a memecoin are disclosed on tape, as is the hosts' own 1000X social token, and the show closes with its standard not-financial-advice disclaimer.
Content Type Live market reaction
Full Mode

⚡ The Bottom Line

  • The Financial Times investigation into Poland's $230mn Venezuelan oil disaster is, on Felman's read, the most bullish crypto story of the year. A sovereign-owned trading arm settled a crude contract in Tether rather than through banks. The money vanished because of counterparty diligence, not because of the rails.
  • Felman reversed his own call live and predicted a 25bp hike — then argued a hawkish Fed is bullish for Bitcoin and gold. The logic: Kevin Warsh hiking is a signal that the Treasury's fiscal posture is out of control, and Warsh loses that fight. The Fed delivered the hike at 2pm ET and both assets barely moved.
  • Van Bourg's counterweight: physical oil is structurally closed to small operators. Minimum ticket sizes run to tens of millions; a Suezmax cargo is roughly $100mn and a ULCC around $200mn. Anyone offering crude for a thumb drive on a Caracas street corner is not selling crude.
  • Copper is the trade Van Bourg wants to own for five years, with leverage. Data centres are short copper; buy the producer basket (COPX) rather than the metal, is Felman's amendment.
  • Both hosts read the Altman–Amodei "pace the frontier" push as a sales playbook, not a safety position — and treat Zuckerberg's refusal to join it as the event that breaks the coordination.

🔑 Key Claims (Ranked)

1. Crypto did not lose Poland its money; unvetted brokers did, and the story is evidence of sovereign-scale crypto adoption ↳ Evidence: Traders acting for a Polish state-owned group handed USB sticks loaded with USDT to Caracas intermediaries in exchange for promised oil cargoes; no banks, no wires (05:06, 06:16). ↳ Caveat: Felman concedes crypto's irreversibility raises the skim risk when a 28-year-old is handed $50mn on a stick 17:28. ↳ Context: PdVSA has demanded stablecoin prepayment on spot cargoes since 2024; the rails were Venezuela's requirement, not Poland's innovation.

2. A Warsh hike is bullish for monetary assets because it signals a fiscal fight the Fed will lose ↳ Evidence: Felman: hiking says "the government is acting irresponsibly" and the Treasury "will distort us" — but Warsh has less political capital than Bessent 51:24. ↳ Caveat: Van Bourg pushes back directly — he made the same "hike is bullish" argument in 2021 and it was wrong 61:41. ↳ Context: The FOMC delivered 25bp unanimously; the median dot now points to one more hike this year.

3. The dovish scenario was the bearish one ↳ Evidence: Felman said a Warsh who "comes in acquiescing to what the Treasury wants" would be the bearish outcome, and that an exceptionally hawkish presser would be "very very very bullish" (52:33, 61:26). ↳ Context: Warsh kept the hawkish line at the presser and again declined to submit a dot to the projection grid.

4. Physical oil has no room for the solo entrant — ticket size is the gate ↳ Evidence: Van Bourg ran a derivatives pod at Vitol for seven years and says every introduction of this shape he passed to the physical desks converted at zero (08:53, 09:11). ↳ Caveat: He carves out an exception for sanctions-adjacent flow, where the brokers are real and usually billionaires 10:33. ↳ Context: Venezuela is no longer sanctions-shadowed — the majors and the US government are in the room, which removes the reason a back-channel broker would exist.

5. Long-end Treasuries are repricing against a rising forward equity return, not just supply ↳ Evidence: Felman argues AI-driven profitability projections raise the compensation investors demand to hold duration, on top of heavy corporate issuance and Treasury intervention 28:26. ↳ Context: Bessent more than doubled buyback operations to at least $4bn from 9 September after the 30-year hit a 19-year high.

6. Copper is the AI trade with no crowd in it yet ↳ Evidence: Van Bourg: the wiring connecting "these thinking sand neuron things is copper. And there's just not enough of it" 32:58. ↳ Caveat: Felman's amendment — own the producers, because miners can raise supply and capture the move, where the metal cannot. ↳ Context: Copper set records above $14,000/tonne on the LME in 2026 against a structural refined deficit; data-centre demand alone is forecast to average roughly 400,000 tonnes a year.

7. AI doomerism is a sales motion, and Meta just broke the cartel ↳ Evidence: Van Bourg maps Amodei and Altman onto the oldest broker trick — "you're going to die… and only we have the antidote because we created the disease" (41:01, 41:22). ↳ Caveat: Both hosts still expect a genuine AI-driven crisis eventually; Van Bourg puts it roughly two years out 44:24. ↳ Context: Amodei published "We Must Pace the Frontier" on 12 September; Zuckerberg publicly rejected a coordinated slowdown on 15 September, the day before this taping.

8. The next crisis will not be a credit crisis ↳ Evidence: Felman: every collapse in his lifetime was credit-related, and credit crises are now solved by money printing — a genie that "will never go back in" 49:05. ↳ Context: Van Bourg's substitute scenario is probabilistic software running critical infrastructure and failing in a way deterministic software could not.

9. Venezuela's opportunity is real but sits below the oil layer ↳ Evidence: Van Bourg redirects the entrepreneurial case to smaller-ticket commodities — cobalt, lithium, minor metals — where a shipment does not cost $100mn 26:01. ↳ Context: Coinbase co-founder Fred Ehrsam is pursuing at least three Orinoco Belt blocks through a vehicle called Primavera — the billionaire-scale version of the trade Felman floated for a 24-year-old.
