☄️ The Reply Front-Run — Price the Reply, Not the Coin

The trade was never "buy the memecoin"; it was "buy a lottery ticket on a specific person publishing a specific two-word answer" — and both hosts had the probability roughly right and still didn't buy.

What It Includes

A pending public request aimed at a person with unusual market-moving reach; a token whose entire thesis is contingent on that person answering; a window during which the odds are knowably below 50% and the payoff is knowably a multiple.

The Practice (Step-by-Step):

  1. Identify a pending ask, not a rumour. The setup here was a published question from a named source, amplified by a large account, sitting unanswered 05:16 · 00:23.
  2. State your probability out loud before the event. rasmr forces exactly this: "Before the tweet, what would you have put the polymarket odds for it at?" 12:46. His own answer was 60% 12:58; Orangie's was 20% 13:06.

  4. Size to the probability, not to the conviction. This is where both of them failed. Orangie's stated blocker is explicit: "in order to make a lot of money on this coin, you got to blast so much money, and I'm just like not about that right now" 34:32.
  5. Check whether the window is actually short. It wasn't: "It was OG and sub 1 mil for 15 minutes" 24:12, and "it kind of played out over days and people could have entered before today's tweet" 27:51.

🪙 The Provenance Test — Which Version of the Narrative Gets the Flow

When one story has tokens on multiple chains, buy the one whose float is old, not the one whose leader is loud.

What It Includes

Two or more tokens with the same narrative; at least one launched long before the catalyst; at least one launched or community-taken-over on the day.

The Practice (Step-by-Step):

  1. Find the token that predates the catalyst and by how much. "it was launched like 2024 September" 03:54 — roughly nineteen months of prior existence.
  2. Look for a verifiable artefact behind it, not just an early contract. Here: a real plush toy that flew on a real mission, a named creator, a documented backstory 05:16–05:30.
  3. Assume the same-day version has concentrated float and treat that as a price ceiling, not a red flag to argue about. The workspace's qwerty_surviving_crypto_2026 interview states the arithmetic: someone could hold 15–20% of a same-day supply and that "sets a cap."
  4. Discount named leadership to near zero. "as much as I respect [him]… he's CTOing a coin that — there's one on ETH, but it's existed for over a year" 02:56–03:04.
  5. Invert the community's moral vocabulary as a sanity check. "if you're against PvP, technically I think the soul one is the PVP one" 03:08.

The Evidence

ETH leg roughly $17M → $164M+; Solana version never cleared ~$2M. Called live at 02:23–03:41, before the split was visible in price.

The Failure Mode

the same test would have told you to buy the older token in cases where the older token is abandoned, contract-compromised or has a hostile deployer. The test as stated has no liveness check. Add one: provenance is necessary, not sufficient.

🩻 The Venue Depth Check — Ninety Seconds Before You Size

Load the book, load a major on the same venue, compare spread as a percentage of price — and do it before the listing convinces you the token is legitimate.

The Practice (Step-by-Step):

  1. Open the order book, not the chart. 11:20
  2. Measure the top-of-book gap in dollars and as a percentage. "there's like $10,000 that separates this spread… and it's like 5% of the [thing]" 12:00.
  3. Run the control. Load BTC or ETH on the same venue and compare 12:18. This is the step that distinguishes a bad venue from a bad pair, and it takes seconds.
  4. Check whether the side you want is actually available. "they're not letting you long it" 86:53 — a listed pair is not necessarily a tradeable one.
  5. Do not read the listing as diligence. "so this is a legitimate team, right?" 11:20 is the inference to unlearn; open, stake-gated listing standards make a listing a payment, not an endorsement.

The Evidence

Aster's depth problem is independently documented for 2026 — the book thins above ~$100K notional, with roughly double Hyperliquid's execution cost at $500K size. The tape's live observation on a brand-new pair is the extreme end of the same distribution.

🧊 The Unrealised-Profit Overhang — Reading Other People's Wallets as a Sell Signal

A screen full of enormous unrealised gains is a distribution risk, not a validation.
