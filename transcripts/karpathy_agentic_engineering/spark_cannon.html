LLMs Are a New Kind of Computer, Not Better Software — Karpathy on Agentic Engineering


Source: Andrej Karpathy · Sequoia Capital · ~30 min

Domain: AI / Software Engineering / Philosophy of Mind

Mode: Full


Speakers:

  • **Andrej Karpathy** — Co-founder OpenAI, former Director of AI at Tesla, independent researcher; coined "vibe coding" and "Software 3.0"


    • Phase 1: Executive Summary


  • **December 2024 was a phase transition, not an incremental upgrade.** Karpathy identifies a specific month when agentic workflows stopped being useful-in-patches and started being reliably useful end-to-end. He stopped correcting model outputs. That shift — from tool to collaborator — is qualitatively distinct from prior AI capability curves. (01:17–02:23)

  • **Software 3.0 is not faster programming — it's a new computing paradigm.** Software 1.0 = code. Software 2.0 = learned weights/neural networks. Software 3.0 = prompting an LLM, where "what's in the context window is your lever over the interpreter." The programming language is now natural language. (02:50–03:36)

  • **Vibe coding raises the floor; agentic engineering preserves the ceiling.** Vibe coding lets anyone build anything. Agentic engineering is the professional discipline of going faster without introducing vulnerabilities, brittle code, or broken abstractions — while staying responsible for the output. (15:57–16:46)

  • **"You can outsource your thinking, but you can't outsource your understanding."** Karpathy's most important quote on education. The human remains the bottleneck for direction, taste, judgment, and system-level comprehension. Agents handle execution; humans must handle understanding. (28:12)

  • **LLMs are "ghosts," not animals.** They lack intrinsic motivation, curiosity, or evolution-shaped drives. They are statistical simulation circuits shaped by pre-training and RL. Understanding what they are (not) helps calibrate trust and usage. (23:41–25:15)

  • **Jagged intelligence is not a flaw — it's a diagnostic.** The car-wash paradox (Opus can refactor 100k-line codebases but recommends walking to a car wash 50m away) reveals that capabilities are determined by verifiability + lab training choices, not general intelligence. Knowing which "circuits" you're in determines whether to trust the model or fine-tune. (10:50–13:35)


    • Phase 2: Thematic Deep Dives


    🖥️ Software 3.0 — LLMs as a New Kind of Computer


    *"What's in the context window is your lever over the interpreter that is the LLM."* (03:26)

    Karpathy's taxonomy:

  • **Software 1.0:** Explicit rules written in code by humans
  • **Software 2.0:** Programs encoded as neural network weights, trained on data (introduced this framing ~2017)
  • **Software 3.0:** The LLM itself is the interpreter; the "code" is the context window; "programming" is prompting

    • The implications are radical. The OpenClaw installation example makes it concrete: instead of a complex shell script to handle all possible system configurations, OpenClaw's installation is a block of natural language you paste to your agent. The agent's intelligence handles the environment variation. A 1,000-line bash script becomes a 200-word prompt.


    The MenuGen example makes the paradigm shift visceral: Karpathy built a React/Vercel app to photograph restaurant menus, OCR them, and generate food photos. Then he saw someone give the photo directly to Gemini and say "use Nana Banana to overlay images onto the menu" — and it just worked, in-context, with no app. His entire codebase "shouldn't exist." It was working in the wrong paradigm.


    💡 **Key Insight:** Software 3.0 does not merely accelerate existing workflows — it invalidates entire categories of app. If a neural network can perform a transformation directly (photo → annotated menu), then an app that breaks that transformation into coded steps is not "slower Software 3.0" — it's the wrong abstraction entirely.

    🌐 **Consensus Check:** Karpathy's "Software 1.0/2.0/3.0" framework is influential but contested in ML research circles. Critics note that "3.0" conflates different phenomena (instruction-following, tool use, agentic behavior). However, as a practitioner mental model for when to stop writing code and start prompting, it has strong utility.


    🎯 Verifiability — The Engine of Jagged Intelligence


    *"Traditional computers can easily automate what you can specify in code. LLMs can easily automate what you can verify."* (10:02)

    Frontier labs train models through reinforcement learning, which requires a verification signal. Tasks with clear verifiable outcomes (code compiles/runs, math has a correct answer) generate training signal. Tasks without clear verification (aesthetic judgment, common sense) do not.


    This produces jagged intelligence:

  • Opus can refactor a 100,000-line codebase or find zero-day vulnerabilities
  • Opus will recommend walking to a car wash 50 metres away because your car needs to be clean when you arrive (a logical failure a child would catch)

    • The car-wash example is Karpathy's sharpest illustration of the jaggedness problem. The capability profile is not "generally smart" or "generally dumb" — it's a strange topography determined by:

  • Whether the domain has a verifiable signal
  • Whether the labs actually trained on that signal

    • Chess case study: GPT-3.5 to GPT-4 showed dramatic chess improvement — not from capability scaling, but because a huge amount of chess data entered the pre-training set. Someone at OpenAI made a curation decision, and a peak appeared.


    🎯 **Against Consensus:** The dominant narrative is that scaling laws produce smooth, general capability improvement. Karpathy's jaggedness framing says improvements are granular and domain-specific, driven by what labs choose to include in training distributions. This is a more pessimistic (and arguably more accurate) view of how capabilities actually manifest.

    🔗 **Connection:** Jaggedness → founder advice. If you're building a startup, find domains that are highly verifiable (you can create RL environments) AND that the major labs haven't prioritized. That's where fine-tuning can give you disproportionate capability that doesn't exist out-of-the-box.


    🏗️ Vibe Coding vs. Agentic Engineering — Two Distinct Disciplines


    *"Vibe coding is about raising the floor for everyone in terms of what they can do in software. Agentic engineering is about preserving the quality bar."* (15:57)

    | Dimension | Vibe Coding | Agentic Engineering |

    |---|---|---|

    | Who | Everyone | Professional engineers |

    | Goal | Build anything, fast | Build fast *without* sacrificing quality |

    | Accountability | None assumed | Full accountability maintained |

    | Code quality | Often bloated, copy-pasted | Clean, intentional, reviewable |

    | Security bar | Undefined | Professional standard |


    Agentic engineering as a discipline involves:

  • Coordinating agents that are "spiky entities — a bit fallible, a little stochastic, but extremely powerful"
  • Designing the spec/plan before delegating to agents
  • Maintaining oversight of aesthetics, architecture, and judgment
  • Catching agent mistakes that humans would never make (e.g., matching users by email address across Stripe and Google rather than using a persistent user ID)

    • 📜 **Analog:** Karpathy's "agentic engineer" resembles the historical role of the architect in construction — not laying bricks, but responsible for structural integrity, safety, and design coherence. The bricks (code) are laid by agents; the architect (engineer) must ensure the building holds.

    The MenuGen user ID bug: Karpathy's agent associated Stripe purchase credits with Google email addresses — without a persistent user ID. Different email addresses for the two services broke the association. A human engineer would never make this mistake; the agent made a "reasonable" but wrong assumption. The human must retain oversight of these system-level decisions.



    👻 Ghosts, Not Animals — What LLMs Actually Are


    *"Coming to terms with the fact that these things are not animal intelligences."* (24:36)

    Karpathy distinguishes:

  • **Animals**: driven by intrinsic motivation, curiosity, empowerment, hunger, fear — all shaped by evolution and embodied experience
  • **LLMs (ghosts)**: statistical simulation circuits. Substrate = pre-training statistics. Post-training = RL bolted on top. No intrinsic drives, no genuine curiosity, no self-preservation

    • Practical implications:

  • Yelling at them doesn't make them work better or worse (no emotional activation)
  • They don't have preferences; they simulate preferences
  • They are "at the mercy of whatever the labs are doing" — capabilities are determined by unseen training decisions
  • You must *explore* the tool: it has "no manual" and works in some settings but not others

    • 💡 **Key Insight:** Karpathy is doing philosophy for pragmatic reasons. If you model the LLM as an animal-like intelligence, you'll over-attribute reliability and be blindsided by failures. If you model it as a ghost (statistical simulation), you'll develop appropriate calibration: powerful in trained circuits, unreliable outside them.

    ⚡ **Tension:** Karpathy says "I don't know that I have here are the five obvious outcomes of how to make your system better" from this framework. He acknowledges it's partially philosophizing. The ghost/animal distinction is a mindset tool, not a decision algorithm. This makes it valuable as a culture-setter but not as a recipe.


    🎓 What Remains Worth Learning — Understanding Can't Be Outsourced


    *"You can outsource your thinking, but you can't outsource your understanding."* (28:12)

    Karpathy sees himself as a bottleneck:

  • He still must know *what* to build and *why* it's worth doing
  • He must direct his agents with precision
  • "Information still has to make it into my brain"
  • He is "becoming a bottleneck of just even knowing what we're trying to build"

    • What agents take over:

  • API detail recall (which argument is `dim` vs `axis`? Which is `reshape` vs `permute`?)
  • Boilerplate generation
  • Filling in implementation details

    • What humans must retain:

  • Understanding of *what the underlying system is doing* (e.g., knowing that a numpy array view shares storage vs. copy — even if you forgot the exact function name)
  • Aesthetic judgment / taste
  • System design and spec creation
  • Oversight of agent outputs

    • Knowledge base habit: Karpathy builds wikis from everything he reads, using LLMs to create different "projections" onto the same information. He reads an article; the LLM synthesizes it into his personal wiki; he asks questions. Each new projection yields insight. This is his operationalization of "outsource thinking, not understanding."


    🌐 **Consensus Check:** The claim that human understanding remains irreducibly necessary is contested at the frontier. Some AGI researchers argue that sufficiently powerful agents could replace even the "understanding" bottleneck. Karpathy's position is that this hasn't happened yet and he's uncertain it ever will. His framing is appropriate for the current moment.


    🔭 What's Next — Neural Computers and Agent-Native Infrastructure


    *"I don't want to do anything. What is the thing I should copy-paste to my agent?"* (25:57)

    Karpathy's two predictions:

  • **Neural computers** — eventually, neural nets become the host process; CPUs become co-processors. Most software doesn't need to exist; neural nets perform transformations directly on raw inputs/outputs
  • **Agent-native infrastructure** — everything is currently written for humans (documentation, APIs, UIs). This must be rewritten. The test: can you give an LLM a prompt and have it deploy MenuGen end-to-end without you touching settings menus?

    • The sensors + actuators framing: AI infrastructure needs to be decomposed into what the agent can sense (inputs) and actuate (outputs), then made legible. Everything in between — human-readable menus, clickable UIs, prose documentation — is friction.


    Agent representation: Karpathy expects agents to represent individuals and organizations: "I'll have my agent talk to your agent to figure out some of the details of our meetings."



    🤝 Hiring for Agentic Engineering — New Rubric Required


    *"If you're giving out puzzles to solve, this is still the old paradigm."* (18:44)

    Current hiring processes (LeetCode, algorithmic puzzles) test pre-agentic engineering skills. Karpathy proposes:


  • Give candidates a *large project*
  • Ask them to implement it using agents
  • Require it to be secure (red-team it with 10 Codex instances trying to break the deployed app)
  • Evaluate: did they coordinate agents well? Is the output quality professional? Did they catch agent mistakes?

    • The 10x engineer trope is obsolete. Karpathy sees people who are good at agentic engineering peaking at far higher multiples — he avoids giving a number but implies it's dramatically above 10x.



    Phase 3: Named Concept Cards


    🌀 Jagged Intelligence — The Car-Wash Paradox

    **One-Liner:** State-of-the-art models can find zero-day vulnerabilities and refactor 100k-line codebases, but recommend walking your car to a car wash 50 metres away — because common sense is outside the RL training distribution.

    What It Reveals: Capability is not smooth or general. It's a topography of peaks (verifiable, lab-prioritized domains) and valleys (unverifiable, deprioritized, or absent from training). This is diagnostic — it tells you which circuits you're in, and whether fine-tuning is needed.


    Practical application: Before deploying in any domain, systematically probe for the valleys. The model will not warn you when it's in a valley.


    👻 Ghosts vs. Animals — Calibrating Your Mental Model

    **One-Liner:** LLMs are not animal-like intelligences with drives and motivations; they are statistical simulation circuits shaped by data and rewards. Treating them as animals leads to over-attribution of reliability.

    Why It Matters: If you expect the model to care about your outcome, you'll stop checking its work. It doesn't care. It simulates caring. The distinction matters for how much you verify, how you structure prompts, and when you trust vs. audit.

    • Phase 5: Protocol Cheat Sheet


    📋 **Agentic Engineering Protocols:**
    - Write detailed spec before delegating to agents — they'll design wrong user ID systems if you don't
    - Maintain oversight of system-level decisions (auth, data relationships, security)
    - Probe for "valleys" in your domain before relying on model output
    - Keep API detail recall to agents; retain conceptual understanding yourself
    - Build personal knowledge bases: different projections onto the same data = insight
    - Hiring: give large project + red-team with agents, not algorithmic puzzles
    - Infrastructure: move toward agent-native (what to copy-paste to agent, not docs to read)
