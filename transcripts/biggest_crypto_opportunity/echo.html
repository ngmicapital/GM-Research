The Stablecoin Neobank Bet: KAST's Founder Says Cards Are A Loss Leader And Most Rivals Die Within 12 Months

Source When Shift Happens · Date n/a · 105 min
Domain Crypto & Markets
Reliability Mixed — headline company metrics (Series A size, valuation, 1M+ users, $100M revenue run-rate) match KAST's own March 2026 raise announcement, but growth figures are self-reported and two competitor benchmarks he cites are materially overstated.
Content Type Founder Interview
Full Mode

⚡ The Bottom Line

  • Raagulan Pathy left Circle because he decided the stablecoin issuer was the wrong layer to own. His thesis: issuer economics are hostage to the Fed funds rate, so the real prize is the consumer fintech sitting on top of the rails — and he says that thesis is now playing out as everyone races to copy it.
  • He predicts most crypto neobanks shut down within 12 months, and it is a unit-economics argument, not a snark. "Cards in themselves are not a profitable business" — the card is customer acquisition; the money is in ten other products, and building those requires infrastructure most card-wrapper startups will not fund.
  • KAST cancelled its token and is converting community points into tokenized equity instead — and he concedes it will be misunderstood for years. His defence is blunt: nearly every comparable token is down 90–95%, and supporting a token requires buybacks that starve the business.
  • The business grew roughly 3x through a 50% Bitcoin drawdown, which he attributes to serving a dollar TAM rather than a crypto TAM — he estimates crypto's addressable market at ~$2.5T against a US-dollar market roughly twenty times larger.
  • His market call: the low is in or near. Made with Bitcoin around $60K, he said "here or hereabouts is roughly going to be the low," expected a return to $100K by mid-2027 rather than January 2027, and said the four-year cycle is intact. BTC has since rallied to roughly $77.5K.

🔑 Key Claims (Ranked)

1. The defensible layer in stablecoins is the fiat plumbing, not the card and not the token ↳ Evidence: Pathy says direct instant payments into 50 countries currently run through 20–30 providers, with a team of ~100 people building the tech, compliance and banking partnerships behind it; he expects money movement alone to be 20–30% of revenue and refuses to white-label it. ↳ Caveat: This is the classic "our moat is that it's hard" argument, and it is unfalsifiable until a competitor tries and fails publicly. ↳ Consensus: Emerging — the market is still pricing distribution and card design, not payout rails.

2. The majority of crypto neobanks and card programmes will be dead within 12 months ↳ Evidence: "They can't make the economics work... they're just losing money and there's no way path that they can see to ever turning profitable." He says several multi-year players have already shut down. ↳ Caveat: He is the incumbent making the shakeout case; his own margin structure is not disclosed. ↳ Consensus: Contrarian for now — capital is still flowing into card launches from exchanges and wallets.

3. Cards are a loss leader; more than half of KAST's 2027 revenue is planned to come from non-card products ↳ Evidence: KAST for Business (a Ramp/Brex competitor, ~3,000 companies on the waitlist, now in beta), a lighter emerging-markets app, credit, trading, virtual USD/EUR accounts, and a Deel partnership for employer-of-record and payroll. ↳ Consensus: Aligned with how Coinbase and Robinhood actually report — both now disclose 12–13 separate business lines above $100M annualised.

4. Points became tokenized equity because a token launch would have starved the business ↳ Evidence: Supporting a token requires buybacks, which consume the cash needed to raise and build; he says existing shareholders took dilution to create room for the community allocation, targeted for Q4 conversion at the then-current share price, roughly one-for-one on notional value. ↳ Caveat: Users earned those points against fees of 1.5–2% on the explicit expectation of a liquid token; there is no listing planned, and liquidity would come only from periodic private buybacks every 6–12 months. ↳ Consensus: Contrarian — almost no consumer crypto company has chosen equity over a token at this stage.

5. Crypto token structures give founders three pockets and the community one bag ↳ Evidence: His account — founders keep equity, receive token allocations, and control the foundation treasury, which publishes nothing; exits happen through OTC deals rather than the open market. ↳ Consensus: Aligned with the observable pattern, rarely stated this plainly by someone who could have taken the same deal.

6. The deposit-ownership blow-up was a drafting failure, not a business model ↳ Evidence: KAST's terms said transferring assets in was a sale with ownership passing to KAST; after a public fight with Ether.fi's CEO, the terms were rewritten to promise redemption of unspent balances, and Pathy points to licensed custodians, bankruptcy-remote structuring and a Big Four three-month due diligence during the Series A. ↳ Caveat: Most of the platform remains custodial today; the self-custodial option was still unreleased at recording. ↳ Consensus: Contested — the incident is exactly the trust risk he names as the thing that could kill the company.

7. Non-custodial is not self-custodial, and the industry blurs it deliberately ↳ Evidence: Pathy's distinction — non-custodial means you can see the balance on-chain inside a smart contract but cannot export the keys; self-custodial means you hold them. Card authorisation risk is the reason competitors stop at non-custodial: a user could pull funds between swipe and settlement. ↳ Consensus: Emerging — a genuinely useful test to apply to every "non-custodial" card on the market.

8. Cost discipline is a deliberate cultural fight, not an accounting exercise ↳ Evidence: A software audit found 80 SaaS tools; he ordered 20 cut without specifying which. His framing: six months of that waste equalled the company's entire early funding. ↳ Consensus: Aligned.

9. Crypto's addressable market is a rounding error next to the dollar's ↳ Evidence: ~$2.5T crypto TAM versus a US-dollar TAM he puts at roughly twenty times that; more than half of KAST's users are not crypto-native. ↳ Caveat: "TAM of the US dollar" is a rhetorical figure, not a measured market. ↳ Consensus: Aligned with the 2026 stablecoin-as-payments thesis.

10. The bottom is in, or close — but not on the January 2027 timeline traders are pricing ↳ Evidence: "Bitcoin is 60, it may if some really bad goes go down towards 50... here or hereabouts is roughly going to be the low." He argued the market absorbed war, the AI funding drain and Michael Saylor's positioning and still held, and that boredom is the base for a rebound. ↳ Caveat: He volunteers that he is "terrible at exact market timing." ↳ Consensus: Contrarian at the time — barely 10% of Kalshi traders had BTC above $100K before January 2027.
