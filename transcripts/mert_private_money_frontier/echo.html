Privacy Is Crypto's Last Uncontested Market — And Zcash Answered A Counterfeiting Bug With A Mathematical Proof

Source The Rollup · Date n/a · 43 min
Domain Crypto & Markets
Reliability Expert opinion, technically strong — Ojha is a primary source on the cryptography and Mumtaz on ecosystem structure, but both are ZEC holders and the episode takes no bear case; two of Mumtaz's factual asides are already wrong.
Content Type Technical Interview
Full Mode

⚡ The Bottom Line

  • The Orchard counterfeiting vulnerability was found because Zcash went looking for it, and the fix was not a patch but a mathematical proof. Multiple teams formally verified that the new Ironwood circuit's implementation matches its spec, which converts "could there be a second undetected bug?" from a judgement call about auditor competence into a claim that would require the proof engine itself to be false.
  • The turnstile is the reason a soundness bug in a $1.7 billion shielded pool was survivable. Because value crossing between pools is metered, the blast radius was bounded to Orchard rather than Zcash's whole supply — and because the turnstile did not trigger during migration, the team can now say with high confidence the bug was never exploited.
  • Ojha's ranking of the quantum problem is the most useful framework in the episode, and the top item is not cryptography. It is commitment to a plan. Second is whether you can hold funds safely without acting; Zcash solved that in Ironwood, Bitcoin has not. Privacy against a quantum adversary is third, and the final upgrade is fourth because frozen pools make it a non-event.
  • Mumtaz's strategic framing is that privacy is crypto's last PvE — the only market where nobody is competing. "Everybody's trying to be a trading app or a launchpad... but who's trying to be private money?" He runs Helius on Solana and holds ZEC personally, and treats the two as non-rival.
  • There is no Solana privacy pitch in this episode at all — which is itself the finding. Ojha says the choice was explicitly between building a privacy layer on Solana and making Zcash win, and they chose Zcash because "uncompromising privacy... has to be at the base layer." Neither man argues for L1-privacy-as-a-feature.

🔑 Key Claims (Ranked)

1. Formal verification changes the risk class of a ZK privacy chain, not just its bug count ↳ Evidence: The proof obligation reduces to a simple statement — if a payment's proof passes, the spender owned the notes and total inputs equal total outputs, so spending five ZEC cannot produce more than five ZEC out 12:44–13:04. ↳ Caveat: It proves implementation-matches-spec. A wrong spec would still be wrong, and Ojha says Zcash "has never had a problem where we've designed a wrong spec" — which is a claim about the past, not a guarantee. ↳ Consensus: Emerging — formal verification of production ZK circuits at this scale is genuinely uncommon.

2. Commitment to a plan is the number-one quantum problem, ahead of any cryptography ↳ Evidence: Ojha's four-rung ladder places plan-commitment first and hold-safely-without-acting second; both are solved in Zcash, while in Bitcoin "it's still kind of unclear — are we adding a new signature? What are we going to do?" 24:17–24:37. ↳ Caveat: Mumtaz undercuts the urgency himself, saying he expects Bitcoin will be ready in time and that quantum has "always the physical layer which you just cannot necessarily speed up." ↳ Consensus: Contrarian framing, aligned conclusion.

3. Privacy is the only crypto category with no real competition ↳ Evidence: "Everybody's trying to be like a trading app or a launchpad... but who's trying to be private money?" — plus an anonymity-set network effect that makes liquidity hard to siphon once it exists 17:49–18:10. ↳ Caveat: Pure market-structure reasoning with no demand-side evidence beyond anecdote. ↳ Consensus: Contrarian.

4. Tachyon eliminates node compute and state size as scaling dimensions entirely ↳ Evidence: Recursive proofs via Sean Bowe's accumulation technique cut transaction size by over 5x and, per Ojha, remove node processing time and state growth as constraints — leaving only consensus bandwidth and wallets 21:13–21:43. ↳ Caveat: Not shipped. Ojha's separate target of 50,000+ TPS at sub-second finality under pure proof-of-work in two to three years is a stated ambition, not a roadmap commitment. ↳ Consensus: Emerging.

5. Wallet sync goes from O(n) to O(1), which is the actual adoption blocker ↳ Evidence: "Today, if we had a million transactions, you'd have to scan a million things. In the Tachyon world, it's O(1). No scanning at all" — with a 6x interim speedup shipped the same day as the recording 22:52–23:28. ↳ Consensus: Aligned — every practitioner in this cluster names the wallet layer, not the protocol, as the frontier.

6. Decentralisation means coordination without a middleman, not the absence of coordination ↳ Evidence: Mumtaz's answer to why Zcash ships upgrades Bitcoin cannot — "one of the main fallacies in crypto is conflating decentralization with being uncoordinated"; Zcash's original launch post established a culture of updating, with explicit carve-outs for what must never change 30:09–33:35. ↳ Consensus: Contrarian in Bitcoin circles, obvious everywhere else.

7. A treasury company taking ~18% of hashrate is decentralising, not centralising ↳ Evidence: Mining concentration is already high and opaque everywhere; adding a publicly listed miner that posts its position on every governance vote is "one extra miner" plus governance transparency 35:16–37:00. ↳ Caveat: This is Cypherpunk Technologies, which is also one of the largest ZEC holders and an investor in ZODL — concentration across three layers at once, which neither speaker addresses. ↳ Consensus: Contrarian.

8. The distribution is unusually clean because ZEC was "down only" for nine years ↳ Evidence: Proof-of-work launch in 2016, no high-FDV low-float structure, no VC unlock cliffs; "the only people remaining are the people who actually believe in the project and picked it up on the open market" 38:30–39:51. ↳ Caveat: Elides the Founders' Reward — 20% of first-era issuance, roughly 2.1M ZEC, to founders, employees and investors — which is exactly the kind of allocation the argument claims Zcash does not have. ↳ Consensus: Aligned on the mechanism, incomplete on the history.
