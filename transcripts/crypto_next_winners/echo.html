Crypto’s Next Winners Won’t Be Blockchains

Domain Crypto Markets — Token Valuation & Value Accrual
Reliability Expert explainer, data-backed on the descriptive claims — Barack builds explicit revenue-and-burn models and shows his assumptions, including the weak ones; the price targets are his own forward projections and should be read as such.

Full Mode

⚡ The Bottom Line

  • The title is a revenue statistic, not a slogan. For most of crypto's history, execution-layer infrastructure produced 95%+ of total protocol revenue. Today applications produce roughly two thirds and execution layers roughly a third — and Austin Barack expects applications to reach 90%+. The surviving token categories, in his framing, are applications and money.
  • This inverts the fat-protocol thesis, and Ethereum's own data is the evidence. Joel Monegro's 2016 argument was that crypto reverses the internet's thin-protocol/fat-application pattern. Since the Dencun upgrade moved rollup data to blobs in March 2024, Ethereum's L1 revenue has fallen roughly 75% year on year — about $157m in August 2023 to about $39m in August 2025 — while the applications on top kept growing.
  • Barack's method is ordinary equity analysis applied to tokens, and the discipline is the transferable part. Build the revenue line, subtract cost of goods and opex, and only then ask what is genuinely burnable. His sharpest observation: a protocol burning 8% of revenue at a 10% EBITDA margin is burning most of its free cash flow, not making a gesture.
  • Buybacks in crypto are functioning as trust instruments, not capital-allocation decisions — and the CLARITY Act's failure makes that permanent. Both speakers treat the bill as the coming fix that will let tokens legally accrue value. On 15 September 2026, eight days after this episode published, it failed a Senate cloture vote 49–50.

🔑 Key Claims (Ranked)

1. Revenue has already migrated from the chain layer to the application layer, and the trend continues ↳ Evidence: Barack cites a Blockworks chart — execution-layer infra once produced 95%+ of crypto revenue; applications now produce roughly two thirds 63:36. Corroborating: Pump.fun and Hyperliquid together accounted for close to 90% of 2026's ~$638m of token buybacks; both are applications. ↳ Caveat: this measures fee capture, not value creation. A chain running at near-zero margin while securing hundreds of billions of application value is a utility that priced itself like one — which was the design goal. ↳ Context: it is the fat-protocol thesis running backwards, and the mechanism is abundance. Fat protocols was a theory about scarce blockspace, and the industry spent eight years engineering the scarcity away.

2. Burn-to-revenue and burn-to-earnings are different ratios, and only the second tells you anything ↳ Evidence: "people say they're burning 8% of revenue" — but at 50% gross margins and 10% EBITDA margins, that 8% is the large majority of free cash flow 11:31. ↳ Context: it is the single most portable analytical point in the episode, and it separates an AI business with real inference costs from a matching engine with near-100% margins.

3. Venice is the thesis in its purest form — and the most assumption-heavy model on the table ↳ Evidence: ~$107m annualised revenue and ~$8.3m annualised burns as of August; $336m and $70m projected for 2027; a 50x multiple on burns implying $3.5bn and a $43.89 target against ~$16 spot 12:36. ↳ Caveat: $29m of the $70m — 40% — comes from Mines, a product that was not live at recording. Barack grades his own model a six out of ten on the optimism scale 32:34. ↳ Context: verified independently, Venice raised $65m at a $1bn valuation on 1 July 2026, led by Dragonfly, and was already profitable at above $70m annualised run-rate.

4. The buyback is a costly signal substituting for a legal guarantee ↳ Evidence: "you need to walk the walk if you're going to say that pre-clarity act" 27:14. Venice has burned low single-digit millions while raising $65m — ten to twenty times the burn — explicitly so it can do both. ↳ Context: the CLARITY Act failed a Senate cloture vote 49–50 on 15 September 2026, so programmatic burns and token warrants are not a bridge to enforceability; they are the standing architecture.

5. Pump's valuation gap is a governance discount, and it comes with a clean test attached ↳ Evidence: ~5x buybacks against 30–40x for Hyperliquid and Lighter, despite leading Hyperliquid on trailing-30-day revenue. The 50% buyback commitment is guaranteed for twelve months and subject to renewal; Barack puts fair value at 6–10x or 10–14x depending on how you handicap that 62:27. ↳ Caveat: his durability argument rests on rejecting the OpenSea comparison after two-plus years of growing revenue — a judgement, not a proof. ↳ Context: the negative-EV objection to memecoin trading proves too much. Lotteries are enormous and durable precisely because people knowingly accept a negative edge for variance.

6. Hyperliquid's growth engine and its earnings engine are now two different products ↳ Evidence: Barack notes HIP-3 RWA markets drove the re-rating on volume while generating little revenue, with crypto perps remaining the cash cow 42:03. Published H1 2026 figures confirm it: $419.3m gross fees, up 31% year on year, with crypto perps at 82.7% and HIP-3 at 11.2% despite roughly half the volume. ↳ Context: the quietly bigger claim is that bankers setting IPO prices will look at a Hyperliquid screen — pre-IPO price discovery for SpaceX, Cerebras and Unitree has run there 46:18.

7. Ether.fi has already changed business and the market is still pricing the old one ↳ Evidence: 65%+ of revenue now from credit-card and borrow activity, ~35% from yield and staking and falling; card volume up from $300k a day to $3–4m a day year on year 52:01. Interest income is 4% of revenue against 60–70% at Nubank. ↳ Caveat: the thesis depends on the neo-brokerage line hockey-sticking rather than plateauing, and on buybacks that only began programmatically in August 2026. ↳ Context: it fell 10%+ alongside Lido on the EIP-8363 issuance-reduction headline — a staking-policy event repricing a business for which staking is now a minority of revenue.
