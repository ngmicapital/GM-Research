A four-month-old conversation.

⚡ The Bottom Line

  • The survival method in a video titled "how to survive trading crypto" is to stop holding crypto. After a 2022 wipeout he moved the core off-venue — a wealth manager at "five, six, seven percent a year" plus untouched cold-storage BTC. The live on-chain balance he admits to is ~40 SOL (~$3.4k).
  • His biggest win in six months arrived while he was playing a video game, and he refuses to call it skill. ASTEROID, taken on Ethereum, a seven-figure win, still unsold — flagged by a friend in a voice call while he was mid-match. He calls the trade "Mickey Mouse."
  • His only causal model for who wins is starting capital, not talent. His group's best "went into this cycle with the most capital" after working hardest through the 2023 bear; skill appears once, as an unexplained residual.
  • He tells his host, live, that his host's commercial partner's app is a gimmick — and that he is $250,000 down on it. The host's reply: "we could disagree." Nothing else in the interview is that unmanaged.
  • His explanation for why a narrative runs on Ethereum and dies on Solana is about float, not culture: a fresh 15–20% supply grab is priced as imminent selling and caps the coin; a two-year-dormant whale is priced as inert.

🔑 Key Claims (Ranked)

1. Ring-fence the capital before you need to; presence does the rest.
↳ Evidence: a 2022 round trip "well over six figures" on NFTs, caused by holding no stables and no off-ramp; fixed structurally, not tactically.
↳ Caveat: he advises daily attendance while running ~$3.4k on-chain.
↳ Consensus: Aligned — standard risk-of-ruin practice, unusual only in a memecoin context.

2. Multi-chain narratives resolve to whichever chain's float is priced as inert.
↳ Evidence: ASTEROID's Ethereum leg ran ~$16M → $164.2M while the Solana version peaked in single-digit millions; he called it live at ~$6M.
↳ Caveat: one worked example, one named exception.
↳ Consensus: Ahead of the standard "chain culture" framing — a supply-overhang argument in memecoin clothing.

3. The edge is a small voice call, not screen time.
↳ Evidence: 4–5 hours/day of group overlap replaced 16-hour solo monitoring; the seven-figure trade arrived as a spoken sentence from a friend.
↳ Consensus: Contrarian to the retail model of paid signal groups and six monitors — corroborated by Cupsey in this archive.

4. Position-taking beats grinding on return-per-hour, and he says so about named peers.
↳ Evidence: Cented ≈ half a million a month for ~12-hour days, versus Trader Pow taking one narrative for a comparable number at near-zero hours (Pow's figure is Qwerty's flagged guess).

5. Perps are a permanent, named loss he does not expect to recover.
↳ Evidence: "lifetime perps" red after 10 October plus a month of emotional trading; his one big leveraged win (TAO) he attributes to no skill.
↳ Caveat: he still takes leveraged shots — the diagnosis did not change the behaviour.

6. PUMP at ~$0.0017 was a deep-value bear-market buy — a call he did not take himself.
↳ Evidence: buybacks at ~35% of supply, $1.32B raised in the July 2025 sales, no successor category. Scoreboard: PUMP $0.0041889 on 4 Sep 2026, ≈ +145%.
↳ Caveat: he admits "I don't know if I've read a pump fun update in like a long time" — and appears not to know that Terminal, his own Solana execution venue, is pump.fun.

7. Never tell a named individual what to buy.
↳ Evidence: he recommended Popcat at ~$1B cap to someone from his hometown; the lesson is asymmetry of consequence, not error.
↳ Caveat: the host punctures it — his "don't buy XRP at $2.50" was followed by a 600% move.

8. The 35%-of-supply low-cap strategy is rational, corrosive, and he will not condemn it.
↳ Evidence: he argues it is why the Solana ASTEROID couldn't run, then declines to call it wrong and exits instead.
↳ Consensus: Aligned with the venue's other practitioners, who name the same mechanism and keep trading.
