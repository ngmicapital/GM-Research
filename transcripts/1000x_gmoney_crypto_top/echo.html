MARKET UPDATE: Live With GMoney, Inflation Is In, Is This The Crypto Top?

Source 1000x · 2026-09-11 · 41 min
Domain Crypto Markets & Macro
Reliability Mixed — an active trader interviewing another active trader on a Friday market session, both talking their own books; positions in Nvidia, CryptoPunks, Quotrons and Fake World Assets are named on tape, GMoney calls Quotrons "probably my biggest bag on chain," and the show closes with 1KX Media's standard disclaimer that hosts, guests and team may hold positions in anything discussed.
Content Type Guest interview / live market reaction
Full Mode

⚡ The Bottom Line

  • The title's question never gets a direct answer, and the implied answer is no. Neither Felman nor GMoney calls a top. Both are adding risk — GMoney into AI semis and small on-chain protocols, Felman into chipmakers he says the market had abandoned a week earlier.
  • GMoney's macro framework is one sentence: if AI is national defence, the capex cycle cannot be allowed to stop. A hike is coming because Kevin Warsh has to protect his credibility, but it will not be a long cycle, because Washington will do whatever it takes to keep the spend going.
  • His tell is the intervention itself. Coordinated yen buying and Treasury purchases of the long end came while the S&P was roughly 5% from all-time highs — the sort of thing you do 20–30% off the highs, not near them. Gold and Bitcoin caught the bid because the market "sniffed them out."
  • Felman's most contrarian claim is that hyperscaler CDS spreads are meaningless. His argument: Google, Microsoft and Meta carry essentially US-government credit risk, they are not levered financial institutions, and widening spreads always come back in.
  • The on-chain half of the episode is about the same idea as the macro half — where new yield comes from. Tokenized equities, an NFT gacha protocol that pays holders for lending their JPEGs to the house, and a token that burns into an NFT which then earns tokenized stock.

🔑 Key Claims (Ranked)

1. The Fed hikes at the next meeting, but it is not the start of a cycle ↳ Evidence: GMoney — "Warsh needs to maintain his credibility, so we've got to hike at the next meeting… I don't think it lasts long" (19:26, 20:07). ↳ Caveat: He says "it seems certain" only after the morning's CPI print moved the odds, not before 20:01. ↳ Context: The Fed hiked 25bp five days later; the median projection then pointed to another hike this year, which is closer to a cycle than his call allowed.

2. Intervening while the S&P is 5% from highs is the signal, not the hike ↳ Evidence: "Why the hell is the US government doing intervention when the S&P is 5% from all-time highs?" — you do that at 20–30% drawdowns (19:26, 19:37). ↳ Context: Japan spent roughly $34bn on a single day in early August defending the yen, with the US Treasury participating directly — an unusual step — and Treasury then doubled its own long-end buybacks.

3. AI capex contagion, if it came, would be contained in days rather than years ↳ Evidence: GMoney's timeline comparison — Countrywide to Lehman ran about two years; Silicon Valley Bank took about two weeks; an AI credit event gets handled "within a week, probably within like 48 hours" (22:12, 22:36). ↳ Caveat: He is explicit that he is playing devil's advocate against his own bullish position 22:56. ↳ Context: The policy reflex is real, but the leverage is growing — hyperscalers issued roughly $182bn of bonds in 2026, a step change from the prior year.

4. Hyperscaler CDS spreads are noise ↳ Evidence: Felman — Google, Microsoft and Meta credit risk is "basically equivalent… to US government credit risk," and they are not running a balance-sheet mismatch (23:46, 24:10). ↳ Caveat: He concedes this is a minority view and that people he has argued it with "think I'm totally nuts" 23:53. ↳ Context: Oracle is the outlier the market is actually repricing — its five-year CDS went from roughly 40bp in mid-2025 to about 180bp — while Meta sits near 75bp, which is closer to Felman's read than Oracle's.

5. Nvidia's neocloud financing turns it into a royalty business ↳ Evidence: GMoney — by financing the capex it now holds residuals across the neoclouds, producing "very capital light revenue" that should carry a higher multiple (25:26, 25:48). ↳ Context: Nvidia reported $96.2bn of revenue for the July quarter, up 106% year over year, and guided to $108bn — and the stock had consolidated for roughly two years going in.

6. Tokenized equities are the next issuer land-grab ↳ Evidence: "We're going to see this massive race between all these issuers trying to dominate tokenized stocks the way Circle and Tether have really dominated stablecoins" 09:24. ↳ Context: Four issuers already anchor the category — Backed's xStocks, Dinari, Robinhood Chain and Kraken, which bought Backed outright; Kraken launched yield-bearing xStocks vaults three days after this taping.

7. Memecoins are an onboarding funnel, not an asset class ↳ Evidence: Felman treats them as conversion tools — easier to get someone to fund a wallet for a lottery ticket than for an index (09:40, 10:02); GMoney will not size them because he cannot hold conviction and refuses the exit-liquidity culture (08:28, 11:45). ↳ Caveat: Both are commercially exposed to the funnel they are describing.

8. Investing is becoming a US national security issue ↳ Evidence: Felman's argument — AI will produce enormous wealth inside a decade, and a large uninvested population becomes "incredibly discontent" and causes societal problems (33:06, 33:26). ↳ Context: This is the mirror image of GMoney's "hyper-unemployment means hyper-gambling, so own the casinos" 32:34.
