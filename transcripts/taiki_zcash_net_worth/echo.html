⚡ The Bottom Line

  • The thesis in one sentence: Zcash is the last unsolved Bitcoin criticism. Programmability produced Ethereum and Solana; privacy is still open, and he argues ZEC closes it.
  • The trade is a ratio, not a price: ZEC at ~1% of Bitcoin's market cap going to 10–15%, explicitly modelled on silver's ~12% share of gold. Verified: ZEC/BTC is 1.04% and silver/gold is 12.07% — both of his anchor numbers are exact.
  • His fundamental — the shielded pool — is reflexive by arithmetic, not narrative. Privacy quality is your share of the anonymity set, so a higher price at a constant coin count is literally a better product. His strongest argument.
  • The most useful segment is the confession. He bought under $400, sized into the June Orchard bug, sold under $300 near the exact low, then bought back at $850+ deliberately. Every checkable detail verifies.
  • He states quantum resistance as a present-tense ZEC property. It is not. Orchard/Halo 2 runs on the Pallas curve — discrete-log based, exactly what Shor's algorithm breaks. Zcash has the better roadmap; neither chain is quantum-safe today.

🔑 Key Claims (Ranked)

1. ZEC re-rates from ~1% to 10–15% of Bitcoin's market cap; that gap is where the return lives.
↳ Evidence: silver holds 12% of gold's cap; Bitcoin has never had a credible second store-of-value; ZEC has a real functional difference.
↳ Caveat: at a fixed BTC cap, 10% implies ~$9,640/ZEC (~9.6x). ZEC has been above 2% of BTC for essentially none of its ten-year life.
↳ Consensus: Contrarian. He concedes it "hasn't happened yet" and answers with reflexivity rather than evidence.

2. The shielded pool is the fundamental, and it is self-reinforcing.
↳ Evidence: verified — 4,878,432 ZEC (28.8% of supply, ~$4.6B) shielded as of 3 Sept 2026, up from 23.1% a year ago and 7.6% five years ago.
↳ Caveat: the Ironwood upgrade (28 July 2026) sealed Orchard and reopened the pool at zero — any single-pool chart crossing July is an artefact. And Grayscale's ETF custodies in transparent addresses, so institutional inflows mechanically depress the very ratio he calls the demand signal.
↳ Consensus: Growth is real; neutral analysts credit shielded-by-default wallets and the SEC's January 2026 no-action outcome, not a cypherpunk awakening.

3. "Same 21M supply as Bitcoin."
↳ Evidence: true as a terminal cap.
↳ Caveat: misleading as present scarcity. ~16.91M circulating vs BTC's ~19.9M — ~4.1M (~24% of float) still to be issued, versus ~5% for Bitcoin. Plus 20% of the block subsidy is diverted under ZIP 1015 (8% grants / 12% lockbox); a 20% Founders' Reward ran 2016–2020.

4. "Zcash is far ahead of Bitcoin on a quantum roadmap."
↳ Evidence: partly right. Tachyon hit testnet 22 May 2026 with Orchard quantum recoverability; ML-KEM (FIPS 203) is committed; hybrid PQ signatures and PQ-extended Unified Addresses specified.
↳ Caveat: shielded pools are not post-quantum today (Pallas/Halo 2 = discrete log). Bitcoin has BIP-360 merged Feb 2026 and BIP-361 published 14 Apr 2026. "Far ahead on roadmap" is fair; "Zcash fits that need" as a live property is not.

5. Ray Dalio's objections to Bitcoin describe Zcash.
↳ Evidence: verified. On All-In (March 2026) Dalio cited no privacy ("any transaction can be monitored and, perhaps, directly controlled") and quantum risk as reasons Bitcoin isn't gold.
↳ Caveat: the inference doesn't follow. Dalio holds ~1% BTC and recommended a 15% bitcoin-or-gold allocation in July 2026 — allocated and unenthusiastic, not sidelined awaiting a private alternative.

6. The June Orchard bug made Zcash antifragile.
↳ Evidence: verified — Taylor Hornby found an under-constrained Orchard Action circuit on 29 May 2026 (Shielded Labs audit, using Anthropic's Opus 4.8); live since May 2022; NU6.2 fork 3 June. ZEC ~-60%, ~$629 to ~$254.
↳ Caveat: Shielded Labs stated there is "no definitive way to determine" whether it was exploited — because of Orchard's own privacy properties. That is his trilemma cashing a cheque, not a clean survival story.

7. Position construction: size × % gain, so second-through-fifth-best ideas get zero.
↳ Evidence: Druckenmiller pyramiding; PTJ's "losers average losers"; Hougaard's Best Loser Wins (verified — Harriman House, 2022; his summary is faithful).
↳ Caveat: he says stops are in place and he won't "go down with the ship" — but never names a level, and his other rule forbids selling winners. On an asset that just did -60% in a day, an unnamed stop is decorative.
