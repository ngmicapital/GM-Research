⚡ The Bottom Line

  • The most successful person in the room says the venue is unfixable, and has no incentive to lie about it. Asked whether the trenches are in a good spot: "No. Absolutely horrible." He earns his whole income there, 14 hours a day, and still can't construct a bull case.
  • His ~$9M P&L contains no large trade. Biggest ever ~$200k, then ~$100k, then a scatter of ~$50k — and 145 losses against 100 wins the day before recording. Small positive-expectancy bets, not winner selection.
  • His bear case is extractive, not cyclical. Deployers hold ~30% of supply, extract ~200 SOL near a $100–130k mcap, and "that liquidity's never being put back into the market." A cyclical bear recovers; a structural leak doesn't.

🔑 Key Claims (Ranked)

1. The trenches are structurally broken, not cyclically weak.
↳ Evidence: "No. Absolutely horrible" (14:23). The fix needs "mass change from the people, top wallets, trading terminals… devs" — every party acting against its own incentive (38:48).
↳ Consensus: Aligned, if anything understated. Pump.fun graduation rate ~0.26% by mid-Jun 2026; Solana daily fees −84% Jan→Jun 2026.

2. Deployers run a permanent liquidity drain via block-zero bundles.
↳ Evidence: a 3–5 SOL dev at ~15%, plus 2–3 sibling wallets at ~1.5 SOL each in the same block; sell the visible dev wallet for optics, hold the real position at floor. "They have all the supply and then there's no risk." (19:39–19:51)
↳ Caveat: the "never comes back" leg is asserted, never measured. Jito atomic block-0 bundles are, however, documented practice — the mechanics check out.

3. He loses the majority of his trades.
↳ Evidence: "I've won 100 trades and I've lost 145 in the last day" (10:28); 10 SOL into goon coin at 29k, out at 55k for ~9 SOL while it ran to 70k without him (10:39).
↳ Consensus: Corroborated. A tracked 7-day window: 13,029 trades / 30.2% hit rate / +$143,079. His posted Axiom P&L — $53M bought / $63M sold / ~19% — is a grinder's ratio, not a sniper's.

4. Vamping is a prisoner's dilemma with the payoff matrix stated aloud.
↳ Evidence: "I fully agree [it's indefensible] but… I can't buy the main coin" (15:33); universal now — "it's everyone vamping" (16:09).
↳ Caveat: neither speaker connects vamping to falling ceilings, though it is the arithmetic cause — each vamp splits a narrative's attention.

5. Creator fees are dead; platform incentive modes replaced them.
↳ Evidence: ~$30k lifetime on creator fees; "fees are like useless now" (21:16–21:21). The meta sorts lexically: "anything with AI… goes on agent mode, and anything else goes on cashback, and then projects go on dev fees" (22:26).
↳ Consensus: Verified. Mayhem Mode launched 12 Nov 2025 — mints +1B tokens, an AI agent trades randomly 24h, then burns the unsold remainder, so its headline P&L is unrealisable inventory. His "last three days" dates the spam wave, not the feature (23:36).

6. The tracked wallet is not the load-bearing edge.
↳ Evidence: streams roughly half the days, "just cuz I'm bored"; off-stream P&L "about the same, maybe a little more" (09:08), because he vamps more freely off camera (09:24).
↳ Caveat: unresolved. He concedes streaming "isn't fair" (08:57) while denying it's where his money comes from. Nobody tests the claim.

7. Pump.fun clears ~$1M/day from a market both men call dead — and neither gets the trend.
↳ Evidence: seven days read live off fees.pump.fun — $832k to $1.5M (25:01). Cented: "the ceiling might be lower, but there's still a bunch of people trading… a lot of the same people."
↳ Consensus: Level right, framing wrong. That $1M is the floor of an ~83% collapse from ~$4.8M/day in Jan 2026 — flat churn revenue is a closed loop paying a toll booth. Separately: the chat's "Axiom has 80% market share" (28:12) is a year stale (~44.6% by Jun 2026), and "Terminal" is the ex-Padre, bought by pump.fun in Oct 2025 — unmentioned by either speaker, including the ex-pump.fun host.
