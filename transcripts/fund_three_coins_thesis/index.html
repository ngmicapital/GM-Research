⚡ The Bottom Line

  • Jain's stated reason for liking Zcash is the one most analysts would call a disqualifier: it has no fundamentals. No cash flow, no revenue, nothing to anchor a multiple to — "so that means that it's worth whatever people say it's worth. That gives you a lot more upside." His target is positional, not numerical: top five by market cap.
  • Multicoin published a $319 base case for HYPE by 2028, built on ~$8 billion of annual earnings at a 20x multiple — and Jain defends all four assumptions as conservative on air. The sharpest one: Hyperliquid holds 30% of perp DEX volume but 59% of open interest, and he argues open interest is the harder number to fake.
  • He is explicitly not a Solana maxi and owns both SOL and HYPE in size, framing them as a bet on spot versus derivatives. His argument for Solana keeping the tokenised-securities business is credible neutrality: "Goldman's not going to settle on Stripe's chain."
  • His screen for every decision is which of four edges is driving it — access, analytical, behavioural, structural — and he says the Zcash trade was almost entirely behavioural. No analytical edge, no information edge beyond calling holders to test whether they still wanted to talk about it.
  • The follow-up segment, recorded weeks after the main interview, has him buying more Zcash into a 50–60% single-day drawdown after the Orchard bug disclosure, on the specific evidence that the turnstile was not draining. That is the most instructive piece of process reasoning in the episode — a falsifiable test, applied with a several-day cooling-off period.

🌅 The Turning-Point Call: Peak Apathy, And Bad News That No Longer Moves Price

Jain opens with a market call, and the mechanism is more interesting than the conclusion. He identifies turning points by three things: sentiment fully bottoming out, bad news failing to send markets down, and good news failing to send them up 02:56.

His concrete instance: "We had some pretty bad news this last month with some pretty significant hacks and that didn't cause a bunch of sell-offs. That was a big sign right there" 03:25. Add "adoption tailwinds... where there's just been this disconnect between price and what's fundamentally happening," and he calls it a perfect storm 03:37.

💡 Key InsightThe diagnostic is asymmetric information response, not sentiment level. A market that has stopped reacting to news in the direction of the prevailing trend has exhausted the marginal seller — which is a structural observation rather than a mood reading, and is checkable in real time. 03:12

Kevin presses on the harder version: how do you buy before the bottom is confirmed? Jain's answer is psychological rather than technical:

"One of the signs for me that works really well for me is when do I feel like an idiot for being in the industry. And when I feel really dumb, I know I should probably buy. And when I feel like an absolute genius, probably should take something off the table." 04:42

He is careful that this is not repeatable market timing — "I don't think you can pick the exact moment, that would require magic, it's impossible" 04:29 — and grounds it in self-knowledge accumulated over cycles 04:57. The second input is social: talk to other market participants, because "it's the same people, they've been around for a while" 05:10. Asked which side is harder, he says both, then concedes his own asymmetry: "For me, it is easier to identify when I feel like an idiot" 05:43.

In the follow-up segment, recorded weeks later, he gives the fuller version:

🎯 Against Consensus"With the current macro backdrop, assuming there's no extrinsic factor, I do think the bottom is in because we've seen peak apathy" 75:34. His important qualifier is the shape: "This is unlikely to be like a sharp V-shaped bottom. We rarely see those in this market. What we usually see is a collapse and then everyone gets bored and goes away. And like we're clearly in that phase right now" 76:24. He also names the specific macro exit — a US–Iran escalation taking all risk assets down. Since recording, Bitcoin has moved from roughly $60K to about $77.5K, consistent with a grinding rather than vertical recovery. 75:34

🎯 Four Sources Of Edge — The Screen Applied To Every Decision

This is the most transferable framework in the episode, and Jain uses it as a gate rather than a checklist.

"I only want to invest where I have edge. If you don't have edge, just go buy the index and move on." 36:22

Access or information edge. "Can you call people and do they pick up the phone? Do they call you when there's a deal or something's going to happen?" He ties this to timing — knowing what is about to happen tells you when, not whether 36:48.

Analytical edge. "How well do you actually understand the asset... Do you understand the cash flows? Do you understand where they're coming from? Do you understand what the multiples should be? Do you understand the risks?" 37:12.

Behavioural or psychological edge. "How well do you know yourself? How do you manage when you feel like an idiot at the bottom of the market and how do you manage when you feel like a genius at the top?" He adds the input people forget — managing when something unrelated in your personal life distorts judgement 37:35.

Structural edge. Fund duration, capital source, infrastructure — dismissed as an actionable lever because "most people can't change that" 38:26.

💡 Key InsightThe sharpest observation is about mismatched self-assessment. Retail traders who say "I'll buy low and sell high" are not claiming analytical or information edge — they are implicitly claiming behavioural edge, "the hardest one to actually have an edge on" 38:00. The framework's value is that it forces you to name which edge you are relying on, and most people have never asked. 38:02

The gate is absolute: "unless you know which source of edge is driving the decision that you are making, you shouldn't be making that decision... because if I don't have a source of edge, I don't want to do it" 39:07.

Applied to Zcash, his answer is unusually self-effacing. Access edge: he called the people who were bullish a year earlier to check whether they still were 40:28. Analytical edge: "there isn't that much to analyse. We understand the privacy model, but so does everyone else. I don't think I understand the asset better than anyone else" 41:37. And the decisive one: "I think the biggest thing was the behavioural or psychological edge on the Zcash decision. It was seeing that sentiment peaked, crashed out, but still held fairly strong" 41:52.

🔗 ConnectionEdge framework → sizing. Because Zcash rests on behavioural rather than analytical edge, it cannot be sized like a cash-flow asset — which is why he calls it his most obvious idea while holding "a smaller position primarily because of liquidity and market cap constraints" 16:01. Edge type determines both conviction and the constraint on expressing it. 16:01

His method for testing other holders' commitment deliberately avoids the question everyone asks. "I don't ask people did you sell. I think that's personal... it's more judging their emotion about it. Do they still feel attached to it? Do they still want to talk about it to their friends?" 40:52. A sale may reflect a house purchase, not a change of view — so measure attachment, not position.

📋 Protocol Cheat Sheet
  • Before any trade, name which of the four edges is driving it. If you cannot, don't.
  • Test other holders' conviction by whether they still want to talk about it, not by asking whether they sold.
  • Enter in thirds: one third immediately, one third on a defined DCA schedule, one third opportunistic — finish the opportunistic bucket when the DCA finishes.
  • Insert a deliberate gap between deciding and executing. Jain uses at least one workout.
  • Never trade on a day of extreme volatility: apparent volume is high but real liquidity is thin.
  • Sell only for three reasons: you like something else better, the thesis is invalidated, or the market has priced in many years of future success.

🪙 Zcash: The Absence Of Fundamentals Is The Feature

Jain's Zcash case is a deliberate inversion, and he states it without hedging.

"The other things that I really like about Zcash are there are no fundamentals. There's no cash flow to go and tie this to. There's no revenue numbers to go look at... So that means that it's worth whatever people say it's worth. That gives you a lot more upside." 17:55

The supporting logic is a store-of-value argument: with a company you pay a multiple on something measurable, whereas "with a store of value, you could even make the argument that the bigger it gets, the better it is because it can store more value... or in this case more private value" 18:18. Scale is self-reinforcing rather than diluting.

The position is disclosed: "Multicoin has accumulated a pretty significant Zcash position, pretty significant portion of the total supply," sized smaller than conviction would justify purely because of liquidity and market-cap constraints 16:09.

The entry story is the more instructive part. Multicoin owned none during the previous year's run-up, and Jain's initial read was cynical: a long-dormant token nobody watches can have a coordinated attention boom engineered, which translates to price 16:39. What changed his mind was the aftermath of the retracement — "I talked to a lot of those people who were really bullish on Zcash last year and they were still bullish. They were still sticking to their guns" 17:14.

💡 Key InsightHe names the distinction precisely: "the hard part is distinguishing something that has a genuine tailwind, a genuine trend versus a splash in the pan excitement because some people were talking about it. Is it a hot money game or are people actually buying into the story here?" 17:36. The retracement is the test — a coordinated pump has no survivors after one, a narrative does. 17:36

His valuation method follows from having nothing to model:

"When it comes to something like Zcash, I think about it, where is it on the list? Is it number 20? Is it number 15? Is it number 10? Is it in the top five? I think it's going to go top five." 22:32

He adds a virtue of the method that a dollar target lacks: it adjusts for the market. "My price target for Zcash is different if Bitcoin's at 80K versus if Bitcoin's at 200K... Everything is relative to each other" 22:53.

As of 14 September 2026 ZEC trades at $1,118.92 with a market capitalisation of $18.95 billion, placing it around 9th or 10th overall on CoinGecko's list, and roughly 7th among non-stablecoin crypto assets (behind BTC, ETH, BNB, XRP, SOL and TRX). It is now larger than Dogecoin and Monero. "Top five" remains a stretch, but the move from outside the top twenty to the edge of the top five is substantial. 🏛️ Bitcoin Is Captured, And The Quantum Debate Will Force A Fork

Jain's Zcash thesis is inseparable from a negative claim about Bitcoin, and he does not soften it.

"Bitcoin feels pretty captured to me at this point. It's BlackRock, it's MicroStrategy, it's institutionally captured." 20:15

The prediction that follows is specific in mechanism and vague in timing: "as the debate around quantum risk on Bitcoin develops you are going to see that corporate capture show up where they're going to be on one side of a fork. And I think that a lot of the cypherpunk early Bitcoin people are going to be on the other side" 20:34.

The broader frame is a barbell. He supports the institutional end — "I think stablecoins are great. I think RWAs are great... We're investing across the infrastructure to enable all of that" 18:46 — while naming what it is not: "those are very centralized. They're meant to be centralized... those assets are not a decentralized, unseizable, censorship-resistant store of value. They can be frozen" 18:57.

🎯 Against ConsensusThe industry's 2026 posture, in his characterisation, is "please regulate us, please come and issue centralized assets on our chains" 19:48. Jain's position is that this is genuinely good and simultaneously a loss of direction — "you have to look back at what are the principles the industry was founded on, and it was founded on self sovereignty" — and that Zcash is where the other end of the barbell now lives. 19:30

His evidence for the rotation is social rather than quantitative: a wave of early Bitcoiners publicly discussing Zcash over the preceding six to twelve months, which both he and Kevin treat as a leading indicator 21:03.

🧵 Running ThesisThe cypherpunk-versus-institutional barbell is the organising idea behind the whole portfolio — stablecoins, RWAs and tokenised equity at one end, Zcash at the other, and nothing in the middle that pretends to be both. Status: developing, and unusually legible as a portfolio construction principle rather than a slogan.

⚖️ Solana Versus Hyperliquid: He Owns Both, And Credible Neutrality Is The Tiebreaker

Asked whether he has changed his mind on Solana, Jain reframes the question as one of time frame and then declines the maximalist trap entirely.

"One thing I am not is I'm not a maxi. I'm not a maxi about anything. I think it's really important to not get married to a position, not get married to an idea, and always be willing to revisit." 07:55

He remains bullish on Solana's architecture as "the right technical architecture for internet capital markets," requiring "a permissionless open-source chain that integrates everything into one platform" 06:36. But he acknowledges the loss plainly: "we have seen derivatives volumes really go over to Hyperliquid" 07:06. His resolution is probabilistic — own both, and understand the real question as "what are you more bullish about, derivatives trading or spot trading?" with Solana the leader in spot and the likely home of tokenised securities 07:18.

The differentiator he expects to decide the institutional business is credible neutrality, and his examples are concrete rather than theoretical 08:30. His argument is competitive rather than ideological:

"Goldman's not going to settle on Stripe's chain, right? ... And JP Morgan is not going to settle their trades on DRW's chain. They are not going to give their competitors that much power. They're going to look for the credibly neutral layer." 10:20
🔍 Source Verified✅ Confirmed, with one nuance. Galaxy Digital tokenised its Class A common stock on Solana via Superstate's Opening Bell platform — the first SEC-registered public equity issued directly on a major public chain — and in 2026 extended it so GLXY tokens can be used as DeFi collateral through Kamino, a protocol Multicoin also owns. Bullish tokenised its own ordinary shares on Solana, becoming the first NYSE-listed company to fully tokenise its equity cap table, demonstrated at Consensus Miami 2026 and administered by Equiniti; tokenised BLSH began trading on Bullish Exchange in August 2026. Jain's phrasing implies the Equiniti acquisition is complete — the $4.2 billion deal is announced with an expected close in January 2027. Sources: PR Newswire (Galaxy/Superstate); Bullish investor communications; GlobeNewswire, 12 August 2026.

He is careful that this is a trade-off, not a verdict. Hyperliquid's "lack of credible neutrality and the limited validator set... enables better performance," and its users make an informed exchange: "we are okay with the opaque validator set and the opaque validator code as long as we're able to verify the chain and we're able to see that the exchange is solvent in real time" 08:55. Solana has multiple open-source clients and a far more distributed validator community — "it's not even comparable, but that does come at a cost. It's not free" 09:32.

⚡ TensionAt 07:55 he says he is "not a maxi about anything" and sizes probabilistically; at 12:44 he says "you should be very concentrated in your best ideas. Why do you have any money in your 10th best idea?" Both are defensible — concentration within a category and agnosticism about which competitor wins — but the two principles pull directly against each other when the two best ideas are in the same category, which is precisely the question Kevin asked.

📊 The $319 Hyperliquid Base Case, Defended Assumption By Assumption

The follow-up segment covers Multicoin's published Hyperliquid valuation, and Kevin opens with the right question: "By definition, you're talking your book. What stops me from discounting the entire evaluation framework as self-serving rationalization?" 67:26.

Jain's answer is the correct one: "Oh, you can discount it. Look, we are talking our book, and you should make your own decisions. We are just laying out what we think is a defensible argument and a framework on how we think about the valuation. But you can implement your own numbers" 67:37. He directs readers to the methodology rather than the headline, and discloses that the published bull/base/bear trio is a simplification of a wider internal scenario set 68:17.

🔍 Source Verified✅ Confirmed against the primary source. Multicoin published "Hyperliquid (HYPE) Analysis & Valuation" on 25 June 2026 with a base case of roughly $319 by 2028 — about 5x from a then-current price near $63 — built on approximately $8 billion of annual earnings at a 20x multiple, implying roughly $160 billion fully diluted. The report cites 2025 revenue of about $873 million on roughly $2.9 trillion of trading volume, user growth from 301k to 923k, and more than 59% of open interest across DeFi perp markets. Sources: multicoin.capital, 25 June 2026.

The four assumptions, and his defence of each:

Derivatives volume growth at 35% CAGR. Realised growth from 2020 to 2025 was about 45% per year, so the model haircuts history by roughly a quarter — his reasoning being the law of large numbers, over a window of only two years: "we're not making a projection for 10 years out and compounding at this rate" 69:38.

DEX share of derivatives rising from 16% to 32%. The one he flags himself: "this is probably the assumption that is hardest to have certainty about because we have less backwards looking data... DeFi derivatives really didn't take off until hype." His base rate is the move from almost nothing in 2022 to 16% today 70:37.

Hyperliquid holding 30% market share. His strongest argument, and a measurement critique rather than a forecast. "Volume is an easily gamed number... You can see that when they have tons of volume and no open interest or no liquidations, which tells you that that's just market makers trading with each other without anyone taking directional risk" 71:55. Hyperliquid sits at 30% of volume but 59% of open interest, "and I think that's a better marker for what long-term market share looks like... that requires real capital to be on there" 72:25. He expects share to rise as rival incentive programmes wane, but held it flat 73:12.

USDC balances growing in line with volume. The most mechanical assumption: the volume-to-open-interest ratio is held constant, implying traders take the same leverage as historically, which produces linear growth from volume through open interest to stablecoin collateral 73:36.

💡 Key InsightThe open-interest-versus-volume distinction is the single most portable piece of analysis in all three episodes. Volume can be manufactured by market makers trading with each other; open interest requires someone to post capital and hold directional risk. Any perp DEX market-share claim should be checked against OI share and liquidation activity before it is believed. 72:04
🔍 Source Verified⚠️ The position has moved since, and the reporting sits awkwardly with his stated policy. On-chain trackers cited by 24/7 Wall St. report that Multicoin built roughly 4 million HYPE in February and March 2026, then began transferring to Coinbase Prime from 21 July 2026 — starting with about 395,570 HYPE (~$23.8M), followed by further tranches through August — reducing roughly three-quarters of that stake and leaving around $90.5 million. Transfers to an exchange are evidence of transfers, not confirmed sales, and Multicoin has not publicly said which of its three stated sell reasons applied. But a reduction beginning one month after publishing a 5x, two-year target is a fact readers should weigh against the framing in the interview. Sources: 24/7 Wall St., 10 September 2026; CoinGecko, 14 September 2026.
⚡ TensionAt 24:41 he says "we don't actively trade this stuff. It's too hard," and at 48:05 he limits selling to three reasons: something better, thesis invalidation, or exuberant overvaluation. At a HYPE price about 27% above the report price and roughly a quarter of the way to his own base case, none of the three obviously applies to a three-quarter reduction. This is the clearest open question in the episode, and it is answerable only by Multicoin.

🧮 Position Construction: Sizing As Art, Entry In Thirds, And Bitcoin As Cash

Jain's process content is the densest part of the main interview, and it holds together as a system.

On sizing. "Sizing is an art, not a science. You can sit there and build these models with false precision and try and tell yourself that you have some scientific way of sizing it" 12:05. He allows one exception — an HFT shop with second-long holding periods — and rules it out for everyone else: "when you're a longer-term investor, trying to size things just quantitatively I think is a trap" 12:25. His actual inputs are relative probability of winning, the size of the pie, and judgement 12:50. He adds two constraints most public commentary omits: fiduciary duty to investors with their own time frames, and tax — Multicoin owned SOL long before it owned any HYPE, so rebalancing carries a cost 14:19.

On the regret-minimisation test. "Sit down and imagine yourself a year, two years in the future and imagine that you were wrong about one of the two assets and see which one would make you feel dumber" 14:56. It converts an intractable comparison into a psychological one a human can actually answer.

On entry. The framework he volunteers is the most directly usable thing in the episode:

"When I make an investment decision, I break it into thirds... I will buy a third immediately. I will then buy a third on a defined schedule... And then for the last third, I'm opportunistic during the DCA period." 32:48

Each third is built against a specific regret. The immediate third protects against wanting a better entry that never comes — "I was right about the actual decision that I wanted to make, but I let my emotions take over" 34:31. The DCA third accepts that he will not time the bottom. The opportunistic third protects against having no cash when the price falls and he still likes it 35:00.

On selling. Three reasons only: "One is we like something else better. Two is the thesis has become invalidated. Something went wrong. Or three is the market has really overvalued this thing and given it credit for many years into the future and has gotten exuberant" 48:05.

On cash. Multicoin stays fully deployed at all times because that is the product promised to investors, and uses Bitcoin as the cash instrument 46:47. Risk management therefore happens through beta rather than cash weight: "when you feel euphoria you want to lower the beta, so selling some of the higher risk things but still buying Bitcoin... that way you still maintain exposure in case you were wrong about the timing" 47:14.

💡 Key InsightUsing Bitcoin as fund cash is an elegant answer to a real problem for a fully deployed mandate — it lets the manager reduce idiosyncratic risk without going flat on the asset class, so being early on a top call costs relative rather than absolute performance. The cost is that it offers no protection in a broad drawdown, which is exactly the regime the fund has just come through. 46:53

On valuing revenue-generating tokens. For Solana and Hyperliquid he uses a different frame entirely — "These things are businesses. Solana is a business. Hyperliquid is a business. They are meant to go and generate cash flow and that is the primary thing that gives those tokens value" 27:17. The inputs: project the drivers forward rather than reading current revenue; establish what claim token holders actually have on that revenue; anchor against your realistic universe of options rather than a fantasy multiple; and add a discount rate reflecting execution risk, where he places Ethereum as less risky than Solana, which is less risky than newer assets 26:42.

📜 AnalogHis illustration of the "universe of options" constraint is Berkshire Hathaway sitting on roughly $400 billion in cash because nothing clears its bar 28:28. The figure is accurate — Berkshire's cash and short-term Treasury position hit a record $397.4 billion at the end of Q1 2026, more than a third of its market value, with roughly $339 billion in T-bills. His point is that an unrealistic hurdle rate is itself a decision, with a cost. 28:28
🎯 Against ConsensusOn technical analysis: "I am not at all a fan of technical indicators. I don't think they're helpful. I think it's astrology for men... you drew these triangles on charts and then Iran bombs a ship going through the Strait of Hormuz and your charts don't mean anything" 24:49. His underlying claim is that most price movement originates in real-world events, not chart structure — a direct rejection of the dominant retail methodology in this market. 24:49

The self-aware version of all of this comes when he describes the third-party valuation consultants his fund is required to hire: "if you knew how to model prices, you wouldn't be in your accounting job. You'd be in my job" 30:15. His conclusion: "you have to consider these numbers, but make a qualitative decision" 30:34.

🔬 The Zcash Bug, Handled In Real Time: A Falsifiable Test And A Several-Day Pause

The follow-up segment contains the best worked example of the frameworks in action.

Jain's account: the Zcash core team announced it was reviewing its code for bugs using an AI tool, found a bug in the Orchard shielded pool that would have allowed double-spending within that pool, and patched it. The market's first reaction was indifference; the second was panic, on the fear of an infinite mint 59:38.

His structural defence has three layers. Transparent Zcash addresses work like Bitcoin and carry no exposure. Zcash uses shielded pools rather than base-layer privacy deliberately, to keep the base layer auditable — a trade-off he notes he has written about for years, contrasting Monero, which is private at the base layer and cannot be audited at all 62:42. And each pool has a turnstile counting coins in and out: "No more coins can come out of the pool than have already gone into a pool" 61:24.

💡 Key InsightWhat makes this section valuable is that he converts the fear into a falsifiable prediction with a short deadline. "If someone had taken advantage of this bug, we should expect them to be draining the pool in order to monetize their ZEC and sell it. But we're not seeing that behavior. In fact, the pool is actually fairly steady" 61:39. An attacker who did nothing for a week is not a plausible attacker. 61:39

His process is the part worth copying. On the day of a 50–60% drawdown he did nothing: "I don't like trading on days of extreme volatility because liquidity is thin. It looks like there's a lot of volume but actual liquidity is often thinner because people are scared to trade" 65:25. Any decision must be either a predetermined plan or the product of unhurried analysis, with a deliberate physical circuit-breaker: "I like to make sure I get at least one workout in between making a decision and executing the decision because it clears my mind" 66:09. The decision took several days, and the trigger was the turnstile evidence failing to appear 66:26.

🔍 Source Verified✅ Confirmed, and his timeline prediction was right. The Orchard soundness bug was a flaw in the zero-knowledge circuit — in the elliptic-curve components of the Halo 2 proving system — found in late May 2026 by independent researcher Taylor Hornby during analysis supported by Shielded Labs, after roughly four years in the proof circuit. Jain said Ironwood was "expected to launch in July," would be formally verified, and that the old pool would be restricted to migration only. Ironwood (NU6.3) activated on 28 July 2026 at block 3,428,143, sealing roughly $1.7 billion of shielded value, with Orchard limited largely to withdrawals through the turnstile and a machine-checked proof of more than 2,700 theorems. Sources: The Block; CoinDesk, 28 July 2026.
🔍 Source Verified⚠️ One attribution is imprecise. Jain says the team re-ran the codebase "with some advanced AI models. I believe they use Mythos for this in partnership with Anthropic" 63:15. Published accounts of the Ironwood work credit formal verification plus audits using frontier models, and the bug itself was found by a named human researcher rather than by a model. The specific vendor-and-partner pairing he names could not be confirmed from primary sources; treat it as his recollection. Sources: The Block; Cryptonews coverage of Zcash formal verification.

His conclusion, and the action: "I think it's a non-event. I think the market overreacted... So we actually increased our Zcash exposure after that event because we saw it as market irrationality rather than a fundamental change to the thesis" 64:27. He also gives the mechanical reason the drawdown was so violent: "There were some leverage, a lot of people with stop losses in place. And all of that got wiped out based on this event" 62:23.

🌊 Ethereum's Missing Plan, And The Resilience That Surprised Him

Jain's Ethereum answer is a governance critique rather than a technical one, and it is notably unmalicious.

"I don't know what their plan is. They had this plan with layer 2s and heterogeneous scaling environments that were all going to settle down to the base layer. And now they seem to have abandoned that. They want to go scale the L1 again." 48:49

He notes the whiplash for builders: "they want people to use the L1 after they spent, you know, what, six, seven years telling everyone use L2s" 49:10. Crucially he treats this as an inherent cost of decentralisation rather than a failure of individuals: "there is no one who can actually say this is what Ethereum is... The foundation doesn't have that much power. They don't want that much power. Vitalik doesn't want that much power" 49:26.

💡 Key InsightHis closing line is the sharpest formulation of the coordination problem in the episode: "the market is not a great leader. The market is a great follower" 49:47. Decentralised governance excels at ratifying a direction and is structurally poor at choosing one — a cost that shows up as strategic drift rather than as visible failure. 49:47

He then concedes something a competitor's investor rarely does: "I have been genuinely surprised at the resilience of the market cap despite all of the setbacks that they faced" 49:58. His supporting observations: most spot volume on Solana, most derivatives volume on Hyperliquid, TVL that is "old TVL that existed from a long time ago," and stablecoin growth where "Ethereum on a percentage basis is not winning" 50:07. His explanation for the gap is one sentence: "people think it's a store of value. They think it's a better Bitcoin" 50:34.

🌐 Consensus CheckEthereum's market capitalisation sits at roughly $306.8 billion today — about five times Solana's $59.4 billion and sixteen times Hyperliquid's $17.8 billion — so the resilience he found surprising has persisted. Whether that is a store-of-value premium, as he argues, or a durable settlement-layer franchise his activity metrics do not capture, is the live disagreement. 50:01

🧠 Why He Stayed After His Co-Founder Left: The Ten-Year Framework

Kevin asks the uncomfortable question directly: why are you still here when your co-founder left?

Jain's on-the-record answer about Kyle Samani is careful and warm — respect for the partnership, "everyone's on good terms" 51:17. Asked the harder version, he admits the shock: "I will be honest, I was surprised when Kyle wanted to leave. That was not something I expected at all" 53:09.

🔍 Source Verified✅ Confirmed. Kyle Samani stepped back from Multicoin Capital on 4–5 February 2026 after nearly a decade as co-founder, saying he would take time off and explore other areas of technology, with reporting citing AI and robotics. Day-to-day operations continue under Tushar Jain and Brian Smith. Samani said he would continue to advocate for Solana and to invest personally. Sources: CoinDesk, 4 February 2026; The Block.

What follows is a framework rather than a sentiment. He rejects "live every day like it's your last" because it produces the wrong answer: "if today was my last day, I wouldn't be here with you. I'd be with my kids" 53:26. He equally rejects assuming indefinite time — "I promise you we're all going to die" 53:47. What he uses instead is a ten-year horizon: long enough to force a serious answer about meaning, short enough to prevent indefinite deferral 54:05.

Applying it, he lands on three things. Impact. Winning — defined not as a number but as "I like being right when other people are wrong," with the addendum that "I told you so is even better when it's backed up with numbers, because then there's no debate about it" 54:42. And meaning, located in a structural claim: "blockchains are the first principles correct way to think about what capital markets are going to be in the future. This is what AIs are going to use to transact with each other" 55:32.

📜 AnalogHis illustration of legacy plumbing: "literally when you buy a stock there's a paper stock certificate sitting at DTCC that is the actual stock and you just own a receipt of it through many layers of intermediaries" 55:53. The DTCC does hold securities in immobilised form with beneficial ownership tracked through intermediary layers — the point being that today's system is not digital-native but a paper system with computers bolted on. 55:53

He closes with a story he half-remembers, and the hedging is the point: Mark Zuckerberg turning down a billion-dollar acquisition offer because "if I had a billion dollars, I would just start another social media company. So why would I leave this one?" 56:39.

Phase 3: Specific Item Breakdown

🟡 Zcash (ZEC) — High Conviction, Constrained By Liquidity

The position he calls his most obvious idea, sized smaller than conviction because of market-cap and liquidity limits, targeted positionally at a top-five ranking rather than at a price.

The Thesis: Zcash is the cypherpunk end of a barbell whose other end is stablecoins, RWAs and tokenised equity. It has no cash flow and no revenue, which removes the valuation ceiling — "it's worth whatever people say it's worth" — and for a store of value, scale is self-reinforcing rather than dilutive. Bitcoin is institutionally captured; when the quantum-risk debate forces a fork, the cypherpunk cohort needs somewhere to go.

The Evidence: Multicoin holds a significant portion of total supply. The entry test was behavioural: those loudly bullish before the previous retracement were still bullish afterwards, which distinguishes a narrative from a coordinated attention pump. He claims no analytical edge — "so does everyone else" understand the privacy model. He also cites a wave of early Bitcoiners publicly engaging with Zcash over the prior six to twelve months.

The Catalyst: The Orchard bug resolution: Ironwood launched in July, formally verified, old pool restricted to migration. Independently confirmed — Ironwood (NU6.3) activated 28 July 2026, sealing roughly $1.7 billion of shielded value behind a machine-checked proof. Since recording ZEC has risen to $1,118.92 and a $18.95 billion market cap, around 9th–10th overall and roughly 7th excluding stablecoins.

The Stated Risk: He names the constraint himself: liquidity and market cap limit the size he can take. The deeper risk is structural to his own argument — an asset with nothing to value can reprice either way on sentiment alone, and his edge here is behavioural, which he elsewhere calls the hardest to possess. He does not address regulatory or delisting risk anywhere.

🔵 Hyperliquid (HYPE) — A Published $319 Base Case, And A Position That Has Since Shrunk

The most quantified call in the episode — roughly 5x over two years on four assumptions he defends as conservative — and the one where subsequent on-chain behaviour raises the sharpest question.

The Thesis: Hyperliquid is a business that generates real cash flow, and the token's value derives from that. Derivatives trading is where crypto volume has migrated; Hyperliquid dominates it; and the platform is broadening beyond perps. He owns it alongside Solana rather than instead of it, framing the pair as derivatives versus spot exposure.

The Evidence: Multicoin's 25 June 2026 report: base case ~$319 by 2028 from ~$63, on roughly $8 billion of annual earnings at a 20x multiple, implying about $160 billion fully diluted. Supporting data: ~$873 million of 2025 revenue on ~$2.9 trillion of volume, user growth from 301k to 923k, and more than 59% of open interest across DeFi perp markets. His strongest argument is methodological — volume is gameable, open interest is not.

The Catalyst: Perp DEX share of crypto derivatives doubling from 16% to 32% by 2028, and rival volume-incentive programmes winding down, which he expects to lift Hyperliquid above the flat 30% held in the model. HYPE has since reached a record near $82 and entered Hashdex's crypto ETF as its fifth-largest holding.

The Stated Risk: He flags the DEX-share assumption as having the least supporting data and invites readers to discount the report as book-talking. The unstated risk is disclosed by the record: on-chain trackers report Multicoin reduced roughly three-quarters of the 4 million HYPE accumulated in February–March 2026, beginning 21 July — one month after publication — leaving around $90.5 million. Transfers are not confirmed sales, and Multicoin has not said which sell reason applied.

🟣 Solana (SOL) — The Credible-Neutrality Bet On Tokenised Securities

Still the architecture he believes internet capital markets require, now held as the spot-and-issuance side of a paired bet rather than as a single-chain conviction.

The Thesis: Internet capital markets need "a permissionless open-source chain that integrates everything into one platform," and Solana is that. The decisive commercial advantage is credible neutrality: competitive institutions will not settle critical business on infrastructure a competitor controls, so the tokenised-securities business flows to the neutral layer.

The Evidence: Solana leads spot trading volume. Bullish tokenised its own ordinary shares there — the first NYSE-listed company to fully tokenise its equity cap table, demonstrated at Consensus Miami 2026 — and Galaxy issued tokenised Class A stock via Superstate, the first SEC-registered public equity on a major public chain, later usable as DeFi collateral through Kamino. Multiple open-source clients and a far more distributed validator set than Hyperliquid's.

The Catalyst: Continued TradFi issuance. Bullish's $4.2 billion Equiniti acquisition, expected to close January 2027, is designed to give it end-to-end tokenisation infrastructure — a structural rather than promotional commitment.

The Stated Risk: He states the loss plainly: derivatives volume has gone to Hyperliquid, and "what makes more money for the chain? That's a really hard question to answer." In his own discount-rate framework Solana carries more execution risk than Ethereum, and credible neutrality costs performance.

🟢 Ethena (ENA), Kamino And Morpho — A Category Bet On Leverage Provision

Not a single-token call but a basket, on the view that these protocols are converging on one job — matching lenders wanting yield with borrowers wanting leverage — with strong returns to scale.

The Thesis: "Ethena is converging with Morpho. All of these things, their job is they have lenders who want to earn some yield and they have borrowers who want leverage. That's it." For Ethena the lenders hold USDe and the borrowers are derivatives traders paying funding; for Morpho and Kamino it is collateralised borrowing. Same job, different packaging. The category has clear returns to scale — a large borrower must go to the largest lender, so second place has no reason to exist.

The Evidence: Multicoin owns Ethena, Kamino ("the biggest and I think most secure on Solana") and some Morpho, deliberately spreading across paths because "I don't know which path leads to the ultimate winner." He rates Guy Young among the most competent founders in DeFi, which lowers the execution-risk term in his three-number framework.

The Catalyst: Consolidation. If returns to scale are as strong as he argues, the category should concentrate — and owning several names holds the eventual winner without predicting it.

The Stated Risk: He names none beyond path uncertainty, and the record since has been unkind to this token specifically. ENA trades at roughly $0.14 with a $1.42 billion market cap, about 89% below its April 2024 high of $1.52, having bottomed near $0.07 in June 2026. USDe supply peaked around $14.5 billion and Q1 2026 gross protocol revenue fell 32% quarter-on-quarter to roughly $65 million. The category thesis may be right while this expression underperforms — which is why he owns several.

⚪ Bitcoin (BTC) — Held As Fund Cash, Not As A Thesis

Multicoin has "practically no" Bitcoin position in the conviction sense; it uses BTC as the cash instrument in a permanently fully deployed fund.

The Thesis: The fund promises investors full deployment at all times, so it needs a low-beta parking asset rather than dollars. "If we get new money into the fund or we sell something we'll buy Bitcoin with it, and when we want to invest in something else we spend the Bitcoin." Risk is managed through beta, not cash weight: lower beta into euphoria, redeploy into drawdowns.

The Evidence: Stated as current policy, with the qualifier that "given where we are in the market cycle I want to own higher risk things." The index alternative would work similarly, "but the index is mainly Bitcoin and ETH."

The Catalyst: None — a portfolio-construction choice, not a directional view. The directional view is separate: peak apathy reached, bottom probably in, chop rather than a V.

The Stated Risk: Unstated by him but structural: Bitcoin-as-cash offers no protection in a broad drawdown, which is the regime the fund has just come through. His other stated view — that Bitcoin is institutionally captured and heading for a quantum-driven fork — is an awkward companion to using it as the reserve asset.

⚫ Ethereum (ETH) — The Negative Case, Held With Visible Uncertainty

Not a short and not a position — a strategic critique of a chain whose direction nobody can state, paired with an honest admission that its market cap has defied his expectations.

The Thesis: The L2-centric roadmap appears abandoned in favour of scaling the L1 again, after six or seven years of telling builders the opposite. Nobody has the authority to say what the plan is — the Foundation does not have that power and does not want it — so direction has to emerge from a market that "is not a great leader. The market is a great follower."

The Evidence: Most spot volume has moved to Solana; most derivatives volume to Hyperliquid; TVL is legacy rather than growing; and on stablecoin growth "Ethereum on a percentage basis is not winning." Against all that, the market cap has held — his explanation being that holders treat ETH as "a better Bitcoin."

The Catalyst: None identified. He gives Ethereum the lowest execution risk of the assets he discusses, a point in its favour in his discount-rate framework even as the growth case weakens.

The Stated Risk: He states the risk to his own view rather than to the asset: "I have been genuinely surprised at the resilience of the market cap despite all of the setbacks." At roughly $306.8 billion — five times Solana's — the store-of-value premium has persisted, and he offers no mechanism by which it unwinds.
