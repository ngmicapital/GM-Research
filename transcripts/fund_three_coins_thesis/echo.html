Multicoin's Tushar Jain: Zcash Because It Has No Fundamentals, Hyperliquid At A $319 Base Case, And Solana Because TradFi Will Not Settle On A Competitor's Chain

Full Mode

⚡ The Bottom Line

  • Jain's stated reason for liking Zcash is the one most analysts would call a disqualifier: it has no fundamentals. No cash flow, no revenue, nothing to anchor a multiple to — "so that means that it's worth whatever people say it's worth. That gives you a lot more upside." His target is positional, not numerical: top five by market cap.
  • Multicoin published a $319 base case for HYPE by 2028, built on ~$8 billion of annual earnings at a 20x multiple — and Jain defends all four assumptions as conservative on air. The sharpest one: Hyperliquid holds 30% of perp DEX volume but 59% of open interest, and he argues open interest is the harder number to fake.
  • He is explicitly not a Solana maxi and owns both SOL and HYPE in size, framing them as a bet on spot versus derivatives. His argument for Solana keeping the tokenised-securities business is credible neutrality: "Goldman's not going to settle on Stripe's chain."
  • His screen for every decision is which of four edges is driving it — access, analytical, behavioural, structural — and he says the Zcash trade was almost entirely behavioural. No analytical edge, no information edge beyond calling holders to test whether they still wanted to talk about it.
  • The follow-up segment, recorded weeks after the main interview, has him buying more Zcash into a 50–60% single-day drawdown after the Orchard bug disclosure, on the specific evidence that the turnstile was not draining. That is the most instructive piece of process reasoning in the episode — a falsifiable test, applied with a several-day cooling-off period.

🔑 Key Claims (Ranked)

1. Zcash goes top five, and the absence of fundamentals is why the upside is large ↳ Evidence: "There's no cash flow to go and tie this to... that means that it's worth whatever people say it's worth." For store-of-value assets he argues size compounds credibility — the bigger it gets, the more value it can store. ↳ Caveat: The identical logic applies to every asset that has ever gone to zero; it is a statement about the absence of a valuation ceiling, not about the presence of a floor. ↳ Consensus: Contrarian in framing, increasingly consensus in direction — ZEC now ranks around 9th–10th overall and roughly 7th excluding stablecoins.

2. Bitcoin is institutionally captured, and a quantum-risk fork will expose it ↳ Evidence: "It's BlackRock, it's MicroStrategy, it's institutionally captured." His prediction is that as the quantum debate develops, corporate holders and cypherpunk holders end up on opposite sides of a fork, and Zcash becomes the home for the second group. ↳ Caveat: A conditional prediction with no date and no trigger specified. ↳ Consensus: Contrarian.

3. HYPE base case $319 by 2028, roughly 5x from the report price of ~$63 ↳ Evidence: 35% CAGR in crypto derivatives volume (against ~45% realised 2020–2025); DEX share of derivatives rising from 16% to 32%; Hyperliquid holding 30% share; USDC balances growing in line with volume. Implies ~$8B annual earnings at a 20x multiple, roughly $160B fully diluted. ↳ Caveat: He volunteers that the DEX-share assumption "has the least data behind it," and invites readers to discount the whole thing as book-talking. ↳ Consensus: Aggressive, and he knows it — the base case is above most sell-side ranges.

4. TradFi issuers will choose the credibly neutral chain, which is Solana and is not Hyperliquid ↳ Evidence: "JP Morgan is not going to settle their trades on DRW's chain." Bullish tokenised its own equity on Solana and Galaxy has issued stock there; Hyperliquid's opaque validator set is the trade-off that buys its performance. ↳ Caveat: He is careful that this is a trade-off, not a ranking — Hyperliquid users knowingly accept opacity for verifiable solvency and performance. ↳ Consensus: Aligned among institutional issuers, contested among traders.

5. Sizing is an art; quantitative sizing is a trap for long-horizon investors ↳ Evidence: "Why do you have any money in your 10th best idea?" His tools are relative probability of winning, size of the addressable pie, and a regret-minimisation exercise — imagine being wrong about each and see which makes you feel dumber. ↳ Consensus: Aligned with concentrated-manager orthodoxy, against risk-parity practice.

6. There are exactly four sources of edge, and if you cannot name yours, do not trade ↳ Evidence: Access/information, analytical, behavioural, structural. He says most retail traders believe they have behavioural edge, which is the hardest one to actually possess. For Zcash he claims mostly behavioural edge plus some access; explicitly no analytical edge. ↳ Consensus: Aligned with institutional practice, rarely stated this cleanly in public.

7. Multicoin is actively managed, not actively traded — and technical analysis is "astrology for men" ↳ Evidence: The whipsaw failure mode — sell into strength, fail to get the drawdown, buy back higher, then capitulate at the top. He argues cycle timing is possible but involves roughly one decision a year; micro-timing is not. ↳ Caveat: On-chain trackers report Multicoin reduced roughly three-quarters of the HYPE stake it built in February–March 2026, starting in July — weeks after publishing the $319 target. That may fit his third stated sell reason, but it sits awkwardly beside the framing. ↳ Consensus: Aligned in principle; the practice is now an open question.

8. The entry framework is thirds: a third immediately, a third on a DCA schedule, a third opportunistic ↳ Evidence: Built explicitly around regret minimisation — the immediate third protects against never getting a better entry; the DCA third against not timing the bottom; the opportunistic third against having no cash when you still like it lower. ↳ Consensus: Aligned, and the most directly usable framework in the episode.

9. The bottom is probably in, but expect chop rather than a V ↳ Evidence: Peak apathy; people leaving the industry; bad news — including significant hacks — no longer sending prices down. "This is unlikely to be like a sharp V-shaped bottom. We rarely see those." ↳ Caveat: He names the macro exit explicitly: a US–Iran escalation would crash all risk assets regardless. ↳ Consensus: Was contrarian; Bitcoin has since risen from roughly $60K to $77.5K.

10. Ethena is not a bet on Ethena — it is a bet on the leverage-provision category ↳ Evidence: He argues Ethena, Morpho and Kamino are converging on the same job: match lenders wanting yield with borrowers wanting leverage. Multicoin owns several, because he does not know which path reaches the winner. ↳ Caveat: Of the three named, the Ethena token has performed worst — ENA is roughly 89% below its 2024 high despite USDe supply having peaked near $14.5B. ↳ Consensus: Emerging.
