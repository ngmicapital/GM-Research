Grayscale's Zcash ETF Holds The Transparent Half — Its Own Head Of Research Says The Value Is In The Other One

Source The Rollup · Date n/a · 26 min
Domain Crypto & Markets
Reliability Expert opinion, well-sourced — Pandl's numbers check out against Grayscale's own published research, but this is a launch-day interview for a product he is selling, and the host does most of the bullish framing.
Content Type Analyst Interview
Full Mode

⚡ The Bottom Line

  • The entire valuation case is one number: Zcash trades at 0.9% of Bitcoin's market capitalisation, and Grayscale thinks the market is pricing financial privacy far too cheaply. Pandl's benchmark — which he repeatedly refuses to call a price prediction — is that 5% of Bitcoin's market cap implies roughly $4,000–$4,500 per ZEC.
  • The structural irony goes unchallenged: Grayscale's ETF holds transparent, auditable ZEC in Coinbase Custody, and Pandl says outright that the shielded side is "really where the value is coming from." He tells listeners the ETF gives exposure, not "the full experience," and then encourages them to go look at shielded Zcash separately.
  • The argument is a market-share argument, not a cash-flow or network-usage argument. Bitcoin holds roughly 90% of Grayscale's Currencies Crypto Sector; the claim is simply that a category with one competitor is anomalous, and that Solana's emergence in smart contracts is the template.
  • Pandl's three claimed "second-mover advantages" are financial privacy, a faster development culture around quantum readiness, and cross-chain reach via intents. The third is the most underrated: Zcash does not need merchant acceptance, because shielded ZEC can settle into stablecoins on Solana or Ethereum through NEAR Intents.
  • The fee is 2.5% — roughly ten times a typical Bitcoin ETF — and Grayscale has committed the first year's fee revenue back into the Zcash ecosystem. On launch-day AUM that is roughly $7.6 million a year, which makes a US-listed ETF a material Zcash funding source alongside the protocol's own dev fund.

🔑 Key Claims (Ranked)

1. Zcash is undervalued because the currencies category has no competition, not because of any Zcash-specific metric ↳ Evidence: Bitcoin holds about 90% market share of Grayscale's Currencies Crypto Sector, while smart contracts saw Solana take real share from Ethereum; Pandl argues the same dynamism is overdue and will "probably make Bitcoin stronger" 07:56–09:32. ↳ Caveat: It is a relative-value analogy, not a valuation model — nothing in it says what privacy is worth, only that 0.9% seems low. ↳ Consensus: Emerging — this is the institutional version of a thesis that was fringe eighteen months earlier.

2. The 5% scenario implies roughly $4,000–$4,500 per ZEC ↳ Evidence: Verified against Grayscale's own published scenario table, released the same day: 2% → $1,622, 3% → $2,433, 5% → $4,054, 10% → $8,109 by 2030 13:58–14:26. ↳ Caveat: Pandl says twice on air that this is "not a price prediction," and Grayscale labels the figures scenarios — but the press converted them into a price target within hours. ↳ Consensus: Contrarian on magnitude, aligned on direction.

3. The ETF deliberately does not give you the thing that makes Zcash valuable ↳ Evidence: "The trust holds transparent and auditable Zcash in Coinbase custody like our other ETF products... with the ETF, you are not getting the full experience" 20:00–20:20. ↳ Caveat: Every ETF-held and custodian-held ZEC is permanently excluded from the shielded pool, which is the metric Zcash's own developers use as their primary KPI. ↳ Consensus: Aligned — Pandl is unusually candid about this, and it is the most important structural fact in the episode.

4. Zcash's cross-chain reach removes the merchant-adoption problem entirely ↳ Evidence: "We don't really need to have every merchant in the world accept Zcash for this asset to serve as a private hub with universal access" — shielded value can settle as stablecoin payments on Solana or Ethereum through intents 05:05–05:36. ↳ Consensus: Emerging — it reframes ZEC as private settlement collateral rather than as spendable currency.

5. Quantum readiness is a genuine Zcash-versus-Bitcoin differentiator right now ↳ Evidence: Bitcoin's quantum-hardening effort "has not transpired quite yet" while Zcash has been "a lot more proactive"; Pandl lists it second among the three second-mover advantages 03:34–04:59. ↳ Consensus: Aligned — independently corroborated by Zcash's ZIP 2005 recoverability work and 2027 post-quantum target.

6. The "sell the fact" reading of an ETF conversion has already been falsified once ↳ Evidence: Identical arguments were made about the Bitcoin ETFs, which "proved to be dead wrong" — they were the starting point of the 2024 rally, not the end 16:47–17:13. ↳ Caveat: He cannot predict the behaviour of nine years' worth of locked-up trust holders and says so. ↳ Consensus: Aligned.

7. Institutions discovered privacy by trying to use public blockchains, not by reading cypherpunk literature ↳ Evidence: "As traditional institutional investors have explored blockchain technology, they immediately recognize that privacy is a missing element" — and Pandl expects the privacy layer to spread from currencies to smart contracts, stablecoins, tokenised assets and DEXes 10:53–11:53. ↳ Consensus: Emerging, and the most durable idea in the episode regardless of what ZEC does.

8. Intents are what an agentic payment experience will feel like ↳ Evidence: "To me intents is what I expect an agent experience to feel like. I just want something to happen" — with the mechanism hidden 25:43–26:05. ↳ Caveat: The host led him into this and it is the least substantiated passage in the interview. ↳ Consensus: Speculative.
