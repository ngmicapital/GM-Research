⚡ The Bottom Line

  • The user count the internet called fake holds up under arithmetic. "A little over 700,000" 20:47 against a 22–23 June announcement citing 600,000+ (USV used 625,000) — and their own disclosed run-rate of 12,000 sign-ups/day 24:15 closes the gap exactly.
  • The no-token position survived ten weeks and a venue change nearly verbatim, and hardened into a preference for an IPO. "When you launch a token, it becomes the entire product" 71:26; asked IPO-or-token, "probably the former" 71:19.
  • The April roadmap shipped and shipped incomplete. Perps went live 11 June via Hyperliquid and Trade.xyz, and are described here as "very bare bones. There's no social features yet" 36:06 — from the company whose entire thesis is the social layer.
  • The single most checkable disclosure in either FOMO interview: FOMO pays its routers a monthly retainer not to capture positive slippage 31:56–32:19. "We will actively lose money to make sure the user gets the absolute best execution" 32:29.
  • Nine days after a $550M valuation, the posture is defensive, not triumphant. "Fortifying our balance sheet… was specifically so if the market does turn, we have money to build through that" 43:27, into the host's "pretty bad crypto days right now" 43:45.

🔑 Key Claims (Ranked)

1. ~700,000 lifetime sign-ups, ~30% funded conversion, 12,000 sign-ups/day

↳ Evidence: 20:47, 21:44, 24:15, 24:47 — 7,500 first deposits the prior day.

↳ Caveat: lifetime sign-ups, volunteered as such 21:28. Implied funded base ~210–220k. Unaudited.

↳ Consensus: Aligned. Converges with the 625,000 in the Series B materials; the first FOMO user claim in this workspace that does.

2. "Crypto consumer is not a big enough category" — the TAM argument that kills the genre

↳ Evidence: daily crypto-native users put at "maybe like 50, 100,000" 04:28; build instead for "the mass consumers, which is the rest of the world" 05:16.

↳ Consensus: Contrarian inside crypto, orthodox in consumer fintech. Identical to the Head of Growth's frame in the April interview — the doctrine survives a change of speaker.

3. No token; an IPO is the more likely instrument

↳ Evidence: both founders answer "No plans for that" in unison 71:09; "probably the former" 71:19; "I'm not sure that Robinhood or Coinbase, if they weren't public, would have as many users" 72:18.

↳ New vs April: a conditional appears — a token only "in a compliant way where you actually have ownership of the product" 71:34.

4. The Robinhood fee comparison is built from the worst end of their range and the best end of FOMO's

↳ Evidence: "85 bips each way" 35:08 vs Robinhood's documented ~35–85bps spread and a 3–5bps advanced tier; "Robinhood has maybe 100 to 150 crypto assets" 38:13 vs ~30 on retail trackers.

↳ Caveat: FOMO's own schedule (50bps long-tail / 5bps majors, 35:51) matches third-party documentation — but the ~$0.95 per-trade minimum on Solana goes unmentioned behind "basically free" 34:57.

5. Four named escapes from being a cyclical fee business

↳ Evidence: a FOMO Gold subscription 42:33; native stablecoin yield 43:01; lending vaults with Morpho/Veda-style partners 44:14; and a paid alpha-data API — "every agent, every infrastructure platform… needs to subscribe" 68:02.

↳ Consensus: Emerging. None of this exists in the April file, because nobody asked what happens in a bear market.

6. The moat is the social graph, and nothing else is claimed as one

↳ Evidence: "Everybody can come on chain… but you don't have the social graph. The best traders on chain are on FOMO" 41:08–41:16. On-ramps and KYC are explicitly declined as moats — "that's not a competitive advantage" 62:37.

↳ Caveat: perps shipped without the graph attached 36:06, so the transfer thesis is still untested.

7. AI commoditises execution and raises the value of the social layer

↳ Evidence: "The edge of execution is… going to get evened out and commoditised… what actually becomes important is the research" 67:26–67:34; "humans reason through repetition and through analogy" 67:45.

↳ Consensus: Contrarian. The default forecast is that agents make social finance noise; this argues the opposite.

8. Named misses, volunteered

↳ "One of our big misses was we weren't on BNB chain early enough" 45:03 — which reframes the April file, where BNB read as a win. ETH mainnet, an April "about a week" estimate, has vanished from the conversation.
