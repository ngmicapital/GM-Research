All Eyes on AI. Where Does Crypto Fit? | EP 77

Source Good Game Podcast · 2026-09-15 · 91 min
Domain Crypto Markets, AI & Venture
Reliability Expert practitioner discussion — both hosts run a crypto accelerator and see private startup data, and both disclose holdings (Zcash, Bitcoin, the hyperscalers, Nvidia). Opinionated by design, with positions stated openly.
Content Type Two-host analytical conversation
Full Mode

⚡ The Bottom Line

  • The hosts return after a sixteen-month hiatus and call the market a first inning, not a relief rally. Their evidence is participation rather than price: retail app downloads at cycle highs, sidelined OGs who sold in the $60,000s, and the observation that bear-market bounces are not this violent.
  • The dominant on-chain meta is "crypto alchemy" — meme tokens paired with tokenized stocks — and it has started a real legal fight. AMC's chief executive is publicly demanding Robinhood stop; Robinhood's answer is that a token which changes no shareholder right is not the issuer's to veto.
  • Their sharpest and most contrarian argument is about valuation, not narrative. Trading products have near-zero switching costs and no durable earnings, so applying equity multiples to them is a category error. A P/E of 20 is not cheap; five is generous; for on-chain games, 0.5 is fair.
  • Computer use, not frontier reasoning, is what they think just changed. Both now automate substantial parts of their working lives with agents, one has fired his accountant, and the shared conclusion is blunt: pure software startups are no longer viable.
  • The most substantive AI-and-crypto claim is that compute, energy and semiconductors are becoming tradable asset classes — and this is happening in traditional venues first. CME has a compute futures launch dated for early October; Nvidia has signed agreements with six large asset managers to make GPUs financeable collateral.

🔑 Key Claims (Ranked)

1. Trading products do not deserve equity multiples because their earnings are not durable ↳ Evidence: A P/E of 20 asserts that twenty years of discounted earnings equal today's price — and no trading product has twenty durable years 44:08. ↳ Caveat: They apply the same logic to Hyperliquid, which one of them holds. ↳ Context: The clearest transferable idea in the episode, and it generalises well beyond crypto.

2. The on-chain bull market is being led by tokenized stocks and stock-paired meme tokens ↳ Evidence: Robinhood, Base, Solana and BNB are all fighting for the same flow; Robinhood has briefly out-traded Solana on DEX volume 11:44. ↳ Caveat: One host calls most of it "a flash in the pan" and both agree it is a video game with perhaps ten to twenty per cent substance 09:04. ↳ Context: Robinhood Chain launched in July on Arbitrum technology; the AMC dispute escalated in the week before recording.

3. Coinbase is losing the consumer front end because of an archetype mismatch, not engineering ↳ Evidence: Ten years of arguing that Bitcoin means financial freedom sits badly with selling speculative tokens; the app now serves neither the crypto native nor the normie 13:00. ↳ Context: Coinbase keeps the institutional and custody trust; Robinhood wins the user-facing product.

4. On-chain front ends will capture the value as infrastructure commoditises ↳ Evidence: Chains, launchpads and lending all commoditise; whoever aggregates them becomes the browser of the on-chain world, on a three-to-five-year horizon 38:00. ↳ Caveat: Front ends themselves have almost no defensibility, which is the same argument applied one layer up.

5. Two different Zcash theses, held by two people who both own it ↳ Evidence: One buys memetics — 21 million supply, quantum resistance, "the last 1000x" 28:21. The other buys evolvability: Zcash is "Bitcoin that is capable of changes," and privacy and quantum resistance are byproducts 29:15. ↳ Caveat: One of them owns considerably more Bitcoin than Zcash and frames the position as a hedge rather than a replacement.

6. Computer use is a step-function change, and it kills pure-software startups ↳ Evidence: Automated email triage, founder diligence, a complete tax filing, and the flat claim "you can't do pure SaaS anymore" 68:58. ↳ Context: Meta's Muse launched days earlier running on a dedicated secure virtual machine — the architecture one host specifically cites as making him comfortable handing over credentials.

7. AI-lab safety messaging is read as regulatory capture rather than disclosure ↳ Evidence: Both treat the existential-risk publicity as partly fundraising and partly a bid to shape regulation — "capital, regulations. That's all it is" 66:40. ↳ Caveat: They then both accept the underlying probability estimate, putting their own number at or slightly below ten per cent. Their objection is to the messaging, not the risk.

8. Compute, energy and semiconductors are all becoming markets, and crypto's claim is price discovery ↳ Evidence: Compute "could trade like oil" 73:35; energy is the US bottleneck while compute is China's; "anytime you need price discovery, on-chain is the way to go" 75:25. ↳ Context: This is where the episode's AI-and-crypto thesis is strongest, and also where traditional venues are moving faster than on-chain ones.
