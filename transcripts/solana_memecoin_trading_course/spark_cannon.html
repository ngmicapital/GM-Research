Executive Summary

  • The core of the course is a five-question screen run before any buy, and FOMO is explicit that failing it means you do not have a trade. What kind of trade is this, is the attention and volume real, who controls the supply, who is already in and how much profit are they sitting on, and what would prove the thesis wrong. "If you cannot answer them, you do not have a trade yet. You just have a coin you want to buy." 03:05–03:53
  • Bundling is the single highest-value screen he teaches, and he applies it asymmetrically. On tokens under an hour old, a high bundle percentage means the deployer bought most of the supply and will sell into incoming volume. On tokens a day or two old that have held dips, he says the bundle number stops mattering. 19:37–43:28
  • He splits traders into two styles and states the distribution himself: the "new pair high volume gambler" is roughly 90% of participants; the narrative trader he recommends being is "like the top 10%." He does not claim the second style is easier — he claims it is survivable while holding a job. 09:41–15:56
  • The chart section is the course's weakest link and FOMO says so unprompted: "I am horrible at explaining how charts work and why I do what I do. It's kind of just like a gut feeling." He teaches Fibonacci retracement, the 0.618 golden pocket and support/resistance, while conceding many traders consider TA on memecoins worthless. 40:27–40:34
  • The risk warnings he actually gives are specific and operational, not boilerplate. Dedicated trading wallet, only money you can afford to lose, never share seed phrase or private key, never connect through links in replies or Telegram messages, always copy the contract address from the original source because "there can be 20 coins with the same name." 02:19–03:05
  • One load-bearing factual claim in the course is materially wrong. FOMO says competing terminals charge "upwards of up to 5%" per buy and per sell. 🎯 The Five Questions Are The Actual Course — Everything Else Is A Screen That Answers One Of Them

FOMO front-loads the framework at 03:05 and then explicitly structures the rest of the video around it, returning to it at 33:24 and again at 40:34.

"Every screen I'm about to show you answers one of those questions. If you cannot answer them, you do not have a trade yet. You just have a coin you want to buy."

The five, in his order:

  1. What kind of trade am I taking? A quick gamble, a narrative trade, or something he wants to hold and be part of 03:12–03:24. At 33:26 he operationalises this as a money question: "Do I have money that I could just kiss goodbye? Then go on meme scope, gamble away."
  2. Is the attention and volume real? 03:27 — answered by the volume-plus-fees-paid filter pair and the bundle check.
  3. Who controls the supply? 03:31 — answered by the holders panel and bundle percentage.
  4. Who is already in the token and how much profit are they sitting on? 03:35 — answered by the top traders panel.
  5. What would prove my thesis wrong? 03:40 — the only question with no screen behind it, answered by imagination.
💡 Key InsightThe framework's real function is sequencing, not filtering. Every tool in the video already exists on every terminal; what most traders lack is an order of operations that forces the supply and profit-distribution questions before the chart question. FOMO's contribution is putting "who is already in and what are they sitting on" ahead of "does the chart look good." 03:35

Question five gets the most interesting treatment because it has no data screen. FOMO works it through live on a position he holds: what could actually break this? For a token built on a viral animal, "the inventor of the word comes in and deploys his own token," or the underlying character turns out to be AI-generated, or — his most concrete example — a developer whose GitHub repo goes viral spawns a community coin, then "creates his own coin to collect his own fees and that vamps the original one that you bought" 44:51–46:07.

He also flags a real case: news that someone had made contact with the "owner" of a viral animal and offered to rehome it, which coincided with a dump from all-time highs — though he is careful to say it "was probably just a coincidence" 45:11–45:36.

⚡ TensionAt 35:38 FOMO says "I'm not changing my thesis just because the price went down or a specific influencer sold." At 43:14 he says he avoids heavily bundled tokens "unless... if you look at the chart and you notice that there are signs of demand for it when it's on dips." The first is thesis-independence from price; the second makes price action a thesis input. Both are defensible in isolation; the course never reconciles them.

His trade plan, stated at 34:54, is four commitments made before clicking buy: maximum position size, what has to stay true to keep holding, where he takes profit, and what makes him exit completely. The sizing rule is explicit and inverse: "The lower the market cap and the liquidity, the smaller I size in" 35:11–35:29.

🪤 Bundling Is The Highest-Value Screen In The Course — Applied Asymmetrically By Token Age

The bundling explanation, running from 19:37 to 22:17 and revisited at 41:52–43:28, is the most operationally useful segment in the video.

The tell FOMO teaches is chart-shaped first: a large green candle immediately at launch, a small dip, then a second large green candle. "Usually what that means is that the person who deployed or launched this token... he bundled the token" 19:37–20:04.

The confirmation is in the holders panel. Icons next to holders mark bundlers, which FOMO describes as snipers — wallets running bots that buy the instant a token launches — while noting that in practice "it literally is the developer" 20:04–21:52.

"This guy bought a lot of the supply to trick new traders that don't know any better."

He walks a live example to its conclusion. A token he declined to buy topped at $12K market cap and dumped continuously; its top traders had all made money and it had drawn nearly $50,000 of volume against the flat $1,800 on unbundled launches nearby. His read: the bundlers brought the volume themselves, "whether they were triggering trending bots on Telegram or sharing it to specific groups," and the other launches "fell flat... because they did not bundle it and they did not drive volume to the token" 20:47–21:45.

The most instructive part is the second-order case at 41:15. He compares two tokens: one at a higher market cap with lower volume, another at a lower market cap with 10,000 transactions, far more volume, and 63% bundled. The lower price on the higher-volume token is the diagnostic — "as soon as buy volume comes in, the bundler is selling. So it kind of just evens out... It's going to be a stagnant token essentially."

💡 Key InsightBundled supply does not usually show up as a crash. It shows up as a token that absorbs real buy volume and refuses to move. That signature — heavy transactions, heavy volume, flat or declining price — is more actionable than any warning badge, because it survives the terminal's bundle estimate being wrong. 41:38

The asymmetry is deliberate and he states it plainly. "Bundles don't really worry me when it's a token that has already gone up" 42:26–42:36. His example: a three-day-old community takeover showing 55% bundled that ran from $100K to nearly $400K, dipped to $150K, and held on an uptrend. His caveat on the data is worth noting — "sometimes this is incorrect anyway. It's a good guideline to follow" 43:02–43:07.

The stated rule that emerges: avoid heavily bundled tokens unless they are at least a day or two old, show demand on dips, and have an active X community 43:07–43:26. And he scopes the whole screen: bundling "matters mostly when it comes to like these tokens that are under an hour old" 40:48.

🔗 ConnectionBundle screening → the five questions — this is question three (who controls the supply) and question two (is the volume real) collapsing into a single check. FOMO never says so explicitly, but the bundle percentage is the only screen in the course that answers two of his five questions at once, which is why it earns the most airtime. (19:37 → 03:27)

🐾 Two Trader Archetypes, And FOMO Names The Distribution Himself

The taxonomy at 09:41–15:56 is unusually honest about who wins.

The new pair high volume gambler — "trenchers." These sit on the Meme Scope screen 24/7 watching every token created, hovering the thought-bubble icon to read the deployer's attached thesis and linked source, running wallet trackers that ping constantly 09:56–10:35. The dream is buying under $50K market cap, holding through migration, and selling at $200–300K for a 10–20x 11:32.

FOMO's objections are time and competition, not morality: "you're competing against kids that are in front of a computer for 16 hours a day, and they know this stuff like the back of their hand" 11:16. He says he was there himself, and credits it with teaching him to spot narratives 12:05.

The narrative trader. His preferred style, illustrated through a trader he calls World, who bought $1,500 of Jimothy at $175K market cap, sold roughly $12,000 around $5.48M market cap, and was still holding $110,000 of the position 12:21–12:50.

"This is the guy that I want to be... I don't want to be the guy that's buying and selling a million tokens day after day after day. That'll make me go literally insane."
🔍 Source Verified✅ Confirmed, and FOMO's timeline is accurate. JIMOTHY launched via Pump.fun on 16 July 2026, named after a Seattle raccoon with a rare congenital spinal condition that gives him an unusually short, stocky appearance. It surged roughly 52x in 24 hours to a ~$22M market cap on 18 July, and later climbed above $40M with $44M of volume following White House social media attention. FOMO's "started picking up volume on July 16th in the morning" and the 175K→5.48M trade both sit inside that documented range. Source: KuCoin flash; Yahoo Finance; CoinDCX; Bitcoin World.

The distribution claim is the section's most valuable line, because it inverts the usual course pitch. At 15:44 FOMO says of the narrative style: "this trading style is like the top 10%. Not everyone does this" — and immediately recommends beginners start with the gambler screen anyway, "just to understand what 90% of people do."

🎯 Against ConsensusMost memecoin instructional content sells speed and early entry as the edge. FOMO argues the opposite: the earliest-entry style is the crowded, time-intensive, structurally disadvantaged one, and the edge is in holding a narrative longer than the people who found it first. That is a genuinely contrarian position inside his own genre. 13:21

His own stated criteria for a narrative worth holding: a sticky narrative that "feels like it'll go the distance," holders steadily growing even while volume and price decline, attention outside the crypto Twitter bubble, and buyers who "aren't pumping and dumping tokens 24/7" 22:35–23:11.

🔗 Connection"Holders growing while volume declines" → the top traders profit screen — these are the same measurement from opposite ends. Rising holders during a drawdown means the existing profit-holders are not distributing, which is precisely what question four asks. It is the one leading indicator in the course that does not require a chart. (22:52 → 43:30)

👛 The Information Desk: Wallets, Telegram And A Second Monitor Of Curated Tweets

The wallet-tracking method at 26:09–28:01 opens with a prohibition rather than an instruction.

"Don't sort by the biggest profit and immediately copy the first wallet."

The four criteria he screens on instead — hold time, tokens bought per day, repeatability across tokens, and drawdown profile — are broken out with his exact framings in the Phase 3 card below. The one worth flagging here is the last, because it is not about the wallet at all.

💡 Key InsightThe drawdown screen is the only criterion in the course about the copier rather than the copied. A wallet with a high-variance path can be genuinely profitable and still uncopyable at small size, because the copier exits on the drawdown. That is a portfolio-construction argument smuggled into a wallet-selection screen, and it is the most sophisticated idea in the video. 27:30

Telegram he splits into influencer channels and utility channels, and he is notably self-implicating about the first — "I know I sound kind of contradictory cuz I have my own Telegram channel" — offering the test of whether posted tokens dump immediately afterward 28:18–29:18. The structural warning behind his due-diligence instruction is the single most useful line in that stretch:

"Is this the exit liquidity group chat?"

Some chats are the real chat; "more often than not sometimes the chat that you're in is actually the exit liquidity chat — or like the chat where they send the contract address after they've accumulated in like a side chat" 29:31–30:01.

On X, the architecture is curated lists pinned to the timeline plus X Pro decks on a second monitor, running beside the terminal and wallet tracker. He names the accounts he tracks by role — Tio for low-market-cap entries, Voss as a bag worker on Jimothy, Enigma and Godric for technical analysis, Lux Pro for new-pair volume 31:20–31:49 — and third-party real-time tweet feeds (J7 Tracker, UXTO) aggregating posts from the White House, Elon Musk and news sources. His illustration of why: if Trump misspells a name, "that will 100% be turned into a memecoin and then everyone's going to rush to buy it" 32:28–33:20.

🔍 Source Verified❌ Outdated as stated. FOMO says the X Pro deck hack is available "if you're an X blue check mark holder" 30:38. Since 26 March 2026, X Pro (pro.x.com, formerly TweetDeck) requires a Premium+ subscription — roughly $40/month — not basic Premium or a blue check. Anyone following this instruction on a standard Premium plan will find the tool inaccessible. Source: Engadget; Thurrott; GIGAZINE (27 March 2026).
📜 AnalogThe two-monitor setup — terminal plus wallet tracker on one screen, curated social feed on the other — is structurally identical to a traditional day-trading desk running a Level 2 book beside a newswire. The instrument changed; the information architecture did not. What differs is that the "newswire" here is the fundamental, since attention is the only input driving price. 31:49

📉 The Chart Section Is The Weakest Part Of The Course, And FOMO Says So First

The technical analysis segment runs from 36:57 to 40:34 and again at 47:00–49:20, and FOMO repeatedly undercuts his own authority in it.

"I am horrible at explaining how charts work and why I do what I do. It's kind of just like a gut feeling."

He is equally direct earlier: "I'm still definitely not a master when it comes to like charts and whatnot, but I try to be logical" 37:23, and on the Fibonacci tool, "I'm not a TA expert by any means. This is just from like copying other people and studying how they work" 39:04.

The four tools he demonstrates — the measuring tool used as a selling-pressure proxy rather than a predictor, Fibonacci retracement with the 0.618 "golden pocket" and 0.786 levels, one-second-chart wicks read as one seller versus broad distribution, and the support/resistance flip worked live on Troll — are specified in the Phase 3 card below.

His honest summary of why any of it works is the most defensible statement in the section: "we all look at the same lines, we all draw the same lines and circles and stuff. So we're trading similarly across the board" 49:10. Reflexivity, not prediction.

⚡ TensionAt 40:20 FOMO says "a lot of people say that TA and memecoins is BS. But I still use them. I definitely do think that they work." At 40:27 — seven seconds later — he says his chart decisions are "kind of just like a gut feeling." A method he cannot articulate is not evidence that the method works; it is evidence that he cannot distinguish it from intuition. He is the one who surfaces this, but he does not resolve it.

The one unambiguous recommendation in the section is a price call: with Troll around $40M, having previously said $35–40M was the worst case, "I would probably recommend buying troll here" 47:24–47:31.

🛡️ The Risk Warnings He Gives, And The Outcome Distribution He Doesn't Show

FOMO opens with a standard disclaimer — "nothing in this video is financial advice. I'm not a financial adviser. This is purely for entertainment purposes only" — and then calls it "mumbo jumbo" 00:47–00:56. But the operational security block that follows at 02:19–03:05 is substantive and worth recording verbatim in substance:

  • Use a wallet dedicated to trading, and "only put in an amount that you can genuinely afford to lose."
  • "Never share your login or your seed phrase or private key with literally anybody."
  • "Don't connect your wallet through random links and replies or telegram messages."
  • Copy the contract address from the original source and verify name, ticker and chart before acting — "there can be 20 coins with the same name if you simply just search for the name on literally any trading terminal."

His summation of that block is the strongest risk line in the video: "None of this is exciting, but one bad click can wipe out every good trade before you even made it" 03:00–03:05.

Elsewhere he is consistent about position sizing on gambles — "just use a small percentage of your portfolio... I gamble sometimes too and I try to manage my risk when it comes to gambling as well" 34:40–34:54 — and about quitting: "Don't force it. Like if you truly don't have time or if you try this meme scope trading style for like a few weeks and it's just not your thing," rotate to higher-conviction established tokens instead 46:40–47:10.

Against that, the framing claims are considerably more optimistic. He opens by saying memecoins are "one of the most profitable sectors that I have ever experienced," having tried dropshipping, a digital marketing agency and an AI agency, and describes coming into the space with over $30,000 in debt and coming out with "well over seven figures in realized profit" 00:00–00:47. He characterises the skill requirement as being "chronically online" with "a bit of taste when it comes to culture," and says "you'll be fine. I promise" 05:08–05:28. He calls it "the easiest side hustle or sector that I've ever dabbled with," having started at the end of 2023 and gone in properly at the beginning of 2024 25:44–26:04.

🔍 Source Verified⚠️ The market has genuinely improved, and the ceiling is still very low. Pump.fun trader profitability hit a two-year high of 73.3% in April 2026, up from a 30.1% low in June 2025 — so the "this is working right now" framing is supported. But the distribution behind it is narrow: roughly 2.05 million wallets, 65.1% of the profitable group, earned between $1 and $500 in April 2026, and 99.6% of Pump.fun traders have never locked in profits above $10,000. The study measures realized PnL only, so it excludes holders who never sold. CoinGecko's own read attributes the improvement to attrition — active wallets fell from a 5.2 million peak in May 2025 to 1.8 million by December 2025, leaving a more experienced base. Source: CoinGecko research via Phemex, BeInCrypto, Brave New Coin, The Market Periodical (2026).
🌐 Consensus CheckFOMO's outcome is real and his method is coherent, but the base rate he is drawn from is the extreme right tail of a distribution where the modal profitable outcome is under $500. The same data that supports his "it is easier than it used to be" claim also shows that sub-$10,000 lifetime profit describes 99.6% of participants. Both facts are true simultaneously, and only one of them is in the video. 00:38
📜 AnalogHe notes that a memecoin's underlying subject dying "wouldn't be a bad thing unfortunately in meme coins. Like we we all remember what happened with Peanut" 46:19–46:26. That is accurate: PNUT rose to roughly a $130M market cap after Elon Musk posted about the euthanised squirrel. The attention mechanism he teaches is indifferent to the valence of the attention, which is the cleanest illustration in the video of what "attention is the fundamental" actually means. 46:23

Phase 3: Specific Item Breakdown

🟣 The Five-Question Screen — The Course's Actual Deliverable

A fixed pre-trade sequence that forces supply and holder-profit questions ahead of the chart, and which FOMO says you must be able to answer or you do not have a trade.

The Questions

  1. What kind of trade am I taking — gamble, narrative, or conviction hold? 2. Is the attention and volume real? 3. Who controls the supply? 4. Who is already in the token and how much profit are they sitting on? 5. What would prove my thesis wrong?

How Each Is Answered

Q1 by honest money classification ("do I have money I could kiss goodbye"). Q2 by the volume + fees-paid filter pair and bundle check. Q3 by the holders panel and bundle percentage. Q4 by the top traders panel. Q5 has no screen — it requires imagining a specific concrete event.

The Rule

"If you cannot answer them, you do not have a trade yet. You just have a coin you want to buy." 03:48

A real, verified zero-platform-fee multichain trading app that does what FOMO says it does — but his reason for preferring it overstates the competition's fees by 5–10x.

What It Is

Non-custodial trading super app. Fund in USD, one balance, trade across Solana, Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum and additional EVM chains with no bridging. Supports limit orders, DCA, stop-loss and take-profit. Zero platform fees.

The Wrong Claim

What It Shows

🟣 The Volume + Fees Paid Filter Pair — The Best-Reasoned Screen In The Course

Volume can be manufactured; accumulated fees require real transactions, so the two rising together is FOMO's trigger to open a chart.

Why Volume Alone Fails

"Bots can trade with each other, teams can manufacture activity, and one wallet can create a lot of noise." 16:27

Why Fees Paid Works

"In order for fees to accumulate, it has to be real traders with real transactions." 16:41

The Trigger

Both picking up together. Then open the chart and check buy/sell activity, holder growth, distribution, bundles and which wallets are causing the move 16:44–17:06.

No Magic Numbers

FOMO explicitly refuses thresholds — "the market changes and people's ceilings change" — and says new launches should not share thresholds with migrated tokens 17:13–17:21.

🟣 The Bundle Check — Highest-Value Screen, Applied By Token Age

On tokens under an hour old, bundled supply means the deployer will sell into your buy; on tokens a day or two old that have held dips, FOMO says the number stops mattering.

The Chart Tell

Big green candle at launch → small dip → second big green candle. "Usually what that means is that the person who deployed or launched this token... bundled the token." 19:47

The Panel Confirmation

Bundler icons in the holders list. Bundlers are sniper bots buying at launch — "but also consider it that it is the developer itself." 20:41

The Stagnation Signature

High transactions, high volume, flat or declining price. FOMO's example: 63% bundled, 10,000 transactions, high volume, price going nowhere — "as soon as buy volume comes in, the bundler is selling... It's going to be a stagnant token essentially." 41:38

The Exception

"Bundles don't really worry me when it's a token that has already gone up" 42:26. His stated condition set: at least a day or two old, demand visible on dips, active X community. He also flags that the terminal's bundle estimate "sometimes is incorrect anyway" 43:02.

🟣 The Top Traders / Holder Profit Screen — Question Four

Read the unrealised profit sitting above you, because that is the selling pressure you will run into.

What To Look At

Whether the wallets already in the token are in profit or loss, how much they have sold relative to their gains, and how long they have been holding.

FOMO's Live Read

On a position he held: one wallet up $8,000 having sold only $700, several that had sold nothing, one that sold $1,000, one $500, and a single wallet that had sold $4,000. His conclusion — "the majority of the top 20 holders have actually been holding... and most of them have been holding for more than a day, which is nice" 43:30–44:29.

Why It Matters

"If it does dip, it doesn't mean that it's a bunch of newer holders that are just going to be itching to sell on the first red candle." 44:29 The inverse also applies: at a low market cap where everyone above you is up "a ridiculous amount of money," a dump can cascade 43:39.

🟣 Wallet Tracking — Four Criteria, And One Prohibition

Screen tracked wallets on hold time, trade count, repeatability and drawdown profile — and never copy the top of a profit leaderboard.

The Prohibition

"Don't sort by the biggest profit and immediately copy the first wallet." 26:27

The Four Criteria

Hold time (seconds / minutes / hours / days). Tokens bought per day and trades taken. Whether profit came from one lucky trade or a repeated process. Drawdown profile — avoid wallets swinging +$5K, −$10K, +$30K.

The Reasoning On Drawdown

"If you're starting with a small port, these drawdowns and crazy ass moves are just going to mess you up mentally 100%." 27:30 This is a copier-fitness criterion, not a wallet-quality one.

The Starting Rule

"Start by tracking a small select few of wallets that meet your criteria or your trading style." 27:53

🟣 Pump.fun, Bonding And Migration — The Structure Underneath Everything

A token in the pre-migration / pre-bonded phase has not yet reached decentralised exchanges. On migration it becomes tradable on DEXes such as Jupiter and Meteora, and in some cases centralised exchanges.

FOMO's Position

🟣 The Pre-Trade Plan — Four Commitments Before Clicking Buy

Position size, hold condition, profit target and exit trigger, all decided before entry — the section FOMO calls the point of the entire analysis.

The Four

Maximum position size. What has to stay true for me to keep holding. Where I am willing to take profit. What makes me leave the trade completely. 34:57–35:11

The Sizing Rule

Inverse to market cap and liquidity. "I'm not going to put the same amount of money into a token that was created 20 minutes ago that I would put into an established coin that's survived multiple drawdowns and is more than a few weeks old." 35:16

The In-Trade Monitoring

Whether volume keeps returning, whether the important holders are still holding, and whether the original reason for buying is still true. "I'm not changing my thesis just because the price went down or a specific influencer sold." 35:29–35:49

The Exit

Trim to plan on the way up; on a broken thesis, "I leave and I don't look back," then log why you entered, what you missed and what you would do differently 35:49–36:07.
