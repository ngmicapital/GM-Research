The Memecoin Method Is A Screening Sequence, Not A Chart Skill

Source fomo | solana meme coin trader · Date n/a · 49 min
Domain Crypto & Markets — Memecoin Trading Instruction
Reliability Mixed — the screening methodology is specific, internally consistent and executable, but the course carries an affiliate placement whose supporting fee claim is wrong by 5–10x, and one tool access requirement is out of date.
Content Type Instructional Course / Tutorial
Full Mode

⚡ The Bottom Line

  • The deliverable is a five-question pre-trade sequence, not a set of indicators. Trade type, volume authenticity, supply control, holder profit, and falsifier. FOMO: "If you cannot answer them, you do not have a trade yet. You just have a coin you want to buy."
  • Bundling is the highest-value screen taught, and it is applied asymmetrically by token age — decisive under an hour old, deliberately ignored on tokens a day or two old that have held their dips with an active community.
  • He names the distribution against himself: the new-pair gambling style is roughly 90% of traders, the narrative style he recommends is "like the top 10%" — and he still tells beginners to start with the gambling screen to learn what most people do.
  • The chart section is the weakest part and he says so: "I am horrible at explaining how charts work and why I do what I do. It's kind of just like a gut feeling."
  • One load-bearing claim is materially wrong. Competing terminals do not charge "up to 5%" per trade — Axiom, BullX, Photon and GMGN charge 0.5%, Trojan and BonkBot 1.0%. What actually costs 3.2–6.6% round-trip is fees plus priority plus slippage plus MEV, and a zero-fee terminal removes only the smallest piece.

🔑 Key Claims (Ranked)

1. The five-question screen is what separates a trade from a purchase ↳ Evidence: FOMO structures the entire video around it, returning to it at 03:05, 33:24 and 40:34, and explicitly maps each screen he demonstrates to one of the five questions. ↳ Caveat: Question five — what would prove my thesis wrong — has no data screen behind it and depends entirely on the trader's imagination. ↳ Consensus: Aligned with general trading discipline; unusual in memecoin instruction, which typically leads with entry speed.

2. Bundling is the single most useful screen on new pairs ↳ Evidence: The chart tell is a big green candle at launch, a dip, then a second big green candle; confirmation is bundler icons in the holders panel. The stagnation signature — high transactions, high volume, flat price — is the deployer selling into incoming buy flow 41:38. ↳ Caveat: FOMO explicitly ignores bundle percentage on tokens over a day or two old that hold dips with an active X community, and notes the terminal's bundle estimate "sometimes is incorrect anyway" 43:02. ↳ Consensus: Aligned — this is standard practice among experienced Solana traders and the mechanics are accurately described.

3. Competing terminals charge "upwards of up to 5%" per buy and sell — WRONG ↳ Evidence for the correction: Published schedules put Axiom, BullX, Photon and GMGN at 0.5% per transaction and Trojan and BonkBot at 1.0%; cashback tiers push effective rates to 0.5–0.8%. ↳ Caveat: Total round-trip cost on a Pump.fun migration trade — fee + 0.1–0.3% priority/Jito + 0.5–2% slippage + MEV — does reach 3.2–6.6%. But that is not a platform fee, and a zero-fee terminal removes only its smallest component. ↳ Consensus: The clearest factual error in the course, sitting directly under the affiliate placement disclosed at 49:18.

4. Azura works as described, and the mechanics taught are terminal-independent ↳ Evidence: A real non-custodial trading super app — fund in USD, one balance, trade across Solana, Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum and further EVM chains without bridging, with limit orders, DCA, stop-loss and take-profit, and no platform fee. ↳ Caveat: Everything methodologically useful — Meme Scope filtering, bundle checks, top-trader reads, wallet tracking — exists on every competing terminal. ↳ Consensus: Aligned.

5. Volume plus fees paid is a better screen than volume alone ↳ Evidence: "Bots can trade with each other, teams can manufacture activity, and one wallet can create a lot of noise" 16:27, whereas "in order for fees to accumulate, it has to be real traders with real transactions" 16:41. ↳ Caveat: FOMO deliberately gives no threshold numbers, and says thresholds should differ between new launches and migrated tokens — so the screen is directional, not mechanical. ↳ Consensus: Aligned, and the best-reasoned single idea in the video.

6. Track wallets on hold time and repeatability, never on biggest profit ↳ Evidence: Four criteria in order — hold time, tokens bought per day, whether profit came from one lucky trade or a repeated process, and drawdown profile 26:36–27:38. ↳ Caveat: The drawdown criterion is about the copier's psychology, not the wallet's edge — "if you're starting with a small port, these drawdowns... are just going to mess you up mentally 100%" 27:30. ↳ Consensus: Emerging — more sophisticated than standard copy-trading advice.

7. The Telegram group you are in may be the exit-liquidity chat ↳ Evidence: "More often than not sometimes the chat that you're in is actually the exit liquidity chat — or like the chat where they send the contract address after they've accumulated in like a side chat" 29:41. ↳ Caveat: FOMO applies the same test to his own channel, which is unusually candid for the format. ↳ Consensus: Aligned.

8. X Pro decks are available to blue check holders — OUT OF DATE ↳ Evidence for the correction: Since 26 March 2026, X Pro (pro.x.com, formerly TweetDeck) requires an X Premium+ subscription at roughly $40/month. Basic Premium no longer grants access. ↳ Consensus: Factually superseded, though the underlying two-monitor idea works on any multi-column tool.

9. Named example tokens all check out ↳ Evidence: JIMOTHY launched via Pump.fun on 16 July 2026, named after a Seattle raccoon with a rare congenital spinal condition, surging ~52x in 24 hours to ~$22M by 18 July and later above $40M. TRIPLET is the Tung Tung Tung Sahur Italian-brainrot token. FARTCOIN traded near $145–170M in September 2026. PNUT reached roughly $130M after the euthanised-squirrel news cycle — exactly the point FOMO makes at 46:23 about attention being indifferent to valence. ↳ Consensus: Aligned; the timelines given on air are accurate.

10. "You'll be fine. I promise" — true of the current market, but the right tail is narrow ↳ Evidence: Pump.fun trader profitability hit a two-year high of 73.3% in April 2026, up from a 30.08% low in June 2025, so the "this works now" framing is supported. ↳ Caveat: 65.1% of that profitable group made $1–$500 in the month, and 99.6% of Pump.fun traders have never locked in profits above $10,000. The study counts realized PnL only. CoinGecko attributes the improvement to attrition — active wallets fell from 5.2M in May 2025 to 1.8M by December 2025. ↳ Consensus: Both facts are true at once; only the favourable one appears in the video.
