🧱 "Hold the Floor": The Method, and Why It Is a Slippage Rule Wearing a Conviction Costume

"Finally had a catalyst. Ape like as much size as I can because I hold the floor." 04:02 — Thokani

Everything Thokani does well in this interview runs through one idea, and rasmr — who admits "I get spooked if I put that much in" 03:41 — is right to press him on it. The definition, in full:

"If you're like buying a chart with like not too much volume, and let's say like in Railgun I bought I slipped it up maybe like 2 3%. Most I can lose if someone dumps on me, I still after I lose like 5 to 10%. That's what barely means to like hold the floor." 05:04

And the counter-case, which is the part that actually teaches:

"Instead of like blasting a coin into volume… let's say coin was at 20 mil the day before, and next day it's at 30 mil. If I buy at 30 mil, it can retrace all the way back down to 20 mil. I lose 30%. That's like a bit spooky, especially for aping size." 05:22

The three conditions, as he gives them, are: (a) a chart that is "bottomed" with no recent volume expansion 05:39; (b) "the fundamentals are better and I have some type of edge catalyst" 05:53; (c) enough illiquidity that his own entry only slips the price 2–3%. Condition (c) is what makes the size safe, and it is doing all the work.

💡 Key Insight

Key Insight — "hold the floor" is not a conviction concept, it is a maximum-adverse-excursion budget, and that is why it lets him size up. Read the two paragraphs together and the logic is arithmetic, not belief. A coin that has already run 50% has a known air pocket beneath it — the level it came from. A coin that has not moved has no air pocket, because the last significant volume happened at roughly the current price, which means somebody else's cost basis is sitting right underneath his. His downside is therefore bounded by other people's entries, not by his own stop. That is why "why not ape as much as you can if you hold the floor" 06:31 is internally consistent rather than reckless: he has swapped an unbounded loss for a bounded one and spent the savings on size. Almost no retail framing of memecoin risk is stated this cleanly.

⚡ Tension — the method's most important constraint is admitted, and then buried. "There aren't really many coins you can do this with. Like most of my P&Ls have come like from outside of FOMO this month and it's just mostly just trading base coins" 06:22. This is a substantial concession sitting inside a FOMO-leaderboard interview: the strategy the whole conversation is about applies to a small, rare set of setups, and the majority of his stated month's P&L came from ordinary majors traded elsewhere. Neither speaker returns to it. Anyone taking the method away from this video should take that sentence with it.

🎯 Against Consensus — he buys illiquidity on purpose, which inverts standard risk teaching. rasmr states the orthodoxy as a question: "so you like buying coins that have no volume, at least on chain" 05:34, in a tone of disbelief. Conventional risk management treats thin books as a reason to reduce size, because exit slippage rises with position size. Thokani's inversion holds only because his exits are staged over hours-to-days into a catalyst-driven volume expansion — the Railgun push, the Banker fundamentals — not into the same thin book he entered. Remove the catalyst and the trade becomes precisely the trap the orthodoxy warns about. He never states this dependency; it is the load-bearing assumption under the whole method.

🔗 Connection: "hold the floor" 04:02 → the round-tripping confession 00:28. These are the same problem seen from two ends. Bounding the downside on entry is what lets him carry size; failing to take profits is what gave the size back five times in eight months. The method solves the entry half of the risk problem and, on his own account, does nothing for the exit half. The fix he eventually adopts is not analytical at all — it is a mechanical off-ramp rule 22:20.

💡 Key Insight — the edge was not analysis and he does not pretend it was. The actual discovery was someone else's, on a public repository, available to anyone watching commits. That is a monitoring edge, and monitoring edges are the kind that can be industrialised — a GitHub watcher on the Kohaku repo would have fired the same alert, with no group chat required. The fact that a $200m market-cap move began with an unannounced code push is the most actionable thing in this interview and neither speaker notices.

But the same tape carries a broadcast buy and a private exit, and rasmr does not ask about the gap.

📜 Analog: The GitHub-push trade is the on-chain descendant of the EDGAR filing scrape — the pre-2010 equities edge where the alpha was not in reading a filing better but in seeing it a few seconds earlier than the tape. Same structure, same short half-life, same eventual fate: it survives exactly as long as the number of people watching the repo stays small. He was already competing with at least one other person 04:29 on this one.

📋 Phase 3: The Four Distinct Plays

🧱 Hold the Floor — Buy the Illiquidity, Not the Momentum

Size is safe when the distance to the last real volume is small, so buy charts nobody has moved yet and let the catalyst supply the exit liquidity.

What It Includes

A pre-formed thesis on a coin already on the watchlist; a bottomed chart with no recent volume expansion; a specific, dated catalyst; and an entry small enough relative to nothing that it only slips the book 2–3%.

The Practice (Step-by-Step)

  1. Maintain a watchlist of coins you already liked at higher prices — the self-check is "I liked that at like a higher price. Why would I not like it now?" 03:56.
  2. Require a catalyst, not a narrative: "the fundamentals are better and I have some type of edge catalyst" 05:53.
  3. Check the chart is bottomed and thin, not extended: reject anything that ran 20m → 30m yesterday 05:22.
  4. Size so your entry slips the price ~2–3% 05:13. That slip is your position-size governor.
  5. Accept a maximum adverse excursion of 5–10%, not the 30% a momentum entry would carry 05:15.
  6. Exit into the catalyst-driven volume, not back into the thin book you entered.

The Evidence

Railgun — $55k in, $65k out (+118%) over ~48 hours 06:06. Banker — $60k in, $30k out (+50%) 06:06. Both same week, both this construction.

The Constraint He Names

"There aren't really many coins you can do this with" 06:22. Most of his stated month came from ordinary majors traded elsewhere.

🛰️ The Repo-Push Catalyst — Trade the Commit, Not the Announcement

The tradeable window opens when code lands in a public repository, which is hours to days before the press release.

What It Includes

A protocol whose upside depends on being integrated somewhere; the integrator's public repository; and a monitoring path — his was a group chat, but a commit watcher does the same job.

The Practice (Step-by-Step)

  1. Identify the dependency: RAILGUN's upside required wallet-level distribution, which required Kohaku 04:17.
  2. Watch the integrator's repo, not the token's.
  3. Fire on the push: "someone pushed the GitHub like a few minutes ago… Railgun was like implemented" 04:36.
  4. Enter immediately at whatever the thin book gives you; the thesis is already formed, so there is nothing to research.
  5. Exit into the formal announcement, not after it. He was out 09:25 before the 25 May SDK release.

The Evidence

RAIL +50% on Friday 22 May 2026 on the repo discovery alone; $1.55 → $3.49 by 24 May; market cap ~$78m → ~$201m.

The Half-Life

He was already competing with at least one other person who saw the same push 04:29, and he was laughed at when he passed it on 04:48. The edge is attention-scarcity, and it decays the moment the repo is on more than a handful of watchlists.

⚙️ Delta-Neutral Airdrop Farm — Pay Fees on One Venue, Hedge on the Free One

Generate points on the fee-charging venue you want an airdrop from, hold the offsetting position on the zero-fee venue, and your only cost is a number you can compute in advance.

What It Includes

Nado (Kraken team's spot + perp DEX on Ink, airdrop pending) as the points venue, Lighter as the zero-fee hedge venue, and BTC or ETH as the instrument.

The Practice (Step-by-Step)

  1. Open the position on Nado — "use NATO [Nado] just to farm the airdrop" 13:08.
  2. Hold the opposite side on Lighter — "you're paying no fees on Lighter. You only paying fees on NATO" 14:34.
  3. Restrict to BTC/ETH only, because Nado's book is thin: "you got to only trade like BTC ETH and stuff" 14:08.
  4. Size the carry against an expected-value estimate of the airdrop — "just do some good math" 14:26.
  5. Farm early in the points window while it is "very underfarmed" 14:00.

Who It Is For

rasmr identifies the audience correctly — smaller accounts, "they don't need the liquidity" 14:11.

The Unpriced Risks (his words do not cover these)

protocol and bridge risk on a new L2 venue; funding-rate divergence between two books; sybil-screening rules that can void the points; and no guarantee the airdrop happens at all. His only stated basis for the payoff is "I don't think NATO is going to be that bad of an airdrop" 14:34 — an impression, not a model.

🪜 The Off-Ramp Ratchet — The Rule He Built After Five Round Trips

Convert profits out of the venue on a schedule rather than on a view, because on his own record the alternative failed five times in eight months.

What It Includes

A fixed practice of moving realised profit out of the trading account — off-ramping — decoupled from any market opinion.

The Practice (Step-by-Step)

  1. Recognise the pattern first: "the last 8 months I've probably ran up my FOMO to like 200k back to 40k maybe like five times" 00:28.
  2. State the rule as a prohibition, not a target: "there's no grace in round tripping" 22:20.
  3. Off-ramp aggressively and often — "I off-ramp it like a good amount" 00:19, in the same breath as a $175k month.
  4. Park it, do not redeploy: "buy cycle lows. Make 8X next cycle easy" 22:24.
  5. Keep personal burn low so the off-ramped capital is not consumed — one $6k watch against a mid-six-figure book 27:43.

The Evidence

Entirely self-reported and entirely negative — this is a rule derived from five documented failures, which is the strongest kind of evidence a single trader can offer about himself and still not evidence that the rule works.

⚠️ The other edge of this guard

aggressive off-ramping is the correct response to his failure mode, and it is also how you exit a trend early. He does not price that cost anywhere on tape, and the "8X next cycle easy" claim at 22:24 is the part of the rule with no support at all behind it.
