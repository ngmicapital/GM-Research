⚡ The Bottom Line

  • This is a treasury story, not a conviction story. The Fed cut to zero, $500M of corporate cash stopped earning, and Saylor went looking for a replacement asset. Bitcoin appears three minutes into a fourteen-minute answer.
  • The reasoning is engineering, not finance. Feedback time constants, adiabatic systems, half-lives. The 21M cap matters to him because it sets a leak rate to zero, and the whole thesis lands as a rule-of-72 division by zero.
  • "I was right" is two claims, and only one is settled. The 2020 tranche multiplied. The aggregate position — 845,050 BTC at an average $76,052 against bitcoin near $79,425 — sits about +2.4% as of early September 2026.

🔑 Key Claims (Ranked)

1. The Fed's zero-rate policy, not a bitcoin thesis, triggered the decision
↳ Evidence: "I had $500 million of cash" 03:56–04:10; FY2020 10-K confirms $456.7M cash at 31 Dec 2019, plus short-term Treasuries.
↳ Consensus: Aligned — a textbook financial-repression response, unusual only in its destination.

2. Bitcoin is the first absolutely scarce commodity; everything else is scarce by cost
↳ Evidence: "Silver's not. Gold's not. Palladium's not. Diamonds aren't. Even land isn't" 07:44–07:55, with Boston and Miami Beach landfill as the counterexamples.
↳ Consensus: Contrarian — orthodox monetary economics treats fixed supply as a defect, not a feature.

3. He did not understand money before Bitcoin — stated on the record
↳ Evidence: "I was first in my class [...] at MIT, but I didn't really understand money" 03:44–03:55.

4. The half-life framing: 72 ÷ issuance rate, and zero issuance gives infinity
↳ Evidence: dollar ≈10 years, gold ≈36, Bitcoin unbounded 10:36–10:49.
↳ Caveat: supply growth is not purchasing-power decay. Long-run US CPI has averaged nearer 3% than 7%, so the realised dollar half-life is materially longer.

5. Time-horizon mismatch beats strategy quality
↳ Evidence: "You can't have a four-year plan when your boss has a four-week plan" 02:43–02:48. His floor: four years for a business, a decade for a great one 03:03–03:20.

6. Adoption is generational, because the incumbents have no incentive
↳ Evidence: Planck paraphrase plus "It's young people that have everything to gain, nothing to lose" 13:15–13:27.
↳ Caveat: this is a sociology of adoption, not a property of the asset — it makes disagreement unfalsifiable.

7. The "$25 million a year" the treasury lost is not supported by the numbers
↳ Evidence: 2019 short-duration yields of 2.1–2.4% on a $456.7M–$578.3M treasury imply $10–14M; $25M needs ~4.3–5.5%. Exact interest-income line not retrievable — unresolved.
