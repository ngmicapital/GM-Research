Physics And Incentives: Mert Mumtaz Ranks Solana, Zcash And NEAR — Two Convictions And One Asymmetric Bet

Full Mode

⚡ The Bottom Line

  • Mumtaz screens assets on exactly two criteria — "physics and incentives" — and uses them to rank Solana and Zcash as high conviction and NEAR as a smaller, explicitly higher-risk asymmetric bet. Everything else in the interview is downstream of that two-variable filter, including his dismissal of Ethereum's L2 strategy as "fighting physics."
  • His answer to "why own SOL if the chain succeeds but the token doesn't capture value" is that block space is not a commodity. Liquidity is sticky, state is impossible to replicate, and anyone trading competitively on Solana — his example is a Citadel or a Jane Street racing to place a trade first — must hold and spend SOL to win the fee auction.
  • He predicted with "100% certainty" that Solana's inflation-reduction proposal would pass around autumn, roughly two months from recording. It passed in late August 2026 — narrowly, at 67% of participating stake against a two-thirds threshold. The compute-proportional burn he floated as a second step has since become a live proposal.
  • On Zcash he separates two things the market conflated: a genuine four-year counterfeiting bug in the Orchard circuit, and a fake "Zcash is down" panic caused by block explorers that had not updated their nodes. The turnstile mechanism caps the worst case at pool insolvency, not infinite supply.
  • The calls have aged extremely well in eleven weeks. ZEC traded around $450 with a $6–7B market cap at recording; it is $1,115 and $18.9B today, helped by a Grayscale spot ETF he never mentions. SOL has gone from roughly $60 to $101. Neither move was predictable from his arguments, but both are consistent with them.

🔑 Key Claims (Ranked)

1. Two variables decide everything: physics and incentives ↳ Evidence: Physics means not architecturally evading the scaling problem — his charge against Ethereum's L2 roadmap is that moving load off the core engine caps everything downstream. Incentives means an ecosystem where each participant benefits from others succeeding, versus L2s in "a knife fight against each other." ↳ Caveat: Both terms are his own coinages and neither is measurable; the framework is a heuristic dressed as a test. ↳ Consensus: Contrarian on Ethereum, increasingly aligned on monolithic-chain scaling.

2. Builder flow is the leading indicator; crypto Twitter is a lagging one ↳ Evidence: As an angel investor he saw a sharp uptick in Solana startup pitches from mid-spring into Consensus Miami, while sentiment was at its worst. "A trader can always make money by following the wave... fundamentally, they're always kind of following what's happening. They're a lagging indicator." ↳ Caveat: Deal flow visible to one well-known investor is a biased sample, and it is unauditable by anyone else. ↳ Consensus: Emerging.

3. "Solana doesn't compete with Hyperliquid" — it builds the conditions for someone else to ↳ Evidence: He rates Hyperliquid as "a much better Binance" rather than a successful general blockchain, noting Hypercore does comparatively little and the model rests on front-end distribution via builder codes. The Solana Foundation's job, in his framing, is chain quality; competing with Hyperliquid is a builder's job. ↳ Caveat: He concedes Hyperliquid "certainly could" become the general platform. ↳ Consensus: Contrarian — most commentary frames this as a direct chain-versus-chain contest.

4. Block space is not a commodity, and that is the entire SOL investment case ↳ Evidence: "Why don't you go do your trades on Cardano then?" Liquidity is sticky and state cannot be replicated; competitive execution, NFT and collectible drops, and account storage all require SOL, and staking returns MEV and trading fees while protecting against inflation. ↳ Consensus: Contrarian — the commoditised-block-space thesis has been consensus among Ethereum-aligned analysts for years.

5. Solana's monetary policy is being fixed on a two-part schedule: halve the disinflation timeline, then add a compute-proportional burn ↳ Evidence: He said the inflation proposal would pass "around fall... two months out." SIMD-0550 (also SGP-0002) passed in late August 2026 with 67% of participating stake, doubling the disinflation rate from 15% to 30% and pulling the 1.5% terminal rate forward to roughly 2029 from 2032. ↳ Caveat: He claimed 100% certainty for something that cleared a two-thirds threshold by a fraction of a percent. ↳ Consensus: Was contested; now settled by vote.

6. The Zcash "counterfeiting bug" panic conflated a real vulnerability with a fake outage ↳ Evidence: A soundness bug in the Orchard circuit could in theory mint shielded notes inside that pool. The turnstile caps the damage: the pool can be made insolvent, but supply cannot be printed beyond what went in. Separately, block explorers that had not updated after the security patch produced a viral, false "Zcash is down" claim. ↳ Caveat: He concedes the real criticism — investors bought private money without understanding that privacy means you cannot audit supply directly. ↳ Consensus: Aligned with the technical record.

7. Formal verification is the dividing line between serious and unserious crypto, and AI forces the issue ↳ Evidence: "Crypto has been built... using the Facebook approach versus the space flight approach. And with AI, that just won't cut it anymore." Zcash's new Ironwood pool ships with a machine-checked proof — more than 2,700 theorems — that the same class of bug cannot recur. ↳ Caveat: His headline example of AI-era risk, the Drift hack, was a social-engineering and privileged-access attack, not an AI-discovered code bug. ↳ Consensus: Emerging and, on the evidence since, correct in direction.

8. Zcash at 10% of Bitcoin is a conservative first target, not a ceiling ↳ Evidence: At recording ZEC was roughly 0.45% of Bitcoin's market cap. His argument is optionality — once supply soundness is provable, a transparent-only coin is "strictly inferior technologically," so the question becomes why anyone would decline the option to shield. ↳ Caveat: His own arithmetic on air is loose — he computes a 10x to $70B and prices it at ~$4,000, while 10% of Bitcoin is closer to $150B, roughly a 21x. ↳ Consensus: Contrarian, but far less so than three months ago.

9. NEAR is the low-conviction, high-asymmetry third position ↳ Evidence: Founder pedigree (Illia Polosukhin, co-author of "Attention Is All You Need"), working sharding that Ethereum abandoned, an AI edge, and cross-chain KYC-less spot settlement. He explicitly does not hold a large position and is "still forming my thesis." ↳ Caveat: "A very high-risk play cuz it depends on them actually ironing those things out." ↳ Consensus: Contrarian — NEAR is largely written off by the market.

10. Operational excellence is underrated alpha ↳ Evidence: His most concrete reason for taking NEAR seriously is how the team behaves in practice — "they're in there like a Navy SEAL team. They know what they want. They have deliverables." Kevin agrees that invoicing and follow-through reveal more about a team than any deck. ↳ Consensus: Aligned, and rarely stated as an investment criterion.
