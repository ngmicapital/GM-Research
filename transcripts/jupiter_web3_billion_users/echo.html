Jupiter's Meow Concedes The Token Story Broke — And Bets The Next Chapter On "Web4"

Source When Shift Happens · Date n/a · 81 min
Domain Crypto & Markets
Reliability Expert opinion (self-interested) — a founder narrating his own protocol's strategy and his own mistakes; the operational history is first-hand and checks out, but every forward-looking claim is an unaudited pitch and the headline revenue figure is stated loosely.
Content Type Founder Interview
Full Mode

⚡ The Bottom Line

  • The most valuable thing in this interview is a confession, not a roadmap. Meow names three specific failures behind JUP's price collapse — incoherent narrative, a DAO structure that pitted contributors against token holders, and a buyback nobody knew existed — and accepts personal authorship of all three.
  • "Doing the right thing" turned out to be worth nothing uncommunicated. Jupiter has routed 50% of protocol revenue into JUP buybacks since February 2025, and Meow says he polled people who did not know it was happening. His conclusion: the token game is a different game from the product game.
  • The "$300 million a year" figure quoted in the episode is fees, not revenue. DefiLlama's trailing-year numbers put Jupiter at roughly $306m in fees but roughly $92m in protocol revenue — so the implied "$150m of buybacks" is off by around 3x.
  • Web4 is a distribution thesis dressed as a taxonomy. Stripped of the numbering, the claim is that crypto is where the web was before user-generated content: the tech works, almost nobody participates in creating or governing money, and whoever makes that participation easy captures the next decade.
  • Jupiter is explicitly refusing to be a protocol. The stated goal is an "iPhone moment" for DeFi — own the web app, the mobile app, the APIs, the validator, a custom VM and an onchain identity layer — which is a vertical-integration bet, not a DeFi-primitive bet.

🔑 Key Claims (Ranked)

1. JUP's problem was never the mechanism; it was that nobody could connect the mechanism to a future ↳ Evidence: Meow says token holders could not see the link between Jupiter's roadmap and JUP's value "despite the 50% thing," and that the launch framing as a governance token anchored the narrative permanently. 66:02–67:17 ↳ Caveat: This is the explanation most flattering to the team — it locates the failure in communication rather than in tokenomics, unlock schedule or the 10bn supply. ↳ Consensus: Emerging — "buybacks alone don't bid a token" became widely accepted across DeFi through 2025.

2. The DAO work-group structure created a structural conflict between contributors and holders ↳ Evidence: Meow says votes that allocated budget to work groups "repeated many many many times" and built mistrust, and that he was "too late to see" it; he calls it one of three root causes of JUP's problems. 69:11–71:12 ↳ Caveat: He frames the amounts as "quite small in comparison to everything," which sidesteps the separate complaint about the team's own voting weight. ↳ Consensus: Aligned — Jupiter suspended DAO voting in June 2025 citing a "breakdown in trust," which is the same diagnosis from the other side.

3. Crypto today is a casino, and that is the funding mechanism for everything else ↳ Evidence: Meow invokes the "two truths at once" framing — acknowledge the casino, keep building a better one, and use the speculative flow to finance the rails of a new financial system. 19:53–21:05 ↳ Caveat: It is also a convenient argument for a venue that earns fees from that speculation. ↳ Consensus: Contrarian in tone, aligned in substance — few founders will say it this plainly on camera.

4. Web4 is "social money": mass participation in creating and governing money, not just trading it ↳ Evidence: The web1/web2 analogy — web3 built internet money, but the number of people creating, governing or providing liquidity for it is "extremely low," which he reads as the pre-web2 moment. 29:01–32:07 ↳ Caveat: The numbering is marketing. There is no independent definition of web4 and no metric attached to it in the episode. ↳ Consensus: Contrarian — most of the industry is arguing for institutional adoption, not retail creation.

5. The omnichain problem is social, not only technical ↳ Evidence: Meow's example is a widely-held token bridged to Solana attracting near-zero volume; he concludes there is "a very strong tribalism element" on top of the liquidity, UX, security and accounting problems. 60:41–61:12 ↳ Consensus: Emerging — bridging teams have spent five years solving the technical half and have little to show for demand.

6. Jupiter's edge is span, not any single product — "triple-S": full surface, full suite, full stack ↳ Evidence: Web plus mobile plus APIs; Ultra, lending, perps, portfolio; own validator, own VM work, own data layer — described as necessary conditions for a consumer-grade experience. 38:44–42:45 ↳ Caveat: Every component claim in this section is self-graded ("probably the best mobile app," "most widely integrated APIs") with no third-party benchmark offered. ↳ Consensus: Contrarian — the DeFi-primitive orthodoxy says do one thing and compose.

7. "Users, evangelists and token holders are one community" was a naive model ↳ Evidence: Meow says he treated all three as a single group and that the realisation they have different objectives and mental models was the year's hardest lesson. 75:33–78:53 ↳ Consensus: Emerging — a repeatable failure mode across every token with an active contributor programme.

8. Capital allocation at Jupiter runs on "energy," with reflection as the only brake ↳ Evidence: Meow says the core decision metric is intuition about people and demand rather than market sizing, and immediately volunteers that it "might be wrong" and needs periodic audit. 52:52–55:20 ↳ Caveat: He himself flags that he is unsure whether this is bullish or bearish for JUP. ↳ Consensus: Contrarian — and unusually candid for a protocol with nine figures of annual fees.
