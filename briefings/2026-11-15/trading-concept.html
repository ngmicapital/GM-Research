Alpha — Absorption

Sunday, 15 November 2026 · Sydney, Australia · Issue TBD 11 min read
TL;DR

Absorption is what happens when aggressive market orders keep hitting a price and that price refuses to move, because a larger passive order on the other side keeps refilling. It matters because it shows who is losing the fight before price confirms it. Practical takeaway: heavy one-sided volume at a level that holds is a warning that the aggressor is running out of fuel, but only act once the level, the refill and the lack of displacement all agree.

§01
What It Is
Passive size winning the contest at one price
Absorption is heavy aggressive volume that fails to move price because passive size keeps refilling.

Absorption is the moment aggression stops working. Market orders keep landing on one price, the tape prints heavy volume on one side, and price refuses to follow through. A larger passive limit order sits on the other side and keeps being replenished as it gets hit. The aggressor pays the spread to fill and gets nothing in return for it.

Treat absorption as an event, not an indicator. You will not find it on a moving average or in a candle body. You read it on a footprint or the DOM, and you only confirm it after the level has held. An identical footprint can appear at a level that breaks on the next attempt, so the signal describes what the book already did, not what it promises. This builds on Footprint Charts (Issue 13 Nov), where the bid-by-ask split at each price was the raw material; today is what you conclude from that split when price does not move.

There are two versions. Sell-side absorption happens at lows: sellers hit the bid heavily, the bid holds, and the low stays put. Buy-side absorption happens at highs: buyers lift the offer, the offer refills, and the high stays put. Both are the same contest, passive size beating aggressive size at one price.

AggressorThe side crossing the spread to fill now.
Passive sizeResting limit orders waiting to be hit.
RefillNew size arriving at the same price as the old size is consumed.
ExhaustionThe aggressive side running dry, with thin volume at the tip.
FootprintBid by ask volume at each price inside one bar.
Remember If you only remember one thing: absorption is heavy aggression that fails to move price. Confirm it with the level and the refill, never with the volume alone.
§02
Mechanics
Four reads on the footprint and DOM
Aggressors keep trading into a level, the footprint prints heavy on one side, and the passive side refills until the aggression runs dry.

The mechanic is a race between two queues at one price. Aggressive orders consume resting liquidity. When resting liquidity is replenished as fast as it is consumed, price stays put. Read it as a count, not a feeling.

Step 1, tag the aggressor. Prints at the bid are sell-initiated; prints at the ask are buy-initiated. Step 2, stack by price. On the footprint, find a price where the bid column dwarfs the ask column across several consecutive rows. Step 3, check the response. If price fails to make a new low while that bid volume keeps printing, the passive side is winning. Step 4, look for the refill. On the DOM the bid is being hit, but size keeps reappearing at the same level.

Absorption candidate = heavy aggressive volume at level  AND  displacement through level ≈ 0
Heavy means large relative to the bar's own volume. Displacement is how far price travels past the level while that volume prints. Both conditions are needed.
Sell-side check: Delta < 0 at the low  AND  new low fails to print  → passive bid is defending
Delta is ask volume minus bid volume for the bar. Require a level that matters: a prior value area low, a session low, or a high-volume node.

The opposite of absorption is a sweep, the subject of the previous issue: aggression that eats through levels. Absorption is aggression that fails to eat.

§04
In Practice
Level, displacement, refill, invalidation
Require a level that matters and zero displacement; heavy volume alone is not absorption.
1

Find the level first

Absorption only means something at a place the market already respects. Use a prior value area edge, a session extreme, or a high-volume node. Heavy volume in the middle of nowhere is just a busy bar.

2

Demand displacement of zero

The test is effort against result. Heavy aggressive volume with price moving several ticks through the level is a breakout or a sweep. Heavy volume with the level holding is absorption.

3

Wait for the refill to fail or hold

On the DOM, count how often the bid reappears after it is hit. Delta recovering while the level holds is your confirmation. A bid that stops refilling is the signal that the defence is over.

4

Invalidate on acceptance

Define the stop beyond the absorbed extreme, not inside it. If price accepts below the level on closes, the passive side has lost, and the absorption read is void.

§07
Common Traps
Where the absorption read goes wrong
Calling every heavy-volume bar absorption, when a breakout or sweep looks identical until price moves.
1
Calling any heavy volume absorption

Heavy volume with price running through the level is a breakout or a sweep. Require zero or near-zero displacement before you use the word.

2
Trading the wall instead of the refill

Walls pull. A large bid that vanishes when touched is a failure, not support. Watch whether size reappears at the same price after it is hit.

3
Ignoring the level

Absorption away from a level that matters is noise. Anchor it to a value area edge, a session extreme, or a volume node before you act.

4
Confusing absorption with exhaustion

Absorption means a passive side is winning. Exhaustion means the aggressive side has run out. The footprints look different, and so do the next moves.

!
Overhyped: the single-bar absorption signal

Many vendor alerts label absorption after the fact, and definitions vary between platforms. A single absorbed bar has weak standalone edge. Use it as a qualifier on a level you already trade, not as a trigger.
