4 ⚖ Regulatory & Legal Radar

The live regulatory risk is the data-centre and AI build-out rulemaking that the midterm result has put in play, not a new crypto-specific law, so that is the one I would price into AI names this week. Democrats took both chambers, and many winning candidates campaigned on data-centre rules or moratoriums. That is the overhang on AI capex, and it is why the sector sold off even before the Nvidia news.

Active Legislation

  • Stablecoin liquidity: reported contraction in stablecoin supply, which narrows the on-ramp for new crypto buyers.

Today's Decision Points

  • US spot BTC ETF flow reporting, Monday 16 November: the first print after the seven-day outflow streak.
  • Nvidia board process: the search for a permanent CEO and any disclosure from the investigation into the former CEO's conduct.
Regulatory read: the rule risk is data-centre restrictions, which hit AI capex and therefore Nvidia-linked names. For crypto, the bigger regulatory-type driver this week is the MSCI index decision, not a US crypto bill. I would not trade off a legislative headline without a bill number and a committee date.

Learn: circular funding and why it matters. When a chip supplier invests in or lends to the companies that buy its chips, its reported revenue and the demand for its products can look stronger than end-customer demand alone. Analysts call this circular funding. The risk is that a leadership shock or a funding squeeze can hit the supplier and its financed customers at the same time, which is what happened in the neocloud names this week.
AI read: spending is intact but the sector now carries a governance discount. I would stay small in Nvidia-financed exposure and prefer the rivals until the CEO question is settled. The signal that would change the view is a named successor with a clear capex plan.

7 🌎 Geopolitical Calendar

The Iran conflict and the oil price it is driving are the live geopolitical channel into rates, so I would sell risk rallies while Brent holds near $96. Oil is feeding the inflation print that is pushing the Fed toward hikes, and that is a more direct equity and crypto transmission than the headlines themselves.

🏴 US–Iran conflict and oil (Active)
Status: Brent is near $96 and WTI near $89. President Trump said the US does not need a deal with Iran, which pushed oil higher on Thursday. Earlier in the week, signs of restraint had pulled prices lower.
Transmission Mechanism: higher oil feeds headline inflation, which keeps the Fed on a hiking path, which lifts the 10-year and cuts into growth multiples and crypto liquidity.
Reversal Signal: a credible diplomatic step that takes Brent back toward $80, combined with a 10-year close below 5%.
🏴 US–China chip-security angle in the Nvidia case (Active)
Status: reporting on the Nvidia board investigation alleges that the former CEO's second relationship involved a person described as a Chinese intelligence asset. The allegation is unverified in this run and has not been confirmed by the company or authorities.
Transmission Mechanism: any confirmed national-security element would widen the export-control and China-revenue discount on US semis, which hits Nvidia and its supply chain first.
Reversal Signal: the board names a successor and the investigation is closed without any security finding.
🏴 Midterm policy shock to AI infrastructure (Active)
Status: the Democratic midterm sweep has raised expectations of data-centre restrictions. The Nasdaq entered a technical correction earlier this month on that concern.
Transmission Mechanism: tighter siting or power rules slow the AI build-out, which compresses the capex outlook for the chip and cloud names that are the largest equity weights in the index.
Reversal Signal: a clear federal framework that sets standards without moratoria, or a hyperscaler capex guide that holds up through the rule debate.
Geopolitical read: oil is the transmission belt. Every headline that lifts Brent pushes the Fed path higher and hurts growth assets, including crypto. I would not fade the oil move until there is a signed step toward a deal.
