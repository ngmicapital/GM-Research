Louisiana Court Bars 31 Prediction-Market Entities as CFTC Sues the State

“Gambling has been regulated by states for 250 years,” wrote Louisiana’s attorney general on X. Liz Murrill, Louisiana Attorney General — X post as reported by The Block, 14 Nov 2026

Louisiana Attorney General Liz Murrill sued Kalshi, Polymarket, Robinhood, Coinbase, Gemini and others in state court on 12 November, alleging that their sports, election and awards contracts are unlicensed gambling. The same day a state judge issued a temporary restraining order barring the defendants from offering those event contracts to Louisiana residents without state licences, for 10 days unless extended. The order names 31 entities in total.

The CFTC and DOJ answered on 13 November with a federal suit to void the state order, making Louisiana the twelfth state the CFTC has sued over these contracts. Kalshi and Polymarket US had already filed preemption suits in federal court in October. Kalshi has cut off access for Louisiana users, and Coinbase has framed the episode as fragmented enforcement while federal appeals courts still weigh the same preemption question.

📎 Historical Precedent: the Nevada motion to hold Kalshi in contempt, which our 6 July issue flagged, is the same preemption fight in another forum. Louisiana’s 2025 Gaming Control Board advisory already treated sports event contracts as gambling.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

The core question is whether Commodity Exchange Act preemption shields CFTC-registered event contracts from state gambling statutes. Louisiana’s pleading treats the contracts as sportsbook wagers under a new label, and no court has resolved that framing. The Brief's read: the 10-day TRO is the near-term operational risk, and the first federal ruling on the CFTC’s emergency posture will matter more than the state merits in the short run.

🔄 Contrarian Take

Murrill’s position rests on the claim that states have policed gambling for centuries and that federal registration does not change the product; CFTC Chairman Michael Selig has called the suit “naked lawfare.” The Brief's read: the states’ police-power argument is the stronger one for sports-outcome contracts, while election contracts give the CFTC a cleaner preemption story.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Robinhood runs its event contracts through Robinhood Derivatives, a CFTC-registered entity, and sits directly in the dispute. Kalshi and Polymarket are private, so public-market exposure runs through the brokers and exchanges named in the order. The Brief's read: venues with a federal registration and a preemption record gain; sportsbook-style products without state licences lose the most.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟩 Bullish HOOD — a CFTC-registered derivatives arm gives Robinhood a preemption argument a licensed sportsbook lacks.

🔴 Bearish COIN — Coinbase is a named defendant and falls under the same TRO.

⚪ Neutral Kalshi and Polymarket — private companies, so no listed exposure.

CounselTrack the removal, preemption and emergency-motion filings in the CFTC/DOJ suit and in Kalshi’s federal case.
ComplianceConfirm that Louisiana geoblocks are live for every named entity, including Coinbase and Robinhood.

CFTC’s Retail Crypto Rulebook Draws Exchange Interest as CLARITY Stalls

The proposal was a “critical step” toward keeping the United States the crypto capital of the world. Michael Selig, Chairman, CFTC — as quoted by CNBC, 13 Nov 2026 [secondary; CFTC release not fetched]

CFTC Chairman Michael Selig told CNBC that major crypto exchanges and new entrants are signalling they will opt into the agency’s newly proposed rules for digital assets. The package, Regulation CTX and Regulation CAM, offers a federal pathway for exchanges under existing CFTC authority and codifies crypto transaction rules for retail commodity trades. The CFTC issued an advance notice on 5 October, after the Senate failed to advance the CLARITY Act on 15 September.

Cryptobriefing reads the same remarks as the industry aligning with the CFTC while hopes for comprehensive legislation fade. CNBC notes that Democrats’ 2026 congressional gains are expected to bring closer scrutiny of crypto regulation and of the President’s crypto ties in 2027, which makes the agency route the more durable one in the near term.

📎 Historical Precedent: SEC Chairman Paul Atkins’ Project Crypto in 2025 used rulemaking rather than enforcement to reset crypto policy, and the CFTC is now following the same template.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Regulation CTX and CAM would make the CFTC the default federal home for exchanges while CLARITY stays stuck. The Brief's read: counsel should map which CFTC registration an opt-in requires, because the perimeter question, not the substantive rules, will drive who can use the framework. The CFTC gains; state regulators and SEC-track token issuers lose ground.

🔄 Contrarian Take

Selig’s claim of strong exchange demand comes from his own interview and is not yet backed by filings. The Brief's read: these are proposals, and a 2027 Congress with more Democratic scrutiny could reshape or reverse them, so opt-in demand should be discounted until final rules issue.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Exchanges with CFTC-registered derivatives arms, such as Coinbase and Robinhood, are best placed to opt in first. Polymarket US is also positioning inside the federal perimeter. The Brief's read: the framework rewards firms that already hold federal registrations and penalises offshore venues that want a US product without one.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟩 Bullish COIN — a federal exchange framework lowers state-by-state friction for spot and derivatives.

🔴 Bearish Unregistered offshore venues — no listed ticker, but they lose the US access route the framework creates.

⚪ Neutral HOOD — already federally registered, so the rules add little new.

📋 What To Do — Before 1 July
TraderWatch which exchanges announce opt-in before assuming any US venue gains a new product set.
CounselMap Regulation CTX and CAM against your client’s current CFTC registration perimeter.
CompliancePull the CFTC release and confirm the comment deadline, which our sources did not verify.
FounderDecide whether a CFTC registration path is worth pursuing before the lame-duck window closes.

NYDFS Sets Crypto Advertising Standards, With Compliance Due 10 May 2027

“For more than a decade, DFS has set the global standard for virtual currency oversight and consumer protection.” Kaitlin Asrow, Acting Superintendent, NYDFS — statement, 12 Nov 2026

The New York State Department of Financial Services issued guidance on Thursday 12 November setting expectations for crypto firms’ advertising and marketing. Firms must adopt policies and procedures that prevent false or misleading content, and must implement the measures by 10 May 2027.

The guidance requires accurate content, clear disclosures and an established governance and review process. It says advertising must not be deceptive or exaggerated, and must not claim there are no trading fees while omitting other costs. Firms must also set clear roles for advertising sign-off.

📎 Historical Precedent: NYDFS’s 2015 BitLicense regime set the licensing template that other states and regulators later followed.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

This is supervisory guidance rather than a numbered rule, but examiners use it as the yardstick for licensed-entity reviews. The Brief's read: fee-disclosure language and affiliate or influencer promotion are the first examination targets, and the governance-and-review requirement is the heavy lift for smaller licensees.

🔄 Contrarian Take

Existing federal and state consumer-protection law already prohibits deceptive advertising, so a separate crypto standard may add cost without much new coverage. The Brief's read: the guidance adds value mainly by naming the exact claims examiners will test, so the duplication argument is weaker than it looks.

🏗️ Industry Implications

NYDFS-licensed firms such as Gemini must rebuild marketing approval workflows before May. The Brief's read: firms with mature compliance functions can absorb the cost and gain share from promotion-heavy rivals, and offshore apps serving New York users face the sharpest pressure.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟩 Bullish COIN — a mature compliance function is a relative advantage under a firm marketing standard.

🔴 Bearish Promotion-led offshore apps — no listed ticker; their New York acquisition model faces tighter review.

⚪ Neutral HOOD — brokerage promotion is covered, but the cost impact is likely small.

📋 What To Do — Before 1 July
TraderExpect fee-disclosure changes on New York-facing venues before the compliance date.
CounselReview existing marketing copy against the guidance’s prohibition on omitted costs and exaggerated claims.
ComplianceAssign named owners for advertising approval and periodic review, as the guidance requires.
FounderBudget for a pre-approval marketing review before launching any New York promotion.

White House Names Four Nominees to Vacant SEC and CFTC Seats

Ji Kim, chief executive of the Crypto Council for Innovation, [paraphrased] said bipartisan representation is essential to durable rules that protect markets and support innovation. Ji Kim, CEO, Crypto Council for Innovation — post on X, as reported by The Block, 12 Nov 2026

The White House nominated four people to fill vacant seats at the SEC and CFTC, the first such nominations in roughly a year. Nathan Anonick and Matthew Rowland are nominated as CFTC commissioners, and Kristin N. Levine is nominated to succeed Hester Peirce at the SEC. A fourth nominee, Kristin Johnson, has spoken regularly on digital-asset regulation, but our sources do not confirm which seat she would fill.

Anonick and Rowland have worked on the CLARITY Act, which means the CFTC’s new commissioners would inherit the agency’s current crypto rulemaking. Peirce led the SEC’s Crypto Task Force, so her successor will shape how the SEC handles token classification after Project Crypto.

📎 Historical Precedent: the 2022 confirmation gap at the CFTC left the agency short of commissioners for months, which slowed its digital-asset work.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Confirmation timing will decide whether the CFTC can finish its proposed rules before the new Congress sits in January 2027. The Brief's read: the CFTC is the likelier beneficiary, because its nominees have direct CLARITY experience and the agency is already carrying the crypto rulebook.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Exchanges planning CFTC opt-ins benefit from commissioners who already know the bill. The Brief's read: SEC-track token issuers face more uncertainty, because the Peirce succession changes the staff-level posture on token classification.
