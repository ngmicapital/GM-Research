Alpha — Order Flow Imbalance (OFI)

Saturday · 23 May 2026 · Sydney, Australia · Issue #29 11-min read
TL;DR

Order Flow Imbalance (OFI) is the flow-based cousin of bid/ask imbalance — instead of measuring the static state of the book at one moment, it sums every signed change in best-bid and best-ask size between events: every limit-order add, cancel, and execution. Cont, Kukanov & Stoikov showed in 2014 that OFI is the single strongest linear predictor of short-horizon price change, with a slope inversely proportional to depth — and it is what fired underneath BTC's 5/22 break from $77.5K down to $75.3K while the spread looked deceptively normal.

§01
What It Is
State vs flow · the Cont/Kukanov/Stoikov reframing
OFI is a time-series of signed contributions from every book event at the best bid and best ask — adds and cancels included — that quantifies the net flow of liquidity supply and demand.

Most retail traders learn order-book reading through bid/ask imbalance — the ratio of resting size on the two sides at a given instant. It is a state measurement: a snapshot. Order Flow Imbalance is the flow measurement of the same object. Rather than asking "what's sitting on the book right now," OFI asks "what just changed." Every limit-order arrival, cancellation, and execution at the best prices contributes a signed quantity, and OFI is the running sum of those contributions over a chosen window. The reframing is small in description and enormous in predictive power.

The idea was formalised by Rama Cont, Arseniy Kukanov, and Sasha Stoikov in "The Price Impact of Order Book Events" (Journal of Financial Econometrics, 2014). On a sample of 50 NYSE stocks they showed two results that mattered: first, OFI explains short-horizon price change with an R² that routinely exceeds 60–70% in 10-second buckets — order-book state alone is barely better than coin-flip across the same windows. Second, the slope of the price-change-on-OFI line is inversely proportional to book depth: thin books move more per unit of imbalance, exactly as a microstructure model would predict. (Cont/Kukanov/Stoikov 2014)

Why does flow trump state? Because the resting size you see on the book is a residue — it includes orders posted hours ago that may already be stale to the people who placed them. The event stream — what is arriving and what is being yanked, this second — is the part that carries new information. A market maker who just cancelled $2M of bids tells you more than the $5M of bids that have been sitting there since the previous hour. Cube Exchange states it cleanly: "OFI is a proxy for how hard it has become to trade on each side of the market. When it gets harder to buy than to sell, price tends to rise."

For the crypto operator: OFI is the metric that fires before price moves — it is upstream of CVD (which only records executed flow) and upstream of bid/ask imbalance (which only sees the current state). It is what HFT market-makers compute internally to skew their quotes, what execution algos read to set urgency, and what increasingly powers the short-horizon directional models behind crypto market-making books at Wintermute, Cumberland, and Jump.

§02
Mechanics
The Cont/Kukanov/Stoikov event formula · price-impact slope · MLOFI extension
For every book event compute a signed quantity using the four cases of how best-bid and best-ask prices move; sum those contributions over a fixed window to get OFI; expect price change to be a near-linear function of that sum.

The mechanic is a one-line formula applied per book event. Define the four cases of how the best bid P^B and best ask P^A can change between events n-1 and n — for each event compute the contribution e_n, then aggregate.

Per-event contribution (Cont/Kukanov/Stoikov 2014): e_n = I{P_n^B ≥ P_{n-1}^B} · q_n^B − I{P_n^B ≤ P_{n-1}^B} · q_{n-1}^B − I{P_n^A ≤ P_{n-1}^A} · q_n^A + I{P_n^A ≥ P_{n-1}^A} · q_{n-1}^A where P^B, P^A = best bid / ask price q^B, q^A = size at that level I{·} = indicator function (1 if condition true, else 0) OFI over window T: OFI_T = Σ e_n for all events n in T Linear price-impact model: ΔP_T ≈ λ · OFI_T where λ ∝ 1 / depth Multi-Level OFI (Xu, Cont & Stoikov 2018) — top K levels: MLOFI_T = Σ w_k · OFI_T^(k) for k = 1…K (weights typically 1/√k) Normalised OFI (cross-asset comparison): NOFI_T = OFI_T / V_T where V_T = total traded volume in T
Note: the formula handles all four causes of best-level change in one expression — (a) bid price rises (new bid joins) → demand up, (b) bid price falls or size at it drops → demand down, (c) ask price falls (new ask joins) → supply up, (d) ask price rises or size drops → supply down. Adds, cancels, and executions are all natively included because each shows up as a price/size change between events.

Three properties make OFI useful in practice. First, it is computable in real time from L2 websocket data with no special licensing — the Binance, Bybit, OKX, and Coinbase order-book deltas are public. Second, the linear relationship ΔP ≈ λ · OFI holds across timescales from milliseconds to minutes; only the slope λ changes (steeper when depth is thin, flatter when depth is thick). Third, the residual variance after subtracting λ · OFI is roughly white noise — meaning OFI is genuinely consuming the predictable component of price change, not just correlating with it.

The Multi-Level OFI extension (Xu, Cont & Stoikov 2018) widens the lens beyond top-of-book. In a crypto perp where the best-bid quote churns every few milliseconds, a single-level OFI gets fragmented; aggregating across the top 5 or 10 price levels with depth-decaying weights (typically 1/√k) recovers a smoother, higher-R² signal. QuantVPS: "MLOFI accounts for imbalances across multiple price levels instead of focusing solely on the top-of-book."

The most important conceptual point: OFI dominates CVD and bid/ask imbalance as a short-horizon predictor specifically because it includes cancellations. When a market maker pulls $5M of bids without anyone hitting them, CVD sees nothing — no trade happened. Bid/ask imbalance only sees the new (lower) bid size. OFI sees the entire $5M of removed supply, contributes it to the running sum, and your model fires. This is why OFI catches institutional positioning shifts that CVD misses entirely.

§04
In Practice
Building an OFI signal · windowing · how to use it without a co-lo
The one rule: pair OFI with depth — an OFI of −20 BTC means nothing without knowing whether the book is $5M deep or $500K deep; the slope λ is inverse to depth, so the same OFI moves price 10× more on a thin book.
1

Stream the L2 Diff, Not the Trade Tape

OFI lives in the order-book delta feed — Binance depthUpdate, Bybit orderbook.50, OKX books5. Subscribing to trades only gets you CVD, which misses cancellations entirely. Bookmap, Sierra Chart, and any decent quant stack (Hummingbot, Tardis, CoinAPI) expose the full delta stream. If your "orderflow" tool only shows you executed prints, you are reading half the book.

2

Use Two Windows — Fast and Slow

Compute OFI over both a fast window (1–10 seconds) and a slow window (30–120 seconds). Fast OFI is the execution-timing signal — it tells you which side of the spread the next tick will favour. Slow OFI is the directional signal — it tells you whether you should be biased long or short over the next 5–10 minutes. The two diverging (fast pos, slow neg) is itself a signal: the trend is fading despite a local bounce.

3

Always Divide by Depth

The slope λ in ΔP ≈ λ · OFI is inversely proportional to book depth — so the same nominal OFI is meaningful on a thin book and noise on a thick one. Compute OFI / median top-5-level depth over the same window. That ratio is comparable across instruments, across sessions, and across regimes. Treat unadjusted OFI prints as data, not signal.

4

Watch the Sign, Confirm With Spread

A strong negative OFI alongside a widening spread is the dangerous combination — makers are pulling AND aggressors are hitting, which is the toxic-flow signature from Issue #23. Strong OFI with a stable narrow spread is healthy participation — flow without panic. The OFI sign tells you direction; the spread tells you whether liquidity providers agree that the move is informed or noisy.

Worked Example — OFI as an Entry-Timing Overlay
SetupYou have a swing short on BTC, taken from VWAP rejection at $77,800. Looking to add at a retest of the $77,200 lower edge of value.
Without OFIPrice retraces to $77,200. You add a half-clip short blindly. Stop above $77,800.
With OFI overlayStream BTCUSDT depthUpdate. Compute 10s OFI and 60s OFI continuously. Watch as price retests $77,200.
Scenario A — add10s OFI prints −18 BTC; 60s OFI prints −85 BTC. Spread stable. Top-5 depth steady at ~$3M. Bid cancels accelerating. Add the second clip. Flow confirms the level is rejecting.
Scenario B — skip10s OFI prints +6 BTC; 60s OFI prints +40 BTC. Bids re-stacking on every probe. Spread tight. Skip the add. Flow disagrees with your level; you are about to be the late seller.
Why it mattersSame chart, same level, opposite flow signature. OFI is the conditional probability filter you would otherwise need an entire prop desk to provide.
R math impactSkipping just one wrong add per month at 0.5R saved compounds to ~6R/year — typically the difference between a profitable and break-even swing book.

Timeframe fit: OFI's predictive R² is highest on 1s–60s horizons (where it routinely exceeds 60%); useful out to 5–10 minutes as a directional overlay; degrades to noise beyond 30 minutes. Use it for execution timing and short-horizon bias, never as a standalone swing signal. Best venues: Binance / Bybit perps (deep public L2 feeds), Coinbase spot (cleanest US tape), Hyperliquid (full L2 with cancellation info).

BTC −2.8% in 13 Hours — A Textbook Sustained Negative OFI

Yesterday's Morning Edge flagged a potential short-squeeze trigger at $78,140. The opposite happened. BTC closed Friday's Asia session at $77,491 and proceeded to grind 2.8% lower over the next 13 hours to $75,329, with the heaviest hours showing 14,000–18,000 BTC of spot volume — multiples of the 24h average. There was no single shock print. There was no headline. What there was: sustained negative order-flow imbalance that built quietly through the US afternoon and exploded through the Asia handover.

Look at the hourly cadence. 13:00 UTC: first negative tick — 14,182 BTC traded as price slipped from $77,491 to $76,734. Bid-side depth on Binance was visibly thinning while ask-side persisted. 18:00 UTC (04:00 AEST): another 11,242 BTC, price $76,714 → $76,498, this time with bid cancellations accelerating ahead of executions — classic flow-leads-print. 19:00 UTC (05:00 AEST): the decisive break. 17,912 BTC of spot volume — the largest hourly print of the week — drove price from $76,498 to a low of $75,620. 00:00 UTC 5/23 (10:00 AEST): the follow-through hour, 8,179 BTC of sustained sell flow taking price from $75,513 to $75,184. The OFI signal would have fired strongly negative from the 13:00 UTC bar onwards and never inverted.

§07
Common Traps
Where retail OFI tools mislead · and where the metric is genuinely overhyped
The single biggest mistake: trading OFI without depth normalisation — every regime has a different λ, and an OFI of −15 BTC means catastrophe in a thin book and noise in a thick one.
1
Confusing OFI with CVD

CVD only counts executed market orders, signed by aggressor. OFI counts every limit add, cancel, AND execution at the best levels. If a market maker pulls $5M of bids without anyone hitting them, CVD prints zero — OFI prints −5M and predicts the next tick down. Most retail "orderflow" platforms are CVD products, not OFI products. Know which you are reading.

2
Ignoring Depth — Trading Raw OFI

The price-impact slope λ is inversely proportional to top-of-book depth. The same OFI signal on a Sunday Asia session (thin) and a US-open hour (thick) means completely different things. Always normalise: OFI ÷ median depth. Otherwise you will treat noise as signal during US hours and miss real signal on weekends.

3
Single-Level OFI on Crypto Perps

BTC and ETH perp best-bid/ask churn dozens of times per second — the top-of-book is too noisy on its own. Use Multi-Level OFI (MLOFI) over the top 5–10 levels with 1/√k weighting. Single-level OFI looks like white noise on a 1-second chart; MLOFI shows the real flow.

4
Treating OFI as a Standalone Strategy

OFI's hit rate is around 53% directional on a per-tick basis. After fees and slippage that is not enough to trade. Use OFI as an overlay on existing setups — confluence with structure, VWAP, profile levels — not as the trigger itself. The traders who tried to build pure-OFI bots in 2018–2020 mostly lost money to transaction costs.

5
Honesty Check — "Real-Time OFI" Retail Products

Several paid platforms now sell "real-time OFI" indicators for $99–$299/month. Most are dressed-up Multi-Level Depth Imbalance — state, not flow — because true OFI requires processing the full L2 delta stream, which most retail UIs cannot do in real time. If the product cannot tell you the breakdown of adds vs cancels vs trades, it is not OFI. Build the metric yourself from raw websocket data, or accept that you are paying for a fancier version of bid/ask imbalance.
