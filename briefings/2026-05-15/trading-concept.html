Alpha — VWAP & Anchored VWAP

Friday · 15 May 2026 · Sydney, Australia · Issue #21 11-min read
TL;DR

VWAP is the volume-weighted average fill price over a window — every contract that traded, weighted by size, averaged into one line. It is the running scoreboard of who is currently winning: buyers above it, sellers below it. Session VWAP is the institutional execution benchmark; Anchored VWAP (AVWAP) is the trader's version — drop the anchor at a swing high, a swing low, a halving, a CPI print, an IPO, and the resulting line shows the average price paid by everyone who participated since that event. Trade with it as bias, against it for mean reversion, and watch the bands for stretched flows. The trade is rarely "is the indicator showing X" — it's "where did the smart money get filled, and are they currently in profit or pain."

§01
What It Is
The volume-weighted fair-fill line
VWAP is the average price every contract traded at, weighted by size — one number that summarises where the day's business actually got done.

Strip away the chart and a market session is just a list of trades: price, size, time. A simple arithmetic mean of those prices is misleading because a single block of 500 BTC matters more than fifty 0.01-BTC retail clips. VWAP — Volume Weighted Average Price — is the fix: sum every (price × size), divide by total size, get one number that is the true average fill for the session. It is what the market actually paid, weighted by what actually changed hands.

The metric was institutional before it was retail. The first algorithmic VWAP execution ran in 1984, when James Elkins at Abel Noser bought Ford Motor stock paced against the day's volume profile to minimise market impact — a benchmark a buy-side desk could measure their broker against. Forty years later, "VWAP slippage" is still the standard report card for institutional execution: did you fill above or below the session's volume-weighted average. Buying below VWAP is good; selling above VWAP is good. The benchmark created the trade, and the trade created the level traders watch.

The retail-trader version arrived later but reframed it more usefully. Instead of a session benchmark you fight against, treat VWAP as information: it tells you the average price every participant in this window paid, which tells you who is in profit and who is in pain. Price above a rising VWAP = aggregate longs winning, supply is currently quiet. Price below a declining VWAP = aggregate longs losing, every dip-buyer is underwater. That asymmetry is the edge — not the line itself but what it implies about the participants standing on either side of it.

Anchored VWAP (AVWAP), popularised by Brian Shannon from 2002 onward, is the same calculation with one change: the anchor isn't the session open, it's any point you pick that mattered. A swing high. A halving. A FOMC. A liquidation cascade. The resulting line is the average price every participant has paid since that event — and it acts as a magnet for re-tests because thousands of participants anchored their P&L to that exact moment. This is the version most useful for crypto, where the relevant clock isn't "today's open" but "since the cycle low" or "since the ETF approval."

§02
Mechanics
The formula · session reset · band construction
VWAP = Σ(P × V) / Σ(V) over the window. Anchor where you want — every participant since the anchor inherits this average.

The formula is two lines and almost embarrassingly simple — that simplicity is the feature, not a bug:

VWAP = Σ(Pⱼ × Vⱼ) / Σ(Vⱼ)

where Pⱼ = price of trade j (usually typical price = (H + L + C) / 3 of the bar)
        Vⱼ = volume of trade j
        j runs from anchor → now
Equity convention uses typical-price bars; crypto futures often use just the close. Difference is <0.1% on a normal day.

Three properties fall out of this directly:

1. It is volume-weighted, not time-weighted. Twenty bars of dead chop weigh less than one bar of climactic volume. VWAP is dominated by where the size traded, which is exactly the price level that matters for execution and reversion. This is why VWAP and SMA can diverge sharply during one-sided sessions — the SMA averages bars; VWAP averages business done.

2. Session VWAP resets, AVWAP doesn't. Session VWAP starts fresh each new trading day (in crypto, the exchange's defined session — Binance is 00:00 UTC, CME is 17:00 CT Sunday open). AVWAP runs from your anchor until you remove it; it can run for years off a single halving anchor and still produce a meaningful line because the cumulative volume gives it weight.

3. The bands are real standard deviations, not Bollinger-style fakeouts. VWAP bands take the cumulative variance of price around the running VWAP, weighted by volume, and plot ±1σ / ±2σ / ±3σ envelopes. Touches of the outer band are statistically rare — at ±2σ on a normally-distributed session, only ~5% of traded volume sits outside. Crypto sessions aren't gaussian (fat tails), so the bands compress in chop and stretch in trends, which is informationally useful in itself.

One subtle point worth internalising: VWAP is a cumulative calculation. Early-session prints carry less weight as the day progresses because the denominator grows; late-session size can drag a sleepy VWAP around violently. This is why VWAP at 03:00 UTC behaves nothing like VWAP at 23:00 UTC — and why the 18:00–23:00 UTC window (US close) is the most predictive for next-session positioning.

§04
In Practice
Bias filter · mean reversion · anchor selection
Use session VWAP as the day's bias filter and AVWAP as your structural retest line — confluence between the two is the entry, not either one alone.

The mistake most retail traders make is treating VWAP like a moving average — buy crosses, sell crosses, repeat. That's a curve-fit to the noisiest signal it produces. The actual edge sits in where the line is anchored and what it implies about participant P&L. Four ways to deploy it that actually carry weight:

i

Session VWAP as bias filter — not a signal

Above rising session VWAP, only take longs at the band stretches that pull back to it. Below declining VWAP, only take shorts. The line itself isn't the trade — it's the permission slip that filters out half your setups every day.

ii

Anchor at the event everyone is anchored to

For BTC: halving (Apr-2024), ETF approval (Jan-2024), the prior cycle low (Nov-2022), the most recent flush. These are the AVWAPs that every institutional desk is also watching — the level acts because the size is anchored to it.

iii

±2σ band touch + bias agreement = mean reversion trade

Long only when price tags −2σ and session VWAP is rising. Short only when price tags +2σ and VWAP is falling. Counter-trend at extremes only when the trend itself is intact — never in chop where the bands are meaningless.

iv

Confluence with HVN / POC / round numbers

An AVWAP that lines up with a volume profile HVN, a daily POC, and a round number isn't four signals — it's one fat zone the market is going to react to. Where these levels stack is where size sits; trade the stack, not the lines.

Quotes
Where smart desks frame this
Shannon built the modern AVWAP framework; Dalton built the language of fair value. Both are saying the same thing — price returns to where business got done.

"When prices are above a rising AVWAP the trend is 'innocent until proven guilty.' When prices are below the declining AVWAP the trend is considered 'guilty until proven innocent.'"

Brian Shannon, CMT · Alphatrends · alphatrends.net · 2024

"It is surprising how often the exact VWAP/AVWAP level will halt a retracement of the prevailing trend."

Brian Shannon, CMT · Maximum Trading Gains With Anchored VWAP · Book · 2023

"Markets exist to facilitate trade. Price moves away from value in search of activity and returns to value when activity stops."

§07
Common Traps
Where the line lies to you
VWAP isn't a moving average and the bands aren't Bollinger Bands — the most expensive mistakes come from treating it like either one.
1.
Trading the line, not the participant P&L

Buying every VWAP touch as if the line were Bollinger middle ignores the actual signal: who is positioned and where. A VWAP retest in a downtrend is a short — same line, opposite trade. The line is context; the participants are the trade.

2.
Anchoring at random points and calling it confluence

An AVWAP is only as powerful as the anchor is universally recognised. Anchoring at a random Tuesday open produces a line nobody else sees — and one with no participant cohort defending it. Use halvings, ETF approvals, CPI prints, all-time highs, all-time lows. Events with cohort memory.

3.
Fading +2σ in a trending tape

Bands compress in chop and expand in trends. A +2σ touch in a parabolic move isn't extension — it's the new regime. The mean-reversion trade only works when the trend is intact and the bands are stable; otherwise you're catching a falling knife with a statistical justification.

4.
Using session VWAP in 24/7 markets without an exchange convention

Crypto has no universal session. Binance VWAP resets at 00:00 UTC; CME futures roll on Sunday 17:00 CT; TradingView lets you pick. Three different platforms can show three different "session VWAPs" at the same moment. Pick one convention per chart and stop comparing them.

5.
Overhyped: "VWAP cross" strategies

The retail YouTube cottage industry around "buy when 5-min closes above VWAP" is curve-fitting noise. Session VWAP gets crossed dozens of times in any given chop session — the signal-to-noise is awful. The valid use is structural (bias, retests, anchor confluence), not mechanical (cross-and-trade). If a strategy can be coded in three lines of Pine, it's been arbed away.
