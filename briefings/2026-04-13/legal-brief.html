CFTC Names Six-Member Innovation Task Force to Set Rules for Crypto, AI, and Prediction Markets

"The Innovation Task Force brings together a leading team that exhibits deep expertise and an enthusiastic commitment to deliver clear rules of the road for American innovators." — CFTC Chairman Michael S. Selig, April 10, 2026

On April 10, CFTC Chairman Michael Selig formally named the six-member Innovation Task Force (ITF), led by senior adviser Michael J. Passalacqua. Members include Hank Balaban (formerly Latham & Watkins), Sam Canavos (formerly Patomak Global Partners), Mark Fajfar (CFTC Office of General Counsel), Eugene Gonzalez IV (formerly Sidley Austin), and Dina Moussa (CFTC Market Participants Division). The task force will develop regulatory frameworks across three domains: crypto assets and blockchain, artificial intelligence and autonomous systems, and prediction markets and event contracts.

The ITF launch follows the March 11 SEC–CFTC Memorandum of Understanding and the joint interpretive release on crypto asset classification (March 17). It signals the CFTC moving from enforcement-pause mode to active rulemaking, consistent with the agency's pivot under Chairman Selig toward "clear rules of the road" for the industry.

📎 Historical Precedent: The CFTC's LabCFTC (2017) was the agency's prior innovation hub — the ITF has a harder mandate: binding rulemakings, not just engagement.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis The ITF membership is notably practitioner-heavy: three members come from private practice (Latham, Sidley, Patomak), a composition that favours industry-compatible rulemaking. The three-domain mandate — crypto, AI, prediction markets — mirrors the scope of the CLARITY Act's CFTC provisions, suggesting the ITF will generate regulatory proposals that feed directly into the legislative process. Firms operating prediction markets (Kalshi, Polymarket) and tokenized derivatives platforms face the highest near-term exposure: the ITF will define what "event contracts" can lawfully offer. The CFTC wins jurisdictional surface area; platforms get faster legal certainty.
🔄 Contrarian Take Task forces of this type routinely produce discussion papers rather than binding rules. With the CLARITY Act still pending Senate floor debate, the ITF may defer hard questions on DeFi and decentralised derivatives to Congress — reducing near-term regulatory certainty even as it signals intent. Industry lawyers who read the ITF launch as a green light to structure novel prediction market products may be moving too fast.
🏗️ Industry Implications Kalshi and Polymarket benefit immediately: a CFTC-sanctioned framework for event contracts provides the regulatory home base they've needed since the 2023–24 political prediction market battles. Coinbase and CME's futures businesses gain a coordination partner for derivatives innovation. Exchanges offering crypto perpetuals for US customers remain in limbo until the ITF defines the commodity/security line with greater precision. AI-driven trading firms face a new disclosure layer if the ITF's AI-and-autonomous-systems workstream produces conduct rules.
📊 Portfolio Impact
Bullish [Prediction market platforms, CFTC-regulated derivatives venues, tokenized collateral products]: Regulatory clarity path now visible.
Bearish [Unregistered offshore perpetuals platforms serving US users]: ITF rulemaking will tighten US access requirements.
Neutral [Spot crypto traders]: ITF scope is derivatives-focused; no immediate impact on spot market structure.

CLARITY Act Nears Senate Markup as Armstrong Reversal and Treasury Backing Shift the Odds

"We are ready to implement the CLARITY Act as soon as Congress acts." [paraphrased] — SEC Chair Paul Atkins and CFTC Chair Michael Selig, joint statement, April 9, 2026

The Digital Asset Market CLARITY Act is approaching a Senate Banking Committee markup targeted for late April. On April 9, Coinbase CEO Brian Armstrong reversed his months-long opposition and publicly backed the bill; Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent followed with a Wall Street Journal op-ed endorsement on the same day; SEC Chair Atkins and CFTC Chair Selig confirmed joint readiness to implement. The convergence of executive branch support and industry backing marks the bill's clearest path to a Senate floor vote since passing the House 294–134 in July 2025.

The CLARITY Act's latest draft contains a yield ban on stablecoins — prohibiting passive yield on payment stablecoin balances — a provision that directly affected Armstrong's calculus: Coinbase's USDC yield product generates significant revenue, and the ban closes that loophole. Armstrong's reversal is widely read as a concession that legislative certainty on market structure is worth more to Coinbase than the yield product.

📎 Historical Precedent: The 2010 Dodd-Frank Act similarly required major financial industry concessions before final passage; CEO endorsements in the Senate run-up historically correlate with final passage within 60–90 days.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis The stablecoin yield ban in the CLARITY Act interacts with the GENIUS Act's reserve requirements: together, they shape a US stablecoin market where yield generation is prohibited for payment instruments but permitted for tokenized money market products. Counsel advising stablecoin issuers must now distinguish between "payment stablecoins" under GENIUS and "digital securities" under CLARITY — the boundary determines whether yield can be offered. Circle loses the yield story; Ripple's RLUSD (payments-focused) is structurally unaffected. The Senate Banking Committee markup — likely late April — will be the key substantive moment: watch for DeFi carveout amendments and cross-border transaction provisions.
🔄 Contrarian Take Senate floor debate remains weeks away at best, and midterm dynamics could stall the bill after markup. Armstrong's reversal may reflect Coinbase's private intelligence that the yield ban is non-negotiable — meaning the company has already priced in the revenue loss, not that the ban is good for the industry. Analysts projecting CLARITY Act passage before Q3 2026 are pricing in optimism that Senate leadership has not yet confirmed.
🏗️ Industry Implications Circle (USDC issuer) faces direct revenue pressure from the yield ban; its OCC bank charter application and IPO timing are now tied to CLARITY Act passage. Ripple's RLUSD gains a structural advantage as a payments-first stablecoin unaffected by the ban. DeFi protocols that rely on stablecoin yield strategies face the most uncertainty until DeFi carveout language is finalized in Senate markup. Crypto exchanges broadly benefit from the CFTC/SEC market structure clarity the bill provides.
📊 Portfolio Impact
Bullish [Market structure clarity beneficiaries — exchanges, custodians, tokenization platforms]: Bill passage removes the #1 regulatory uncertainty overhang on US institutional allocations.
Bearish [Circle, USDC yield products, DeFi yield protocols]: Stablecoin yield ban directly cuts revenue models.
Neutral [Bitcoin, Ether spot holders]: CLARITY Act's classification provisions reconfirm Bitcoin and Ether as digital commodities under CFTC jurisdiction — no change from current SEC/CFTC guidance.
📋 What To Do
Trader Watch late-April Senate markup date as a catalyst — a successful markup is likely to generate a positive price reaction across the broad crypto market.
Counsel Begin mapping client stablecoin products against the CLARITY Act's payment stablecoin definition now — the yield ban vs. digital securities distinction will require product restructuring for many issuers.
Compliance Flag any DeFi yield strategies that rely on payment stablecoins for compliance review; Senate markup DeFi carveout language will determine permissible structures.
Founder If building a stablecoin product with yield features, engage legislative counsel immediately to participate in Senate markup comment process before provisions are locked.

SEC Submits "Regulation Crypto" to White House OIRA, Opening a Path to Startup Fundraising Exemptions

"We'd love to have reactions and everything else. It's not a rule as such but obviously we need to know how it's functioning and if people have problems with it or not." — SEC Chair Paul Atkins, on Reg Crypto, April 2026

The SEC has submitted its "Regulation Crypto" rulemaking to the White House Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs (OIRA) for review — a procedural step that precedes final publication in the Federal Register. The rulemaking focuses on the Securities Act of 1933 and covers fundraising mechanisms and startup exemptions, including a two-tiered safe harbour: up to $5 million over four years for early-stage token projects and up to $75 million annually for established projects, both with streamlined disclosure requirements.

Reg Crypto also formalises the five-part token taxonomy from the March 17 interpretive release — Digital Commodities (BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP under CFTC), Digital Securities (under SEC), Payment Stablecoins, Digital Collectibles, and Digital Tools — providing the classification framework around which fundraising exemption eligibility turns. Chair Atkins confirmed the SEC will proceed with rulemaking regardless of Congressional midterm outcomes.

📎 Historical Precedent: The SEC's Reg CF (crowdfunding) exemptions under the JOBS Act (2012) took 3 years from White House submission to effective date; Atkins has signalled intent to move faster.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis OIRA review typically runs 60–90 days for significant rulemakings; Reg Crypto's Securities Act focus makes it a "significant" rule under EO 12866, meaning OIRA must complete review before the SEC can publish. Lawyers advising token issuers should note that the $75M annual exemption for established projects — if confirmed in final text — would allow projects currently operating outside the SEC's framework to regularise without registering securities. The key legal question: how does the SEC define "established project"? This will determine whether tokens like Solana-ecosystem protocols can access the higher tier. Winners: crypto startups and established DeFi projects seeking US fundraising access. Losers: traditional broker-dealers who served as placement agents for private SAFT rounds.
🔄 Contrarian Take Reg Crypto's OIRA submission does not guarantee favourable final text. OIRA review is often where politically sensitive provisions get softened — the $75M exemption ceiling and the "no registration required" safe harbour are precisely the type of provisions that attract inter-agency objection from Treasury or DOJ. The fundraising framework may emerge from OIRA materially narrowed.
🏗️ Industry Implications Coinbase, as the SEC's largest compliant US exchange, benefits from a clear securities-vs.-commodity line that reduces ambiguity about which assets require securities treatment. Token launchpads and IDO platforms gain a legal pathway for US retail participation if the exemption tiers hold. Legal tech firms offering token disclosure automation (e.g., Vertalo, Securitize) will see compliance demand spike once final rules publish. Offshore issuers that structured products to avoid US securities law face renewed pressure to re-enter the US market under the exemption framework.
📊 Portfolio Impact
Bullish [Token launchpad platforms, US-based token issuers, compliant US exchanges]: Legal pathway for US retail fundraising removes the largest structural barrier to institutional-quality token projects raising in the US.
Bearish [Offshore private placement intermediaries, Cayman SAFT structures]: US exemption pathway reduces demand for offshore structuring workarounds.
Neutral [Bitcoin, Ether holders]: Classified as Digital Commodities under the taxonomy — Reg Crypto does not affect spot market treatment.
📋 What To Do
Trader No immediate action required — OIRA review takes 60–90 days; monitor for final rule publication as a catalyst for US token issuance activity.
Counsel Draft a client memo now on Reg Crypto's two-tiered exemption framework and how it interacts with existing Reg D, Reg S, and Reg CF exemptions — clients will ask this week.
Compliance Map existing token issuance programmes against the five-part taxonomy; flag any assets classified as Digital Securities that are currently treated as commodities under internal policies.
Founder File written comments with the SEC's Crypto Task Force (comments accepted on an ongoing basis) on the "established project" definition — the $75M tier eligibility criteria will be set in final rulemaking.

UPDATE: Australia's Digital Assets Framework Act Enters 18-Month Commencement Window — 400 Platforms Must Now Plan for AFSL

"Investors will benefit from protections familiar under the AFSL regime, plus specific requirements recognizing digital assets' unique features." [paraphrased] — Steven Pettigrove, Piper Alderman, April 2026

The Corporations Amendment (Digital Assets Framework) Bill 2025 received Royal Assent on April 8, 2026, commencing an 18-month implementation window (commencement: April 9, 2027). The Act creates two new regulated products — Digital Asset Platforms (DAPs) and Tokenised Custody Platforms (TCPs) — both requiring an Australian Financial Services Licence from ASIC. Of approximately 400 crypto platforms currently operating in Australia, only around 10% hold existing ASIC registrations; the majority face a material compliance lift.

ASIC has granted a sector-wide no-action position until June 30, 2026, creating a short runway for licensing applications before the full framework commences. Platforms with total transaction volumes under A$10 million annually are exempt from DAP licensing requirements.

⚖️ Practitioner Analysis Australia's AFSL framework — now applied to digital assets — is among the most disclosure-intensive retail licensing regimes globally: ASIC expects Financial Services Guides, Product Disclosure Statements, best-interests duties, and dispute resolution scheme membership. Platforms accustomed to AUSTRAC registration alone face a compliance cost step-change. The 18-month window sounds long but ASIC licence processing historically runs 6–12 months; platforms that don't file by Q3 2026 risk being unlicensed at commencement. Winners: established players like Coinbase Australia, Kraken, and OKX AU who already have operational infrastructure. Losers: smaller local exchanges that cannot absorb compliance costs.
🏗️ Industry Implications Coinbase Australia and Kraken Australia are positioned to benefit from regulatory consolidation — smaller non-compliant platforms will exit or be acquired. The A$24 billion tokenized market estimate positions Australia as a potential RWA hub if ASIC licences are granted efficiently. GlobalBlock, Independent Reserve, and BTC Markets face competitive pressure: file early and leverage compliance as a differentiation tool, or risk market share erosion to global players with resources to absorb AFSL costs.

Nineteen US State AI Bills Enacted in April 2026, Led by Utah's Nine-Law Package and New York's Frontier AI Law

"Large-scale AI developers operating in New York will face new transparency, safety reporting, and accountability requirements for frontier AI models." [paraphrased] — New York S 8828, signed April 2026

As of mid-April 2026, 19 new state AI laws have been enacted — bringing the 2026 total to 25 — with 27 additional bills having passed both chambers and awaiting implementation. Utah leads with nine AI-related bills signed by Governor Spencer Cox, covering AI literacy education, non-consensual deepfakes, health insurer AI disclosure, and AI in defamation law. New York enacted S 8828, creating a regulatory framework for large-scale "frontier" AI developers with transparency and safety reporting obligations.

The legislative surge creates a complex multi-jurisdictional compliance landscape for AI developers and deployers — particularly where state laws conflict with the White House's December 2025 Executive Order asserting a federal preemption posture toward state AI regulation. The AI Litigation Task Force established by that EO is now evaluating state laws for challenge on preemption grounds.

⚖️ Practitioner Analysis New York's frontier AI law applies to "large-scale AI developers" — a definition that, if drawn broadly, could capture crypto AI tools, trading algorithm providers, and automated compliance systems. Counsel advising AI-integrated fintech clients must assess whether their systems meet the threshold. Colorado's AI Act (effective June 30, 2026) adds another layer: high-risk AI systems in financial services face impact assessment and disclosure obligations. The White House preemption posture may suppress some state laws, but New York's political autonomy and the state's role as a financial centre make a federal challenge legally and politically difficult.
🏗️ Industry Implications AI-driven trading platforms (Two Sigma, Jane Street, crypto quant funds) operating in New York face disclosure obligations under S 8828. Health-tech and insurance firms in Utah face new AI-in-prior-authorisation rules. Crypto AI infrastructure providers — including AI agents used for yield optimization in DeFi — may fall within scope of multiple state definitions if they meet a "large-scale" developer threshold. The CFTC ITF's AI-and-autonomous-systems workstream (Story #1) is a parallel federal process that may eventually preempt state-level AI conduct rules for derivatives markets specifically.

ESMA Updates MiCA Register (April 9) as July 1 End of Transitional Grandfathering Approaches — Unauthorized Providers Face Hard Cut-Off

"Member State competent authorities should treat 'last minute' applications for authorisation with considerable caution and be ready to enforce against the unauthorised provision of crypto-asset services." [paraphrased] — ESMA Statement on MiCA Transitional Measures, December 2025 (remains operative)

ESMA updated the interim MiCA register on April 9, 2026 — its weekly snapshot of authorized CASPs and registered white papers across EU member states. The update is operationally significant: the transitional grandfathering period permitting pre-MiCA providers to continue operating under national law expires July 1, 2026 (79 days from today). Entities that have not received MiCA authorization by that date must cease EU operations or face enforcement by national competent authorities (NCAs).

ESMA has issued guidance warning NCAs to exercise caution with last-minute applications and to stand ready to enforce. With the July 1 cliff now the dominant near-term EU crypto compliance deadline, firms without authorization in at least one EU member state are in a race against a hard stop — not a soft transition.

⚖️ Practitioner Analysis The July 1 cliff is not a uniform deadline: each EU member state set its own grandfathering period up to July 1. Some NCAs (notably France's AMF and the Netherlands' AFM) have been processing CASP applications at pace; others have significant backlogs. Counsel advising exchange clients must check whether their MiCA application has advanced beyond "submitted" status with the relevant NCA — an ESMA "in review" status does not protect against the July 1 cut-off in jurisdictions that don't provide automatic extension pending review. Firms using passporting strategies (obtain one CASP license, passport EU-wide) must have that single NCA authorization confirmed before July 1 or pause EU operations entirely.
🏗️ Industry Implications OKX, Bybit, and other non-EU headquartered exchanges that have been operating under national grandfathering provisions are most exposed: each must have an authorized EU entity by July 1 or exit EU retail markets. Coinbase Europe (authorized in Ireland), Bitstamp (Luxembourg), and Kraken (Ireland) are structurally advantaged. The cliff creates a market consolidation opportunity: authorized CASPs will absorb user bases of exiting competitors. RWA tokenization platforms marketing to EU institutions must also confirm their CASP status — issuing whitepapers for non-compliant assets triggers immediate liability after July 1.

📌 Brief Notes

FDIC + Treasury Dual GENIUS Act NPRMs (April 7). The FDIC Board of Directors approved an NPRM implementing GENIUS Act requirements for FDIC-supervised Permitted Payment Stablecoin Issuers (PPSIs): 2-business-day redemption mandate, segregated reserve assets, and explicit confirmation that stablecoin reserves are not insured on a pass-through basis to holders. In parallel, Treasury issued an NPRM establishing "substantially similar" standards for state stablecoin regimes — permitting nonbank state-chartered issuers with up to $10B in outstanding stablecoins to operate under state oversight if certified equivalent. Both NPRMs carry 60-day comment periods after Federal Register publication. Comment deadline estimated ~June 8, 2026.
UK FCA: Final Crypto Rules Expected H2 2026. Skadden's April 2026 client alert confirms the FCA's consultation papers (CP25/41 and related) closed mid-February 2026. Final policy statements are expected in H2 2026, with the FCA's Application Gateway for crypto firm authorizations opening September 30, 2026. The full UK cryptoassets regime under the FSMA 2000 (Cryptoassets) Regulations 2026 comes into force October 25, 2027. Firms not yet mapped against FCA's proposed prudential, conduct, and DeFi rules should initiate gap analyses in Q2 2026.
ABA Presses OCC to Pause Crypto Bank Charter Reviews. The American Bankers Association has urged the OCC to pause national bank charter reviews for Coinbase National Trust Company, Ripple Labs, Circle, BitGo, Paxos, and Nomura's Laser Digital until GENIUS Act rulemaking is complete. The ABA cited FTX (~$8B in client fund losses) and Celsius ($1.2B balance sheet deficit) as evidence that charter approvals ahead of finalized regulatory frameworks create systemic risk. The OCC has not publicly responded. This positions a banking-industry vs. crypto-industry regulatory battle at the OCC as the near-term institutional custody framework develops.
