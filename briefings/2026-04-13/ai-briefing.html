Model Landscape

Google's Gemma 4 31B — Apache 2.0, free to run, #3 open model globally — is the strongest weight-for-weight open release since Llama 3.1, and it just launched quietly on April 2.
[Launch][Benchmark]

Gemma 4 31B — 89% AIME, 86% Agentic Tool Use, Apache 2.0, #3 Open Model

Google released Gemma 4 on April 2, 2026 — four variants: E2B, E4B, a 26B Mixture-of-Experts, and a 31B Dense. The headline number is a step-change from Gemma 3: the 31B Dense scores 89.2% on AIME 2026 (Gemma 3 27B: 20.8%), 80.0% on LiveCodeBench v6 (Gemma 3: 29.1%), and 86.4% on Agentic Tool Use (Gemma 3: 6.6%). GPQA Diamond: 84.3%. [Sources: Google DeepMind blog, HuggingFace Gemma 4 post, Analytics Vidhya — confirmed across 3 sources]

The 31B Dense holds the #3 spot on the Arena AI open model leaderboard; the 26B MoE is #6. Most significantly: Apache 2.0 licence — fully free for commercial use, fine-tuning, and local deployment. The 26B MoE is purpose-built for on-device inference (supported in Google AI Edge Gallery and LiteRT-LM, both currently trending on GitHub).

Context 1M token Multimodal Apache 2.0

⚡ Implication: Gemma 4 31B is the first open-weight model that credibly competes with GPT-5.4 and Claude Sonnet 4.6 on coding and agentic benchmarks — at zero API cost if you run it locally. If you need a privacy-preserving or cost-zero option for analytical tasks, this is worth deploying this week.

Claude Ecosystem

The OpenClaw controversy — Anthropic blocking its most popular open agent harness while the creator moved to OpenAI — is the most revealing story about Anthropic's platform ambitions since Cowork launched.
[Update][Workflow]

OpenClaw Banned — Anthropic Closes Subscription Coverage, Temporarily Bans Creator

Timeline: On April 4, Anthropic announced Claude subscriptions would no longer cover OpenClaw usage — users would need to purchase an API usage bundle separately or supply their own API key. The Register reported the shift hit thousands of users with cost increases of up to 50× their previous monthly spend. On April 10, Anthropic temporarily banned Peter Steinberger (OpenClaw's creator) from accessing Claude entirely — the ban came days after the pricing change. Steinberger, who had already moved to OpenAI, publicly accused Anthropic of copying OpenClaw features into Cowork and then cutting off the open harness. [Sources: TechCrunch (×2), The Register, XDA Developers, AIBusiness — confirmed across 5 sources]

Anthropic's stated rationale: third-party tool usage was never permitted under their Terms of Service and put outsized strain on systems. The timing — Claude Cowork achieving GA the same week — fuelled the "copy then block" narrative. Steinberger's allegation of feature appropriation is unverified but plausible given the functional overlap between Cowork and OpenClaw's architecture.

⚡ Implication: If you use OpenClaw or any third-party Claude agent harness (not Claude Code or Cowork), audit your ToS compliance and cost model now. The precedent is set: Anthropic treats subscription compute as non-transferable to third-party platforms. Not a dc workflow issue directly, but important ecosystem context.
[Update]

Claude Cowork Now Generally Available — Analytics, OpenTelemetry, Enterprise RBAC

Claude Cowork went generally available on April 9 for macOS and Windows inside Claude Desktop. Enterprise features shipping with GA: Analytics API support, usage analytics, OpenTelemetry monitoring integration, and role-based access controls — enterprise admins can now organise users into groups with custom capability restrictions. This is the production enterprise play for organisations running Claude-native workflows. [Source: Releasebot — confirmed]

Note: Cowork (desktop collaborative agent) is distinct from Claude Managed Agents (API agent harness, covered in Issue #17). Cowork is the consumer/enterprise desktop experience; Managed Agents is the developer API surface.

⚡ Implication: If you're evaluating Claude for team workflows rather than solo use, Cowork's GA + RBAC makes it production-ready for Enterprise. Monitor only for dc's individual setup.
[Update]

Web Search + Programmatic Tool Calling Now GA — No Beta Header Required

Anthropic's web search tool and programmatic tool calling are now generally available — both previously required beta headers in API requests. Additionally, API code execution is now free when used alongside web search or web fetch. [Source: Anthropic API Release Notes — confirmed]

⚡ Implication: If you build any Claude API integrations with tool use, you can now drop the beta headers. The free code execution with search/fetch is a cost reduction for any workflow that combines browsing and computation.

OpenAI's economic policy paper (robot taxes, public wealth funds, containment playbooks) is the most strategically revealing document OpenAI has published — it tells you exactly how they think AGI disruption unfolds.
[Regulatory][Funding]

OpenAI's "Industrial Policy for the Intelligence Age" — Robot Tax, Public Wealth Fund, Containment Playbooks

OpenAI published a 13-page policy document on April 6 titled "Industrial Policy for the Intelligence Age: Ideas to keep people first." The proposals: (1) a robot tax on automated labour — taxing AI/robot output at the rate the human it replaced would have been taxed; (2) a Public Wealth Fund giving Americans automatic equity stakes in AI companies and infrastructure, modelled on Alaska's Permanent Fund with annual citizen dividends; (3) a 32-hour (four-day) working week framed as an "efficiency dividend" from AI productivity; (4) a tax restructuring shifting burden from labour to capital as corporate AI profits expand and labour income shrinks. [Sources: TechCrunch, The Next Web, Winbuzzer, OpenTools — confirmed across 4 sources]

The document also includes "containment playbooks" — government coordination plans for scenarios where dangerous AI systems cannot be recalled. Sam Altman's framing: OpenAI is proposing a social contract for the superintelligence era before Congress debates AI legislation. Timing is not coincidental — it positions OpenAI as a policy thought leader ahead of expected IPO proceedings.

⚡ Implication: Monitor only — no immediate action. The containment playbook acknowledgment is the most honest public statement any frontier lab has made about autonomous system risk. The wealth fund proposal, if adopted, would be the defining policy response to AI-driven unemployment. This document is worth reading in full — it's the closest thing to an AGI transition plan any company has published.

Research Radar

Tufts' neuro-symbolic AI cuts robot energy use by 100× while improving accuracy — a reminder that the path to efficient AI may run through hybrid symbolic-neural approaches, not pure scale.
[Research]

Neuro-Symbolic Visual Language Action Models — 100× Energy Reduction, 95% Task Success Rate

Tufts University · Published April 5–7, 2026 · Accepted: ICRA 2026 Vienna (May)

One-line finding: Combining neural networks with symbolic reasoning in a robotic control system achieves 95% task success (vs 34% for standard VLA models) while using 1% of the training energy and 5% of the inference energy.

Method: The team built a neuro-symbolic Visual Language Action (VLA) model that doesn't brute-force through trial and error — it reasons symbolically about task structure (like solving Tower of Hanoi by planning steps, not pattern-matching). Training took 34 minutes vs 1.5 days for the baseline. Energy savings were consistent across both training and execution. [Sources: ScienceDaily, SciTechDaily, Engineering & Technology Magazine, NerdLevelTech — confirmed across 4 sources]

Why it matters in 6 months: The AI industry's energy problem — data centres consuming city-scale power — may have a structural solution that doesn't require building more power plants. If neuro-symbolic approaches generalise beyond robotics to LLM inference (not yet demonstrated), the economics of AI deployment change fundamentally. The ICRA 2026 presentation in May will be the next validation point.

[Research]

The AI Scientist-v2 — First AI-Generated Paper to Pass Peer Review (SakanaAI)

SakanaAI · arXiv 2504.08066 · First AI-generated workshop paper accepted by peer review

One-line finding: SakanaAI's AI Scientist-v2 autonomously proposed hypotheses, ran experiments, analysed data, wrote a paper, and received an average reviewer score of 6.33 — sufficient for acceptance at a major workshop had it been human-generated.

Method: The system uses a progressive agentic tree search guided by an experiment manager agent. Unlike v1 (which relied on human-authored templates), v2 generalises across ML domains and incorporates a VLM feedback loop for figure quality. Each paper costs approximately $140 in compute and takes ~15 hours. [Sources: GitHub SakanaAI, arXiv, HuggingFace, Scientific American — confirmed across 4 sources]

Why it matters in 6 months: The research pipeline is beginning to recurse — AI improving AI research, not just assisting it. The immediate implication: conference and workshop reviewers will need verification mechanisms for AI-generated submissions. The compounding implication: benchmark performance curves will steepen as AI-generated research proposes and validates its own improvements. The AI Scientist-v1 paper was also published in Nature in 2026 — signalling that the top-tier scientific community accepts this work as legitimate.
