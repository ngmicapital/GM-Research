⚖️ Regulatory & Legal Radar

The most important development this week is the CLARITY Act markup hearing. This bill explicitly assigns BTC, ETH, SOL, and XRP to CFTC jurisdiction, removing the SEC's claimed authority and eliminating the regulatory overhang that has kept conservative institutional allocators cautious. The SEC-CFTC MOU (March 11) and joint guidance (March 17) have already moved the overton window; the markup is the next concrete legislative step.

The SEC Reg Crypto safe harbor (at OIRA since April 6) unlocks legitimate US capital formation: startups raise up to $5M over four years, established projects up to $75M annually, both with streamlined disclosure. This catalyses the next wave of institutional-grade token issuance once enacted.

🌐 Geopolitical Calendar
🚨 CRITICAL: US-Iran Strait of Hormuz Naval Blockade

Event: On April 12, JD Vance confirmed US-Iran talks have failed. Trump declared a US Naval blockade on the Strait of Hormuz, with US Navy vessels authorised to intercept all ships transiting without US authorisation.

Transmission mechanism: The Strait carries ~20% of global oil supply. A sustained closure removes ~19M bbl/day from global markets — hence WTI spiked to $115 on April 10. At $104 today, the market is partially repriced but not fully pricing a prolonged closure. Direct knock-ons: US CPI via fuel prices blocks the Fed from cutting; consumer discretionary margins compressed globally; war-risk insurance premiums up 1,000%+ disrupting Asian shipping routes; Australian fuel prices spike with RBA-amplified inflation effects.

What reversal looks like: Back-channel US-Iran diplomacy through Oman or Qatar (historical mediators) — no signals of this currently. Alternatively, a coordinated IEA/US SPR release to cap oil prices (possible but not yet announced). Until either occurs, $90–$110 WTI is the structural range. A move above $115 signals resumed escalation.

⚠️ US Tariff Regime — 10% Global Import Duty Active Since Feb 24

Event: The US has imposed a 10% global import duty under Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 since February 24, 2026. This came after the Supreme Court ruled broad IEEPA tariffs unlawful in January 2026. China-specific tariffs remain politically contested.

Transmission mechanism: 10% blanket tariff raises US manufacturer input costs, feeds PPI and CPI, slows global trade volumes. Combined with WTI at $104, the stagflation picture is the clearest since 2022. The AUD and ASX 200 are specifically exposed via Australia's China trade dependency. Nearly 85% of 2025's US employment gains occurred before the April 2 tariff escalation — the 2026 labour market is already feeling the drag.

What reversal looks like: Congressional override or further judicial challenge. The 73% Kalshi odds for 10–19.99% China tariffs by July 1 suggest partial de-escalation expected — but the Hormuz context makes this optimistic.

⚠️ Australian Stagflation — RBA at 4.10%, May Hike Consensus

Event: RBA hiked in both February and March 2026. Major bank consensus (ANZ, CBA, NAB, Westpac) expects another 25bps in May, taking the cash rate to 4.35%.

Transmission mechanism: Higher rates compress Australia's heavily-indebted housing market, slow consumer spending, and pressure bank margins. AUD is caught between a hawkish RBA (supportive) and weak China trade (negative). The WTI-driven fuel spike adds directly to Australian CPI. ASX 200 Monday open likely gaps lower on global risk-off and energy import costs.

What reversal looks like: A materially softer Australian CPI print or a significant China demand shock. Neither is imminent.
