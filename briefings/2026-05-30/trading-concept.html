Alpha — Volume Spread Analysis

Saturday · 30 May 2026 · Sydney, Australia · Issue #36 11-min read
TL;DR

Volume Spread Analysis (VSA) reads every bar as a contest between effort — the volume — and result — the bar's spread and where it closes; when the two disagree, professional money is usually doing the opposite of what the candle implies. The edge is not the catalogue of pattern names — it's a single question asked at a level that matters: did this volume produce the move it should have? When heavy effort buys almost no result, supply or demand is being absorbed, and the obvious read is the wrong one.

§01
What It Is
Effort vs result · the original order-flow read
VSA reads each bar as effort (volume) against result (spread + close position), exposing whether professional money is supporting the move or quietly opposing it.

VSA is the bar-chart ancestor of everything in modern order flow. Long before footprints, CVD, or liquidation heatmaps, Richard Wyckoff codified the idea that price is the result of effort, and effort is measured by volume. Tape reader Tom Williams — a former syndicate trader — systematised Wyckoff's third law into the named framework now called VSA, and Anna Coulling carried it into the modern retail era as Volume Price Analysis. Same engine throughout: read each bar's volume against the size of the move it produced and where it closed.

The position to hold: volume without a proportional result is the signal, not the move itself. A wide-range down bar on huge volume that closes back near its high did not just fall — it fell, met a wall of buying, and got pushed back up. The candle is red; the message is bullish. VSA exists to stop you reading the colour and start reading the relationship between effort and result. That relationship is invisible on a price-only chart and approximated — often expensively — by the order-flow tools that came after it.

Three numbers carry the whole method, and you already have all three on any chart: the bar's volume (how much effort), its spread — high minus low (how much room the move was given), and its close position within that range (who finished in control). VSA is just the disciplined, repeatable reading of those three together, bar after bar, in the context of the trend they sit inside.

If you only remember one thing Volume is the effort; the bar's spread and close are the result. When effort is large and result is small, someone is absorbing — fade the obvious read, not the move.
§02
Mechanics
Three numbers per bar · harmony vs divergence · the signal taxonomy
For every bar, read three things — volume, spread, and where the close sits in the range — then ask whether the effort produced the result it should have.

Start with the close-position calculation, because it's the part most traders skip. The colour of a candle tells you open-versus-close; VSA cares about close-versus-range. A close at the top of the bar means buyers finished in control regardless of whether the bar is red or green; a close at the bottom means sellers did. Quantify it and the readings stop being subjective.

The three inputs: Spread = High − Low Volume = effort spent on the bar Close pos. = (Close − Low) / (High − Low) → 1.0 = closed at the high (buyers won) → 0.0 = closed at the low (sellers won) Harmony (trend continues): wide spread + high volume + close at the extreme → effort produced result — the move is real, expect continuation Divergence (the signal): high volume + narrow spread, OR close against the move → effort spent, no result — supply/demand is being absorbed
Note: "high" and "low" volume are always relative — compare a bar to the recent average (say a 20-bar volume mean), never to an absolute number. A 150k-BTC daily bar is heavy in one regime and average in another. Context sets the threshold; there is no fixed line.

Everything else in VSA is a named instance of harmony or divergence at a particular place in the trend. The taxonomy below is worth knowing — but treat the names as shorthand for the underlying effort-vs-result physics, not as a checklist of magic patterns. The same bar means opposite things depending on whether it prints into a markup, a markdown, or a range.

VSA signalVolume · spread · closeWhat it means
Selling climaxUltra-high vol · very wide down spread · close mid/highPanic selling absorbed by strong hands — supply exhausting near a low
Stopping volumeHigh vol · down bar · close back near the highLarge buying halts a fall — a brake applied under the market
No demandLow vol · narrow up spread · close mid/lowAn up-bar professionals aren't supporting — rally with no fuel
No supplyLow vol · narrow down spread · close mid/highA down-bar with no selling pressure left — sellers absent
UpthrustHigh vol · wide spread · pokes a high, close at/near lowA failed breakout that traps buyers — distribution into strength
TestLow vol · dips into prior supply · close back upSmart money checking for remaining sellers — clear test = go-ahead

The test row is where VSA becomes tradeable rather than descriptive. A stopping-volume or selling-climax bar is a candidate bottom, never a confirmation — supply can reload. The confirmation is the test that follows: a later down-bar on conspicuously low volume that dips toward the climax low and closes back up. Low effort on the retest means the sellers are gone. That sequence — climactic effort, then a low-volume test that holds — is the spine of how the method is actually used, and it's exactly the structure BTC is flirting with on the current daily (§05).

§04
In Practice
Context · the level · the test · a worked BTC sequence
The one rule: a VSA bar is meaningless in isolation — it only counts at a level, in the right trend context, and confirmed by what the next bar does.
1

Context Decides the Meaning

The identical bar is bullish in one place and bearish in another. A no-demand bar only matters in an up-move running into resistance; stopping volume only matters after a sustained decline. Always read the bar relative to the trend it sits inside — never as a free-floating pattern.

2

Anchor to a Level

An effort/result divergence at a random price is noise. The same divergence at a prior swing low, a value-area edge, a VWAP, or a liquidation cluster carries weight. The level supplies the structural reason to care; the volume read supplies the timing. Demand both.

3

Wait for the Test

Climactic or stopping volume is a candidate, not a confirmation. The trade is the test that follows: a low-volume bar that revisits the extreme and refuses to extend. Low effort on the retest is the market telling you the opposing side is gone. No clean test, no entry.

4

Bias High, Trigger Low

Set the effort/result bias on the daily or 4h — that's where institutional bars are honest. Drop to 1h/15m for the test and the entry trigger. A lower-timeframe VSA signal that fights the higher-timeframe effort balance is a scalp at best, never a position.

§07
Common Traps
Five ways traders misread the bar
The single biggest mistake: trading a VSA bar in isolation — without a level, the right context, and a confirming test, the named patterns are just decoration.
1
Trading the Signal, Not the Context

"No demand" inside a strong markup is meaningless; stopping volume mid-trend with no level under it is noise. A VSA bar is only information relative to where it prints. Strip the trend and the level away and you are pattern-matching candles — exactly the habit VSA is supposed to cure.

2
Reading the Colour Instead of the Close

A wide red bar that closes back near its high is strength — buyers absorbed the sell-off. A green bar that closes near its low is weakness. Traders who react to candle colour rather than close-within-range get the message exactly backwards at the most important bars.

3
Assuming High Volume = Reversal

High volume with a wide spread and a close at the extreme is harmony — it confirms the move, it doesn't oppose it. Heavy effort is only a warning when the result fails to match it. Selling everything because "volume spiked" fades plenty of perfectly healthy trends.

4
Trusting Dirty Volume Data

Crypto volume is fragmented across venues and laced with wash trades and derivatives churn. A single-exchange volume bar can mislead badly. Benchmark against aggregated spot volume, and remember the effort number is only as honest as the feed producing it — VSA on bad volume is astrology.

5
Overhyped: The "Smart Money Footprint" Mythology

Half the VSA marketing — "see exactly what the institutions are doing" — is selling certainty that isn't there. The named patterns are subjective and easy to spot only in hindsight; rigorous back-tests rarely show a clean edge from the labels alone. What survives is the underlying physics — effort vs result at a level, confirmed by a test — not the pattern zoo or the mind-reading claims. Treat VSA as a lens that sharpens a thesis, not a system that generates one.
