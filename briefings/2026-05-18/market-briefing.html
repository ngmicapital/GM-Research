Key Dynamic — Good Data Is Bad News: The Empire State beat is the second consecutive upside manufacturing surprise. Under a normal Fed regime, this would be unambiguously bullish. Under Warsh’s incoming framework — where the market has re-priced to ~50% probability of a 2026 rate hike — strong growth data simply means the Fed has more room to tighten. The gold decline (typically a safe-haven bid) alongside the yield spike suggests the market is not pricing a financial stability event but rather a sustained higher-for-longer rate path. That is a different, more persistent kind of headwind for equities.
Friday’s sell-off has the character of a sustained repricing, not a one-day flush. The macro configuration — oil above $105, 30Y above 5%, 50% hike probability, and the FOMC minutes due Wednesday — argues for defensive positioning until yields stabilise. The key Monday watch is whether the S&P holds above 7,380 (the 50-day MA) or whether follow-through selling accelerates into a test of 7,200 support.

7 🌐 Geopolitical Calendar
🚨 STRAIT OF HORMUZ — CEASEFIRE ON “LIFE SUPPORT”
Status: Trump has rejected Iran’s ceasefire counteroffer, calling it “garbage” and warning the ceasefire is on “life support.” Iran claims 30 vessels transited the Strait in two days; independent vessel-tracking data shows only approximately 4 tankers (carrying ~2 million barrels each) have passed since May 10.

Oil Market Impact: WTI is at $105.42 (+4.2% Friday) and Brent at $109.26 (+3.55%). Both benchmarks are up 45%+ since the conflict began February 28. JPMorgan forecasts global oil inventories could fall to 6.8 billion barrels by September — record low territory. UBS expects inventories to approach all-time lows by end of May. The IEA has characterised this as “the largest supply disruption in the history of the global oil market.”

Inflation Transmission: WTI $105 → US energy CPI dominant → PPI 6.0% YoY (confirmed) → CPI ~3.8% → Fed cannot cut → Warsh hike risk → 10Y/30Y at cycle highs → all risk assets under discount rate pressure.

Watch Monday: Any Trump social media post on Iran negotiations. Any new vessel seizure or attack report. EIA inventory data Thursday is the first hard data point on the drawdown trajectory.
🚨 FOMC MINUTES — WEDNESDAY MAY 20 (WARSH ERA BEGINS)
Status: Kevin Warsh officially presides as Fed Chair starting with the June 18–19 FOMC meeting. Wednesday’s minutes are from the April meeting — the last under Powell — but with a historic number of dissents and Warsh’s shadow already over policy. These minutes are the first public window into how the FOMC discussed the inflation/energy pass-through and whether hike language entered deliberations.

Market Sensitivity: The market has priced approximately hawkish-hold as the base case. Any hike language = 10Y above 4.65%, fresh sell-off in risk assets. Any “looking through” energy CPI language = relief rally. The base case (hawkish-hold) is the least volatile outcome; surprise risk is asymmetric to the hawkish side.

SocGen View: Subadra Rajappa noted the Iran war’s energy spike will complicate Warsh’s ability to deliver the lower rates he has championed and that President Trump has demanded. This creates a structural conflict between political pressure (cut) and economic necessity (hold or hike).
🚨 TRUMP–XI SUMMIT AFTERMATH — TARIFF STATUS UNCHANGED
Status: The Beijing summit produced: 200 Boeing jet orders, trade and investment councils, “constructive strategic stability” language. What was NOT agreed: semiconductor deal (H200 China access unchanged), tariff reduction framework, Hormuz joint mechanism, or any Taiwan concession.

Economic Read-Through: Tariffs remaining in place means the goods import inflation pass-through continues, compounding the energy CPI pressure. Any semiconductor deal announcement would be a major positive for NVDA and the broader AI supply chain.

Watch: Any follow-up China trade negotiation announcement, particularly on semiconductor exports, before or after NVDA earnings Wednesday.
Three compounding geopolitical headwinds remain fully intact entering the week: Hormuz (oil/inflation), Warsh’s first minutes (rate uncertainty), and the Trump–Xi anticlimax (tariffs persist). The single relief trade remains a credible Hormuz ceasefire signal — any genuine de-escalation collapses crude by $10–15/bbl, drops yields 15–20bps, and triggers simultaneous equity and crypto rallies. Trump calling Iran’s offer “garbage” makes this scenario less likely, not more, in the near term.
