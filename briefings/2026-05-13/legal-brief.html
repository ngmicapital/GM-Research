UpdateSenate Banking Drops 309-Page CLARITY Substitute Text on Tuesday 12 May — Amendments Closed COB Same Day, Markup Thursday 14 May 10:30 AM Dirksen Room 538; Warren Statement Calls Bill a "Turbocharge" for "Donald Trump's Crypto Corruption" Over Missing Ethics Section; Section 404 Yield Compromise Held, BRCA-Style Developer Carveout Embedded in Title III

"This bill will turbocharge Donald Trump's crypto corruption. The President and his family have raked in at least $1.4 billion in gains from crypto deals alone, and yet this bill stunningly includes zero provisions to prevent that." — Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-MA), Ranking Member Senate Banking, statement issued Monday 11 May night ahead of 12 May text release

Chairman Tim Scott (R-SC), Sen. Cynthia Lummis (R-WY) and Sen. Thom Tillis (R-NC) released the long-awaited 309-page substitute text of the Digital Asset Market CLARITY Act on Tuesday 12 May, transmitting it formally to committee members and setting a close-of-business 12 May deadline for amendments before Thursday's 10:30 AM ET markup in Room 538 of the Dirksen Senate Office Building. The substitute carries forward the Tillis-Alsobrooks stablecoin yield compromise (Section 404 — bans deposit-equivalent yield, preserves "bona fide activities" rewards), an embedded developer carveout patterned on the Blockchain Regulatory Certainty Act (Title III), and a three-bucket asset taxonomy splitting ancillary assets, digital commodities (CFTC), and payment stablecoins.

What it does not carry is an ethics provision restricting senior federal officials from profiting from crypto businesses while regulating them. Sen. Elizabeth Warren's Monday-night statement — quoted above — frames the entire ethics battle for Thursday: Democrats are signalling they may withhold the votes needed for the bill's eventual 60-vote floor passage unless Schiff/Warren/Gallego amendments adding restrictions are accepted. The committee splits 13-11 Republican-Democrat; all 13 Republican votes plus at least seven Democrats are needed for any version that survives a Senate filibuster.

The White House lead on the legislation, Patrick Witt, has publicly maintained the administration "won't stand for a bill that targets the president," but on private calls (per CoinDesk, 11 May) has reportedly conceded the 4 July signing target is "mathematically tight" if the bill does not move out of Banking on 14 May. Coinbase CEO Brian Armstrong and Blockchain Association CEO Summer Mersinger publicly backed the substitute text the same day it dropped. Polymarket odds on Clarity passage in 2026 sat at 64% Tuesday morning, up from 46% three weeks ago.

📎 Historical Precedent: Dodd-Frank conference (2010) and Sarbanes-Oxley (2002) both passed Senate Banking on near-party-line votes with ethics provisions tacked on at markup, not in chair's text. Pattern repeats here.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

The Davis Polk client alert (12 May, "What's in the substitute") flags two operative drafting risks for crypto issuers: (i) the Section 404 "bona fide activities" carve-out is defined functionally rather than enumeratively, leaving rewards programs exposed to FDIC and OCC supervisory re-interpretation under the GENIUS Act implementing rules; and (ii) the Title III decentralization test still uses "control" definitions susceptible to the same SDNY interpretation that produced the Tornado Cash conviction. Cooley's 12 May memo separately warns the ancillary-asset classification borrows enough from Hester Peirce's earlier Token Safe Harbor to invite renewed Howey-test litigation around staking rewards. The Brief's read: if the bill clears Banking Thursday but loses the ethics fight on the floor, the substantive crypto provisions still ship in 2026 — but with a court-stress-test deadline measured in weeks, not months.

🔄 Contrarian Take

Public Citizen's 12 May statement (Bartlett Naylor) argues the bill's real problem is not Trump-specific ethics but the deposit-flight risk embedded in any stablecoin-yield carve-out: "Banks lose roughly $6.6 trillion of insured retail deposits the moment any compliant yield vehicle out-pays them by 100bps." The American Bankers Association (Rob Nichols, 9 May letter) made the same point. The Brief's read: the contrarians may be right that deposit-flight, not ethics, is the load-bearing structural risk — but ethics is the only one that can derail Thursday's vote.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Per Galaxy Research's 12 May note, the substitute's Section 404 language preserves the commercial economics Coinbase ($COIN), Circle ($CRCL), Ripple, Paxos, and Robinhood ($HOOD) lobbied for since Q3 2025: yield-bearing wrappers via tokenized money-market funds remain compliant. Banks (JPMorgan, Citi, BofA, Wells) get their core deposit franchise protected only if FDIC's June 9 implementing-rule comment window narrows the "bona fide activities" definition — which is the precise lever the trade-coalition letter (ABA/ICBA/BPI/FSF/MBA, 8 May) targets. The Brief's read: if the bill ships substantially as drafted, expect $COIN/$CRCL to gap up 6–10% intraday on Thursday signing optics; $HOOD second-order. Bank reaction muted because the implementing-rule fight is the real one.

It is only the eighth de novo national bank charter the OCC has issued since 2015. Augustus disclosed a concurrent $40 million capital raise led by Peter Thiel's Valar Ventures and Creandum, with additional checks from founders at Ramp, Deel, and Circle.

Augustus (formerly Ivy) reports 10× year-over-year growth and is already clearing billions of euros annually through European banking subsidiaries; it handles euro clearing for Kraken. CEO Ferdinand Dabitz, 25, becomes the youngest CEO of a federally chartered US bank in modern history (~140 years).

📎 Historical Precedent: Anchorage Digital Bank, N.A. received the first OCC crypto-custody trust charter in January 2021. Augustus is the first non-trust national bank charter explicitly built on stablecoin settlement and AI-driven clearing rails.

The Brief's read: the OCC's willingness to issue a full national charter (not trust-only) to an explicitly stablecoin-centric institution is a structural signal that the GENIUS Act's federal-pathway architecture is being operationalised in supervisory practice ahead of the implementing-rule effective date.
