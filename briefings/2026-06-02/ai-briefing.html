§01 — Model Landscape

June is a model-retirement month, not a launch month — the live action is migrating off four sunsetting endpoints while Opus 4.8 keeps the intelligence lead.

June's deprecation wall — four endpoints sunset in 26 days ⚡

DeprecationMigration

The headline this week isn't a release — it's a retirement calendar. Gemini 2.0 Flash and Flash-Lite shut down June 1 (already executed). Claude Sonnet 4 (claude-sonnet-4-20250514) and Opus 4 (claude-opus-4-20250514) retire on the API June 15 — Anthropic points migrations to Sonnet 4.6 and Opus 4.7. GPT-4.5 leaves ChatGPT June 27 after a 30-day sunset, and Google's gemini-3.1-flash-image / gemini-3-pro-image previews go dark June 25.

If you still have a model ID hard-coded from a 2025 pin, this is the fortnight it breaks. The June 15 Anthropic cutoff is the one most likely to surprise an agentic pipeline — requests to the retired IDs will start erroring, not silently downgrading. Audit now; it's a five-minute grep against a midnight outage.

Opus 4.8 holds the index lead at 61.4 — but it's a lead, not a lap

Leaderboard

Per the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, Opus 4.8 sits at 61.4, ahead of GPT-5.5 (60.2), Gemini 3.1 Pro (57) and Grok 4.3 (53). On the headline aggregate the top three are inside ~4 points — close enough that routing decisions shouldn't be made on it alone. Where Opus 4.8 actually separates is coding: SWE-bench Verified 88.6% (vs GPT-5.5's 58.6% and Gemini 3.1 Pro's 54.2%), plus SWE-bench Pro 69.2%, Terminal-Bench 2.1 74.6% and 1890 Elo on GDPval-AA.

The general-intelligence gap is now a rounding error; the coding gap is a chasm. For the agentic/dev workloads that dominate Tony's usage, the Opus 4.8 default is justified on SWE-bench alone — not on the composite index, where you could swap to GPT-5.5 and barely notice.

Gemini 3.5 Pro — still "held for June"; 3.5 Flash gets forced on June 8 ⚡

RoadmapPending

Gemini 3.5 Flash is the global default and, from June 8, can no longer be turned off for Gemini Enterprise users. The capable variant — 3.5 Pro — remains committed for "June 2026" per Pichai's I/O remarks but has not shipped. That's the one open frontier launch on the near calendar; everything else this month is housekeeping.

§04 — Open-Source Pulse

The GitHub race has moved from star-counting to sub-agent spawning, while the open-weight frontier consolidates around DeepSeek-V4, Mistral Medium 3.5 and a now-GA Grok 4.3.

QwenPaw v1.1.10 (June 1) — agents that spawn agents ⚡

ReleaseAgents

QwenPaw shipped v1.1.10 on June 1 with a spawn_subagent tool that lets an agent recursively dispatch its own sub-agents, plus Tencent Yuanbao added to its channel family. It's a small release but a representative one: the trending-repo competition in 2026 is no longer about a chatbot wrapper's star count, it's about who has the cleanest multi-agent orchestration primitive — the same pattern Claude Code's dynamic workflows are chasing.

Octoverse pegs the platform at 4.3M AI repos and +178% YoY growth in LLM-focused projects. Signal-to-noise is collapsing; the durable repos are the ones shipping orchestration plumbing, not yet-another-agent-list.

Open-weight frontier — DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Mistral Medium 3.5, Grok 4.3 GA

Open weights

The current open(ish)-weight leaders: DeepSeek-V4-Pro (1.6T total / 49B active MoE, 1M context, MIT), Mistral Medium 3.5 (128B dense, 256K context, Modified MIT — a rare dense release at that scale), and Grok 4.3, now general-availability on Microsoft Foundry and OCI for agentic workloads. The gap to closed frontier models on coding has narrowed but not closed; for cost-sensitive self-hosted inference these are the names to benchmark against Opus/GPT before paying per-token.

Funding round-up — Shield AI $1.5B, ElevenLabs $500M

Venture

Defense-autonomy player Shield AI reportedly closed a $1.5B Series G (part of a ~$2.25B package) at a $12.7B valuation, and ElevenLabs raised a reported $500M Series D at $11B — voice and defense remain the two non-foundation-model categories still commanding mega-rounds.

EU AI Act — August 2 enforcement start, US still statute-free

Regulation

The countdown that matters is now the August 2, 2026 applicability date: high-risk-system rules (Annex III), Article 50 transparency obligations and member-state enforcement go live, and each country is meant to have a regulatory sandbox stood up. The US, by contrast, still has no federal AI statute — Trump's December 2025 executive order signals consolidation of oversight but leaves Colorado and California state frameworks as the binding edge. Build for the EU bar; it's the one with a date and teeth.

§06 — Workflow & Tools

Coding-agent economics just got repriced — GitHub Copilot went usage-based June 1 — while Claude Code's auto-mode reached every major cloud.

GitHub Copilot flips to usage-based AI credits (June 1) — 1 credit = $0.01 ⚡

Billing

All Copilot plans moved to usage-based billing on June 1. Each plan now bundles a monthly AI-credit allotment (1 credit = $0.01), and usage is metered on token consumption — input, output and cached — at each model's listed API rate. Seat prices are unchanged: Business $19/user (incl. $19 credits), Enterprise $39 (incl. $39), Pro+ $39 (incl. $39). Critically, code completions and next-edit suggestions stay unlimited and free; it's the chat/agent calls that now burn credits. Existing Business/Enterprise orgs get extra grace credits June 1 – Sept 1, poolable across the org.

This is the whole industry's direction of travel — Anthropic's /usage per-category audit and Agent-SDK metering were the same signal last week. Heavy agent users will see real bills; light autocomplete users won't notice. The actionable move is to instrument now, before the September grace credits expire and the first uncushioned invoice lands.

Claude Code

§07 — Research Radar

Two papers worth tracking: the hard limits of LLM agents verifying their own intent, and structure-aware KV-cache for tree-of-thought reasoning.

Reframing LLM Agent Security as an Agent–Human Interaction Problem (arXiv 2605.24309)

AgentsSafety

A systematic comparison of two routes to intent alignment — LLM-based automated verification vs human-in-the-loop confirmation — across 59 academic papers, 21 production agent systems and 26 security plugins (as of April 2026). The argument: current LLM capabilities are insufficient to close the intent-alignment gap on their own, so the security frontier is the interaction design between agent and human, not a smarter verifier model.

This lands directly on the memory-poisoning-in-multi-agent thread we've been carrying. The practical takeaway for anyone running auto-push-to-main bots or dynamic workflows: keep a human confirmation gate on irreversible actions — the paper's evidence is that a second LLM checking the first isn't a substitute.

ArborKV (arXiv 2605.22106) — structure-aware KV-cache for tree-based reasoning

Efficiency

A KV-cache eviction framework that exploits the search dynamics of tree-of-thought-style inference: cache reuse in tree-structured reasoning is governed by branch topology, so eviction decisions should be structure-aware rather than recency-based. The relevant trend — frontier reasoning models spend more compute exploring branches, and naive KV management is increasingly the bottleneck on how deep that exploration can go affordably.
