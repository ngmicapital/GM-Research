Cover Story
The One-Person Billion-Dollar Company Has Arrived

Matthew Gallagher launched Medvi — a GLP-1 telehealth startup — from his LA home in September 2024 with $20,000 and no employees. Using ChatGPT, Claude, and Grok, he had AI write the code, generate website copy, produce ad creatives, and run customer service. He outsourced the regulated infrastructure (licensed physicians, pharmacy, compliance) to partners, retaining only the customer relationship.

The result: $401M in sales in 2025 (first full year), a 16.2% net margin — nearly triple the margins of Hims & Hers — and a trajectory toward $1.8 billion in 2026 revenue. His only hire: his brother Elliot. Sam Altman predicted this two years ago. It's now verifiable fact.

"AI helped one man and his brother build a $1.8 billion company." — New York Times, April 2026

Education & AI
Alpha School: Top 1% Academic Performance in Two Hours Per Day

Joe Liemandt — who dropped out of Stanford to build Trilogy Software, became the youngest Forbes 400 member, and then quietly built ESW Capital into a prolific software acquirer — is now running Alpha School, a K-12 AI education experiment he has backed with $1 billion of personal capital.

The model: two hours of AI-driven personalised instruction per day, consistently placing students in the top 1% of standardised tests. A full grade level is mastered in 20–22 hours. The remaining school day is devoted to leadership, entrepreneurship, financial literacy, and public speaking. No lectures. No moving forward until material is mastered. Liemandt's thesis: the traditional classroom was designed for a narrow band of learners and wastes everyone else's time.

"The fix isn't more money or better teachers — it's rebuilding from scratch around mastery, motivation, and AI." — Joe Liemandt
Source: Shane Parrish / Knowledge Project · Alpha School
Biotech
Tobacco Plant Engineered to Produce Five Psychedelics at Once

Researchers at Israel's Weizmann Institute (Asaph Aharoni's lab) published in Science Advances this week: they inserted nine genes — drawn from mushrooms, plants, and the Colorado River toad — into Nicotiana benthamiana (a tobacco relative) using agroinfiltration. The modified plant now produces five psychedelic compounds simultaneously: psilocybin, psilocin, DMT, bufotenin, and 5-MeO-DMT.

The team used AlphaFold3 to optimise enzyme performance, achieving a 40-fold increase in 5-MeO-DMT yield. The plants cannot pass modified genes to offspring (proof-of-concept only). The case for "pharming" psychedelics this way: the Colorado River toad — one of the primary natural sources of 5-MeO-DMT — is increasingly threatened by overexploitation for therapeutic use.

"This could offer a simpler, more sustainable alternative to chemical synthesis for psychedelic research." — Science Alert
Source: Tim Ferriss 5-Bullet Friday · Science Advances · phys.org · ScienceAlert

Shane Parrish's lead insight this week cuts straight to the core of reputation economics. Credibility is built in private — conversations no one overhears, deals where you leave money on the table, credit given away, the hard thing said instead of the easy one. Decision-Making
The Samurai Seven-Breath Rule: Decisiveness as Discipline

Tim Ferriss surfaced a passage from Hagakure (Yamamoto Tsunetomo, ~1716) this week that deserves serious attention as a decision framework. The principle: major decisions should be reached within the space of seven breaths — with intense, fresh, undelaying spirit. Lord Naoshige: "When matters are done leisurely, seven out of ten will turn out badly."

The practical translation: prolonged deliberation is often not deeper thinking but paralysis dressed as prudence. An intense, clear-headed state where you commit fully and move — rather than going "hither and thither" — produces better outcomes seven times in ten. The caveat: this requires prior preparation. The samurai who could decide in seven breaths spent years training their judgment before the moment arrived.

"It is a matter of being determined and having the spirit to break right through to the other side." — Yamamoto Tsunetomo
Source: Tim Ferriss 5-Bullet Friday · Hagakure
Sleep Optimisation
The Science-Based Pre-Sleep Stack: Chamomile + Glycine + Electrolytes + Magnesium

Dan Go's protocol, backed by citations in his newsletter this week:

Chamomile binds to GABAergic receptors — the same pathway as anti-anxiety medications, but far more gently. It quiets the nervous system without sedation. Glycine (amino acid) lowers core body temperature, accelerating the natural pre-sleep temperature drop; studies show faster deep sleep onset and improved next-day alertness. Electrolytes address nocturnal urination — typically not an over-hydration problem but a mineral deficiency that prevents water retention in cells. Magnesium glycinate amplifies the effect, with a 2025 RCT (Nat Sci Sleep) showing improved sleep quality in healthy adults reporting poor sleep.

Protocol: brew chamomile tea two hours before bed. Add glycine powder. Take electrolytes. Supplement magnesium glycinate separately or stacked. Dan reports this as the single highest-impact sleep intervention he's tried.

Source: Dan Go / 4 Minute Fridays · BMC Complement Altern Med · Front Neurol · Nat Sci Sleep
Metabolic Health
Three Minutes Per Hour: The Simplest Metabolic Intervention

A 2025 meta-analysis (Advanced Exercise and Health Science) examined the acute effects of sedentary breaks in middle-aged and older adults. A representative study protocol: 3-minute movement breaks every hour — marching in place, squats, push-ups, heel raises. After a 12-week period, the results were significant:

Fasting blood glucose improved. Insulin sensitivity increased. Waist circumference dropped. Blood pressure and HDL cholesterol both moved in the right direction. The mechanism: muscle contractions pull glucose out of the bloodstream without requiring insulin — bypassing the entire insulin-signalling pathway. The most potent timing is immediately post-meal when blood glucose is rising. 3 minutes × 8 hours = 24 minutes total daily movement, with an outsized metabolic return.

Practical Rule
Set a 50-minute timer. When it fires: 3 minutes of any movement. Repeat throughout the workday. Prioritise the break after lunch.
Source: Dan Go / 4 Minute Fridays · Advanced Exercise and Health Science (Yin et al., 2025)
Goal-Setting
Goals as Identity Engineering

Jim Rohn via Shane Parrish: "The ultimate reason for setting goals is to entice you to become the person it takes to achieve them." This reframes goal-setting entirely — the goal is not primarily the outcome but the identity transformation required to pursue it. A well-chosen goal is a character specification for the person you're engineering yourself to become.

Ryan Holiday's complementary insight from the same issue: external accomplishment never produces lasting okayness. The sufficiency you're looking for doesn't arrive via the milestone — it has to be cultivated internally, independent of outcomes. The paradox: pursue goals with full intensity for the identity they forge, not the external state they promise.

On the Horizon
4 themes
1
The Solopreneur Economy Becomes Real Infrastructure
Medvi is not a hack — it's a structural proof of concept. As AI handles code, copy, analytics, and customer service, the bottleneck in business shifts entirely to: (a) identifying a high-margin market, and (b) assembling the right outsourced compliance and fulfilment partners. The next wave won't be SaaS — it'll be AI-enabled service businesses with 1–5 people running at 9-figure revenue. Watch for the first $5B one-person company within 18 months.
2
AI Education Scaling to Developing Markets
Liemandt's stated ambition — a billion kids on sub-$1,000 AI tutoring tablets — is not as far-fetched as it sounds given the Alpha School results. If 2 hours of AI instruction genuinely outperforms 6+ hours of traditional classroom time, the ROI argument for low-cost AI education in emerging markets is overwhelming. The policy battle will be over institutional disruption, not technology feasibility.
3
Psychedelic Pharming as a Conservation Strategy
The Weizmann study reframes the psychedelic supply problem from a chemistry challenge to an agricultural one. As therapeutic demand for 5-MeO-DMT, psilocybin, and DMT grows — and as toad and mushroom populations face pressure — greenhouse tobacco may become a serious supply pathway. The next step is yield optimisation at commercial scale and regulatory frameworks for "pharmaceutical crops." Watch Science Advances and psychedelic biotech startups for follow-on work.
4
Decisive Leadership vs. Consensus Culture
Thatcher's observation — that consensus is often the avoidance of necessary decisions — is becoming more operationally relevant in an era where speed and conviction separate winners from laggards. The leaders and founders gaining ground right now tend to decide fast, communicate clearly, and tolerate being wrong over being slow. Watch for a broader cultural recalibration away from stakeholder-management theatre toward accountable decisiveness, especially in technology and investment contexts.
