§01 — Model Landscape

Google ships Gemini 3.5 Flash to GA and OpenAI defaults everyone to GPT-5.5 Instant — but on the hardest coding benchmark, the Anthropic family still owns the top three slots by a wide margin.

Gemini 3.5 Flash goes GA — default-on across Gemini Enterprise ⚡

LaunchGoogle Gemini 3.5 Flash hit general availability and, from June 8, is enabled by default and cannot be turned off for Gemini Enterprise users. Google's framing is aggressive: it calls 3.5 Flash its "most intelligent model for sustained frontier performance on agentic and coding tasks" and claims it outperforms the larger Gemini 3.1 Pro on key benchmarks at Flash speed and price. Alongside it, Google previewed Gemini Omni ("generate any output from any input, starting with video") and shipped Deep Research Max, an autonomous research agent. Cleanup: gemini-3.1-flash-image-preview and gemini-3-pro-image-preview retire June 25.

The Flash-beats-Pro claim is the tell. Google is collapsing its own lineup toward the cheap/fast tier and forcing it on Enterprise — a volume play on inference economics, not a capability flex. If a Flash-class model genuinely tops the prior Pro, the "Pro" tier loses its reason to exist, which is why the image-preview deprecations are landing the same month.

GPT-5.5 Instant becomes the ChatGPT default; GPT-5.6 still a rumour

UpdateOpenAI GPT-5.5 Instant is rolling out to all ChatGPT users, replacing GPT-5.3 Instant as the default. OpenAI's headline number is a 52.5% reduction in hallucinated claims versus 5.3 Instant on high-stakes prompts (internal evals). OpenAI also refreshed GPT-Rosalind for life-sciences work — stronger medicinal-chemistry and genomics performance with bioinformatics plugins. GPT-5.6 remains a market expectation for June, not a confirmed product — no release note exists yet. Rumour

SWE-bench Pro refresh: Anthropic sweeps the podium, GPT-5.5 lands 7th ⚡

Benchmark The SWE-bench Pro coding-agent leaderboard updated June 9. The Anthropic Mythos/Fable family leads at roughly 80%, with Claude Opus 4.8 at 69.2% and Opus 4.7 (Adaptive) at 64.3%. The next tier is entirely Chinese open-weight labs: Qwen 3.7 Max (60.6%), MiniMax M3 (59.0%), then GPT-5.5 and Kimi K2.6 tied at 58.6%. SWE-bench Pro uses actively maintained repos with no public ground-truth leakage — it's the post-contamination benchmark of record after SWE-bench Verified's credibility took a hit earlier this year.

The ~11-point gap between Opus 4.8 (69.2%) and GPT-5.5 (58.6%) on a leakage-resistant benchmark is the most decision-relevant number in this issue: for agentic coding, the Anthropic stack isn't just ahead, it's a tier ahead. Note GPT-5.5 sits inside the open-weight cluster — meaning a free, self-hostable Chinese model now matches OpenAI's flagship on real software-engineering work.

§02 — Claude Ecosystem

The Mythos thread resolved: Claude Fable is the public productisation of Anthropic's cybersecurity model — and its zero-day capability is a direct input to the crypto-security risk picture in §03.

Claude Fable ships — Mythos goes public at ~2x Opus pricing ⚡

LaunchSecurity On June 9 Anthropic released Claude Fable, the public version of the Mythos model that ran in restricted preview from April via Project Glasswing. Fable's headline capability is offensive-grade defense: it can "autonomously discover and chain zero-day exploits across major operating systems and browsers" and identify vulnerabilities in smart contracts, blockchain node software, and infrastructure — pitched for faster security audits and defensive patching. Access runs at roughly double current Opus pricing tiers, and the rollout adds "enhanced safeguards to limit misuse." This is enterprise/institutional, not a consumer model.

This is the carried Mythos story finally landing as a product — and the pricing (2x Opus) tells you Anthropic is treating frontier security capability as a premium, gated SKU, not a general upgrade. The line that matters for Tony: Fable "compresses exploit timelines dramatically in the cryptocurrency market." A model that can chain smart-contract zero-days autonomously is simultaneously the best auditor and the worst-case adversary for DeFi — which is why this story doesn't stay in §02 (see §03).

Opus 4.8 is now the everywhere-default; Sonnet 4 / Opus 4 retire June 15

UpdateAnthropic Opus 4.8 (shipped May 28) is the default on Max, Team Premium, Enterprise pay-as-you-go, and the API — "sharper judgement, more honesty about its own progress, longer autonomous runs." The flip side: the original Claude Sonnet 4 and Claude Opus 4 retire from the API on June 15. Anything still pinned to those model IDs breaks in five days.

Action item: grep your configs and scheduled tasks for claude-sonnet-4 / claude-opus-4 (the 4.0 IDs, not 4.5+). Five-day runway to repoint to 4.8 or a 4.x successor before the API starts erroring.

Managed Agents get private sandboxes + private MCP; Apple Swift package lands

Workflow Claude Managed Agents can now run in a sandbox you control and connect to your own private MCP servers — the missing piece for running agents against internal infrastructure without exposing it publicly. Separately, Anthropic shipped Apple Foundation Models framework support via a Swift package: typed Swift outputs can hand off to Claude for multi-step reasoning, code generation, web search, and streaming inside SwiftUI.

§04 — Open-Source Pulse

MiniMax M3 weights drop today and top the open-weight SWE-bench Pro — the Chinese open-weight cluster now matches OpenAI's flagship on real coding work.

MiniMax M3 open weights land — tops open-weight coding, edges Kimi K2.6 ⚡

LaunchBenchmark MiniMax M3 weights are dropping around June 10, and it takes the top open-weight slot on SWE-bench Pro at 59.0%, just past Kimi K2.6's 58.6%. M3 is built around multimodality and long-context speed — an MSA architecture with native vision/video and computer-use — versus K2.6's focus on autonomous agent coordination (agent swarms, 1T-parameter MoE, tool-calling specialisation). Ships open-weight under a permissive (Apache/MIT-class) licence.

The headline isn't M3 vs K2.6 — it's that the entire 58–61% band of SWE-bench Pro is now Chinese open-weight labs (MiniMax, Kimi/Moonshot, Qwen, DeepSeek), and that band sits level with GPT-5.5 at 58.6%. For anyone cost-sensitive on agentic coding, the open-weight option is no longer a downgrade from OpenAI's flagship — it's a lateral move you can self-host. The gap that remains is to Anthropic's 69%+ tier, not to the closed field generally.

Regulatory The long-running EU AI Omnibus thread resolved: negotiators reached a provisional agreement on May 7. §06 — Workflow & Tools

Claude Code's June fixes target exactly the agentic-reliability failure modes: parallel tool calls no longer cascade-fail, and deny-rule globs plus background updates make long-running agents safer to leave unattended.

Parallel tool calls go fault-isolated; deny-rule globs land ⚡

Workflow The most useful June Claude Code change: a failed Bash command in a parallel batch no longer cancels the other calls — each tool returns its own result independently. That directly removes a whole class of "one flaky command nuked the whole turn" failures in agentic runs. Permissions also got sharper: deny rules now support glob patterns in the tool-name position ("*" denies all tools), and there are now fallback models plus stronger cross-session message security.

For anyone running multi-tool or scheduled agents (i.e. this briefing's own harness), fault-isolated parallel calls is the quietly important one — it means a failed grep or git call in a fan-out doesn't take down the legitimate work alongside it. Combined with glob deny-rules, you can now run a long agent with a tight allowlist and trust that a single tool failure degrades gracefully instead of aborting.

Background agents self-update; sub-1000ms MCP timeout bug fixed

Update Background agent sessions now update to a new Claude Code version in the background, so reopening after an update no longer waits on a cold restart. A subtle MCP bug also got squashed: per-server timeout values below 1000ms were being floored to a 1-second watchdog that aborted every tool call — sub-1000ms values are now ignored cleanly (falling back to the default). Minor but real if you'd ever set an aggressive MCP timeout and watched everything mysteriously fail.

§07 — Research Radar

The frontier research theme of the month: agent capability is moving out of model weights and into memory, skills, protocols, and harness — exactly the stack this briefing runs on.

Externalization in LLM Agents (arXiv 2604.08224)

Research A unified review (submitted April 9) arguing that modern agent capability "earlier systems expected the model to recover internally" is now externalized into memory stores, reusable skills, interaction protocols, and the surrounding harness. It lays out a four-part taxonomy — memory (state across time), skills (procedural expertise), protocols (structured interaction), harness (governed coordination) — and traces the historical shift from tuning weights toward building external cognitive infrastructure.

This is the academic framing of how Tony already operates: skills, a persistent memory directory, MCP protocols, and a scheduled harness. The paper's value is as a checklist — if agent performance is now an infrastructure problem rather than a model problem, the lever is better memory/skill/protocol design, not waiting for the next model.

BAGEN: Are LLM Agents Budget-Aware?

Research A new benchmark probing whether agents reason about their own resource budget — do they spend tokens/tool-calls proportionate to task value, or burn the same regardless? Directly relevant to anyone running agents under a token ceiling: budget-awareness is becoming a measurable capability, not just a prompt instruction.

Why Reasoning Fails to Plan (arXiv 2601.22311)

Research A planning-centric analysis of long-horizon decision-making finding that strong step-by-step reasoning does not reliably translate into good multi-step plans — the failure is in horizon and commitment, not local logic. Useful counterweight to the assumption that a better reasoner is automatically a better autonomous agent.
