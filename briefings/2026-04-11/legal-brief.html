Coinbase CEO Backs CLARITY Act After Bessent's "Recalcitrant Actor" Charge — Senate Markup Begins This Week

"It's time to pass the Clarity Act." — Brian Armstrong, CEO Coinbase, via X, 10 April 2026

In a dramatic reversal, Coinbase CEO Brian Armstrong publicly endorsed the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act on April 10, hours after Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent appeared on Fox News labelling Coinbase a "recalcitrant actor" whose yield-on-stablecoin dispute was blocking historic crypto legislation. The Senate Banking Committee is targeting a markup during the two weeks of April 13–24 following the Easter recess, with stablecoin yield negotiations described as "99% resolved."

Senator Bernie Moreno has set an unambiguous deadline: "If we don't get the Clarity Act passed by May, digital asset legislation will not pass for the foreseeable future." SEC Chair Paul Atkins separately confirmed both the SEC and CFTC are "ready to implement" the bill the moment Congress acts, and earlier submitted "Reg Crypto" to OIRA in a coordinated signalling exercise.

📎 Historical Precedent: The JOBS Act of 2012 passed after similar industry-lobby-regulator triangulation; its safe harbour provisions for crowdfunding defined startup capital markets for a decade.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis
The CLARITY Act is the capstone of the US digital asset regulatory stack — it defines which tokens are commodities (CFTC) vs. securities (SEC), creates registration frameworks for exchanges and broker-dealers, and codifies a "sufficiently decentralised" safe harbour for DeFi protocols. Armstrong's reversal removes the last significant industry blocker; the outstanding political risk is now Senate floor scheduling and any eleventh-hour amendment from community banking interests. Counsel should map client token portfolios against the bill's five-part classification test now — the transition clock starts at enactment, not implementation.
🔄 Contrarian Take
Armstrong's reversal likely reflects Coinbase's OCC bank charter approval calculus, not a genuine policy concession on yield. If the stablecoin yield ban survives into the final bill, Coinbase's USDC interest programme faces a direct revenue hit — and the company will have publicly blessed the legislation that kills it. Watch the final yield-ban language very carefully; the "99% resolved" figure comes from Coinbase-aligned sources.
🏗️ Industry Implications
Exchanges Kraken, Binance.US, OKX, and Bybit face immediate registration decisions under the CLARITY Act's framework once enacted. DeFi protocols must decide whether to register or demonstrate sufficient decentralisation for the safe harbour. Coinbase, already holding an OCC conditional approval, is structurally positioned to dominate a regulated market; its reversal may reflect confidence it wins in either outcome.
📊 Portfolio Impact

Bullish [regulated US exchanges, Coinbase, compliant DeFi tokens, institutional on-ramp infrastructure]. Bearish [offshore unregistered exchanges serving US users, yield-bearing stablecoin products if yield ban survives]. Neutral [Bitcoin and ETH — already treated as commodities under the draft framework].

GENIUS Act Implementation Sprint: FDIC Reserves Rule + Treasury AML Rule Filed in 48 Hours

"This proposal will protect the U.S. financial system from national security threats without hindering American companies' ability to forge ahead in the payment stablecoin ecosystem." — Secretary Scott Bessent, U.S. Treasury, 8 April 2026

Federal regulators filed two complementary GENIUS Act implementing rules within 48 hours. On April 7, the FDIC Board approved an NPRM establishing prudential standards for FDIC-supervised permitted payment stablecoin issuers (PPSIs): 1:1 reserve requirements in eligible liquid assets, redemption within two business days, a $5 million minimum capital floor for new entrants, and pass-through FDIC deposit insurance on reserve holdings. On April 8–9, Treasury's FinCEN and OFAC jointly proposed rules classifying all PPSIs as financial institutions under the Bank Secrecy Act — triggering mandatory AML/CFT programmes, suspicious activity reporting, customer due diligence, and a technical obligation to block, freeze, and reject transactions under OFAC sanctions lists.

Both NPRMs carry a 60-day comment period closing June 9, 2026. The GENIUS Act's statutory deadline requires final implementing regulations by July 18, 2026, with full enforcement beginning January 18, 2027.

📎 Historical Precedent: The 2013 FinCEN guidance that classified Bitcoin exchangers as money services businesses under the BSA — this NPRM is the first time that logic reaches formal rulemaking with statutory backing.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis
These rules close the BSA gap that has allowed stablecoin issuers to operate without bank-level AML obligations. The OFAC technical requirement — to block, freeze, and reject specific wallet addresses — is the most operationally demanding: it requires PPSIs to integrate with OFAC's SDN list in near-real-time, similar to what US banks currently do. Any stablecoin issuer targeting the PPSI designation must begin AML programme build-out now; the January 2027 enforcement date is not generous given system integration timelines. Counsel should file detailed comments on the reserve asset eligibility list by June 9 — the current draft definition will determine which Treasury instruments and money market funds count.
🔄 Contrarian Take
The compliance moat created by these rules entrenches incumbents — Circle, Paxos, and Ripple can absorb $5M+ compliance infrastructure costs; smaller issuers cannot. Paradoxically, the GENIUS Act may reduce stablecoin issuer diversity rather than expand it, concentrating the market around three or four large, US-regulated issuers — which is exactly what large traditional financial institutions prefer.
🏗️ Industry Implications
Circle (USDC), Paxos (PYUSD/USDP), and Ripple (RLUSD) are best positioned to absorb compliance costs and gain PPSI designation. Tether, as an offshore issuer not seeking PPSI status, is exempt from these rules — preserving its offshore distribution advantage but increasing friction with US institutional clients requiring regulated counterparties. Compliance technology vendors — Chainalysis, Elliptic, TRM Labs — face strong tailwinds as PPSIs build OFAC screening infrastructure.
📊 Portfolio Impact

Bullish [Circle/USDC, Paxos, RLUSD, compliance tech — Chainalysis, TRM Labs]. Bearish [Tether institutional distribution in US, small stablecoin issuers facing existential compliance costs]. Neutral [Bitcoin, ETH — not categorised as payment stablecoins].

📋 What To Do
Trader Prefer GENIUS Act-compliant stablecoins (USDC, PYUSD) for institutional-grade settlement; Tether's US institutional utility is structurally diminishing under this framework.
Counsel File comments on reserve asset eligibility definitions by June 9 — the current draft list will determine the capital efficiency of stablecoin reserve management for years.
Compliance Begin AML/CFT programme design immediately — January 2027 enforcement date leaves insufficient time for a from-scratch build; budget OFAC SDN integration as the critical-path item.
Founder If issuing a USD-pegged stablecoin, model PPSI licensing costs (capital, reserve management, compliance infrastructure) before launch; assess whether offshore structure provides meaningful regulatory relief long-term.

Australia's Corporations Amendment (Digital Assets Framework) Bill Receives Royal Assent

"The legislation creates two new categories of financial products under the Corporations Act — Digital Asset Platforms and Tokenised Custody Platforms — both requiring AFSL authorisation from ASIC." [paraphrased — Gilbert + Tobin, April 2026]

The Corporations Amendment (Digital Assets Framework) Bill 2025 received Royal Assent on April 8, 2026, making Australia the first major English common-law jurisdiction outside the US and UK to enact comprehensive crypto licensing legislation. The regime creates two new Corporations Act financial product categories: Digital Asset Platforms (DAPs), covering facilities that hold digital tokens on behalf of customers; and Tokenised Custody Platforms (TCPs), covering tokenized representations of non-monetary assets with a single digital token per underlying asset. Both require an AFSL from ASIC.

The regime commences April 9, 2027, with ASIC providing a sector-wide no-action position until June 30, 2026 — incentivising early licence applications. A $10 million annual transaction volume exemption protects small issuers. The Digital Finance Cooperative Research Centre estimates the tokenized market opportunity at A$24 billion annually.

📎 Historical Precedent: Australia's 2017 AUSTRAC registration regime for digital currency exchanges — the first formal Australian crypto regulatory step — now being superseded by full AFSL licensing under the Corporations Act.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis
Australia's framework is deliberately grafted onto the existing AFSL regime rather than creating bespoke crypto law — which means obligations are predictable but demanding. Custody, disclosure, dispute resolution, and client asset safeguarding requirements under the Corporations Act apply in full. The TCP category for tokenized non-monetary assets is the most consequential for RWA markets — it creates a licensed pathway for on-chain tokenization of real property, commodities, and financial instruments. Offshore exchanges with Australian users must either obtain an AFSL, rely on a licensed Australian intermediary, or exit the market.
🔄 Contrarian Take
The June 30 ASIC no-action deadline creates a compliance cliff — a bulk of applications arriving simultaneously risks processing backlogs. ASIC's digital asset registration history has not been expeditious; firms that delay risk operating in a legal grey zone after June 30 even if they've filed, which will create enforcement uncertainty rather than the certainty the Act promises.
🏗️ Industry Implications
Coinbase secured an Australian AFSL ahead of the bill's passage and is positioned as the early-mover advantage holder. Bybit, OKX, Kraken, and local platforms including Swyftx and BTC Markets must file applications or exit. The TCP pathway for tokenized RWAs positions Australia alongside Singapore as an Asia-Pacific hub — directly relevant to institutional players managing Australian property portfolios and commodity exposures.
📊 Portfolio Impact

Bullish [Coinbase (licensed), compliant Australian exchanges, RWA tokenization platforms, AFSL holders]. Bearish [non-compliant offshore exchanges with Australian customer bases facing exit-or-license pressure]. Neutral [Australian retail BTC/ETH holders — transition period insulates near-term activity].

📋 What To Do
Trader Verify your Australian exchange has filed or holds an AFSL before June 30; unlicensed exchanges face enforcement exposure after that date.
Counsel Review client operations against the DAP/TCP definitions in the Corporations Act; commence AFSL variation applications for clients serving Australian digital asset customers.
Compliance Map client user base for Australian customer exposure and prepare ASIC AFSL application documentation under the new DAP/TCP categories before the June 30 no-action deadline.
Founder If launching a DAP or TCP for the Australian market, budget 12–18 months for licensing and engage Australian legal counsel now — ASIC's processing pipeline will grow competitive quickly.

SEC "Reg Crypto" Clears OIRA — Two-Tier $75M Safe Harbor Framework Weeks From Federal Register

"The new 'regulation crypto' has been sent to the White House Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs — it's on the cusp of being published." — Chair Paul Atkins, SEC, April 2026 [paraphrased — CoinDesk]

SEC Chair Paul Atkins confirmed that "Regulation Crypto Assets" — colloquially "Reg Crypto" — has been submitted to the White House Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs for pre-publication review. The proposed framework introduces a two-tier fundraising safe harbour: a Startup Exemption permitting raises up to $5 million over four years with minimal disclosure; and a Fundraising Exemption allowing raises up to $75 million annually with structured financial disclosures but no full IPO-style registration requirement.

Under the framework, most crypto assets — digital commodities, collectibles, utility tools, and payment stablecoins — would be classified as non-securities. Only tokenized traditional securities would remain fully subject to existing securities laws. Atkins unveiled the framework at the Vanderbilt/Blockchain Association Digital Assets summit on April 6.

📎 Historical Precedent: Regulation A+ (2015) established tiered offering exemptions for equity — Reg Crypto applies the same logic to token-based capital formation, replacing the informal "token utility" analysis the industry has been navigating since 2017.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis
Reg Crypto formally ends the "regulation by enforcement" era at the SEC for token fundraising. The $75M annual cap and structured disclosure requirement under Tier 2 are workable for most legitimate crypto projects; the $5M Tier 1 Startup Exemption covers seed-stage protocols entirely. The critical legal question is whether the non-security classification for "digital commodities" and "utility tools" is durable under Howey analysis — the interpretive release from March 17 (SEC-CFTC) provides the doctrinal foundation, but the first court challenge will test the boundaries. Counsel should begin mapping client token structures against the proposed asset classification taxonomy before OIRA review closes.
🔄 Contrarian Take
The $75M cap is significantly below the nine-figure rounds that major crypto projects require. If CLARITY Act passes with its own fundraising framework, Reg Crypto may be superseded or create conflicting disclosure obligations — practitioners should treat this as a bridge rule, not a long-term structure. The OIRA review process can also be used to slow or dilute rulemaking if political dynamics shift.
🏗️ Industry Implications
Web3 startups, DAO token launches, and protocol fundraisers gain an actionable legal pathway for the first time since 2017's ICO boom. Law firms advising crypto issuers — Cooley, Latham, Perkins Coie, Goodwin — face immediate demand for Reg Crypto compliance advisory. Exchanges listing tokens structured under the Tier 2 exemption will benefit from cleaner securities law exposure. Venture funds with token positions in unregistered offerings face reclassification risk under the framework's asset taxonomy.
📊 Portfolio Impact

Bullish [Web3 project tokens structured under the safe harbour, US crypto VC, legal advisory firms]. Bearish [tokens currently relying on informal "not a security" assertions that don't survive the new classification taxonomy]. Neutral [BTC, ETH — already confirmed as commodities under the SEC-CFTC March 17 interpretive release].

📋 What To Do
Trader Tokens that clearly map to Reg Crypto's non-security categories (digital commodities, utility tools) will face lower secondary market regulatory risk — begin reviewing portfolio exposure to tokens with ambiguous classification.
Counsel Map client token structures against the proposed five-category asset taxonomy immediately; begin structuring new token issuances to qualify for Tier 1 or Tier 2 exemptions before the rule is finalised.
Compliance Review existing token issuances that relied on informal "utility token" analysis — the new framework's classification test is more rigorous and retroactive reclassification risk is real.
Founder Hold off on large token raises until Reg Crypto is published in the Federal Register — the $75M cap, disclosure requirements, and non-security classification criteria will be definitive structuring inputs.

OCC Crypto Trust Bank Approvals Draw ABA and ICBA Demand for Immediate Pause

"The OCC should not function as a mechanism for companies to sidestep registration with the SEC, the CFTC, or other federal bodies with jurisdiction over digital assets." [paraphrased — American Bankers Association letter to OCC, February 2026]

The American Bankers Association and Independent Community Bankers of America have jointly demanded the OCC immediately pause crypto national trust bank charter reviews, following conditional approvals for Coinbase (April 2), Circle, Ripple, BitGo, and Paxos in a wave of eleven applications filed over 83 days. The OCC's new National Trust Bank Rule, which took effect in April 2026, created the legal pathway these approvals rely on.

The ABA's February 12 letter raised three concerns: charters being used to bypass SEC/CFTC registration, inadequate consumer protection standards compared to insured depositories, and the risk of accelerating crypto firm entry before the CLARITY Act's regulatory framework is settled. The banking lobby's opposition reflects competitive anxiety — crypto-native trust banks would be authorised to hold custody of digital assets, receive deposits as reserves, and issue payment stablecoins without traditional banking obligations.

📎 Historical Precedent: The OCC's 2020 conditional approval for Anchorage Digital's national trust bank charter — later challenged by state banking regulators — established the contested jurisdictional precedent these approvals now rely on.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis
National trust bank status unlocks significant competitive advantages: federal preemption of state money transmission licensing, access to Federal Reserve master accounts (contingent on Fed approval), and a federally standardised custody framework. The ABA's demand is unlikely to succeed as a hard block — the OCC is an independent bureau and the Trump administration backs these approvals — but it creates litigation risk for each conditional approval as banking groups may seek judicial review. Each "conditional" approval remains incomplete pending a pre-opening regulatory examination, giving the OCC administrative latitude to delay final issuance.
🔄 Contrarian Take
Crypto firms seeking national trust bank status may be taking on a regulatory burden they underestimate. Trust bank supervision involves ongoing OCC examinations, capital adequacy monitoring, and CRA-adjacent community obligations — operational costs that offset the competitive advantages, particularly for firms whose revenue depends on rapid product iteration rather than stable custody fees.
🏗️ Industry Implications
Coinbase, Circle, Ripple, Paxos, and BitGo hold first-mover advantage if their conditional approvals are finalised. Traditional custodians — BNY Mellon, State Street, and Fidelity Digital Assets — face competitive pressure from lower-cost crypto-native trust banks. The ABA pushback has already affected Circle's stock price and RLUSD's positioning narrative, with RLUSD actively marketing its payment-focused model as immune to yield-ban provisions.
📊 Portfolio Impact

Bullish [Coinbase, Ripple, Paxos — first-mover bank charter advantage; institutional custody market]. Bearish [Traditional custodians facing competitive pressure from crypto-native trust banks; Circle if stablecoin yield ban passes]. Neutral [Retail crypto holders — banking charter developments affect institutional infrastructure, not retail access directly].

📋 What To Do
Trader Watch for Federal Reserve master account decisions for conditionally approved crypto trust banks — Fed approval (not just OCC) is the real institutional gateway and remains unresolved.
Counsel For clients with conditional OCC approvals, begin pre-opening examination preparation — governance structure, board composition, risk management framework — to avoid approval delay.
Compliance Model full OCC trust bank examination requirements, including ongoing capital adequacy and CRA obligations, against business plan projections — compliance costs are higher than most applicants model.
Founder If not already in the OCC pipeline, assess whether state trust charter (Wyoming SPDI, South Dakota trust company) provides equivalent market access with fewer federal compliance obligations.

MAS Integrates USDC and USDT Into Singapore's National FAST Payment Rails

"Only MAS-licensed entities can participate, ensuring compliance with anti-money laundering, counter-terrorism financing, and consumer protection standards." [paraphrased — MAS announcement, April 2026]

The Monetary Authority of Singapore has officially integrated USDT and USDC into Singapore's Fast and Secure Transfers (FAST) payment infrastructure, enabling licensed financial institutions to offer real-time, 24/7 SGD-to-stablecoin conversion at the national payment layer. The integration creates instant settlement rails between traditional banking and the two largest dollar stablecoins, accessible only through MAS-licensed entities. Compliance requirements — AML, CTF, and consumer protection — apply in full to all FAST-stablecoin transactions.

📎 Historical Precedent: Singapore's 2022 MAS-regulated stablecoin framework finalization established the SCS (single-currency stablecoin) licensing category that FAST integration now operationalises.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis
FAST integration is the first instance of a G10-adjacent central bank payment infrastructure natively supporting stablecoin settlement. It creates a licensed, regulated on/off-ramp that competes directly with traditional FX and payment channels — and positions Singapore as the APAC hub for compliant stablecoin-enabled cross-border payments. StraitsX and Paxos Digital Singapore are positioned to dominate the regulated stablecoin issuer market here; Circle's SGD expansion is the immediate competitive question.
🏗️ Industry Implications
Circle's expansion of USDC stablecoin settlement services across India, Singapore, and the Gulf (announced April 8) aligns directly with this infrastructure unlock. DBS Bank, OCBC, and UOB — all MAS-licensed — face competitive pressure to offer FAST-stablecoin products or cede fintech market share to payment specialists. For remittance corridors (Southeast Asia, South Asia), licensed stablecoin settlement via FAST will be materially cheaper than SWIFT.

MiCA Grandfathering Window Narrows to June 30 — ESMA Flags Last-Minute Application Caution

"National competent authorities should treat 'last minute' applications for authorisation under MiCA with considerable caution and be ready to enforce against the unauthorised provision of crypto-asset services." — ESMA Statement on MiCA Transitional Measures, December 2025

The final grandfathering period under MiCA expires June 30, 2026 across the EU member states that elected the maximum 18-month transition. After that date, CASPs operating without MiCA authorisation face active enforcement by national competent authorities (NCAs). ESMA has explicitly told NCAs not to be "processing-backlog lenient" on late filers — last-minute applications should be scrutinised heavily, and firms that continue operating without authorisation post-June 30 should expect enforcement action, not administrative tolerance.

📎 Historical Precedent: FCA's extended crypto registration window (2020–2022) created a backlog of 300+ applicants and enforcement uncertainty — ESMA is explicitly seeking to avoid this pattern.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis
June 30 is a hard enforcement cliff for CASPs in the maximum-transition member states (most of the EU). Firms without a filed application by mid-May risk being unable to secure authorization before the deadline. The passport right under MiCA — authorisation in one EU member state granting access to all 27 — creates a race to register in the most permissive or most expeditious NCAs. Cyprus, Malta, and Luxembourg are seeing concentrated application volumes. Counsel should advise clients to file no later than May 15 to preserve any transition-period argument.
🏗️ Industry Implications
Binance, OKX, Bybit, and Kraken have EU CASP authorisation processes underway, but timeline certainty varies. Smaller European crypto exchanges and DeFi front-end operators are the most exposed to the June 30 cliff. MiCA-authorised firms gain significant competitive advantage: EU passport, institutional client access, and insulation from enforcement fragmentation across 27 national regimes.
