Tillis Ethics Provision Becomes Second CLARITY Act Roadblock — Senate Banking Markup Pushed to May, Bill Now Stalled on Two Distinct Fronts

"There has to be ethics language in the bill before it leaves the Senate, or I'll go from one of the people working on negotiating it to voting against it." — Sen. Thom Tillis (R-NC), to Politico, late April 2026

The Digital Asset Market Clarity Act, the bill the crypto industry treats as its 2026 legislative finale, lost its April markup window this week as Sen. Thom Tillis (R-NC) — until now a constructive Republican negotiator — publicly conditioned his vote on inclusion of ethics language barring federal officials, including the President, from sponsoring or issuing digital assets. TD Cowen flagged Tillis as the bill's "latest roadblock" in an investor note circulated 28 April.

The CLARITY Act now faces two distinct blocking issues. The first — stablecoin yield treatment — Tillis himself believes can be resolved within two weeks; he is leading negotiations between banking and crypto lobbies on draft text that would prohibit passive yield but permit narrowly defined activity-based rewards. The second — the ethics provision — is the messier one. Sens. Adam Schiff (D-CA) and Ruben Gallego (D-AZ) are leading the Democratic push, with explicit reference to the Trump family's $1B+ stake in World Liberty Financial and the USD1 stablecoin. White House crypto adviser Patrick Witt is steering Republican counter-negotiations alongside Sens. Cynthia Lummis and Bernie Moreno.

Senate Banking Chair Tim Scott (R-SC) has not announced a markup date. Lummis confirmed a May target on X: "We are going to markup the Clarity Act in May." Galaxy Digital's Alex Thorn now puts 2026 passage odds at "50-50, possibly lower"; Polymarket has it at ~47%, down from 82% in February.

📎 Precedent: The 2010 Dodd-Frank derivatives title also stalled on adjacent ethics fights (Lincoln pushout) before final passage — every market structure reform attracts a values fight in conference.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Per the TD Cowen note circulated by The Block, ethics language is now a binary precondition for Tillis, not a negotiating chip — meaning a Republican single-vote loss in committee unless either he or two Democrats flip. The compounding effect: two unresolved tracks (yield + ethics) negotiating in parallel against a closing window. The Brief's read: the May markup target is real but fragile. If Scott marks up without locked ethics text, Tillis votes no and the bill dies in committee; if Scott waits for ethics text, recess and August deadline pressures push the bill into post-Labor Day floor action — exactly when midterm calendar dynamics start to dominate.

🔄 Contrarian Take

The Brief's read: the ethics fight may actually accelerate passage, not delay it. Tillis is one of the most institutionally cautious Republicans on the Banking Committee; his public demand gives Schiff/Gallego political cover to settle for narrowly drafted language rather than a maximalist anti-Trump provision. A bill that passes the Senate with ethics restrictions on a sitting president's family is also harder for the White House to publicly oppose, because the carve-out becomes the price of crypto-industry support.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Coinbase ($COIN) — the most visible CLARITY beneficiary given its $1.348B stablecoin revenue base in 2025 (19.6% of net revenue per its 10-K) — faces the longest tail risk if the May markup slips. Circle (private, S-1 active), Ripple, and Securitize have a smaller delta because their regulatory ask is mostly preemption of state money-transmitter regimes already covered under GENIUS. World Liberty Financial and any USD1-linked entities face the highest direct exposure: ethics text drafted to bar presidential family issuance would force structural separation or wind-down. The Brief's read: watch DC-based DeFi protocols (Uniswap Labs, ENS) — their CLARITY-dependent ATS modernization play (covered 26 April) loses runway with each week of delay.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟢 Bullish $XRP, $SOL — survive on existing SEC/CFTC March guidance regardless of CLARITY timing; benefit from "law is unnecessary" narrative.

🔴 Bearish $COIN, $HOOD — derate as 2026 passage probability falls; stablecoin-yield ban tightens revenue model.

⚪ Neutral $USDC issuer (Circle), $RLUSD (Ripple) — both already GENIUS-aligned; CLARITY adds upside but isn't structural.

38 State AGs Coordinate Amicus Behind Massachusetts in Kalshi Fight; Wisconsin Pulls Coinbase, Robinhood, Crypto.com Into Dane County Suit — State Counterattack Hits Critical Mass

"Kalshi's aggressive theory of preemption threatens the States' longstanding ability to protect their citizens in this area." — Amicus brief authored by Nevada AG Aaron Ford and Ohio AG Dave Yost, filed 25 April 2026

The state side of the prediction-market fight reached a coordination tipping point this week. On Friday 25 April, attorneys general from 38 states plus DC filed a joint amicus brief supporting Massachusetts AG Andrea Joy Campbell's enforcement action against Kalshi, arguing that states have "traditionally regulated gambling, including sports betting." The brief was led by Nevada and Ohio — both already in active litigation with Kalshi — and pulled in California, New York, Pennsylvania, Michigan, New Jersey, and red-state AGs Alabama, Mississippi, and Tennessee.

The same day, the CFTC filed suit in SDNY seeking to enjoin New York from enforcing its gambling laws against federally regulated event-contract platforms — escalating the federal-state preemption war into a four-front conflict (NY, AZ, CT, IL) on top of the Third Circuit's 6 April Kalshi-favorable ruling against New Jersey.

Then on Thursday 24 April, Wisconsin AG Josh Kaul filed three "public nuisance" complaints in Dane County against Kalshi, Polymarket, Robinhood, Coinbase, and Crypto.com — the first state action to formally name distribution partners (Robinhood/Coinbase route prediction-market orders to Kalshi) as co-defendants. Kaul's complaint relies in part on Kalshi's own Instagram ads claiming "The First Nationwide Legal Sports Betting Platform."

📎 Precedent: The 1992 PASPA litigation (federal sports-betting preemption) and the 2018 Murphy v. NCAA decision invalidating it — the Murphy Court's anti-commandeering reasoning is exactly what state AGs are now invoking in the reverse direction.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Per Norton Rose Fulbright's prediction-markets analysis (April 2026): the doctrinal split is now formally circuit-level — Third Circuit favoring Kalshi (NJ), Nevada district favoring states, with Massachusetts, New York, Wisconsin, Maryland, and Ohio queued. The Brief's read: a SCOTUS cert petition in calendar 2026 is now a base case, not a tail. The Wisconsin novelty matters most: by suing Coinbase and Robinhood as routing parties, Kaul opens a second causation theory that doesn't depend on platform jurisdiction — exchange ATSs and broker-dealer apps face state-law liability for transmitting orders to Kalshi. That's a CFTC preemption argument the agency has not yet briefed.

🔄 Contrarian Take

Per Cleary Gottlieb's prediction-markets paper: the 38-AG amicus is structurally weaker than it looks because it includes seven AGs (Idaho, Iowa, Kansas, Nebraska, North Carolina, South Dakota, Utah) whose states already permit retail sports gambling — undermining the brief's "longstanding gambling regulation" framing. Kalshi's brief in opposition will lean hard on this inconsistency.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Coinbase ($COIN) and Robinhood ($HOOD) are the immediate structural losers — Wisconsin's distribution-partner theory could replicate across the 38 AG coalition within 60 days. Kalshi (private, ~$2B last valuation) loses the geographic-arbitrage strategy that made the unit economics work; per CNBC's 27 April reporting, the routing partners have already started Wisconsin geofence reviews. Polymarket ($POLY tokens, IPO-track) faces direct exposure on its existing US-soldier indictment overhang from last week. The Brief's read: DraftKings ($DKNG) and FanDuel parent Flutter ($FLUT) are the asymmetric beneficiaries — every state win for the AG coalition entrenches their licensed sports-book moat.

CFTC Chair Selig Confirms AI Will Triage Crypto Registration Applications After 20%+ Workforce Cut — Capacity Crisis Reshapes the Enforcement Pipeline

"We're building out systems to automate that, to make it much more efficient. AI tools can review applications, flag certain things for staff, make their jobs easier, and reject things that aren't materially complete." — CFTC Chairman Mike Selig, CoinDesk interview, 27 April 2026

CFTC Chairman Mike Selig confirmed this week — in a CoinDesk interview published 27 April, with parallel reporting in The Block — that the agency is deploying Microsoft 365 Copilot and bespoke AI surveillance tools to triage crypto and prediction-market registration applications, after the agency lost more than 20% of its workforce since FY2024.

The system is functional now: AI-assisted review flags incomplete applications for staff attention and auto-rejects materially deficient filings. CNN's 26 April investigation independently reported that the agency policing the explosively growing event-contract market has been shrinking faster than any peer financial regulator. Selig's posture on prediction markets remained unsubtle: "We are on the case and continue to watch for news."

📎 Precedent: The SEC's 2018 deployment of analytics-driven enforcement (the "ARTEMIS" project under Stephanie Avakian) — same playbook, automation as a workforce substitute, but pre-LLM.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Per the CoinDesk and The Block reporting plus Katten Muchin's April CFTC outlook: the AI-triage shift means registration timelines should compress for technically complete filings (estimated 30–50% faster intake review) but lengthen for novel structures the model isn't trained on — DeFi-native DCMs, multi-asset event contracts. The Brief's read: the deeper consequence is enforcement allocation. With AI handling registrations, every freed-up staff lawyer flows to the prediction-market preemption docket (Story #2). Expect the CFTC's New York SDNY filing to be staffed at unusual depth for a regulator this small.

🔄 Contrarian Take

Per Bipartisan Policy Center commentary on agency-AI deployment: AI-assisted regulatory review carries discoverable bias and FOIA exposure that staff review does not. If Microsoft Copilot rejects a Black-founder DCM application that a comparable applicant had approved, the CFTC inherits a Title VII-shaped problem the SEC does not. The Brief's read: watch for the first FOIA lawsuit asking how the model was trained — that will define the bounds of agency AI for a decade.

🏗️ Industry Implications

DCM applicants in queue (LedgerX/FTX-successor entities, Bitnomial, several stealth event-contract DCMs reported by The Block in March): faster path to registration if filings are clean. Microsoft ($MSFT) is the quiet beneficiary — Copilot Federal becomes a reference deployment for every other independent agency facing the same staffing pressure. The Brief's read: compliance-tech vendors (Chainalysis, Elliptic, TRM Labs) win twice — the CFTC's surveillance side runs on their data feeds, and shrinking agencies have less budget for in-house tooling.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟢 Bullish $MSFT (Copilot Federal reference deal); private compliance-tech (Chainalysis, Elliptic) expansion of agency contracts.

🔴 Bearish Novel-structure DCM applicants — model-untrained filings face longer queues than under human-only review.

⚪ Neutral $COIN, Kalshi — registration is not their bottleneck; preemption fight is.

FCA CP26/13 Defines UK Cryptoasset Perimeter — First Concrete Guidance on Stablecoin Issuance, Custody, Trading Platforms, and Staking Ahead of October 2027 Regime

"The FCA's perimeter guidance is the operational document the UK regime has been missing — without it, firms could not realistically self-classify under the Cryptoasset Regulations 2026." — Travers Smith client alert, 17 April 2026 [paraphrased from analysis]

The FCA published Consultation Paper 26/13 on 15 April, the first formally proposed Perimeter Guidance Manual ("PERG") amendments for the UK's incoming cryptoasset regime. CP26/13 covers seven new regulated activities: issuing UK Qualifying Stablecoins (UKQS), safeguarding cryptoassets, operating qualifying trading platforms, dealing as principal, dealing as agent, arranging deals, and arranging staking.

Critical timing: comment deadline is 3 June 2026; final guidance is targeted for September 2026; the application window for firms seeking authorisation runs 30 September 2026 to 28 February 2027; the regime itself goes live 25 October 2027. Overseas firms selling qualifying cryptoassets to UK consumers must be FCA-authorised regardless of jurisdiction of incorporation.

📎 Precedent: FCA PERG 2 (the original investment-services perimeter guidance circa 2003) — exact same structural document, refreshed for token taxonomy. The 2003 version became the de facto UK fintech operating manual for two decades.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Per Norton Rose Fulbright's UK regime tracker and the Freshfields client alert (April 2026): the most consequential drafting choice is the territorial test for "issuing in the UK" — the FCA proposes a relatively narrow nexus that ties to where the issuance contract is formed, not where the holder resides. The Brief's read: this is the opposite of MiCA's effects-based jurisdiction (which catches any issuer with an EU client). UK-incorporated stablecoin issuers can serve continental retail without dual-licensing — but EU MiCA-authorised issuers (Circle EU, Banking Circle's CSSF stack) must also separately authorise in the UK to issue UKQS. The regime thus resists a single-license passport.

🔄 Contrarian Take

Per CMS Cameron McKenna's CP26/13 review: the staking activity perimeter is intentionally vague and may chill institutional staking through 2027. The Brief's read: the FCA's drafting of "arranging staking" likely captures non-custodial routing protocols (Lido, RocketPool) on a literal read — institutional UK pension funds will avoid until the September final.

🏗️ Industry Implications

UK-domiciled issuers (PayPal UK, Standard Chartered's Zodia, Revolut's stablecoin pipeline) gain a structural moat — they can compete for UKQS labels that EU-only issuers cannot match without separate authorisation. Circle (USDC) faces a strategic decision on UK incorporation versus continued passport reliance. The Brief's read: watch for at least one EU-licensed stablecoin issuer to announce a UK subsidiary by the September final — it is the cheapest hedge against losing UK retail distribution.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟢 Bullish Standard Chartered ($STAN.L), HSBC ($HSBA.L) — UK stablecoin label is now a defined competitive product.

🔴 Bearish Pure-EU stablecoin issuers without UK presence — Circle's USDC EU vehicle faces UK retail revenue loss without separate auth.

⚪ Neutral US-domiciled issuers (Paxos, Anchorage) — UK regime is similar enough to GENIUS that adaptation is incremental.

📋 What To Do
TraderLong UK-bank cryptoasset disclosures (STAN.L, HSBA.L) into the September FCA final.
CounselSubmit comments by 3 June on territorial scope of "issuing" and "arranging staking" — these are the two perimeter calls most likely to move on industry input.
ComplianceMap every UK-resident user touchpoint against the seven new regulated activities; pre-flag any custody/staking offering that drifts into "arranging."
FounderIf you intend UK retail distribution post-October 2027, file for authorisation in the 30 Sep 2026 – 28 Feb 2027 window — late filers face a real backlog risk.

BIS GM Hernández de Cos Warns Stablecoin "Severe Fragmentation" Looms Without Stronger Coordination — Tokyo Speech Frames the G20 2026 Push

BIS General Manager Pablo Hernández de Cos delivered a Tokyo speech on 20 April warning that without stronger international coordination, stablecoin regulation faces "severe fragmentation" and harmful "regulatory arbitrage." The speech read as the BIS's opening salvo on the 2026 G20 financial-stability agenda — and a direct response to the divergence between GENIUS (US, deposit-bank model), MiCA (EU, e-money model), and the FCA UKQS regime (Story #4).

Concrete takeaway: the BIS Committee on Payments and Market Infrastructures (CPMI) will publish a stablecoin cross-border interoperability paper in H2 2026, with a draft IOSCO joint statement targeted for the November G20 Leaders' Summit.

⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Per the Central Banking summary of the 20 April speech and the FSB's 2025 stablecoin progress report: the BIS does not have rule-making authority but does control the FSB peer-review process. The Brief's read: the speech is positioning for a 2027 FSB recommendation that would force MiCA / GENIUS / UKQS reciprocity — meaning a US-licensed PPSI gets EU passport, and vice versa. That is the opposite outcome of the FCA's narrow territorial drafting in Story #4 and will be the next jurisdictional fight.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Tier-1 stablecoin issuers with multi-jurisdiction footprints (Circle, Paxos, Banking Circle) gain disproportionately if reciprocity arrives — single audit, multi-jurisdiction distribution. Smaller national issuers (Korean won-stablecoin pipeline, ASEAN-currency stablecoins) lose competitive ground to global-scale incumbents. The Brief's read: Tether (USDT, El Salvador-domiciled) is the structural loser — reciprocity rules will require home-country supervision the BVI / El Salvador framework cannot deliver.
