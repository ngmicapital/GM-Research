🟢 High — ordinance adopted, Justice Ministry sign-off reported, and the operative date is today.

Update Russia’s Crypto Trading Rules Take Effect Today as Licensed Operators Reach 30

[paraphrased] The central bank wants to approve exchanges’ trading rules before any organized crypto trading begins. Bank of Russia ordinance, as reported by Crypto Briefing, 21 Nov 2026

The Bank of Russia’s ordinance on organized digital-currency trading becomes operative on 22 November. Exchanges may host crypto trading only after the regulator approves their trading rules, and the central bank had registered 30 authorized operators by 18 November, up from four when the first licences issued on 6 October.

Retail access is narrower than the headline suggests. Non-qualified investors can buy approved crypto only through licensed intermediaries, capped at the equivalent of 300,000 rubles (about $3,700) a year. The Moscow Exchange plans a Bitcoin, ether and USDT pilot in December.

📎 Historical Precedent: MiCA’s authorisation-first model, whose transitional period ended on 1 July 2026, is the nearest EU analogue: no service before supervisory sign-off.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

The trajectory is licensing-first, with the central bank acting as gatekeeper for both venues and the intermediary channel retail investors must use. Counsel should treat the approved-rules requirement as the diligence test: a venue’s presence on the regulator’s list matters more than its offshore credentials. The Brief’s read: 30 approved operators within four weeks shows the gate opening quickly, but the December pilot is the real test of whether liquidity follows.

🔄 Contrarian Take

The cap and intermediary rule turn the regime into a controlled trickle rather than a functioning retail market. The Brief’s read: that is the design intent. The cap is a policy choice, not evidence that the ordinance is hollow.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Moscow Exchange (MOEX) is the obvious beneficiary of a first-mover pilot, while offshore venues serving Russian users lose the regulated retail lane. Western-listed exchanges have no direct channel here. The Brief’s read: any cross-border referral needs sanctions clearance first, which will limit foreign participation regardless of the ordinance.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟩 Bullish MOEX (Moscow Exchange) — first mover on the approved-venue list if the December pilot launches.

🔴 Bearish Unlicensed offshore venues serving Russian clients — no listed proxy, but the regulated lane is closing to them.

⚪ Neutral COIN — no direct Russian retail channel and no near-term exposure.

📋 What To Do — Before 1 July
TraderDo not route Russian-client flow to any venue until it appears on the Bank of Russia approved list.
CounselMap OFAC and EU sanctions exposure before onboarding any Russian-linked counterparty or intermediary.
ComplianceRecord how any distribution to Russian residents satisfies the 300,000-ruble annual cap through licensed intermediaries.
FounderPrice any Russia entry off the December pilot results, not the 22 November effective date.

Update Hill and Moreno Concede the Lame-Duck Window Is Closing for the CLARITY Act

“Time kills all deals,” said French Hill, a House lawmaker, on the missed CLARITY window. French Hill, House lawmaker — The Block, 19 Nov 2026

Two of the bill’s most prominent backers now say Congress missed its best chance. Senator Bernie Moreno put the realistic lame-duck output at zero, while Hill said the chances of passage are not high, though lawmakers never fully give up in a lame duck. Both blamed the decision to advance the GENIUS Act separately from CLARITY.

The procedural route is still technically open. Senator Thom Tillis’s motion to reconsider keeps the cloture question alive, but a rerun needs 60 votes. The Senate’s tentative calendar shows recesses from 23–27 November and 21–31 December, which leaves few floor days.

📎 Historical Precedent: the House passed CLARITY 294–134 on 17 July 2025; GENIUS advanced on its own track and was enacted, which is the split Hill now calls a mistake.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

The statutory market-structure perimeter remains unresolved, so SEC and CFTC staff guidance and rulemaking will set operative rules for the coming year. Senate Banking Chair Tim Scott has urged both agencies to set clear rules until Congress legislates, as reported by Tokenpost. The Brief’s read: agency FAQs and rulemaking, not statute, will define token-issuer obligations for the next twelve months.

🔄 Contrarian Take

Forbes analysis argues a lame-duck funding bill or defence bill could still carry crypto provisions, since the House and Senate must pass those anyway. The Brief’s read: attaching the bill still needs 60 Senate votes, and the incoming Senate is more Democratic, so the rider route is weaker than it looks.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Firms already operating under existing registrations gain relative position while token issuers without statutory safe harbour carry the delay cost. CNBC reports that executives are now pointing to tokenization and Wall Street blockchain use as the next catalyst. The Brief’s read: a stalled bill favours licensed incumbents such as Coinbase (COIN) over unregistered issuers.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟩 Bullish COIN — incumbents with existing registrations gain relative to token issuers.

🔴 Bearish Token issuers without statutory safe harbour — no listed proxy; the delay extends the uncertainty discount.

⚪ Neutral BTC — CNBC notes the regulatory bull case has faded and price now depends on other catalysts.

Update Hong Kong Gazettes Bill Extending Licensing to Crypto Dealers, Advisers, Managers and Custodians

“same activity, same risks, same regulation” — the policy principle in Hong Kong’s First Five-Year Plan, which the government invokes for this bill. Hong Kong First Five-Year Plan, as cited by The Crypto Times, 20 Nov 2026

The Virtual Asset Services Legislation (Amendment) Bill 2026 was gazetted on 20 November. It extends regulation to four further activities: dealing, advisory, management and custody. This follows the 16 November report that the government was preparing four licensing regimes.

The bill builds on the existing trading-platform licensing. Whether it passes before year-end depends on Legislative Council scheduling, which this issue has not verified.

📎 Historical Precedent: Hong Kong’s 2023 licensing regime for virtual asset trading platforms, which this bill extends to dealing, advisory, management and custody.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

The SFC would become licensing authority over a wider set of intermediaries, which changes counterparty diligence for any fund or manager using Hong Kong-based dealers or custodians. The Brief’s read: custody is the consequential limb, because its capital and segregation standards will decide which custodians survive the licensing wave.

🔄 Contrarian Take

Layering licences on dealers and advisers raises compliance cost and may push activity toward lighter-touch offshore centres. The Brief’s read: the government’s parity framing is persuasive where these activities already run unlicensed, so the cost argument is weaker than it appears.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Licensed custodians and asset managers in Hong Kong gain a moat, while unlicensed advisory desks face exit. Banks that already run custody arms, such as HSBC (0005.HK), are best placed to absorb the compliance cost. The Brief’s read: the regulated-infrastructure operators benefit most from a larger licensed population.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟩 Bullish 0388.HK (HKEX) — regulated market infrastructure benefits from a larger licensed population. The Brief’s read.

🔴 Bearish Unlicensed advisory and dealing desks — no listed proxy; exit or licensing cost is the risk.

⚪ Neutral 0005.HK (HSBC) — custody-capable bank, with compliance cost offset by franchise value.

📋 What To Do — Before 1 July
TraderCheck which Hong Kong dealers and custodians you use are likely to hold or obtain an SFC licence under the new regime.
CounselRead the gazetted bill text on the Hong Kong Government site for the transitional and grandfathering provisions before advising clients.
ComplianceAdd licence status for dealers, advisers, managers and custodians to third-party onboarding checks.
FounderIf you plan a Hong Kong custody or advisory product, budget for an SFC licence before the bill passes rather than after.

FCA Secures Court Undertakings Restricting HTX’s UK Crypto Promotions

[paraphrased] The FCA states that HTX has exited the UK, with new UK registrations and deposits unavailable. FCA statement, as reported by Global Crypto Regulation Tracker, Nov 2026

The FCA has obtained court undertakings from HTX restricting its UK crypto promotions. New UK registrations and deposits are now unavailable, and the firm must not knowingly communicate unlawful cryptoasset promotions to UK consumers.

The settlement is not an admission of liability, and no new general compliance deadline was announced. The action rests on existing financial-promotion law rather than the perimeter rules still at consultation stage.

📎 Historical Precedent: the UK financial-promotions regime for cryptoassets in force since October 2023, under which unregistered firms’ UK marketing has been the core enforcement lever.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Promotion-led enforcement continues to reach offshore venues while the perimeter rulebook is still being written. The Brief’s read: UK-facing marketing teams should treat these undertakings as the enforcement template, because a breach would move the matter back to court.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Offshore exchanges that rely on UK affiliate marketing lose a distribution channel, while UK-registered platforms gain relative share. The Brief’s read: the cost falls on acquisition budgets rather than on existing custody or trading operations.
