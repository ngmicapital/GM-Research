Model Landscape

The week's biggest model story is mathematical rather than a product launch: a Claude-assisted Riemann proof now has a Lean community endorsement behind it.

⚡ Update: Claude-assisted Riemann proof clears Lean checks

Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge released a proof of the Riemann hypothesis on 20 November, delivered as about 17 million lines of Lean code and developed with Claude. On 21 November the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative issued a joint statement that the proof states the intended theorem, compiles, and passes Lean's kernel plus several independent third-party checkers. The same day, a Stanford-linked team posted a human-only proof of the general hypothesis that is not yet in Lean. Two routes to the same problem now exist, and the formal one is the easier to audit.

GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol ship; Gemini 4 Neon claims the coding lead

OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol in the 16 to 19 November window, and Google launched Gemini 4 Neon in the same period. Google's own figures place Neon first on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index at 58.6, which is a vendor-reported number and should be read that way. OpenAI also says it cut reasoning-extraction attacks on GPT-6.2 Astra from 54.0% to 0.2%, a self-reported safety figure.

METR: task horizons keep stretching, judgement lags

METR's November update says the length of tasks agents can complete is doubling about every 3.6 months, with GPT-6 Astra and Opus 5.5 near 40 hours, though error bars are wide. The same work finds agents matching skilled humans on code but still trailing experts on judgement tasks that lack clear feedback. For buyers, the judgement gap is the constraint that matters more than the headline horizon.

Claude Ecosystem

Claude's app got a substantial redesign on Friday, and its Computer Use beta now reaches paid business tiers.

New mobile design and a Computer Use beta

Anthropic followed OpenAI's app overhaul with a new Claude mobile design built around a bottom tab bar covering Chat, Projects, Code and Artifacts. The same release adds a Computer Use beta that lets Claude view a user's screen and operate approved desktop applications, available on Team and Enterprise plans. This is the clearest case so far of Claude acting on a desktop rather than inside a chat window, which makes the approval controls the thing to test before rollout.

Claude Code keeps shipping on a patch cadence

Claude Code v2.1.329 appeared in the 20 November tool roundup, alongside a Codex 0.171.0 release. That points to a steady point-release rhythm rather than a major version change. Any Claude Code workflow changes should be checked against the release notes before teams upgrade across a fleet.

Riemann work carries the Claude name

The Riemann proof is the most visible Claude-linked result of the week, but it was produced by an Anthropic mathematician working with the model rather than announced as a product. The Lean-verified artefact is the thing to cite, not the claim that Claude solved the problem alone. Full coverage sits in the Model Landscape section.

Decentralized AI

Decentralized GPU and inference networks are signing paying customers while their tokens lag, and that gap is the test to watch.

Bittensor subnet revenue rising while TAO sits far below peak

Forbes reports that Bittensor's subnets now generate about $70 million a year, according to tracker SubConnect, up from roughly $35 million two months earlier. TAO, the largest of the group, trades roughly 70% below its peak after the 5 November AI selloff. Commentary in the same piece argues that token emissions run several times that revenue, so the revenue is real but does not yet cover what the token pays out.

Akash moves toward a GPU forward market on Solana

Akash is developing a Solana marketplace where AI teams could reserve future GPU hours at fixed prices, reported on 18 November. A 17 November notice paused some small migration transfers, which the coverage reads as operational friction even though the portal is expected to stay open for two years. Akash's founder argues demand and token prices can diverge, and notes almost every GPU on Akash is an Nvidia GPU, so Nvidia benefits either way.

Prices were not refreshed in this run, so no new quotes are given; the figures above are the reported ones.

Open-Source Pulse

Open weights are arriving from China, Microsoft and Mistral this month, and licence terms are doing as much work as benchmarks.

Kimi K3.1 Pro weights land on Hugging Face

Moonshot published the full Kimi K3.1 Pro weights on 2 November, two days ahead of its own date. The model is 2.8 trillion parameters with about 104 billion active, and its licence only requires a contract when a company offers it as a service and exceeds $20 million in twelve-month revenue. Internal use is exempt.

Microsoft publishes an on-device 2.3B model

Microsoft put Aion-1.0-Instruct-2.3B on Hugging Face on 18 November as an open-weight small model for Windows Copilot+ and WebGPU hardware. No benchmark results accompanied the release, so it should be read as a deployment artefact rather than a capability claim.

Mistral Large 4 licence check and LongCat-2.5

Mistral Large 4 weights have been public since 29 October under a modified MIT licence. Commercial use is allowed, but offering the model as a hosted service needs a separate Mistral licence. Meituan's LongCat-2.5 also went up under MIT on 9 November, which gives teams a permissive option from a large platform company.

Deals & Regulation

Mistral is the deal story of the day: a $110 million strategic investment and a new Singapore base as sovereign AI demand grows.

Deutsche Telekom's T.Capital invests $110M in Mistral

Deutsche Telekom's venture arm T.Capital has put $110 million into Mistral AI, framed as part of Germany's push for sovereign AI capacity. Mistral opened a Singapore headquarters in the same news cycle, which signals that Asia-Pacific buyers are a priority market for its sovereign offering.

Walmart puts a gateway in front of outside shopping agents

Walmart says its internal AI agent usage has doubled and is introducing a gateway that lets it approve which external automated shopping agents may operate on its platform. This is a commercial access control rather than a rule, and it shows large retailers building their own entry point for agents instead of accepting open access.

OpenAI widens its Space feature

OpenAI expanded its collaborative Space feature to $20-a-month Plus subscribers on Friday, and previewed a redesigned ChatGPT mobile app that gives its dot agent a dedicated tab. The move is aimed at consumer distribution and at keeping paying subscribers engaged inside the app.

Workflow & Tools

Agent stacks are converging on governed tool calls, with gateways for credentials, approvals and audit becoming product categories.

LangSmith Tool Gateway enters public beta

LangChain moved LangSmith Tool Gateway into public beta on 19 November. It centralises credentials, caller identity, access policies, approval flows and audit traces for each agent tool call, and it works with MCP-compatible clients including Claude Code and Cursor. For teams running agents against production systems, this is the layer that makes access reviewable.

Cloudflare Computer gives agents a sandboxed machine

Cloudflare Computer 0.11.4 provides a virtual filesystem inside a Durable Object, exposed through a Computer MCP example that pairs one code tool with a durable workspace, a Worker shell and a full Linux container. Its Launch HN post drew 91 points on Hacker News on 19 November.

Firecrawl Local as a local MCP server

A 19 November README change put Firecrawl Local at the top of the daily roundup. It converts websites into clean markdown, plugs into an agent as an MCP server, and serves the Firecrawl API on localhost, which suits private or offline scraping jobs.

Performativ opens its MCP to private banks

Performativ launched the next generation of its MCP on 19 November, connecting wealth-management client data to ChatGPT and other assistants while applying the firm's own controls to each request. It is a vertical example of the same gateway pattern, applied to regulated client data.

Research Radar

Three papers and reports worth tracking, with the Riemann verification the one to read first.

Lean-verified Riemann proof (Alpöge with Claude)

The proof's value lies in its verification: a joint Lean and Mathlib statement reports that multiple independent checkers accept the exported file. Our sources do not show an arXiv preprint, so the Lean artefact is the primary document. Track whether the human-only Stanford proof gets formalised, since agreement between the two routes would matter more than either alone.

METR time-horizon update

METR reports that the task length agents can handle doubles about every 3.6 months, while measuring it is getting harder. Its wide error bars mean the 40-hour figure for GPT-6 Astra and Opus 5.5 should be treated as a range until replicated.

AI-guided Lean proof refinement (arXiv 2611.10961)

This paper uses AI agents to tighten numerical bounds on Grothendieck's constant and Korenblum's constant, with each bound backed by a Lean proof. The authors also describe a lightweight orchestration framework based on graph-styled partial formalisation. The open question is whether the approach generalises beyond these two constants.
