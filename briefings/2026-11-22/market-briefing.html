Active Legislation

Democrats declined to back it over concerns about presidential family crypto ventures. EST current floor status not confirmed this run.
  • Stablecoin rules: still contested between traditional banks and crypto-native firms, according to the latest reporting I could find. No vote date is confirmed.
  • Read-through: legislative progress would be a medium-term positive for crypto, but it is not a catalyst for the next two weeks.

Today’s Decision Points

  • SEC probe of Nvidia accounting: reported by Reuters on Friday as an enforcement-staff inquiry into potential accounting issues. Investigations are not evidence of wrongdoing, and many close without enforcement action.
  • DOJ antitrust review: reported to be examining Nvidia’s $12.9 billion Hugging Face deal alongside a wider antitrust look at its acquisitions.
  • Fed, 9 December: the policy event that matters most for crypto, with the hike largely priced.
The SEC matter is the most important regulatory item because it hits the AI leader that is currently the largest single driver of index-level risk. It is also a reminder that the current enforcement posture is focused on accounting and disclosure, which is the area where Nvidia’s geographic revenue reporting now draws scrutiny. Any follow-up on the probe scope would be a catalyst for semis more than for crypto.
The regulatory calendar does not offer a bitcoin catalyst this week. The Nvidia probe is the risk to watch, because a widening scope would deepen the AI sell-off and drain liquidity from the broader risk complex, including crypto. Treat any headline on the crypto bill as background noise until a floor vote is scheduled.
5 🤖 AI & Semiconductor Watch

Thesis: the AI capex cycle is fatiguing at the financing layer, not the demand layer. The problem is who pays for the buildout and how governance is perceived, and that keeps semis under pressure even after a strong AI IPO.

Key Movers & Data

  • NVDA: closed Friday at 125.53, down 14.01%, its worst day since January 2025 and a seventh straight loss. The stock fell about 23% on the week.
  • Leadership: CEO Jensen Huang resigned on 12 November after personal revelations that surfaced during the week, and the company now faces an SEC accounting inquiry.
  • Moving averages: 50-day and 200-day levels for NVDA and INTC were not retrieved this run EST. I would not call a trend change without those levels.
  • Index impact: the PHLX Semiconductor Index fell 10.7% on Friday, the biggest single-session loss of the sequence.

Broader AI Landscape

  • Anthropic IPO: raised about $81.3 billion in the public offering and about $107.3 billion including a concurrent private placement, the largest listing on record. The shares opened far above the $83 price and closed Friday at $97.17.
  • Asia read-through: the Nikkei’s roughly 5% drop was led by AI and chip names, including SoftBank and Advantest.
  • Short-seller pressure: AMD was hit by a short report from Hunterbrook, and Micron and CoreWeave swung to losses on Friday.
Why a record IPO can hurt the AI trade: a large new listing adds fresh equity supply, and institutional managers must fund the purchase by selling existing holdings. When a $100 billion-plus raise lands in the same week that the leaders are already falling, the sell-side can absorb the new shares only by pushing out older positions. The sector-level tell is the semiconductor index: when it falls harder than the broad market while the Nasdaq underperforms, the trade is being unwound by funding, not by fresh demand data. Watch for distribution in the leaders before any bottoming process begins.
I read the AI capex cycle as fatiguing at the funding margin. Until Nvidia stops printing lower lows and the semis index reclaims its breakdown, I would avoid adding exposure. The first constructive sign would be a stabilisation in NVDA above the Friday low near 124.50 with volume drying up on down days.

7 🌎 Geopolitical Calendar

Thesis: geopolitics is a secondary driver this week. Two threads matter for pricing: the Middle East oil premium and the US–China chip-revenue question that runs through Nvidia’s probe. I see no third live geopolitical catalyst that justifies a separate alert.

🏴 Middle East Oil Premium (Active)
Status: Oil jumped on 12 November as traders assessed the outlook for the Middle East conflict. WTI now sits in the low-to-mid $80s, but the level is unconfirmed across sources EST.
Transmission Mechanism: Higher crude feeds the inflation print that drives the Fed, so oil spikes reach bitcoin through the rate channel rather than through direct safe-haven flows.
Reversal Signal: A sustained drop in WTI below the low-80s would ease the inflation argument and would be the first real reason to question the 9 December hike pricing.
🏴 US–China Chip Revenue Scrutiny (Active)
Status: Reuters reported the SEC is examining Nvidia’s accounting, and the company is facing separate scrutiny over a former executive’s alleged China links. The probe focus is not confirmed, but reporting points to how geographic revenue is presented.
Transmission Mechanism: Any finding that chip revenue was misclassified by destination would widen the export-control debate and weigh on the semiconductor complex, which feeds into the AI-led index drawdown.
Reversal Signal: A closure of the SEC inquiry without enforcement, or a clear company disclosure that reconciles the geographic revenue figures, would remove this thread.
Geopolitics is a second-order risk this week, and the main transmission is through oil into the rate path. I am not adding a separate geopolitical hedge. The two alerts above are the only live threads I can support with reported data, and the Fed remains the variable that matters most.

  3. NVDA Friday low near $124.50: this is the line that matters after the 14% drop that followed Reuters’ report of the SEC accounting probe. A clean break would open a deeper leg lower in the AI complex, dragging on peers that trade on the same growth narrative. A stabilisation on falling volume would be the first sign of a base, though a single quiet session is not enough. Look for several sessions holding above the level before treating it as durable.
  4. US PCE print on 25 November: the Fed’s preferred inflation gauge is the next hard input for the 9 December hike odds. A softer print is the only clean path to a lower rate-hike probability this month, while a firm reading would reinforce a hike case already priced near 90%. Size positions with the likely volatility around a major data release in mind, and avoid adding risk ahead of the number without a clear plan for both outcomes.
  5. Fed decision on 9 December: the hike is priced at roughly 90% or higher, so the surprise is less likely to be the decision itself than the guidance that accompanies it. Markets have largely absorbed the outcome; the real risk lies in the signal about the path afterward. Track daily spot ETF flows, especially IBIT, into the decision as the real-time read on institutional appetite. Persistent outflows into the meeting would suggest that bitcoin’s defence of $60K is weaker than the price action implies.
