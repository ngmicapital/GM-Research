CFTC Grants Backpack Contract-Market and Clearing Approvals, Opening a Path to US Crypto Perpetuals

[paraphrased] The approvals let Backpack plan a US derivatives venue and clearing organisation built on blockchain infrastructure. Backpack / CFTC approvals, as summarised by KuCoin Crypto Daily Market Report, 17 Nov 2026

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission granted Backpack designated contract market status on 16 November 2026, according to a daily market report. Its affiliated clearing entity had already obtained derivatives clearing organisation registration, which is the pairing a crypto-native venue needs to offer regulated derivatives to US users rather than routing them offshore.

Backpack plans to build a US derivatives trading platform and clearing organisation on blockchain infrastructure. The same report noted a short-term token rally of nearly 18 per cent over 24 hours; treat that price move as noise and read the registration stack as the substance.

📎 Historical Precedent: Hong Kong’s VATP licensing regime, in place since June 2023, is the closest model of a regulator admitting a crypto trading venue into a full licensed market structure.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

The two-step approval (a DCM for the venue, a DCO for clearing) follows the architecture of conventional US futures markets, so the compliance build is closer to derivatives compliance than to crypto licensing. Counsel should map the core designated contract market obligations against the specific perpetual product before any US marketing begins. The Brief's read: the designation is the first step and does not by itself answer product-level questions about retail access.

🔄 Contrarian Take

Federal approval does not end state friction. The CFTC and DOJ sued Louisiana on 13 November to void a state court order against event contracts, a fight this brief tracked on 15 and 16 November. The Brief's read: a DCM badge gives Backpack a federal preemption argument, not immunity, so any US perpetual launch should assume state gaming or consumer challenges.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Solana's ecosystem gains a US-regulated derivatives rail, which could pull liquidity and listings toward SOL-denominated products. Offshore-only perpetual platforms that serve US users through workarounds face a licensed rival. The Brief's read: winners are venues that already hold DCM and DCO stacks; the losers are offshore-only perp venues that depend on US-user flow.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟢 Bullish SOL — ecosystem demand rises if a US-regulated venue builds liquidity on Solana infrastructure.

🔴 Bearish Offshore-only perpetual venues (unlisted) — loss of US-user flow to a licensed rival.

⚪ Neutral CME — incremental competition in crypto derivatives for an incumbent futures exchange.

Hong Kong Passes Crypto-Asset Tax Reporting Law, Putting Transaction Reporting on a 2027 Clock

[paraphrased] The Legislative Council passed the Inland Revenue (Amendment) bill implementing the Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework and an amended Common Reporting Standard on 4 November. Legislative Council, as reported by Crypto Times, 16 Nov 2026

Hong Kong’s Legislative Council passed the Inland Revenue (Amendment) bill on 4 November 2026, bringing the OECD Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework and an amended Common Reporting Standard into local law. Market reporting places the first crypto-asset reporting in 2027.

The tax measure sits beside the licensing bill that Financial Secretary Paul Chan has committed to table before year-end, which would extend licensing to dealers, advisers, managers and custodians. Read together, the two measures close the perimeter from the tax side and the conduct side at once.

📎 Historical Precedent: Hong Kong’s 2023 virtual asset trading platform regime set the template that the new tax and licensing measures build on.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Platforms serving Hong Kong residents will need user-level and transaction-level data that matches the CARF reporting schema before the first reporting year, so KYC and transaction-history systems are a build item, not a memo item. Tax counsel should treat this as a data-architecture project with a 2027 deadline. The Brief's read: the tax limb is the more durable change, because it survives any slippage in the licensing bill.

🔄 Contrarian Take

Reporting regimes can push activity toward unreported offshore venues rather than improve compliance, and that risk falls hardest on mid-sized licensed platforms that carry the full cost. The Brief's read: the risk is real, but it is contained only when reporting and the licensing perimeter arrive together, which is the sequencing Hong Kong has chosen.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Licensed Hong Kong platforms and custodians gain a compliance moat as the reporting and licensing net tightens, while unlicensed advisers and dealers face a narrowing window. Of the two stablecoin licensees approved on 10 April, HSBC and the Standard Chartered-backed Anchorpoint Financial are bank-linked, so bank groups are the early beneficiaries. The Brief's read: bank-backed licensees win; small unlicensed desks lose.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟢 Bullish HSBA.L — bank-backed Hong Kong dollar stablecoin licensee benefits from a tighter perimeter.

🔴 Bearish Unlicensed Hong Kong crypto dealers and advisers (unlisted) — new licensing and reporting cost.

⚪ Neutral 0388.HK — exchange activity is not directly changed by a tax-reporting regime.

ComplianceMap the CARF data fields against current user and transaction records to find collection gaps.
FounderDecide now whether to seek a Hong Kong licence or exit retail services before the bill is tabled.

Thailand’s SEC Proposes Stablecoin Deposit Caps and Wallet-Ownership Rules for Exchanges

[paraphrased] The regulator proposed tighter stablecoin and exchange rules, including a per-customer cap on stablecoin deposits and withdrawals. Thailand Securities and Exchange Commission (draft), as reported by Crypto.com Market Pulse, 16 Nov 2026

The draft would cap stablecoin deposits and withdrawals separately at ฿5 million, roughly US$148,000, per customer and per operator. It would also require users to transact only through wallets they own, and add Travel Rule and suspicious-wallet monitoring duties.

If adopted, the package tightens anti-money-laundering controls while adding friction for both users and regulated operators. Because the cap applies per operator, a customer using several platforms could in principle hold more across them, which is a design point the final text will need to address.

📎 Historical Precedent: Hong Kong’s April 2026 stablecoin round approved two of 36 applicants, showing how licensing can ration stablecoin issuance.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

The per-operator cap reads as a consumer-protection and AML instrument, while the own-wallet requirement is the more consequential control because it targets custodial transfers. Exchanges serving Thai users should budget for Travel Rule tooling now. The Brief's read: this is a draft, and the per-operator design is the element most likely to change in consultation.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Thai-facing exchanges and stablecoin payment rails carry the monitoring cost, and regional flows may shift toward wallet-to-wallet transfers that the draft does not reach. The Brief's read: the rule favours large licensed operators that can absorb monitoring costs.

EU Bodies Seek Nominations for an Industry Group to Standardise Tokenized Securities Settlement

[paraphrased] The ECB, the European Commission and ESMA are inviting nominations for an industry group to set standards for holding and settling tokenized securities across DLT platforms. ECB, European Commission and ESMA, as reported by Crypto.com Market Pulse, 16 Nov 2026

The three EU bodies want nominations for a working group that will write standards for how tokenized securities are held and settled, across distributed-ledger platforms and existing market plumbing. Work is expected to begin in January 2027, with initial standards targeted for early 2028.

Interoperability between DLT venues and established settlement systems is the gating issue for tokenized-stock products. Standards drafted by an industry group will shape what supervisors later accept, so a seat on the group is a policy lever, not an honorific.

📎 Historical Precedent: the EU DLT Pilot Regime (Regulation (EU) 2022/858) was the first attempt to let DLT market infrastructures trade and settle securities under a supervised sandbox.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Firms that plan to issue or custody tokenized securities should map their settlement design against the standards this group will produce, rather than building bilateral workarounds that will need rework. The Brief's read: incumbent settlement systems and large banks are best placed to secure nominations, which tilts the standards toward existing plumbing.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Tokenization platforms and custodians holding EU authorisations gain if standards converge early. A parallel US push, including an NYSE tokenized-stock venue on an Avalanche-based chain reported the same day, will test the same interoperability questions. The Brief's read: standards are a 2028 event, so near-term advantage sits with firms that already hold licences.
