Model Landscape

Alibaba's Qwen4-Max is the newest large release on the board, but the trackers disagree on its date and open-weights timing, so treat both as provisional.

Qwen4-Max arrives with a million-token window and low API list prices

Several release trackers list Qwen4-Max as an API launch on 16 November, with a 1,000,000-token context window and list prices of $1.50 per million input tokens and $4.50 per million output tokens. One aggregator places it at 43 on its own index. A separate tracker dates a 'Qwen 4' debut to 11 November, scores it 58 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5, and says Apache 2.0 weights are promised for 20 December. We could not reconcile these: the 11 November entry may refer to a different generation, or the trackers may simply disagree. Confirm against Alibaba's own release page before acting on the open-weights date.

Read-through: on output tokens, the tracker lists Qwen4-Max at $4.50 against $7.50 for Gemini 4 Hydrogen, so the price gap is the most useful comparison for anyone routing long-context agent workloads.

DeepSeek's V4.1 Flash 1017 is the local-model release people are actually adopting

DeepSeek's V4.1 Flash 1017 has had MIT-licensed weights since 17 October, and one November dispatch notes that it is the smaller model with the strongest local-user uptake, with a community IQ2_XXS build at 10.1 GB and about 273,000 downloads. For teams running models on laptops or edge boxes, this matters more than the frontier-tier leaderboard movement.

Claude Ecosystem

Anthropic's IPO has moved from filing to pricing: the offer price is set tonight in the US, and first trading is due on 19 November under the ticker ANT.

⚡ Update: Pricing tonight, Nasdaq debut on 19 November

Nasdaq's data notice, updated on 11 November, lists Anthropic, PBC Class A shares under ANT on the Nasdaq Global Select Market, with listing on Thursday 19 November. The offer is marketed at $70–$78 per share across 825 million Class A shares, and the final price is set on the evening of 18 November US Eastern time. Reporting that cites CNBC sources and Bloomberg says expected demand is several times the shares on offer and the price may land above the range; those are expectations, not a completed pricing decision. For Sydney readers, the 18 November US evening pricing falls on the morning of 19 November, and the US open at 9:30am Eastern is about 1:30am Sydney time on 20 November (our conversion).

What to check tonight: whether the final price clears $78, whether the share count changes from 825 million, and whether the valuation stays in the reported $1.3–$1.4 trillion fully diluted band.

Four filings in about three weeks

The S-1 was filed on 26 October, an amended version followed on 10 November, and a third update was filed on 17 November, with the IPO website and a free writing prospectus dated 10 November serving as the public terms reference. Reporting puts the private placement at about $26 billion, issued at the IPO price, which would add to the net proceeds. Treat the valuation and placement figures as press-reported until the final prospectus confirms them.

Decentralized AI

Decentralized compute networks are competing on token-burn mechanics and real workloads, and governance questions are getting harder to avoid.

Akash approves a move of AKT onto Solana, but the marketplace has not followed

Akash stakers approved Proposal 346 on 2 November, which moves AKT to Solana as a 1:1 SPL token. The marketplace itself has not migrated yet. Third-party counts also put reported GPU capacity on the network at about 419 units, a figure that is worth tracking against demand rather than against token price.

Akash, io.net and Render converge on supply-burn models

A third-party governance analysis says Akash, io.net and Render have all moved toward supply-burn designs in 2026. It reports that Akash burned 53,520 AKT in its first eight days after activating its burn-mint mechanism on 23 March, that io.net committed to burning up to 12 million IO over its first year, and that Render projects monthly burns of 200,000–300,000 RENDER by late 2026. These are projections and early figures from one source, so verify them against on-chain data before relying on them.

Governance pressure on Bittensor: the Bittensor co-founder conceded in June that the network is not decentralized in the way Bitcoin is, and the project committed to restoring validator competition over eighteen months. The same governance analysis says the end-2026 decentralisation target looks at risk after the Covenant AI departure. Price data was not refreshed for this issue, so no token prices are quoted here.

Open-Source Pulse

Open-weight downloads are concentrated in Qwen and video-generation models, while community quantisations are doing most of the heavy lifting on adoption.

Qwen3.8-27B stays the download leader

The 17 November Hugging Face trending digest puts Qwen3.8-27B at roughly 20,300 likes and about 6.76 million downloads, and calls it the flagship model of the week for its image-text and conversational strength. It has been the top downloaded model across several consecutive digests, so its position reflects sustained use rather than a single spike.

Edge0-145B-NVFP4 pushes the scale of open MoE weights

A 145-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model using NVFP4 quantisation, published as Edge0-145B-NVFP4, appears in the same digest with about 55,000 downloads. It is a niche release, but it shows that very large open MoE checkpoints are now being packaged for lower-precision hardware.

Video and quantised GGUF builds lead the download counts

Lightricks' LTX-2.5 has passed 2.6 million downloads and about 10,900 likes in the 7 November digest, making it the most downloaded video model in the set. Community GGUF conversions are racing ahead too: one Unsloth build of DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 shows roughly 596,000 downloads, while a safety-stripped Qwen3.8 Flash variant shows about 3.5 million. The second figure is a reminder that uncensored quantisations are spreading fast, which matters for any enterprise that pulls community weights.

Deals & Regulation

Outside the IPO itself, the deal flow is strategic capital from the largest cloud and chip companies, plus pricing moves that work as commercial strategy.

Google and Nvidia each reportedly commit about $10 billion to Anthropic

Reporting from 13 November says Google and Nvidia are each committing roughly $10 billion around the listing. That makes the two largest suppliers of AI compute and chips anchor investors alongside the public offering, which is a structural signal for how the company intends to fund its compute bill. The figure is press-reported and should be checked against the final prospectus.

Gemini 4 Argon's introductory pricing is scheduled to double

A deadline calendar says Gemini 4 Argon's introductory price of $2 input and $10 output per million tokens doubles on 1 December. Google is using a time-limited discount to win workloads, and teams that have locked in during the promotion should model the December price before committing.

Product move: OpenAI introduced ChatGPT Images 2.6 in early November, adding animated GIF generation, per the ChatGPT release history. It is a consumer-product expansion rather than a deal, but it shows where OpenAI is pushing its image stack.

Workflow & Tools

The tooling story this week is access and partnership churn: model providers are reshuffling which editors and platforms get which models.

Cursor loses access to OpenAI models

A deadline calendar records that Cursor stopped having access to OpenAI models on 12 November. For developers who depend on Cursor's model picker, the practical step is to audit which models are still routed through the editor and to test fallbacks before the next release cycle.

MCP support is now standard across the big assistants

OpenAI added Model Context Protocol support to ChatGPT apps in September 2025, and in July 2026 those apps were renamed to plugins. That makes MCP a common integration layer across the major assistants, so MCP servers built for one client can increasingly be reused across others.

Pricing to plan around: the GPT-5.6 Sol promotional price ends on 21 November, per the same deadline calendar. Any agent pipeline pinned to that promotional rate should be costed at list price before the cutoff.

Research Radar

No peer-reviewed paper cleared verification in today's search pass, so this slot tracks an open technical question rather than a single paper.

The clearest open problem for decentralized AI is training rather than serving. Aggregated GPU marketplaces already undercut hyperscalers on inference by pooling idle and long-tail hardware, but training across the public internet is much harder and remains the research frontier. Watch for any published result that shows synchronised training across geographically spread, untrusted nodes at useful scale, because that would change the economics that the compute tokens are currently priced on.
