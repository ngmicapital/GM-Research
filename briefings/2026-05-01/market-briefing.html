4 ⚖️ Regulatory & Legal Radar

The CLARITY Act is stuck — and the stablecoin yield debate from the GENIUS Act is the reason. Senator Lummis's Banking Subcommittee was targeting a May markup, but unresolved language around stablecoin yield payments (carried over from the GENIUS Act enacted July 2025) has hijacked the timeline. The SEC's March interpretive release (68-page token taxonomy) provides interim clarity, but statutory market structure remains in limbo. The CFTC is the clear winner so far — the taxonomy pushes most digital assets toward commodity classification under CFTC jurisdiction.

📜 Active Legislation & Guidance
  • CLARITY Act — Senate markup was targeting May but timeline slipping on stablecoin yield debate. Would classify most digital assets as commodities under CFTC.
  • GENIUS Act (Enacted Jul 2025) — Stablecoin framework live. FDIC proposed rulemaking for payment stablecoin issuers. Yield provision unresolved.
  • SEC-CFTC Joint Release (Mar 17) — 5-category token taxonomy. Digital commodities, collectibles, tools, stablecoins, securities. Binding guidance.
  • SEC DeFi Relief (Apr 13) — UI providers operate without broker registration. Major for DEX front-ends.
📋 Enforcement & Upcoming
  • Kalshi 89% US market share — CFTC blessing of regulated prediction markets and perps reshaping derivatives landscape.
  • Hyperliquid vs Kalshi/Polymarket — HyperLiquid proposing to add prediction markets, direct challenge to regulated incumbents.
  • SEC Chair Atkins — "Innovation exemption" framework to fast-track crypto products under review.
  • US Strategic Bitcoin Reserve — Architecture announcement still pending per White House signals.
CLARITY Act delay is a headwind for alt L1s and DeFi tokens that were pricing in May passage. The SEC token taxonomy provides interim floor, but statutory clarity is what unlocks the next institutional wave. Watch the Banking Committee calendar — any forward scheduling of markup hearings is a catalyst.

Learn Something: Why the AI Capex Backlash Is Actually Healthy
META's -10% and MSFT's -5% aren't signs the AI trade is over — they're signs it's maturing. In Phase 1 (2023–2025), the market rewarded anyone spending on AI. In Phase 2 (now), it demands revenue proof. This is exactly how technology cycles work: the infrastructure builders (NVDA, AVGO) get paid first, then the platforms with customer revenue (GOOGL, AMZN) get re-rated, and the pure spenders (META, MSFT) face a "show me" period. The total addressable market isn't shrinking — the market is just getting pickier about who captures it.
The semi trade is no longer a basket — it's a stock-picker's market. Long GOOGL and QCOM for proven AI revenue. Hold NVDA for Vera Rubin catalyst in H2. Reduce META/MSFT until AI capex converts to margin expansion. Watch Anthropic's custom chip plans as long-term NVDA competitive risk.

Status: Dual blockade continues since April 13. Vessel traffic at ~5% of pre-conflict levels. Iran proposed reopening the Strait in exchange for deferring nuclear negotiations — markets were unimpressed (Brent spiked to $114 intraday on reports Trump is being briefed on expanded military options).

Transmission Mechanism: The IEA calls this the largest supply disruption in oil market history. Goldman estimates 14.5 mb/d removed from global production. This flows into energy prices (Brent $110+) → CPI (4.6% in Australia, rising in US) → central bank hawkishness (3 FOMC dissents, RBA hike 71%) → yield curve repricing (10Y at 4.42%) → equity multiple compression risk. The second-order effect: airlines, shipping, and consumer discretionary get margin-compressed while energy and defence rally.

Reversal: Iran's Hormuz reopening proposal was dismissed by traders. The path to reversal requires either a sustained diplomatic breakthrough or a US naval escort corridor. Trump's expanded military briefing suggests escalation is more likely than de-escalation in the near term. Watch for any backchannel signals.
🚨 FOMC HAWKISH FRACTURE — NEW
Status: Three hawkish dissents (Hammack, Kashkari, Logan) — most internal disagreement since October 1992. One dovish dissent (Miran, favoured a cut). Powell as outgoing chair (final meeting) walked the middle but warned oil inflation hasn't peaked.

Reversal: A sharp Hormuz de-escalation that collapses oil $15–20 would instantly reset the inflation calculus and revive rate cut expectations. Without that, the hawkish bias hardens.
