📚 Learn Something: Dark Pools
Dark pools are private exchanges where institutional investors execute large orders without displaying them on public order books. About 40-45% of all US equity volume now trades off-exchange, much of it through dark pools. Why do they exist? When a fund needs to buy $500M of a stock, placing that order visibly would move the price against them before they finish filling. Dark pools let them execute in blocks without signalling intent. The trade-off: retail investors can't see these orders, which critics argue creates an uneven playing field. For traders watching volume data, a spike in dark pool activity (reported with a delay) often signals institutional accumulation or distribution that precedes visible price moves. When you see unusually high dark pool prints in a stock, it's worth asking: who's building a position?
BofA's $1.3T forecast and NVIDIA's Rubin launch validate the demand thesis. But the memory delay risk and the $128B gap between BofA's cloud capex needs ($1T) and consensus ($872B) create an asymmetric setup — the right call is to stay long the picks (NVDA, AVGO) but size for the possibility that the ramp disappoints.
