FCA Publishes Perimeter Guidance Consultation — Five Crypto Activities In Scope, Authorization Opens September 2026

⚡ What this means: The FCA has told crypto firms exactly which activities will require a licence in the UK. If your business touches stablecoin issuance, trading platforms, dealing, custody, or staking, you need to apply for authorization by September 30, 2026 — or stop operating in the UK by October 2027 when the full regime is mandatory.
"Parliament confirmed the scope of regulated cryptoasset activities via statutory instrument on 4 February 2026. This guidance helps firms understand how those activities will be interpreted in practice."
— FCA press release, 15 April 2026 [paraphrased]

The Financial Conduct Authority published consultation paper CP26/x on 15 April 2026, seeking feedback on perimeter guidance for the UK's forthcoming cryptoasset regulatory regime. The consultation closes 3 June 2026, with a final policy statement due autumn 2026 and authorization rules finalized summer 2026.

Five activities fall within scope: (1) issuing qualifying stablecoins, (2) operating cryptoasset trading platforms, (3) dealing in and arranging deals in qualifying cryptoassets, (4) safeguarding cryptoassets, and (5) staking. Firms touching any of these must apply for FCA authorization from 30 September 2026. The new regime comes fully into force in October 2027.

The consultation is the latest in a dense publication series — the FCA has now published CP25/14 (stablecoins/custody), CP25/25 (handbook application), CP25/40 (cryptoasset activities), CP25/41 (admissions & disclosures), and CP25/42 (prudential rules). The perimeter guidance paper sits above all of these, clarifying who must comply with what.

📎 Historical Precedent: The UK's e-money institution registration regime (2011) similarly used perimeter guidance to clarify which payment activities triggered authorization — many firms discovered compliance obligations only after publication.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis
Under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (as amended by the Financial Services and Markets Act 2023), operating a regulated cryptoasset activity without authorization constitutes a criminal offence. The five-activity taxonomy aligns with MiCA's Article 3 service categories, suggesting deliberate convergence that may ease dual-authorization burdens. Winners: large exchanges already registered under the Money Laundering Regulations (Coinbase UK, Binance UK's authorized entity) who have a compliance head start. Losers: smaller custodians and staking providers who have never engaged with FCA registration.
🔄 Contrarian Take
The perimeter guidance consultation is the sixth FCA crypto paper in six months — firms experiencing consultation fatigue may disengage, producing low-quality responses and a guidance regime shaped mainly by larger, better-resourced incumbents. The September 2026 authorization gateway is also tighter than many firms assumed: 17 months is a short runway for a full authorization process that historically takes the FCA 12–18 months per firm.
🏗️ Industry Implications
Staking-as-a-service providers (Lido's UK operations, Coinbase's staking product) now have certainty that staking is a regulated activity — this likely triggers significant compliance expenditure. DeFi protocols facilitating dealing without a UK nexus may argue they fall outside scope, but the FCA's perimeter guidance will need to address automated market makers directly. Custody providers (Fireblocks, Copper) begin authorization prep now; the September 2026 window is not far.
📊 Portfolio Impact
Bullish: UK-licensed exchanges and custodians — clarity reduces regulatory discount on UK revenue.
Bearish: Small unlicensed UK staking and custody operators — compliance costs and authorization timeline will squeeze margins or force exits.
Neutral: DeFi protocols without UK legal presence — perimeter guidance does not obviously reach fully decentralized systems.
📋 What To Do
Trader Note September 30 as the UK authorization gateway date — expect announcements from exchanges about UK-specific terms changes as that deadline approaches.
Counsel Map every UK client activity against the five scope categories immediately; any client touching stablecoin issuance, trading platforms, dealing, safeguarding, or staking must begin FCA pre-application engagement now.
Compliance Submit a response to the consultation by June 3 — the perimeter guidance will define enforcement priorities for years, and industry input on edge cases (DeFi, staking derivatives, wrapped assets) will shape the final text.
Founder If your UK operations touch any of the five activities, budget for FCA authorization now — the September 2026 gateway is 166 days away and the FCA's authorization queue will fill quickly.

CLARITY Act: Scott Publicly Names Three Remaining Obstacles — May Window Is the Last Chance

⚡ What this means: Chairman Tim Scott broke from vague optimism on April 14 and named the three specific issues blocking the CLARITY Act markup: stablecoin yield language, DeFi provisions, and Republican unity on his own committee. Senator Moreno has issued a blunt warning that if it doesn't clear the Senate Banking Committee by May, digital asset legislation likely won't return for years.
"We cannot miss the window for discussion while waiting for the government's bill." [paraphrased — Sen. Moreno warning that May is the last realistic window for 2026]
— Senator Bernie Moreno (R-OH), via FinTech Weekly coverage, April 2026 [paraphrased]

In a Fox Business appearance on April 14, Senate Banking Committee Chairman Tim Scott gave the clearest public assessment yet of the CLARITY Act's remaining obstacles. He named three: (1) stablecoin yield language — the Tillis-Alsobrooks compromise text has not yet been formally released, meaning Scott has no document on which to base a markup date; (2) DeFi provisions — still contested, with Democratic members raising illicit finance concerns; and (3) Republican committee unity — Scott wants all GOP members aligned before a potential party-line vote.

Senator Hagerty expressed confidence the bill could clear the Banking Committee during the current work period and reach the Senate floor before month-end. Senator Moreno provided the starkest assessment: if markup doesn't happen in the April/May window, digital asset legislation may not receive serious congressional attention for years, given the midterm election cycle and legislative calendar compression.

The sequence for markup is: Tillis releases revised yield text → 48-hour review period → Scott sets markup date. As of April 17, the Tillis text has not been published.

📎 Historical Precedent: The JOBS Act (2012) stalled in Senate committee for weeks over disclosure provisions before a single-session floor vote; legislative windows in election-adjacent years are reliably compressed.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis
The CLARITY Act (H.R.3633, passed House 294-134 July 2025) would establish a definitive SEC/CFTC jurisdictional split for digital assets, resolving the Howey test ambiguity that has defined US crypto enforcement for a decade. Scott's public naming of three obstacles is both a negotiating signal and a calendar warning — it suggests the bill is closer than the April 16 "no markup date" framing implied, but the DeFi provisions and ethics language (presidential conflict-of-interest restrictions) remain genuinely contested. If the bill fails in May, the next realistic window is post-2026 midterms.
🔄 Contrarian Take
Scott's three-obstacle framework may be too optimistic. The "Republican unity" problem is not cosmetic — if even two GOP committee members are genuinely undecided, Scott cannot call a party-line vote without risk of embarrassment. The ethics language (restricting the Trump family from crypto profits) has no obvious Republican champion, meaning it survives only if Democrats extract it as a concession — which they show no sign of doing.
🏗️ Industry Implications
Coinbase (whose CEO Brian Armstrong has endorsed the CLARITY Act), Ripple, and Circle have the most to gain from SEC/CFTC jurisdictional certainty. A CLARITY Act failure would benefit Ethereum-native DeFi protocols that have operated successfully under enforcement ambiguity. The banking industry (ABA, BPI) is actively lobbying against the bill's bank charter provisions — their resistance is a structural backstop to passage even if the yield and DeFi issues resolve.
📊 Portfolio Impact
Bullish: XRP, COIN stock — CLARITY Act passage resolves regulatory overhang for Ripple and Coinbase directly.
Bearish: Nothing immediately; failure of the bill = status quo, not new restriction.
Neutral: Bitcoin — unaffected by SEC/CFTC jurisdictional split; already treated as commodity.
📋 What To Do
Trader Assign under 40% probability to CLARITY Act passing the Senate floor before June — do not price in a regulatory catalyst trade until Tillis releases yield text and Scott announces a markup date.
Counsel Brief clients this week that the DeFi provisions in the current House-passed text carry real compliance risk regardless of whether the bill passes — begin modelling both passed and failed scenarios for protocol and exchange clients.
Compliance Flag the May window in board materials — if CLARITY Act stalls, market structure legislation will not return until 2028 at earliest, meaning SEC enforcement posture governs for at least two more years.
Founder The three-week yield debate, the DeFi fight, and the GOP unity problem mean May 2026 is the realistic last window — UK and EU authorization timelines should be accelerated as a hedge against US legislative failure.

South Korea BOK Nominee Reverses on Stablecoins — Democrats Push Independent Bill as 51% Bank Cap Debate Reopens

⚡ What this means: The incoming Bank of Korea governor — who had previously blocked stablecoin regulation by insisting banks must control 51% of any stablecoin issuer — reversed his position on April 16. This removes the biggest single institutional obstacle to South Korea's Digital Asset Basic Act. The Democratic Party is now moving to introduce its own stablecoin legislation rather than wait for a government bill that has been stalled for over a year.
"I was negative about stablecoins in the past, but I believe they can coexist in a complementary yet competitive manner within the future monetary ecosystem."
— Shin Hyun-song, Bank of Korea Governor Nominee, April 2026

BOK Governor nominee Shin Hyun-song publicly reversed his longstanding opposition to non-bank stablecoin issuance on April 16, signaling a willingness to allow stablecoins to "coexist" within the monetary system rather than treating them as a threat to be bank-captured. His predecessor's insistence on a 51% bank ownership requirement for any stablecoin issuer had been the central roadblock in the Digital Asset Basic Act negotiations.

The Democratic Party of Korea, led on digital assets by Rep. Lee Jung-moon (Digital Asset Task Force Chair), announced it will introduce its own stablecoin legislation independently of the government's delayed bill. Rep. Lee was explicit: "We cannot miss the window for discussion while waiting for the government's bill." A National Assembly Political Affairs Committee meeting has been requested to advance the bills.

South Korea's five major exchanges — Upbit (Dunamu), Bithumb, Coinone, Korbit, and Gopax — remain at risk from the parallel shareholder cap dispute: the proposed 20% cap on major exchange shareholders (with a 34% corporate exception) would require governance restructuring at Upbit, where Chairman Song Chi-hyung holds 25.53%.

📎 Historical Precedent: Japan's 2022 pivot from treating stablecoins as securities to a dedicated payment instrument framework (FIEA amendment) was similarly unlocked by a single central bank policy shift, after which legislation moved within six months.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis
The BOK nominee's reversal is legally significant because the 51% bank ownership requirement — which would have disqualified every existing stablecoin issuer — was not a statutory requirement but a BOK policy position. With that position softening, the National Assembly faces a cleaner political path. The Democratic Party's independent bill route is faster but riskier: it bypasses inter-agency consensus (FSC, FSS, BOK, MOEF) that has produced gridlock, but may produce a law with implementation gaps. The 20% shareholder cap on exchanges remains a separate fight that could delay the entire framework.
🔄 Contrarian Take
Shin's reversal may be performative ahead of his confirmation hearing rather than a durable policy commitment. Once confirmed as BOK governor, institutional imperatives around monetary sovereignty and deposit protection typically reassert themselves. The Democratic Party's independent bill strategy has also failed twice before — a rushed law without FSC and BOK sign-off will face implementation resistance regardless of what it says on paper.
🏗️ Industry Implications
Circle (USDC) and Tether (USDT) — both major players in Korean retail — benefit most from a framework that doesn't require bank partnerships. Kakao's Klaytn-linked stablecoin ambitions, previously shelved over regulatory uncertainty, may be revived. Upbit (Dunamu) faces the exchange shareholder cap as a separate governance risk; if the bill passes with a 20% cap, the current ownership structure will require either a divestiture or a regulatory waiver. Korean won-backed stablecoin issuers (most are informal) would face new reserve and audit requirements under either bill.
📊 Portfolio Impact
Bullish: KLAY (Klaytn), Korean exchange tokens — legislative progress reduces regulatory discount on Korean crypto sector.
Bearish: Bank-only stablecoin advocates — BOK nominee reversal removes the institutional argument for bank-captured issuance.
Neutral: BTC/ETH — Korean regulatory framework has limited global price impact absent a dramatic shift in Korean trading volumes.
📋 What To Do
Trader Watch the National Assembly Political Affairs Committee meeting — if the Democratic bill gets a committee date within two weeks, Korean crypto assets may rally on regulatory clarity expectations.
Counsel If any client operates a KRW-backed stablecoin or intends to, retain Korean legal counsel now; both bills impose reserve, audit, and capital requirements that will require immediate compliance roadmapping.
Compliance Map Korean revenue exposure against the proposed 20% exchange shareholder cap and the stablecoin reserve requirements; identify which entities would need restructuring if either bill passes this quarter.

MEDIUM White House CEA [PRIMARY] US Stablecoin Policy
🟢 High Confidence — government primary source

White House CEA Calls Stablecoin Yield Ban Economically Incoherent — $800M Net Welfare Cost, 6.6:1 Ratio

⚡ What this means: The GENIUS Act (enacted July 2025) prohibits stablecoins from paying yield directly to holders, a provision championed by banks who feared stablecoins would drain deposits. The White House's own economic team just published analysis showing this prohibition does almost nothing to protect bank lending — but costs consumers $800 million. This is the White House publicly undermining one of the most contested provisions in the legislation it championed.
"A yield prohibition would do very little to protect bank lending, while forgoing the consumer benefits of competitive returns on stablecoin holdings."
— White House Council of Economic Advisers, "Effects of Stablecoin Yield Prohibition on Bank Lending," April 8, 2026

The White House Council of Economic Advisers published a 24-page economic analysis on April 8 examining the GENIUS Act's prohibition on stablecoin issuers paying yield directly to holders. The CEA found the prohibition would increase aggregate bank lending by only $2.1 billion — a 0.02% increase — while imposing a net welfare cost of $800 million, producing a cost-benefit ratio of 6.6:1. Even under "highly implausible" extreme assumptions, aggregate lending would rise by at most 4.4%.

The analysis noted that the GENIUS Act's yield prohibition contains a structural loophole: yield could still be delivered through affiliate arrangements, meaning sophisticated issuers (Circle, Coinbase's USDC) may route yield-equivalent payments through holding company structures. The CEA noted this loophole without recommending it be closed, which practitioners are reading as tacit administration endorsement of the affiliate structure.

📎 Historical Precedent: The Fed's Regulation Q (1933–2011) prohibited interest on demand deposits on similar deposit-protection grounds; its repeal showed no material increase in bank lending instability.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis
The CEA report is unusual: an administration publishing economic analysis that undermines a provision it signed into law. Read in context of the CLARITY Act's stablecoin yield fight, the report functions as executive branch permission for Congress to reopen the yield question. For GENIUS Act compliance counsel, the affiliate loophole observation is critical — the CEA did not call it abuse, which supports legal structures routing yield through affiliated entities. The report does not change current law but materially weakens the policy justification for yield restrictions going forward.

SEC 2025 Annual Enforcement Review Labels Prior Crypto Crackdown a "Necessary Course Correction" — Seven Cases Dismissed Validated

⚡ What this means: The SEC has published its 2025 enforcement review and used it to formally repudiate the crypto enforcement strategy of the Gensler era. The 583-action, $8.2B year it celebrated in 2024 is now described as misapplied resources that chased registration violations rather than investor harm. For firms still under investigation from 2022–2024 actions, this is new ammunition.
"Prior resources were misapplied... the past year represents a necessary course correction" in crypto enforcement priorities.
— SEC Division of Enforcement, 2025 Annual Review, published early April 2026 [paraphrased]

The SEC's 2025 enforcement annual review, published in early April 2026, explicitly recharacterizes the Gensler-era crypto enforcement campaign as a strategic error. The review notes that seven crypto registration-related cases were dismissed in 2025 as part of the "course correction," and describes the agency's new enforcement priorities as focused on demonstrable investor harm — fraud, market manipulation, and unregistered securities offerings that caused measurable loss — rather than registration status disputes.

The about-face is notable given the same agency's 2024 annual review celebrated crypto enforcement as proof of the SEC's capacity to regulate emerging technologies. The policy shift, which accelerated after Chairman Atkins took office in early 2025, is now formally codified in the annual review, giving it precedential weight in ongoing enforcement disputes.

📎 Historical Precedent: The SEC's 2009 enforcement review following the Madoff scandal similarly reoriented from registration-focused actions toward fraud cases — a shift that lasted through the next two chairmanships.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis
For practitioners representing clients in legacy SEC investigations initiated between 2021 and 2024, the 2025 annual review is a direct resource for Wells response briefs and tolling agreement negotiations. The "investor harm" standard creates a meaningful threshold the SEC must now articulate in any new crypto enforcement action — pure registration violations without evidence of investor loss will face internal headwinds under the new framework. Atkins' SEC is signaling it will be selective; the question is whether the Division of Enforcement's institutional culture has actually shifted or is merely reflecting political guidance from the current chairmanship.
