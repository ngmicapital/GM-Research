4 ⚖️ Regulatory & Legal Radar EST

The CLARITY Act is now the single most important pending binary for crypto markets, and Senate floor scheduling is the only remaining unknown. The bill cleared the Senate Banking Committee 15-9 on May 14 — a wider margin than expected — and now sits queued for full Senate floor consideration. The legislative architecture creates a dual SEC/CFTC regulatory framework: assets functioning as securities fall under SEC jurisdiction at launch; assets that subsequently decentralise migrate to CFTC oversight. This is the market-structure clarity institutional custodians have been waiting for since the 2023 enforcement era. White House liaison has been in active contact with both chambers throughout the markup process.

📄 Active Legislation
  • CLARITY Act (H.R.3633) — Cleared Senate Banking Committee 15-9 on May 14. Senate floor vote: TBD. Creates SEC/CFTC dual-track framework. Direct positive for COIN, MARA, spot BTC ETF demand.
  • Stablecoin Yield Bill — Tillis-Alsobrooks compromise (May 1) proposes restricting stablecoin "yields" that resemble bank deposits. Key sticking point before floor vote on broader crypto bill. Banks vs. fintechs are still negotiating.
  • Bank Tokenization Guidelines — Fed/OCC joint guidance on tokenized deposit frameworks expected Q2 2026. Institutional DeFi enabler.
🎯 Today's Decision Points
  • Senate floor scheduling: Any Senate majority leader announcement of a CLARITY Act vote date is the catalyst. Monitor the official Senate floor calendar at senate.gov.
  • Stablecoin yield compromise: If Tillis-Alsobrooks amendment passes, it clears the path for the combined bill. Stalemate = delay. Resolution = catalyst.
  • CFTC jurisdiction advantage: The CLARITY Act hands the CFTC majority jurisdiction over most crypto. CFTC is historically a lighter-touch regulator than SEC enforcement-era posture — this is structurally bullish for market structure and DeFi.
  • Trump signing certainty: Administration has signalled strong support. Presidential signature probability: >60% conditional on Senate passage.
