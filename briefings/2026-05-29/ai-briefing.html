§07 — Research Radar

The dominant 2026 research theme is the safety shift from informative to agentic LLMs — as models move from answering to acting, the alignment surface changes shape; AgentAlign (arXiv:2505.23020) is the canonical reference for this transition, and the live 2026 work clusters around tool-calling under long-context (Tool-DC), multi-agent orchestration as function-calling RL, and agent privacy.

The informative → agentic safety shift

Research

The through-line across 2026 LLM research is that the role transformation from information provider to action executor introduces new security and alignment risks that don't exist for chat-only models. The canonical framing paper is AgentAlign — "Navigating Safety Alignment in the Shift from Informative to Agentic Large Language Models" (Zhang, Yin, Zhou & Hu; arXiv:2505.23020, published 29 May 2025, ACL 2025) — which improves agent safety via synthetic-data generation, reporting a 35.8% → 79.5% improvement in safety scores while maintaining helpfulness. Note: this is a year-old foundational paper, cited here as the reference frame, not as breaking research. The live 2026 work building on it clusters around: tool-calling reliability when an agent faces many candidate tools in long context (e.g. the Tool-DC divide-and-conquer "try, check, retry" framework), multi-agent orchestration formulated as function-calling reinforcement learning with system-level reasoning, temporal reasoning, and agent privacy.

⚡ So what: Two reads. (a) For the Allen paper's safety-credibility-arc framing, the informative→agentic shift is the cleanest academic articulation of why Anthropic's "say no to revenue" posture (the DoD carve-outs in §02) is a structural safety stance, not a marketing one — the risk surface genuinely changes when models act. (b) For the briefings-stack: the multi-agent-orchestration-as-function-calling-RL thread is directly relevant to the parallel-agent coordination protocol — worth a deeper read when the next coordination project spins up.
