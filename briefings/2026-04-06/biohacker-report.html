TL;DR — Driving Thesis

The dominant signal this cycle is about minimum effective doses working compoundingly. A UK Biobank SPAN study of 60,000 people quantifies it precisely: 5 extra minutes of sleep, 1.9 extra minutes of daily exercise, and a small diet quality nudge adds one full year to your life. At the same time, ACSM just updated its resistance training guidelines for the first time since 2009 — the headline is 10 sets per muscle group per week for hypertrophy — and protein science has converged on 1.2–1.6g per kg body weight daily as the new floor. On the protocol side, BPC-157 remains an evidence-free zone for humans: every claim traces back to one Croatian lab and animal models. The overall directive: optimise the boring basics aggressively before chasing exotic interventions.

01

Longevity & Biomarkers

⚡ SPAN Study: Tiny Changes, Massive Compounding — The Best Longevity Paper of the Cycle Observational

This is the finding to act on. A UK Biobank cohort study (n=~60,000, 8-year follow-up) quantified the combined impact of sleep, physical activity, and nutrition (SPAN) on lifespan and health span. The result is striking: participants hitting all three targets — 7.2+ hours of sleep, 42 minutes of daily physical activity, and a diet quality score of 58+ — lived 9.35 years longer and had a 9.46-year longer health span compared to those with the lowest habits. (Source: eClinicalMedicine / Advisory.com, April 2 2026)

🔬 In Plain English — Health Span
Health span is how long you live in good health — free from chronic disease and functional decline. It's distinct from lifespan (how long you live, period). Maximising health span means you stay sharp and capable right up until the end, rather than spending your final decade in poor health.

The compounding insight is the headline. Researchers showed that combining small improvements across multiple behaviors simultaneously reduces the required change for any single behavior substantially. You don't need to overhaul everything at once:

  • 5 extra minutes of sleep per night
  • 1.9 extra minutes of exercise per day
  • 5-point diet quality score improvement

...adds a full year of life when done together. Single-behavior equivalents are much harder (e.g., 25 extra sleep minutes or 35.5 diet points alone for the same 1-year gain).

Analysis
The study used predictive modelling rather than direct observation, so the 9.35-year figure is projected, not confirmed. [Conflicting Evidence] flag applies here: predictive models overstate precision. But the directional finding is robust — small, consistent improvements compound across all three domains simultaneously, and the required individual change is dramatically lower when you attack all three at once. For dc: a 42-minute daily activity target is achievable within a standard training session. The diet quality metric rewards variety and whole foods over any single superfood obsession.
Targeting Longevity World Congress 2026 — Watch This Space Expert Opinion

The Second World Congress on Targeting Longevity runs April 8–9 in Berlin, a joint initiative of the World Mitochondria Society (WMS) and International Society of Microbiota (ISM). The agenda focuses on four themes: mitochondrial signalling in senescent inflammation, microbiota–brain interactions in aging trajectories, metabolic environments governing tissue repair, and genomic approaches to redefining longevity strategies. (Source: EurekAlert / Targeting Longevity, March 2026)

🔬 In Plain English — Senescent Inflammation
Senescent cells are cells that have stopped dividing but refuse to die — they linger in your body and release inflammatory chemicals. Over time, this produces a low-grade chronic inflammation sometimes called "inflammaging." It's now considered one of the primary engines of age-related disease, from cardiovascular disease to neurodegeneration.

The mitochondrial signalling angle is the most actionable thread — this is the cGAS-STING pathway research area that has exploded in the past 18 months.

🔬 In Plain English — cGAS-STING Pathway
cGAS-STING is your body's built-in alarm system for detecting damaged DNA. When it fires too often — as happens progressively with age — it triggers chronic low-level inflammation that drives most age-related diseases including heart disease, neurodegeneration, and metabolic dysfunction. Researchers are currently investigating whether caloric restriction, exercise, and specific compounds can dampen overactivation of this pathway.
⚡ Implication
Hit all three SPAN targets daily — sleep 7.2h+, move 42 minutes, eat mostly whole foods. The compounding evidence is stronger than any single longevity supplement on the market.

Recovery & Sleep

Fundamentals Card #2 — The Sleep Optimisation Stack

No major sleep research broke this cycle (a daytime function paper from University of Maryland was the main development — covered below). Running Fundamentals #2: the core sleep optimisation stack for a resistance-trained male in his 20s–30s.

Stated Position
Most people spend time and money on sleep gadgets before fixing the three structural pillars: light exposure, temperature, and timing consistency. Get those right first. The supplements below are evidence-graded adjuncts — not primary fixes.

Structural pillars (non-negotiable):

  • Morning light: 10–30 minutes of sunlight within 30–60 minutes of waking. Sets the circadian clock, anchors cortisol timing, improves that night's melatonin secretion.
  • Room temperature: 17–19°C for sleep onset. Core body temperature must drop ~1°C to initiate sleep. A cold room is the most reliable way to accelerate this.
  • Fixed wake time: More important than bedtime. A consistent wake time is the master regulator of sleep architecture. If you can only control one thing, control this.
  • Blue light cutoff: Dim lights and avoid screens 60 minutes before bed. The effect is real but modest — more important if you're sensitive to light. Use Night Mode/Flux aggressively.
🔬 In Plain English — Sleep Architecture
Sleep isn't just "unconscious time." It cycles through four stages roughly every 90 minutes: NREM Stage 1 (light), NREM Stage 2 (memory consolidation), NREM Stage 3 (deep/slow-wave — physical repair and growth hormone release), and REM (emotional processing and learning). Deep sleep dominates the first half of the night; REM dominates the second. This is why cutting even 90 minutes off the end of your night costs disproportionately more REM.

Evidence-ranked supplements:

  • Magnesium glycinate — 300–400mg before bed. RCT Most replicated sleep supplement. Reduces cortisol, supports GABA, improves sleep efficiency in the magnesium-deficient (which is most people). Glycinate form preferred over oxide for absorption.
  • Apigenin — 50mg. Mechanistic Naturally occurring flavonoid (found in chamomile) that binds GABA-A receptors to promote drowsiness without dependence. Used by Huberman as part of his sleep stack. Limited RCT data in humans; mechanism is solid.
  • L-Theanine — 100–200mg. RCT Reduces sleep latency (time to fall asleep) and improves sleep quality scores without sedation. Works synergistically with magnesium. Well-tolerated.
  • Melatonin — 0.5–1mg (not 10mg). Meta Effective for circadian shifting (jet lag, shift work, delayed sleep phase). At 10mg — the common pill dose — you're flooding the system. 0.3–1mg is physiologically relevant. Higher doses do not improve sleep quality and may cause next-day grogginess.
🔬 In Plain English — GABA
GABA (gamma-aminobutyric acid) is the brain's primary "calm down" neurotransmitter. When GABA activity is high, your nervous system quiets down — this is a prerequisite for falling asleep. Both apigenin and alcohol affect GABA receptors, which is why both cause drowsiness. The difference: alcohol disrupts sleep architecture in the second half of the night; apigenin does not.
New: Daytime Function as the True Insomnia Metric Observational

University of Maryland School of Medicine (2026) published research reframing how sleep treatment efficacy should be measured: not just sleep duration or quality scores, but daytime functional impairment. Smartphone-based assessments detected improvements in daytime function more sensitively than traditional recall questionnaires — and detected treatment effects faster.

The implication: if you're waking up feeling foggy, reactive, and unmotivated — that's a more meaningful signal than a 7.8 HRV or 84% sleep score on your Whoop. Subjective morning function is underrated as a recovery input variable.

🔬 In Plain English — HRV (Heart Rate Variability)
HRV is the variation in time between your heartbeats. A higher HRV generally signals a more recovered, adaptable nervous system — your parasympathetic ("rest and digest") branch is dominant. A low HRV signals stress, incomplete recovery, or illness. Whoop and Oura use overnight HRV as their primary recovery metric. The key is your personal trend over time, not an absolute number.
⚡ Implication
Add subjective morning alertness (0–10 rating) to your daily DRC log. If it's consistently below 7, you have a sleep architecture problem that a sleep score alone won't identify.

Nutrition & Fuel

⚡ New RDA: The Official Protein Floor Just Moved Up Expert Opinion Observational

Stanford Medicine published a March 2026 explainer confirming that the new RDA for protein intake has been revised upward from 0.8g/kg body weight to 1.2–1.6g/kg body weight — a 50–100% increase. For a 80kg male: old RDA = 64g/day; new RDA = 96–128g/day. (Source: Stanford Medicine, March 2026)

🔬 In Plain English — RDA
The RDA (Recommended Dietary Allowance) is the official minimum daily intake set by government health bodies to prevent deficiency in healthy adults. It is a floor, not a target — particularly for athletes and resistance trainers, where performance-optimised protein needs are considerably higher than the RDA.

For PPL training, the evidence-based target remains 1.6–2.2g/kg (closer to 2.2g/kg in a caloric deficit to preserve muscle mass). The new RDA of 1.6g/kg essentially confirms the lower bound of what practitioners have recommended for years.

Two additional Stanford findings worth noting:

  • The anabolic window is 24 hours, not 30 minutes: The precise timing of post-workout protein is largely a myth. The window for muscle protein synthesis to be elevated after training extends roughly 24 hours. Getting enough total daily protein matters far more than hitting it within 30 minutes of leaving the gym. Expert Opinion [via Stanford's Dr. Oppezzo]
  • Older adults are the exception: For adults over 40, spreading protein across multiple meals improves muscle protein synthesis compared to front- or back-loading. This matters less for a 20–35 year old. RCT
Plant Protein Diversity Linked to Lower Hypertension Risk Observational

A MESA (Multi-Ethnic Study of Atherosclerosis) study published in the Journal of the American Heart Association found that higher plant-based protein intake — from minimally processed sources — was linked to lower hypertension risk. Participants consuming a greater variety of plant protein sources showed compounding benefits beyond any single source alone. (Source: JAHA, 2026)

🔬 In Plain English — Hypertension
Hypertension means chronically elevated blood pressure (typically defined as 130/80 mmHg or above). It is one of the largest modifiable risk factors for heart attack, stroke, and kidney disease. Even modest reductions in blood pressure significantly reduce long-term cardiovascular risk.

This doesn't mean switching to a plant-only diet. It means diversifying protein sources — adding legumes, tofu, and nuts alongside your meat and eggs — has a cardiovascular benefit beyond just hitting a protein number.

Quick Reference — High-Protein Day Template (80kg male, target 160g)
  • Breakfast: 5 eggs + 200g Greek yoghurt = ~42g protein
  • Lunch: 200g chicken breast + 1 cup edamame = ~60g protein
  • Dinner: 180g salmon + 1 cup lentils = ~48g protein
  • Snack: 30g whey protein shake or 200g cottage cheese = ~25g protein
  • Total: ~175g — 2.2g/kg for 80kg male. Plant sources (edamame, lentils) add diversity without sacrificing protein density.
⚡ Implication
If you're not already at 1.6g/kg+ per day, that is the single highest-leverage nutrition change you can make — it outperforms any supplement. Add one plant protein source per day to diversify without compromising total intake.

Protocols

Protocol Deep Dive: BPC-157 — The Evidence Reality Check

BPC-157 (Body Protection Compound-157) is the most hyped peptide in biohacking circles. The RP Strength channel published a video on April 2, 2026 assessing the peptide landscape — what works, what doesn't, and why enhanced bodybuilders aren't rushing to use peptides. [Expert Opinion via RP Strength / Dr. Mike Israetel]

The honest summary: the entire human evidence base for BPC-157 is essentially zero. Over 100 papers exist, all from preclinical animal models, nearly all from a single group of researchers at the University of Zagreb led by Dr. Predrag Sikiric. That concentration of research from one lab is itself a red flag in science — independent replication is the bedrock of credibility. (Source: STAT News, Feb 2026; University of Utah literature review)

🔬 In Plain English — Preclinical Animal Models
"Preclinical" means the research hasn't reached human trials yet. Animal model studies (usually rats or mice) test compounds in living organisms, but the leap from rodent to human is enormous — many compounds that cure disease in rats fail completely or cause harm in humans. A compound with only animal data cannot safely be assumed to work in people, regardless of how compelling the mechanism sounds.

What animal research shows (in rats):

  • Promotes angiogenesis (new blood vessel formation) [Mechanistic]
  • Reduces inflammation in wound sites [Mechanistic]
  • May enhance tendon, ligament, and muscle healing [Mechanistic]
  • Shows gastroprotective effects [Mechanistic]
🔬 In Plain English — Angiogenesis
Angiogenesis is the process of forming new blood vessels from existing ones. In wound healing, this is essential — new capillaries deliver oxygen and nutrients to repair tissue. The concern is that uncontrolled angiogenesis is also how tumours grow and spread. The long-term implications of stimulating angiogenesis in healthy humans are unknown.
⚠️ Hype Watch

Regulatory status — Australia: BPC-157 is not approved by the TGA (Therapeutic Goods Administration) for any human indication. It is classified as an unapproved therapeutic good. Importing for personal use is a grey area but obtaining it commercially or from compounding pharmacies without a prescription is not legal under Australian therapeutic goods law. Many online sources shipping to Australia are unregulated — purity and composition are unverifiable.

Regulatory status — USA: FDA Category 2 — significant safety concerns. Prohibited in compounding pharmacies since 2023. Treated as an unapproved drug.

The USADA has prohibited BPC-157 in sport (prohibited at all times — in and out of competition). If you compete in any drug-tested sport, this is a hard line.

A University of Utah medical resident conducting a formal literature review concluded the compound "should not be used by humans" without adequate safety and efficacy data. This isn't anti-biohacking conservatism — it's the honest read of the evidence stack.

Stated Position
BPC-157 sits at the intersection of genuine biological interest and almost zero human evidence. The mechanism is plausible; the animal data is compelling; the human data is absent. In Australia, it is also legally problematic to obtain without a prescription. For a healthy, training individual with no acute injury requiring dramatic intervention, the risk-reward calculus currently favours waiting for human RCT data before using it. The RP Strength video framing is correct: enhanced bodybuilders with massive hormonal profiles aren't even rushing to use peptides, which is instructive.
Creatine: The Inflammation Claim Is Not Supported Meta

A new systematic review and meta-analysis (8 RCTs) published in Frontiers in Immunology (2026) tested whether creatine supplementation reduces inflammatory biomarkers in humans. The verdict: it does not significantly reduce CRP or IL-6.

🔬 In Plain English — CRP & IL-6
CRP (C-Reactive Protein) and IL-6 (Interleukin-6) are two of the most commonly measured markers of systemic inflammation in blood tests. Elevated CRP and IL-6 are associated with chronic disease risk. Many supplement protocols claim to reduce these markers — and hence reduce inflammation. The creatine meta-analysis shows this claim is not supported in humans.

CRP (chronic): SMD = −0.11 (not significant). IL-6 (chronic): SMD = −0.06 (not significant). Some hints of benefit under intense endurance conditions, but inconsistent across populations.

What creatine IS robustly proven to do: improve strength, increase lean mass, and — from a separate 16-RCT systematic review (n=492) — significantly improve memory, attention, and processing speed. The anti-inflammation narrative is marketing. The cognitive and performance benefits are real.

⚡ Implication
Keep taking creatine monohydrate (3–5g daily). Do it for strength, hypertrophy, and cognitive function — not inflammation reduction. Don't buy creatine products marketed primarily on anti-inflammatory benefits.

Movement & Load

⚡ ACSM 2026 Resistance Training Guidelines — First Update in 17 Years Meta

The American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM) published its first updated Position Stand on resistance training for healthy adults since 2009 — synthesising findings from 137 systematic reviews representing more than 30,000 participants. This is the most comprehensive evidence review in resistance training science history. (Source: ACSM, 2026; Newswise)

Stated Position
The ACSM Position Stand confirms what evidence-based practitioners have been saying for years, while settling several debates. The headline is predictable but important: consistency trumps complexity. The specific volume recommendation (10 sets/muscle group/week for hypertrophy) is now the evidence-backed floor, not a bodybuilder preference.

Key recommendations by goal:

  • Hypertrophy: ~10 sets per muscle group per week. Higher volume is the primary driver of muscle growth.
  • Strength: 80% of 1RM, 2–3 sets per exercise. Load matters for strength; volume matters less than for hypertrophy.
  • Power: 30–70% 1RM, concentric phase performed maximally fast.
  • Minimum effective dose: 2x per muscle group per week. The biggest gain comes from 0 → any training.
🔬 In Plain English — 1RM (One-Rep Max)
Your 1RM is the maximum weight you can lift for exactly one rep with proper form. It's the benchmark for programming load — training at 80% 1RM means using a weight that's 80% of your maximum. You don't need to test this directly; most coaches use a rep-max calculator (e.g., 5 reps at a given weight estimates your 1RM).

Notably, the guidelines found inconsistent evidence for: training to failure, specific equipment types (machines vs. free weights), and complex periodisation schemes for average healthy adults. These are not required for results — they may add marginal benefit for advanced lifters, but they're not evidence-backed necessities.

Low-Load vs High-Load: Equal Hypertrophy, Different Strength Gains RCT

A 2025/2026 study published in the Journal of Applied Physiology (Journals.physiology.org) found that in trained individuals, low-load and high-load training produce similar hypertrophy outcomes but divergent strength gains — high-load training produces significantly greater strength expression. (Source: JAP, 2025)

The mechanism: strength is partly a neural skill (motor unit recruitment patterns specific to heavier loads). Hypertrophy is more load-agnostic, responding primarily to mechanical tension and metabolic stress — both achievable across a wider load range.

🔬 In Plain English — Hypertrophy vs Strength
Hypertrophy means muscle growth — the actual increase in size of muscle fibres. Strength is your force output — how much you can lift. They are related but separate: you can get bigger without getting much stronger (high volume, moderate load) or stronger without getting much bigger (heavy neural adaptation work, lower volume). PPL programming that chases both needs both load ranges.
What to Change
  • Audit weekly set count per muscle group — target 10+ sets minimum, 15–20 for a lagging muscle
  • Include at least one heavy compound movement (80%+ 1RM) per session for strength expression
  • Prioritise consistency — 3 PPL sessions beats 2 perfect sessions reliably
  • For any muscle group below your MEV (Minimum Effective Volume), add one working set per session before adding any other complexity
What NOT to Change
  • Don't abandon compound movements for isolation — the EMG data and volume efficiency still favour compounds
  • Don't chase failure on every set — evidence doesn't support superior results vs stopping 2–3 RIR
  • Don't add complexity (periodisation, advanced techniques) before nailing 10+ sets/muscle group consistently
  • Don't switch from PPL chasing Upper/Lower — both work; the best split is the one you execute
🔬 In Plain English — MEV & RIR
MEV (Minimum Effective Volume) is the lowest number of sets per week that produces measurable muscle growth for a given muscle — typically 6–8 sets for most people. RIR (Reps In Reserve) is how many more reps you could have done before failure. "2 RIR" means you stopped 2 reps before you couldn't go anymore. Training at 1–3 RIR captures most of the hypertrophy stimulus without the excessive fatigue of true failure training.
⚡ Implication
Check your current set count per lagging muscle group against the 10-set/week floor. If you're below it, add sets before changing any other variable. The guidelines just validated this as the number one lever.
