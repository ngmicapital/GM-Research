TL;DR — Driving Thesis

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and a restricted Mythos 5.2 on 9 November, and Fable 5.2 took the top score of 65 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, ahead of Gemini 4 Carbon at 60. The same week Anthropic opened its IPO roadshow and put Meetings, Sheets and Whiteboards into beta.

First issue since 6 July 2026, so the delta strip compares against that baseline rather than yesterday. Two things to act on: the Fable 5.2 pricing ($8 in, $40 out per million tokens) and the Anthropic IPO terms (825M Class A shares, indicative $70 to $78, Nasdaq ticker ANT). Three to monitor: ByteDance's Muse-style assistant, the 19 November default-on switch for Claude Data on Enterprise plans, and Shield AI's pending Echodyne close.

Model Landscape

Anthropic retook the top of the independent leaderboard this week, while Google, Meta and ByteDance pushed on specialised and agent-focused fronts.

⚡ Update: Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 arrive

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and a restricted sibling, Claude Mythos 5.2, on Monday 9 November. According to PYMNTS, Fable 5.2 scored 65 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, ahead of Gemini 4 Carbon at 60 and Anthropic's own Opus 5.5 at 59. The company says output runs up to 30% faster than Fable 5.1 and that its safety filters now block about 55% fewer legitimate requests. API pricing is listed at $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens. Mythos 5.2 is limited to vetted cybersecurity and life-sciences organisations.

DeepMind's AlphaGenome 2 targets variant effects

Google DeepMind released AlphaGenome 2, a DNA sequence model trained on more than 100 million single-cell profiles spanning 68 tissues. On the TenK10K immune-cell benchmark it identifies causal variants 30% of the time, against 16% for its predecessor. It is a research release aimed at genomics teams rather than a chat product.

Meta's Muse Ember and ByteDance's timeline

Meta added Muse Ember, which has a 1M-token context window and handles text, image, audio and video, to its execution-focused model line. It is reachable through Muse Code and the Meta Model API. Separately, ByteDance says a Muse-style assistant is weeks away, and co-founder Zhang Yiming told staff that matching OpenAI and Anthropic at the frontier is unlikely before 2029 or 2030, blaming smaller training datasets.

Claude Ecosystem

Anthropic is now pushing on three fronts at once: a product push into office suites, a public listing process, and tighter enterprise controls.

Claude Meetings, Sheets and Whiteboards enter beta

Anthropic put three workplace surfaces into beta on 10 November. Claude Meetings joins a video call as a visible participant and begins turning the discussion into tasks while people are still talking. Claude Sheets lets teams build and co-edit trackers and budgets with Claude, and Whiteboards turns a plan into a timeline-based visual workspace that fills in while a meeting runs. Claude Data moved to general availability the same day. Engadget framed the launch as a direct challenge to the office suites of Anthropic's two largest investors.

Anthropic opens its IPO roadshow

The company launched the roadshow for its proposed Nasdaq listing, ticker ANT, on 10 November. The offering covers 825 million Class A shares, of which about 801 million come from the company and the remainder from existing holders, at an expected price of $70 to $78 per share. Underwriters may also take a 30-day option on up to roughly 124 million additional shares. A headline in an AI news roundup says the amended S-1 warns Claude could soon meet an internal threshold for recursive self-improvement; the filing text itself was not checked for this issue.

Enterprise security and project tags

On 5 November Claude Enterprise added a security center, inference hooks for scanning prompts and tool calls, centralised management and broader access controls, several of them in beta. On 6 November beta project tags let admins charge spend to cost codes or client-matter numbers. On Enterprise plans Whiteboards and Sheets are off by default, and Claude Data switches on by default on 19 November.

Claude Code defaults to Fable 5.2, and an AMD port claim

Claude Code made Fable 5.2 the default Fable model in v2.1.321. Anthropic also claims its Mythos model ported Claude to AMD GPUs in three days. If that holds up, it weakens the CUDA lock-in argument, but treat it as an unverified vendor claim until a reproduction appears.

Decentralized AI

Decentralised AI projects are landing distribution deals and protocol upgrades more than headline price moves, and Bittensor has the clearest new customer story.

Bittensor's Chutes subnet picked as Tencent launch partner

Bittensor's Chutes subnet was chosen as a launch partner for Tencent's HY4 flagship model, putting the network in front of a platform with about 1.44 billion monthly users across Weixin and WeChat. Subnet fees and token buybacks are also supporting demand, according to a market write-up. Almanac, a Bittensor-based platform on SN41, reported about $109,000 in fees during October.

Protocol changes: Null Consensus and emissions control

In mid-October Bittensor added a Null Consensus mode that allows up to 2,500 subnet slots and fee-free proof-of-work entry. The v480 Etao upgrade lets subnet owners rebalance emission splits while protecting the baseline share of root stakers. Bittensor's co-founder has also conceded that the network is not decentralised in the way Bitcoin is, and the project has committed to restoring validator competition over eighteen months.

Compute marketplaces: io.net, Render, Akash

io.net aggregates GPU clusters and rents around 1,000 GPUs as a single machine. Render has widened from rendering into GPU AI workloads and now runs on Solana. Akash operates an open cloud with auction-based pricing. The thesis is that agents will provision GPUs on demand, but latency, verification and quality control remain unsolved.

TAO: about $220 after a 3% rise on the Tencent news. Secondary data point only, not confirmed on CoinGecko this run.

Open-Source Pulse

Community attention is still concentrated on Qwen and DeepSeek weights, while the newest releases lean toward quantisation and small local agents.

Qwen3.8-27B still leads Hugging Face

Qwen3.8-27B remains the most-liked and most-downloaded trending model in the November digests, at roughly 19,600 likes and 7.2 million downloads in the 11 November list. DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 sits close behind, with over 5,000 likes in the 8 November digest.

Huawei open-sources MACaQ

Huawei's Cloud and AI lab released MACaQ under Apache 2.0. The repository claims a 122-billion-parameter model can be quantised in 64 minutes on 32.5 GB of device memory, and companion GGUF builds shipped the same day. The claimed prefill and decode speedups for Qwen3.8-27B under W4A4 are 2.33x and 2.58x.

Tether's BotPsy-0.8B for on-device tool calls

Tether AI Research published BotPsy-0.8B, a sub-billion-parameter model derived from Qwen3.5 and tuned for function calling, under Apache 2.0. It scores 44.0 on BFCL v4, up from 23.4 for its base model, and beats a 2B reasoning variant on five of six agent benchmarks despite being 2.5 times smaller.

Runway Praxis-1 ships with open weights

Runway released Praxis-1, a 27B-parameter world-action model, with open weights and code under Apache 2.0 that permits commercial use. Separately, Moonshot published the Kimi K3.1 Pro weights on Hugging Face on 2 November.

Deals & Regulation

Capital is concentrating in a handful of very large AI rounds, and defence-tech consolidation produced the biggest acquisition headline of the week.

OpenAI's first tranche: $11B at a $1.25 trillion valuation

Bloomberg reported on 6 November that OpenAI had raised $11 billion so far in a round valuing it at $1.25 trillion, with SoftBank and MGX among the named backers. The figure is the first close of an open round, so the final total is still to come.

Shield AI agrees to buy Echodyne for up to $5.3B

Shield AI agreed on 5 November to buy radar maker Echodyne for up to $5.3 billion. That consists of $4.8 billion paid at closing plus up to $500 million more if performance targets are met. Shield AI is raising $2.5 billion, mostly to fund the purchase. Crunchbase ranked the raise as the largest US startup round for the week of 31 October to 6 November.

Other large rounds

Generative-media inference platform fal raised $1.3 billion at a $16 billion valuation. Thinking Machines Lab raised $5 billion, and Cohere announced a Series E aimed at raising CAD 7 billion. In China, chip startup Moffett AI raised nearly RMB 1 billion to expand specialised inference chips.

Workflow & Tools

MCP is becoming the default connector layer for creative and enterprise apps, and agent tooling is moving from demos to shipped plumbing.

Adobe opens Creative Cloud to outside agents through MCP

At Adobe MAX on 10 November, Adobe announced a Model Context Protocol endpoint that lets external agents such as ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini drive features in Photoshop, Premiere, Illustrator and InDesign. It runs locally and edits native project files, and a private beta is planned for early 2027. Adobe already had ChatGPT and Claude integrations, so the new piece reaches deeper into the apps themselves.

Webflow's MCP connector for Microsoft Copilot

Webflow released an MCP connector for Microsoft Copilot on 12 November. It lets Copilot agents build pages, manage CMS content and read performance data from Webflow Analyze and Optimize, and agent actions are logged with AI attribution. The connector is free and does not consume Webflow AI credits.

Local emulators and token-saving agent CLIs

AWS released an AWS Emulator, a local tool written largely by autonomous agents that lets developers exercise AWS APIs without an account. Vercel Labs' agent-browser CLI returns compact page text with element references for agents to act on, and its author claims five times fewer tokens, a figure not yet independently checked.

Research Radar

Two papers worth tracking this week both sit on efficiency: cheaper retrieval indexes and cheaper memory.

Token-pooled ColBERT retrieval

A Hugging Face write-up reports that a token-pooling-aware ColBERT keeps 97.8% of its unpooled NanoBEIR nDCG@10 at pool factor 4, using a quarter of the storage, against 94.6% for the ordinary model. The practical signal is that multi-vector retrieval storage can fall without a large quality penalty.

AMD's MEXT memory tiering

AMD says its MEXT technology keeps about 95% of server performance on half the DRAM by offloading cold pages to NVMe storage and using a predictive model to prefetch them. These are vendor figures, so independent benchmarks are the next thing to watch.
