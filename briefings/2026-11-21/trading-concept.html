Alpha — Liquidity Voids

Saturday, 21 November 2026 · Sydney, Australia · Issue 62 11 min read
TL;DR

A liquidity void is a stretch of price where the order book holds almost no resting size, so an aggressive order skips levels instead of trading through them. It matters because the zone it leaves behind is often revisited, and whether it fills, slices through or rejects tells you who is still in control. Builds on Orderbook Depth and Spread (Issue of 18 Nov 2026): that issue covered how a tight quote can sit on a thin book, and this one covers what happens when an order crosses it. Practical takeaway: when BTC broke $62K and then $60K on 20 November, read where the bids were thin before you read direction, and treat any return into the thin band as a test you have to prove with depth, not a shape you can trade on its own.

§01
What It Is
An absence of size, not a candle shape
A liquidity void is a price range the book left almost empty, so aggressive orders skip it rather than trade through it.

A liquidity void is a stretch of price that the order book left almost empty. When an aggressive order arrives, it does not trade through a ladder of bids and offers at every level, because the levels in the gap carry too little size to slow it. The price jumps. My position is that most chart talk about voids confuses the shape left on the chart with the thing that caused it. The shape is often a fair value gap. The cause, when it is real, is thin depth at the moment of the move.

Mechanically a void is an absence, not a pattern. It usually appears as a displacement candle with little overlap against its neighbours, but the candle alone cannot prove the book was empty. You need the depth ladder or the trade-by-trade tape to show that the prices in between had almost no resting size. Without that check you are only looking at a fast move, and a fast move with heavy volume is a different thing that the market has already accepted.

Why it matters to a Wyckoff reader: a void is evidence that one side stopped competing at those prices. Business that was never done in that range tends to remain unfinished, so price often comes back to the zone. Whether it fills, slices straight through, or rejects is the information, and that answer depends on what the tape does when price returns, not on the candle that created the gap.

§02
Mechanics
How a market order walks an empty ladder
A market order larger than the resting size at each level walks the ladder and leaves the skipped levels untraded.

The mechanic runs in four steps. First, a market order larger than the resting size at the best price takes out that level. Second, the next level holds less size than what remains of the order, so it is taken too. Third, if the levels beyond are also thin, price keeps moving until it reaches resting size big enough to absorb the rest. Fourth, the range that was crossed is left with little or no trade on it, and that unfinished range is the void.

Void span = P_end minus P_start, where the cumulative resting size between P_start and P_end is below the aggressive order size
Read this from the depth ladder, not from the candle. A two-tick span on a deep book means nothing. The same span on a thin book is a void.

Thin depth has two sources, and they look the same until the first order lands. One is the session itself, where overnight or news-thin trading leaves the ladder sparse. The other is market makers pulling quotes ahead of an event, which makes a normal-looking book suddenly empty. In both cases the chart shows a fast move and the book shows nothing special until it is hit.

TraitLiquidity void
CauseThin resting size at the levels that were crossed
EvidenceDepth ladder or trade-by-trade tape, not the candle alone
Chart lookDisplacement candle with little overlap against its neighbours
Typical follow-throughReturn into the zone, then fill, slice through, or reject

The useful question is not whether a gap exists but whether the market could have traded there at all. If you cannot answer that from depth, you have a fast move with an unknown cause, and you should size it as one.

§04
In Practice
Four checks before you call a void
Prove the absence in the depth or the tape before you call a candle a void, and treat any revisit as a test.
1

Prove the absence first

Pull the depth at the levels the candle crossed, not the chart alone. If the book shows normal size there, the move was a fast one and not a void.

2

Check for pulled quotes

Ask whether the thin book is a session effect or a quote pull ahead of news. A pull can look like a void for a few seconds, so re-check the ladder after the first prints.

3

Expect a return, not a guarantee

Voids attract price, but they can also be slid through on the next pass. Wait for the reaction on return before you commit size to the zone.

4

Size to the stop, not the story

Place the stop beyond the far edge of the void, since a failed return is the cleanest signal. Keep the risk small enough that a wrong call on the return costs a fraction of the expected move.

Illustrative return-to-void short (not a recommendation)
SetupFailed return up into the 61.5K to 60.5K band after the break
Entry (short, at the band midpoint)61,000
Stop (above the far edge of the void)61,600
Target (last tape low zone)58,700
Risk and reward600 risked, 2,300 targeted, roughly 3.8R

The trade only exists if the return fails on thin volume. If it prints a full bar through the band on heavy size, the setup is void and you are out at the stop.

Timeframe fit: 15m to 1h to read the void and the return, with 4h for bias. The void is an intraday construct, so do not carry it on a daily chart without the lower timeframe confirming it.

§07
Common Traps
Shape mistaken for evidence
The biggest mistake is calling a fair value gap or a fast candle a void with no depth evidence behind the label.
1
Calling every gap a void

A fair value gap formed on heavy volume is an accepted price move, not an empty book. Only thin participation turns the skipped range into a void, so check volume and depth before you attach the label.

2
Trusting a candle without the ladder

One displacement candle cannot prove the book was empty. Pull the depth or the trade-by-trade tape at those prices, because a candle that looks thin can sit on a deep book.

3
Mistaking a pulled quote for a void

Quote pulls before news make a normal ladder look empty for a few seconds. Re-check the book after the first prints, since a void that disappears in seconds was never a stable zone to trade.

4
Fading the first touch

Voids are often slid through on the next pass, so a first touch into the zone is not confirmation. Wait for the reaction and let the failed return, not the arrival, set the trigger.

!
Overhyped: the chart-only void

Most retail void indicators flag a candle shape and draw a box around it. Without depth data the box is a shape, not a measurement. Use the tool as a prompt to go and look at the book, and never as a signal in its own right.
