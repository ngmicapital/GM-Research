Judge Refuses to Block New York From Policing Kalshi, Leaving CFTC Preemption Unproven

“not established a likelihood of success on the merits” Judge Lorna Schofield, US District Court (SDNY), as quoted by Reuters — 20 Nov 2026

On Friday 20 November, Judge Lorna Schofield in Manhattan declined the CFTC’s request to temporarily stop New York Attorney General Letitia James from pursuing an enforcement case against Kalshi. She held that the CFTC had not shown it was likely to win its argument that federal law preempts state gambling and gaming-licence rules for prediction markets.

The CFTC had argued that a New York halt would force Kalshi to cease all operations, because the company is headquartered in the state. CoinDesk reports the judge also sent the case back to the New York Supreme Court, which leaves the state proceeding moving on its own timetable.

Schofield counted three federal appeals courts holding that states may regulate Kalshi and one, from the Third Circuit, holding that New Jersey could not. That tally follows the Fourth Circuit decision of 17 November, and it leaves the Supreme Court as the only forum that can settle the question.

📎 Historical Precedent: The Third Circuit’s April 2026 ruling for Kalshi against New Jersey remains the only federal appellate win in the current series, which is why the New Jersey cert petition carries weight.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

The order confirms that the CFTC cannot count on preliminary relief to stop state enforcement while the preemption question is litigated. Enforcement against Kalshi will therefore run in parallel with the CFTC’s own suits against twelve states, as Reuters reports. The Brief’s read: the practical winners are state gaming regulators, who gain leverage in negotiations even before any merits ruling.

🔄 Contrarian Take

Judge DeAndrea Benjamin, dissenting in the Fourth Circuit, argued that Kalshi’s sports contracts are swaps that fall exclusively within CFTC oversight, as Insurance Journal reports. The Brief’s read: a dissent is not a holding, and the CFTC’s strongest point is the operational one: a New York halt would end the business, which is the harm the court must weigh at a later stage.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Prediction News notes that Robinhood is especially exposed through its Kalshi contract volume, so state enforcement reaches the distribution channel as well as the venue. The Brief’s read: any New York restriction hits retail brokers that route event-contract flow, not only Kalshi itself.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟩 Bullish CME (CME) — a federally regulated derivatives venue gains relative positioning if swap-jurisdiction arguments keep event-style products on CFTC-registered markets. The Brief’s read

🔴 Bearish HOOD (Robinhood) — exposed to state enforcement through Kalshi contract volume.

⚪ Neutral COIN (Coinbase) — no direct exposure in this order; its event-contract plans remain a separate question.

Update Hong Kong Introduces Bill Bringing Crypto Brokers, Advisers and Custodians Under SFC Licensing

“[paraphrased] Brokers, advisers, portfolio managers and custody providers would need an SFC licence or registration.” Virtual Asset Services Legislation (Amendment) Bill 2026, as reported by Coin Turk — 20 Nov 2026

The Hong Kong government published the Virtual Asset Services Legislation (Amendment) Bill 2026 on 20 November, turning the 17 November announcement into a bill with a fixed reading date. The new text would require licensing or registration with the Securities and Futures Commission well beyond exchanges, reaching brokers, advisers, portfolio managers and custody providers.

The amendment extends the AML/CTF Ordinance (Cap. 615) to four new categories of activity: dealing, advising, managing and safeguarding digital assets. First reading in the Legislative Council is set for 2 December. Banks and stored value facilities planning crypto services must also account for the proposed SFC registration duties.

📎 Historical Precedent: Hong Kong’s VATP licensing regime, which since 2023 has covered trading platforms, is the direct analog; this bill extends the same perimeter logic to intermediaries.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Counsel should map advisory, portfolio management and custody arrangements against the four new categories now, because these were previously outside the exchange-only licensing perimeter. The Brief’s read: the commercially significant change is the reach to advisers and custodians, which catches OTC desks and fund managers that have relied on exchange-only analysis.

🔄 Contrarian Take

The industry will argue that overlapping perimeters for the SFC and banking supervisors create dual-licensing cost without added investor protection. The Brief’s read: the argument is provisional until the bill text is reviewed clause by clause, which this brief has not yet done.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Licensed Hong Kong venues and custodians gain a moat, while unlicensed advisers and OTC desks face registration cost. The Brief’s read: HKEX (0388.HK) is positioned to benefit as the operator of the regulated market, and HSBC (0005.HK) must weigh registration before offering crypto services.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟩 Bullish 0388.HK (HKEX) — operator of the licensed market that the perimeter expansion reinforces.

🔴 Bearish Unlicensed Hong Kong-facing advisory and OTC desks (no listed proxy) — face licensing cost or exit.

⚪ Neutral 0005.HK (HSBC) — registration duties apply if it expands crypto services, but no immediate change.

ComplianceFlag any Hong Kong client service that involves advice or custody for licensing review.

Update Hill and Moreno Say Lame-Duck Odds for the CLARITY Act Are Close to Zero

“Time kills all deals,” Rep. French Hill, as reported by The Block — 19 Nov 2026

On 19 November, Rep. French Hill and Sen. Bernie Moreno both said Congress missed its window to pass the CLARITY Act while the momentum from the GENIUS Act was strong. Hill argued the House should have moved the two bills together, and Moreno said the bill grew from about 300 pages to about 600.

Asked what was realistically achievable over the coming weeks, Moreno answered “Zero. Zero.” Hill was slightly more hopeful, saying lawmakers never give up hope in a lame duck, though he said the chances were not high. The two disagree on how much agency rulemaking can achieve; Moreno told The Block that rigorous SEC and CFTC rulemaking could deliver roughly 80% of a framework while still favouring passage of the bill.

The procedural position is unchanged: the Senate failed cloture 49–50 on 15 September, and a motion to reconsider only re-runs the same vote. No CLARITY vote appears on the announced Senate schedule.

📎 Historical Precedent: The GENIUS Act passed on its own track while CLARITY stalled, and the 2026 cloture failure mirrors the pattern in which a bill that cannot reach 60 votes is left for the next Congress.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Forbes’ 11 November analysis argues that the Democratic takeover of both chambers in January lowers the incentive to negotiate a bipartisan deal in the lame duck. The Brief’s read: the realistic route for 2027 is SEC and CFTC rulemaking and staff guidance, which become the operative rules of the road for crypto firms.

🔄 Contrarian Take

Forbes notes that a funding deadline or defence bill could still carry the bill as an attachment, so the lame duck is not closed. The Brief’s read: an attachment still needs 60 Senate votes for cloture, and no Democrat has yet backed the motion to reconsider, so the attachment route is theoretical.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Coinbase has argued that bipartisan engagement remains the route to the bill, as Benzinga reports. The Brief’s read: firms that depend on statutory market-structure clarity, including Coinbase (COIN), face a longer runway, while issuers operating under the GENIUS framework are less exposed to the stall.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟩 Bullish CME (CME) — agency-led rules that keep crypto derivatives onshore favour listed federal venues.

🔴 Bearish COIN (Coinbase) — delayed statutory clarity extends the period of enforcement and rulemaking risk.

⚪ Neutral CRCL (Circle) — stablecoin issuance rests on GENIUS rather than CLARITY, so the stall has limited direct effect.

Democratic Gains Put Crypto Lobbying Under Scrutiny as Coinbase Defends Fairshake Spending

“[paraphrased] Coinbase has worked with Democrats and Republicans since 2015, regardless of which party controlled Washington.” Coinbase executive Shirzad, as reported by Benzinga — 20 Nov 2026

Benzinga reports that Democrats are expected to gain greater influence over financial regulation after the midterms, with Sen. Elizabeth Warren positioned to examine crypto companies’ ties to President Trump. Democratic critics have also focused on Fairshake’s reported $150M midterm spending, which the group says backed candidates of both parties.

Coinbase responded that its bipartisan approach predates the current administration and that the progress on GENIUS and CLARITY depended on cooperation across parties. Ripple’s chief legal officer, Stuart Alderoty, told The Block he does not expect Warren to set US crypto policy even if she chairs the Senate Banking Committee, and expects the SEC, CFTC and OCC to take a larger role.

📎 Historical Precedent: The GENIUS Act passed with bipartisan support in 2025, and both Hill and Moreno cite that momentum as the route the crypto industry failed to use for CLARITY.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

A Democratic-led Senate committee is more likely to pursue oversight and document requests than statute, which means political spending records and Trump-linked ventures are the likely first subjects of scrutiny. The Brief’s read: lobbying and political-spend disclosures should be reviewed now, before requests arrive.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Coinbase (COIN) carries the reputational exposure of the Fairshake spending, while firms with active CFTC and SEC dockets gain a relative advantage if agencies, not Congress, set the rules. The Brief’s read: agency-first strategies favour firms with direct regulatory engagement over those relying on a legislative win.

Ukraine’s Central Bank and Securities Regulator Agree Oversight Split for Draft Crypto Law

“leaving this market unregulated is a real risk.” Vasyl Orieshkin, virtual assets regulation lead, Ukraine Countering Illicit Finance project — as quoted by TokenPost, 20 Nov 2026

Ukraine’s central bank and its securities regulator have agreed amendments to the draft crypto law that divide oversight between them. The National Securities and Stock Market Commission would license digital-asset businesses, while the National Bank of Ukraine would monitor effects on the banking system, the hryvnia and transfers between currency and crypto.

The bill passed its first reading in September 2025 and goes to a parliamentary committee in December. It would align Ukraine’s rules with the EU’s MiCA framework and meet commitments to the IMF, and it would not recognise crypto as money or permit its use as a means of payment. The Brief’s read: the December committee date is the next test, and the licensing-authority assignment determines which regulator firms must apply to.

📎 Historical Precedent: MiCA itself placed authorisation with a single national competent authority per member state, the model this draft law is copying for Ukraine.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Counsel advising firms with Ukrainian exposure should track the December committee for the final assignment of licensing authority, since that decides the application route. The Brief’s read: MiCA alignment makes Ukraine a plausible future passporting market, but only once the licensing regulator is fixed.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Private Ukrainian exchanges and fintechs face licensing cost and central-bank monitoring of hryvnia-linked products. The Brief’s read: no listed proxy is identified in the reporting, so the direct beneficiaries are private firms positioned early for a MiCA-aligned licence.
