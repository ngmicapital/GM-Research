Model Landscape

OpenAI is competing on price per job rather than raw capability, and the independent scoreboards are not yet convinced.

GPT-6.2 Astra undercuts its predecessor, with a catch

OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Astra lists at $8 input and $40 output per million tokens, which is 20% under GPT-6 Astra. The cheaper Sol tier keeps its $2/$10 pricing. Artificial Analysis ranks Astra second, behind Claude Fable 5.2, and finds Sol roughly matches September's top model at about 15% of the cost per task. Both models still gave wrong answers to more than half of that benchmark's factual questions, so the cost story is stronger than the accuracy story.

Gemini 4 Neon: a fourth Gemini 4 model in about seven weeks

Google's Gemini 4 Neon is priced at $3/$15 per million tokens and reaches Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers, Gemini for business, and the paid API. Trackers disagree on the release date, with one dating it to 18 November and another pointing to the 20th, so treat the exact day as unconfirmed. The cadence is the notable part: Google has now shipped four Gemini 4 variants in a short window.

Chinese labs keep pushing open-weight scale

Alibaba's Qwen4-Max arrived on 16 November with a 1M-token context and $1.50/$4.50 pricing, and Tencent's Hy4 is a 770B-total, 49B-active mixture-of-experts model that adds image understanding and computer use. For builders, the pattern is clear: frontier-adjacent capability is now available at a fraction of US list prices.

Claude Ecosystem

The Claude story this week is the IPO, so the useful new angle is the criticism and disclosure around it rather than another price move.

Follow-up: a safety critique lands as the listing approaches

Follow-up to the IPO coverage. More than 100 researchers, founders and scholars signed the so-called MisAnthropic statement, which argues that Anthropic's push toward AI that accelerates its own development conflicts with the company's stated safety message. The signatories are making a reputational argument rather than a legal one, and it will be tested as the stock trades.

Disclosure: the filing runs past 90 pages of risk factors

The filing for the offering carries more than 90 pages of risk factors. For readers tracking Claude's commercial trajectory, that section is the best single place to see what management says could go wrong, from model dependence to competition and compute supply.

Model line-up context

Claude Fable 5.2 went into API availability on 9 November at $8/$40 per million tokens, the same list price as GPT-6.2 Astra. Artificial Analysis places Fable 5.2 first in its ranking cited this week, so Anthropic holds the top spot on that evaluation even as the company's market debut draws the headlines.

Decentralized AI

Decentralized compute projects are shifting from token narratives toward products and contracts, with the pricing story now a secondary signal.

Akash (AKT)
Plans a fixed-price GPU forward market on Solana, with a limited pilot targeted for Q1 2027.
Bittensor (TAO)
Subnet Lium reported $2.70M in GPU rentals from 2,394 billed customers between 10 October and 9 November. TAO was about $190.63 on 17 November, down roughly 14% over seven days.
Render (RENDER)
Network passed 82 million rendered frames by September, with output up 23% in August. Forth integration proposal RNP-024 went to a final vote on 3 November.

Akash plans to sell reserved GPU time forward

Akash has outlined a marketplace on Solana where AI teams could reserve future GPU hours at fixed prices. This is a more concrete product than a token event, because it turns spot-market volatility into something enterprises can budget against. The proposal rides on the broader chain migration, which the network is pursuing under AEP-79 with a completion target of late 2026.

Bittensor subnet revenue meets a weak token

Lium's $2.70M in rentals over 31 days annualises to about $34.6M if the pace holds, though that figure is an extrapolation rather than reported revenue. Demand from paying customers is the point to watch: the same weeks saw TAO test support near $185 after falling from around $285 in mid October.

Render ships more usage and a gateway proposal

Render's frame count and its RNP-024 proposal to bring Forth in as a private gateway for AI models point to usage growth. A mid-November analysis still put RENDER near $1.56, far below its all-time high, so usage and price have decoupled.

Open-Source Pulse

This run did not verify fresh GitHub trending or Hugging Face release data, so the open-source picture below rests on model-tracker entries rather than repository activity.

Mistral's open-weight flagship is still the reference point

Mistral Large 4, released on 29 October, remains Mistral's largest public model, and its licensing sits under the company's open-weight ecosystem terms. Mistral's broader 2026 moves, including a Samsung stake and the Koyeb acquisition, show a company building around open-weight distribution.

Open-weight training platforms are being bought

The clearest open-source business signal this week is Palo Alto Networks reaching for Applied Compute, a post-training and inference platform for open-weight models. That is a sign that enterprises are being sold on running open models inside controlled environments, not only on downloading weights.

What to verify next

Check GitHub trending and new Hugging Face uploads from Chinese labs before the next issue. Several of this week's model launches are not clearly open-weight, and the open versus closed split is worth confirming model by model.

Deals & Regulation

Funding is concentrating into a few very large rounds and strategic acquisitions, led by DeepSeek's $15B raise and Palo Alto's $4.5B purchase.

DeepSeek closes a second external round of about $15B

DeepSeek completed a second external round of about 100 billion yuan, roughly $15 billion, which accounted for more than 99% of the $15.07B that trackers logged across six AI rounds on 20 November. The round drew 31 new investors, many tied to state-owned capital, and it values the company at 2.5 times the $2 billion set in its first round in August. Tencent and CATL are reported among investors, though the lead is not disclosed.

Palo Alto Networks to buy Applied Compute for $4.5B

Palo Alto Networks said on 18 November it will acquire Applied Compute in an all-stock deal valued at $4.5B. The price arrived about 16 days after Applied Compute raised $350M at a $3.25B valuation in a round led by Elad Gil, with NVIDIA among participants, and after the startup reported $100M in annualised revenue run rate. Palo Alto framed the buy as a way to serve enterprises running mixed open and closed models.

Other rounds: Genesis AI and fal

Genesis AI raised $500M in Series A financing at a $3.5B valuation, announced 17 November, with NVentures and AMD Ventures among backers. fal announced a $1.3B Series F led by Bessemer on 3 November. Reported figures for OpenAI's November round vary by tracker, with one citing an $11B first close at a $1.25T valuation, so treat that number as provisional.

Workflow & Tools

Tooling news is thin this run, but the most practical item is Google's Gemini 4 Neon adding agents that can book appointments and check out with payment.

Gemini agents can call businesses and check out

Google's example shows Gemini calling businesses to book appointments, request quotes or check stock, then completing checkout with Google Pay once the user confirms. In Google's own demo the assistant disclosed that it was an AI at the start of the call. For operators, the practical step is to decide how staff will handle AI-originated calls and to test the flow against your own phone line.

Background: a coding tool built on an open model

Cursor's Composer 2, released in March, was built on top of Moonshot AI's open-weight Kimi 2.5. Cursor's parent was acquired by SpaceX, with that deal closing on 14 August, so the tool now sits inside a larger company. The lesson for builders is that open-weight bases are already powering commercial coding products.

Enterprise open-model tooling gets a security owner

Palo Alto's pitch is that enterprises need a secure route to run open-weight models next to closed ones. Expect security teams to start evaluating open-model stacks through the same vendor reviews they apply to other infrastructure.

Research Radar

No new paper cleared verification in this run, so the most useful research signal is a benchmark result on factual reliability.

Factual reliability is still the weak spot

Artificial Analysis found that GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol both gave wrong answers to more than half of its factual questions, even though Sol matches September's top model at a fraction of the per-task cost. The result suggests that cost gains are outpacing accuracy gains on fact-heavy tasks. Track the next release of its factual benchmark to see whether the gap is closing or just being re-measured.
