UPDATE: CLARITY Act — Senate Back From Recess, April 25 Markup Window Expires in Three Days With No Date Set

⚡ What this means: The Digital Asset Market Clarity Act would permanently assign regulatory authority over crypto between the SEC and CFTC, codify commodity status for 16 major tokens including XRP, and resolve stablecoin yield rules. The Senate Banking Committee must schedule a "markup" (a formal drafting session) by April 25 — or the bill effectively dies until 2030, when the legislative calendar reopens after midterm elections. As of this morning, no date has been announced.

"America needs Clarity."

— Senator Cynthia Lummis (R-WY), on X, April 11, 2026. Post reached 168,000 impressions within hours.

The Digital Asset Market Clarity Act (H.R. 3633) cleared the House with a historic 294–134 bipartisan margin in July 2025 — the largest congressional majority ever on a crypto bill. The Senate Agriculture Committee passed its version in January 2026. The sole remaining gatekeeper is Senator Tim Scott (R-SC), chair of the Senate Banking Committee, who has not announced a markup date despite the Senate returning from Easter recess on April 13.

Three unresolved sticking points are holding Scott back: (1) the Tillis-Alsobrooks yield compromise that bans passive yield on stablecoins — opposed by Coinbase, whose USDC yield product generates hundreds of millions in revenue; (2) DeFi regulatory language that Senate Democrats want tightened; and (3) ethics provisions restricting government officials' crypto holdings. Senator Hagerty expressed confidence the bill could clear committee before month's end; Senator Moreno warned that failure to advance before May could shelve it until after the 2026 midterms.

Adding friction: White House crypto czar David Sacks concluded his 130-day term on March 26, 2026 with no replacement named, leaving the West Wing without a dedicated coordinator to break deadlocks between Senate factions. Treasury Secretary Bessent has called the legislation [paraphrased] "the foundational cornerstone for crypto regulation" and framed its failure as a national security concern — but executive-branch lobbying cannot substitute for a committee chairman willing to move.

📎 Historical Precedent: The Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (1999) stalled in Senate Banking Committee for months after House passage before a floor deadline focused minds — a bipartisan deal collapsed and was reconstructed twice before final passage.

⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

If Scott misses April 25, the CLARITY Act enters legislative limbo — the midterm election cycle begins in earnest by June and floor time becomes scarce. Polymarket traders currently price 2026 passage at 72%, but that probability collapses sharply if markup doesn't happen this week. The stablecoin yield fight is the real bottleneck: the GENIUS Act (enacted 2025) created a baseline stablecoin regime, and the CLARITY Act's yield provisions would either entrench or unwind the yield-offering economics that Circle, Coinbase, and Ripple have built. Under 12 U.S.C. § 1811 et seq. (GENIUS Act), yield is currently permitted by ambiguity — CLARITY would resolve that ambiguity in either direction.

🔄 Contrarian Take

The April 25 "deadline" has been stated before and slipped without consequence — the bill is alive as long as Scott keeps it on his agenda. Some observers argue that missing this window actually improves the bill's chances by forcing a post-recess compromise with more Democratic buy-in, producing a stronger final product that can command 60 Senate floor votes. A rushed markup risks a party-line vote and a floor defeat.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Coinbase (COIN) stands to lose the most from the yield ban provision — its USDC reward product is a core retail acquisition tool. Ripple and Circle benefit asymmetrically: their OCC conditional charter approvals are cleaner with GENIUS Act implementation in place regardless of CLARITY's fate. DeFi protocols (Uniswap, Aave) need the jurisdictional clarity between SEC and CFTC that only CLARITY provides — without it, both agencies can credibly claim oversight. Binance US, operating under its ongoing monitoring requirements, watches passively — a lighter US regulatory touch could allow re-entry to the retail market.

📊 Portfolio Impact

Bullish [XRP, COIN, on-chain DEX governance tokens]: Passage codifies commodity classification and resolves regulatory overhang that has suppressed institutional allocation.

Bearish [USDC yield products, fintech stablecoin platforms offering passive yield]: If yield ban survives the markup, Coinbase's USDC reward program faces existential restructuring.

Neutral [BTC, ETH]: Both assets are already classified under the March 17 SEC–CFTC joint interpretation; CLARITY confirms rather than changes their status.

White House Economists Quantify the Yield Ban: $2.1B Lending Gain Versus $800M Net Welfare Loss — Banks Rebut the Math

⚡ What this means: The GENIUS Act's yield prohibition — the rule that prevents stablecoin issuers from paying interest to holders — is supposed to protect bank deposits from draining into crypto. The White House's own economists now say that protection is worth almost nothing: banning yield would push only $2.1B in additional lending to banks while costing consumers $800M in forgone returns. This report is being used in real-time Senate negotiations on the CLARITY Act.

"In short, a yield prohibition would do very little to protect bank lending, while forgoing the consumer benefits of competitive returns on stablecoin holdings."

— White House Council of Economic Advisers, "Effects of Stablecoin Yield Prohibition on Bank Lending," April 8, 2026

The White House Council of Economic Advisers released a 21-page quantitative analysis on April 8, 2026, finding that prohibiting stablecoin yield under the GENIUS Act would increase bank lending by only $2.1 billion — a 0.02% improvement in total US lending — while costing consumers an estimated $800 million in foregone returns. The model used Federal Reserve and FDIC deposit, lending, and liquidity data, plus academic estimates of consumer asset-switching behaviour. The cost-benefit ratio came out at 6.6: costs outweigh benefits by a factor of 6.6-to-1.

The American Bankers Association pushed back on April 13, arguing the CEA modelled the wrong scenario and that deposit flight risk is non-linear — small outflows can destabilise community banks disproportionately. The ABA's critique was procedural: the CEA analysis assumes rational, slow-moving consumer behaviour, while bank runs are panic-driven. The ABA did not dispute the $2.1B lending figure directly.

📎 Historical Precedent: The 1999 CEA analysis of the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act's interstate banking deregulation similarly found consumer welfare gains exceeded protective costs — used successfully to rebut bank lobby opposition.

⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

This report is designed to give Senate waverers political cover to vote against the yield ban — specifically the Tillis-Alsobrooks compromise that bans passive yield on stablecoins. The White House releasing an economic brief that directly undercuts the banking lobby's core argument is unusually aggressive executive-branch involvement in legislative drafting. The CEA's finding that "even worst-case assumptions produce only $531 billion in additional lending" — framed as the absolute ceiling — is a deliberate reframing of the debate. Counsel advising fintech clients on the yield question should expect the CEA report to appear in markup testimony; be ready to respond to ABA counter-briefs on non-linear systemic risk.

🔄 Contrarian Take

Economic models that show small aggregate effects can mask distributional problems the model doesn't capture. The ABA's argument that community banks face disproportionate systemic risk from even modest deposit outflows has some empirical support in regional bank failure literature. If the CEA model is wrong — and it uses acknowledged simplifying assumptions — the Senate yield-ban debate could reverse rapidly if a community bank stress event occurs in the interim.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Coinbase's USDC yield product (currently ~4% APY) is the direct commercial target of the yield ban — the CEA report strengthens Coinbase's lobby position significantly. Circle benefits either way: it issues USDC but earns revenue on reserves, not on yield passthrough. Ripple's RLUSD product was designed without yield mechanics, so RLUSD's competitive position improves if Coinbase's yield offering is banned. Fintech platforms like Paxos and BVNK operating cross-border stablecoin settlement pipelines face no direct yield-ban exposure.

📊 Portfolio Impact

Bullish [COIN, yield-bearing stablecoin platforms]: CEA report weakens the primary legislative rationale for yield prohibition; probability of ban drops.

Bearish [Regional US bank equities]: If yield is permitted and stablecoins scale, deposit migration risk is real even if the CEA minimises it.

Neutral [Tether/USDT]: GENIUS Act applies to US-regulated stablecoins; Tether remains offshore and unaffected by yield ban mechanics.

📋 What To Do

Trader Track whether the Tillis-Alsobrooks yield-ban text survives markup — if yield is permitted in the final bill, COIN is a strong near-term long on the regulatory catalyst.
Counsel Cite the CEA report in any client comment letters on the FDIC GENIUS Act rulemaking — it establishes White House economic policy supporting yield permissiveness.

High FDIC.gov [PRIMARY] Prudential Regulation / Stablecoin
🟢 High confidence — FDIC Board-approved NPR, Federal Register published April 10, 2026

FDIC Opens 60-Day Comment Window on GENIUS Act Prudential Standards — Reserve, Capital, and Redemption Rules for Stablecoin Issuers

⚡ What this means: The FDIC is now building the detailed rulebook for how banks can issue or supervise payment stablecoins under the GENIUS Act. This second GENIUS rulemaking covers the practical mechanics: what reserves must be held, how fast redemptions must be processed, and whether stablecoin reserves get FDIC deposit insurance. The 60-day comment period runs until approximately June 8 — any firm planning to issue or custody stablecoins through an FDIC-supervised institution needs to respond.

"The proposed rule would generally require PPSIs to redeem a payment stablecoin within two business days... deposits held as reserves backing a payment stablecoin would not be insured to payment stablecoin holders on a pass-through basis."

— FDIC Notice of Proposed Rulemaking, April 7, 2026 [paraphrased from regulatory text]

The FDIC Board of Directors approved this second GENIUS Act NPR on April 7, 2026, published in the Federal Register on April 10. The proposed rule targets FDIC-supervised Permitted Payment Stablecoin Issuers (PPSIs) and Insured Depository Institutions (IDIs) that engage in stablecoin-related activities. Key provisions: two-business-day redemption requirement; full reserve asset segregation; capital and risk management standards; and a clarification that stablecoin reserve deposits at IDIs are not FDIC-insured on a pass-through basis to holders — a significant consumer protection disclosure requirement.

A companion FinCEN/OFAC rulemaking published the same day establishes AML/CFT and OFAC sanctions compliance obligations for PPSIs, treating stablecoin issuers as financial institutions under the Bank Secrecy Act. Comments on both rules are due ~June 8, 2026. This is the FDIC's second rulemaking; the first (December 2025) established application procedures for IDIs seeking stablecoin issuance approval.

📎 Historical Precedent: The OCC's 2020–2021 stablecoin guidance (Interpretive Letter 1179) was the prior attempt to give banks a pathway to stablecoin issuance — the FDIC NPR goes further by setting legally binding prudential standards rather than interpretive guidance.

⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

The two-business-day redemption requirement is shorter than most DeFi settlement cycles and will structurally limit which reserve asset types are permitted — overnight repos and T-bills qualify; longer-duration instruments do not. The no-pass-through insurance clarification is consequential: stablecoin holders cannot claim FDIC insurance on backing reserves, which distinguishes payment stablecoins from tokenised deposits (where deposit insurance passes through). Firms planning to operate as PPSIs should model liquidity stress under this redemption window now; the comment period ends before the CLARITY Act vote, so comment letters can address both rulemakings simultaneously.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Circle and Paxos, both holding state money transmitter licences, face the most immediate application burden — they must decide whether to pursue FDIC-supervised PPSI status or continue under state frameworks. Coinbase National Trust Company (conditional OCC approval received April 2) will need to align its custody operations with the IDI custodial standards in this NPR. JPMorgan Chase and Bank of New York Mellon, already exploring tokenised deposits, benefit from the deposit-vs-stablecoin clarification which protects their tokenised deposit products from being swept into the PPSI regime.

📊 Portfolio Impact

Bullish [established stablecoin issuers with banking relationships — Circle, Paxos]: Clear prudential framework reduces regulatory risk premium and supports institutional adoption at scale.

Bearish [offshore stablecoin issuers, Tether competitors]: US banks will preference PPSI-regulated counterparts, compressing offshore issuers' US distribution.

Neutral [BTC/ETH]: No direct impact on commodity crypto assets.

📋 What To Do

Trader No immediate price catalyst — this is a compliance-layer development; watch for comment period headlines that could signal FDIC hardening or softening the redemption timeline.
Counsel File comment letters by June 8 addressing the pass-through insurance exclusion and the two-business-day redemption window — both will reshape product structuring for any client in stablecoin custody or issuance.
Compliance Map your firm's stablecoin activity against the PPSI definition now — entities operating as de facto stablecoin custodians for IDIs may be swept in unexpectedly.
Founder If your reserve architecture uses money market funds or T-bills, confirm settlement timelines are compatible with the two-business-day redemption requirement before finalising product design.

High SEC.gov / Sidley Austin [PRIMARY/SECONDARY] DeFi / Broker-Dealer Registration
🟢 High confidence — SEC Staff statement effective immediately, April 13, 2026; confirmed via primary source

SEC Staff Grants Five-Year No-Action Relief for Non-Custodial DeFi Front Ends — Peirce Calls for Permanence

⚡ What this means: "Broker-dealer" registration under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 normally requires any entity that routes securities transactions to register with FINRA and the SEC — a process costing millions and carrying ongoing compliance burdens. The SEC's Division of Trading and Markets has now said it won't pursue broker-dealer registration cases against DeFi front-end interfaces (websites/apps that connect users to decentralised protocols) — provided those interfaces are non-custodial (they never hold user funds) and non-discretionary (they don't decide how or where trades are routed on the user's behalf). This is a no-action letter, not a rule — it has no legal force and expires in five years.

"The staff Statement is an interim step, which I hope will be replaced by a more permanent regulatory approach."

— Commissioner Hester Peirce, separate statement, April 13, 2026

On April 13, 2026, the SEC's Division of Trading and Markets issued a staff statement carving out non-custodial, non-discretionary DeFi user interfaces from broker-dealer registration under Section 15(a) of the Exchange Act. The "Covered User Interface Provider" category covers front ends that: route transactions solely through self-custodial wallets; charge fees consistently without payment for order flow; do not solicit or recommend specific trades; and maintain written cybersecurity and fraud-prevention policies. The statement takes effect immediately and expires in five years absent Commission action.

Key conditions: interfaces must disclose their non-registered status prominently, disclose transaction routing parameters and MEV risks, and assess trading venues against objective criteria including liquidity, neutrality, and auditability. Payment for order flow — a major source of CEX revenue — is explicitly prohibited for covered interfaces. Commissioner Peirce issued a concurring statement supporting the relief but calling on the Commission to formalise it through notice-and-comment rulemaking rather than leaving DeFi developers reliant on temporary staff guidance.

📎 Historical Precedent: The SEC's 1998 no-action letter for electronic communication networks (ECNs) provided similar temporary relief for non-discretionary trading venues and was eventually formalised through Regulation ATS — a playbook Peirce appears to be invoking explicitly.

⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

This statement materially reduces the legal risk for DeFi front-end operators that have been operating in a grey zone since the SEC's 2023 dealer definition rulemaking. The five-year sunset is the critical caveat — operators building businesses on this relief face a 2031 cliff if the Commission doesn't codify it into rule. The "DINO" (DeFi In Name Only) carve-out preserving enforcement authority for de-facto centralised platforms is significant: the SEC retains the ability to pierce the DeFi label for anything that functions as a centralised intermediary. Operators relying on this relief should document their non-discretionary, non-custodial architecture thoroughly — the conditions are affirmative defences in any future enforcement inquiry.

🔄 Contrarian Take

Staff statements have no legal force — they can be withdrawn the same day with a new Chair in office. Any firm building regulatory business strategy around a five-year staff no-action position that can be rescinded unilaterally is taking material political risk. The EU's MiCA framework has already brought DeFi-adjacent services under CASP licensing; US firms operating globally cannot rely on SEC no-action relief to satisfy non-US regulators.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Uniswap Labs, Matcha, CoW Protocol, and other front-end providers have been the most exposed to broker-dealer registration risk — this statement directly derisks their current operating models. a16z crypto's DeFi fund portfolio benefits broadly; a16z submitted an economic brief to the SEC Crypto Task Force in August 2025 supporting exactly this safe harbour approach. Coinbase's institutional DeFi access products must carefully navigate the custody and discretion conditions — any fee-sharing or routing preference arrangement with the underlying protocol could trigger the DINO exclusion.

📊 Portfolio Impact

Bullish [DeFi protocol governance tokens — UNI, AAVE, COW]: Front-end operator uncertainty was the primary institutional adoption barrier; relief removes it for five years.

Bearish [FINRA-registered broker-dealers with DeFi products]: Level-playing-field concerns arise if non-registered competitors can operate identically without compliance costs.

Neutral [ETH, BTC]: Infrastructure story, not a token classification story.

📋 What To Do

Trader DeFi protocol tokens (UNI, AAVE) are positioned for a structural re-rating as institutional DeFi access expands — monitor for product announcements from institutional front-end providers over the next 90 days.
Counsel Audit client DeFi front-end operations against the four conditions immediately — especially the no payment-for-order-flow requirement and the MEV disclosure obligation, which most existing interfaces do not currently satisfy.
Compliance Draft and implement the required written policies on cybersecurity and fraud/fee-manipulation protection; without these policies in place, the no-action relief does not apply even if all other conditions are met.
Founder Document your non-custodial, non-discretionary architecture in a legal memo now and build the required disclosures (non-registration status, routing parameters, MEV risks) into your next product release.

Medium FCA.org.uk [PRIMARY] UK Crypto Regulation
🟢 High confidence — FCA consultation paper, primary source verified

FCA Opens CP26/13: Cryptoasset Perimeter Guidance Consultation — Six Activities, June 3 Deadline, October 2027 Regime

⚡ What this means: The UK's Financial Conduct Authority is consulting on who will need to be "authorised" (licensed) to offer crypto services when the new UK crypto regime goes live in October 2027. "Perimeter guidance" means the FCA is defining the boundaries of regulated activity — which services are in-scope for licensing and which fall outside. Any firm offering services to UK customers needs to read this and respond by June 3 to influence the final rules.

"The proposed guidance supports the FCA's aim for an open, sustainable and competitive crypto market people can trust."

— FCA, CP26/13 press release, April 15, 2026

On April 15, 2026, the FCA published Consultation Paper CP26/13 setting out proposed guidance on the perimeter of the UK's new cryptoasset regulatory regime, which will come into force on October 25, 2027 under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) Regulations 2026. The consultation covers six regulated activities: issuing qualifying stablecoins; operating cryptoasset trading platforms; dealing as principal; dealing as agent; arranging deals; and arranging qualifying cryptoasset staking. Comments close June 3, 2026; final guidance is expected September 2026, when the authorisation gateway simultaneously opens for applications (September 30 – February 28, 2027).

The FCA separately indicated that DeFi guidance and operational resilience guidance for DLT users will follow in later 2026 consultations. Until October 2027, the UK crypto market remains subject only to financial promotions rules and financial crime requirements — there is no licensing requirement in force today. The CP signals the FCA is taking a functional, activity-based approach to perimeter definition consistent with the FSMA model.

📎 Historical Precedent: The FCA's CP19/3 (2019) consultation on cryptoasset perimeter guidance under existing FSMA categories set the early template — CP26/13 is the sequel covering the new bespoke crypto regime introduced by the Cryptoassets Regulations 2026.

⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

The June 3 comment deadline is tight for global firms needing to coordinate responses across legal, compliance, product, and government affairs functions. The staking activity inclusion is the most commercially consequential — the FCA's guidance on what constitutes "arranging qualifying cryptoasset staking" will determine whether liquid staking protocols like Lido and platforms like Coinbase International need UK authorisation. Firms operating under the EU's MiCA CASP framework should not assume EU authorisation satisfies UK requirements — post-Brexit, the UK regime is entirely distinct with no equivalence pathway currently announced.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Binance, Kraken, and Coinbase International — all of which are FCA-registered for AML purposes under the Temporary Registration Regime — must assess whether their current service footprint in the UK will require full authorisation under the new regime. OKX's newly-launched MiFID-compliant X-Perps product (announced April 15) uses EU regulatory infrastructure; UK access will require separate FCA authorisation under CP26/13's trading platform activity definition. UK fintech staking platforms and liquid staking protocols face the most novel perimeter question — the guidance here is genuinely being written in real time.

Medium CoinTelegraph / PYMNTS [MEDIA] SEC Rulemaking
🟡 Medium confidence — Atkins statement confirmed; OIRA timeline unspecified

SEC Chair Atkins Confirms "Regulation Crypto" Is at OIRA — Startup and Fundraising Exemptions Due "Shortly"

⚡ What this means: Before the SEC can publish a formal proposed rule in the Federal Register for public comment, the rule goes to OIRA (Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs at the White House) for review — the final quality-control stage. Atkins publicly confirmed this submission on April 7, meaning the SEC's comprehensive crypto fundraising and token classification rules are weeks, not months, away from appearing for public comment. This is one of the most consequential SEC rulemakings in a generation.

"We will have reg crypto that we will be proposing here shortly. It's in fact at OIRA right now, which is the next step before being published."

— SEC Chair Paul Atkins, Digital Assets and Emerging Technology Policy Summit, April 7, 2026

SEC Chair Paul Atkins confirmed on April 7, 2026, at the Vanderbilt Digital Asset Summit that the SEC's comprehensive crypto rulemaking — internally called "Regulation Crypto" — has been submitted to OIRA for pre-publication review. The proposed rule centres on three provisions: a startup exemption allowing early-stage token projects to raise capital over several years with reduced disclosure requirements; a fundraising exemption for token sales; and a safe harbour that removes securities treatment from tokens once a project achieves sufficient decentralisation. The framework builds on the SEC–CFTC joint interpretation of March 17, 2026, which established a taxonomy of digital commodities, securities, and stablecoins.

OIRA review typically takes 90 days but can be expedited or extended; the White House's pro-crypto stance under the Atkins SEC suggests expedited treatment is likely. Once published in the Federal Register, a minimum 60-day comment period applies before finalisation. Final rules are not expected before Q4 2026 at the earliest.

📎 Historical Precedent: The SEC's Regulation A+ (2015) overhaul under Chair White — also an OIRA submission — took 8 months from OIRA submission to final rule; Regulation Crypto may move faster given White House coordination.

⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

The decentralisation safe harbour is the most legally significant element — it creates a statutory endpoint at which SEC oversight terminates, something no prior securities law framework has included. Practitioners advising token projects need to understand what "sufficient decentralisation" means operationally (number of validators, token distribution metrics, developer concentration); the rulemaking comment period is the opportunity to define those parameters. The startup exemption parallels Regulation Crowdfunding but at larger scale — expect significant comment letters from venture capital and investment banking on disclosure thresholds and investor accreditation requirements.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Layer-1 protocols in their early growth phases — new chains launching in 2026-2027 — are the primary beneficiaries of the startup exemption. Existing tokens in regulatory limbo (those not named in the March 17 joint interpretation taxonomy) will use the comment period to argue for commodity classification or safe harbour eligibility. Coinbase and Kraken benefit from reduced enforcement overhang on listed tokens; the exchange liability question for listing unlicensed securities diminishes substantially once Regulation Crypto is in force.

EU 🟡 Holding MiCA transition cliff (July 1) is the dominant near-term concern — 15+ exchanges still on grandfathered registrations. ESMA enforcement posture will define whether EU becomes a model regime or a compliance minefield. DeFi guidance still absent from MiCA framework.
UK 🟡 Holding FCA CP26/13 is technically constructive — an open, activity-based consultation — but the October 2027 regime entry date means the UK remains in regulatory limbo for another 18 months. Post-Brexit divergence from MiCA is now structural. DeFi guidance consultation not until later 2026.
SG 🟢 Loosening MAS sandbox expansion for stablecoin trade finance settlement, finalised stablecoin framework taking effect mid-2026, and tokenised bill issuance signals Singapore is actively building competitive crypto infrastructure. Regulatory certainty advantage over UK and EU is widening.
AU 🟡 Holding DAF Act Royal Assent April 8 is a milestone, but the 18-month implementation clock and June 30 INFO 225 expiry mean Australian firms face near-term licensing decisions without finalised standards. ASIC roadmap published April 20 provides a timetable but not answers.
