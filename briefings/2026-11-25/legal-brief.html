Update South Korea Unveils Digital Asset Act to Lift ICO Ban and Cap Exchange Stakes

“[paraphrased] The draft would reopen token sales in Korea, a restriction in place since 2017.” Financial Services Commission, Virtual Asset Committee briefing — announcement reported by crypto.news, 24 Nov 2026 [paraphrased]

The earlier readiness report has now become a named bill. The Financial Services Commission presented the Digital Asset Industry Innovation and User Protection Act to its Virtual Asset Committee on 24 November, with FSC Chairman Lee Eok-won in the chair. Reporting says it is expected to reach the National Assembly by 25 November under Rep. Yoo Dong-su, who chairs the Political Affairs Committee.

Three provisions carry the weight. Token sales would be allowed again after nine years. Bank-led stablecoins would be permitted. Exchange shareholders would face a 20% ownership cap, with exceptions reaching 34%. Firms would also bear liability for customer losses caused by hacks and system failures, which moves operational risk from a supervisory concern into a balance-sheet one.

📎 Historical Precedent: South Korea’s 2017 ICO restriction is the rule this bill would reverse; the 2026 bill is the first serious attempt to replace it with a licensing-and-disclosure model.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

The ownership cap and the loss-liability clause will matter more to exchange operators than the ICO reopening. Caps of this kind convert a licensing question into a capital-structure question, forcing concentrated domestic holders to restructure or accept dilution. The Brief’s read: the 34% exception is the tell. It signals the cap is a negotiating position with room to move, not a hard wall, and the final text will likely be shaped by whoever secures the exception.

🔄 Contrarian Take

The strongest counter-argument is that a 20% cap deters the strategic and foreign capital an exchange needs to fund custody and compliance build-outs, leaving domestic incumbents protected and smaller entrants stranded. The Brief’s read: that concern is real but secondary. Without a cap, the liability clause would be harder to justify politically, and the bill is designed to pair the two.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Domestic exchange operators with concentrated holder bases face the largest restructuring cost. Token issuers gain a domestic primary-market route for the first time since 2017, and bank-led stablecoin issuers gain a licensed rail. The Brief’s read: capital will flow first to bank-affiliated stablecoin projects and only later to token sales, because the liability regime makes the stablecoin path cheaper to underwrite.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟩 Bullish Korea-facing token issuers (sector) — a reopened primary market gives new listings a domestic route.

🔴 Bearish Concentrated-ownership exchange operators (sector) — the 20% cap forces dilution or restructuring.

⚪ Neutral Global exchanges (sector) — no direct exposure until the text is final and the cap exceptions are settled.

📋 What To Do — Before 1 July
TraderDo not price a Korean ICO relaunch until the National Assembly text confirms the sale and listing rules.
CounselMap the 20% cap and 34% exception against any client shareholding that would cross the threshold.
ComplianceLog the customer-loss liability clause as a new operational-risk control item for any Korean-facing venue.
FounderIf you plan a Korean token sale, model the bank-led stablecoin route as a competitor for the same liquidity.

SEC Staff Clears Molecule’s Research Tokens Under No-Action Relief

“will not recommend enforcement action” if the offer proceeds as described in counsel’s opinion SEC Division of Corporation Finance, no-action statement to LabsCo (Molecule) — as reported by The Block, 24 Nov 2026

The SEC staff said on 24 November that it would not recommend enforcement against Molecule over utility tokens that let people fund scientific research. The relief depends on counsel’s opinion and on LabsCo selling the Project Tokens in the manner described, without Securities Act registration and without registering the tokens as a class of equity under Exchange Act Section 12(g).

The request reached the Division only the week before, so the turnaround was short. The Block counts this among a run of crypto no-action letters over the past year, including DoubleZero, Fuse and Canton.

📎 Historical Precedent: the 3 November 2026 Canton Coin no-action response used the same reliance-on-counsel structure, and the staff described it as an enforcement position rather than a legal conclusion.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

The operative feature is the reliance-on-counsel condition, not the token itself. Issuers that can reproduce the Molecule facts can seek the same comfort without a Commission vote, which shifts the weight onto the outside opinion as the document the staff will later examine. The March 2026 Commission interpretation is the framework the letter applies. The Brief’s read: expect a wave of requests that mirror this structure, and expect the staff to test each one against the specific token mechanics.

🔄 Contrarian Take

Staff letters bind no one. A future Commission or enforcement division can depart from them, and the Canton letter itself warned that different facts could produce a different conclusion. The Brief’s read: the risk is not that Molecule is overturned but that issuers over-read a fact-specific letter as a safe harbour for materially different token designs.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Decentralised-science and research-token projects gain a template for US offerings without registration, which lowers the cost of raising capital for early-stage scientific infrastructure. Securities counsel with crypto practices gain demand for opinion work. The Brief’s read: the winners are firms that can document token mechanics to the letter’s standard; the losers are issuers relying on the letter without matching its facts.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟩 Bullish Research-token and DeSci infrastructure (sector) — a template for US offerings without registration.

🔴 Bearish Token projects that rely on the letter without matching its facts (sector) — enforcement risk remains.

⚪ Neutral Listed US securities exchanges (sector) — no direct effect on the listing perimeter.

📋 What To Do — Before 1 July

ComplianceAdd the letter’s conditions to the monitoring file for any token already sold under similar counsel reliance.

Update Ukraine Postpones Rada Review of Bill 10225-d, Leaving the Oversight Split Open

“[paraphrased] The draft drew on the EU’s MiCA regulation and forms part of Ukraine’s commitments to the EU and the IMF.” Ukrainian securities commission, as reported by Bitcoinworld, 25 Nov 2026 [paraphrased]

The new development is procedural. The Verkhovna Rada postponed its 24 November review of Bill 10225-d, which leaves the crypto regulation and tax measure at second reading. The finance, tax and customs policy committee had renewed its recommendation on 23 November after further talks with the National Bank of Ukraine, and the Rada published the second-reading comparison table on 24 November.

The substance is familiar. Individuals would owe income tax on the year’s positive net result from virtual-asset transactions, while crypto-to-crypto swaps would be exempt. Exchanges and custodians would need authorisation and report annual transactions for Ukrainian residents. The securities commission would supervise crypto service providers, while the central bank would oversee the parts of the market that touch the currency and the banking system.

📎 Historical Precedent: the central bank previously rejected an earlier crypto draft over foreign-token and monopoly risks, so the oversight split is a repeat of the earlier institutional dispute.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

The swap exemption and the annual reporting duty are the commercial trade-offs. The oversight split is the real contest, because it decides whether custody and stablecoin-adjacent activity sit with the securities commission or the central bank. The commission’s own statement ties the draft to MiCA, so the EU template will be the reference point for any final text. The Brief’s read: the postponement shows the central bank can delay the bill, not block it, and the reporting regime will likely survive any compromise.

🔄 Contrarian Take

The central bank’s earlier objections were about foreign tokens and market concentration, and a MiCA-modelled bill does not answer them automatically. If the NBU keeps its veto over currency-adjacent activity, the securities commission could regulate service providers that cannot actually settle in hryvnia. The Brief’s read: this is the strongest objection, and the comparison table will show whether it has been written out.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Ukrainian exchanges and custodians that obtain authorisation gain a licensed-market position once the regime applies. Offshore platforms serving Ukrainian residents face reporting obligations and may lose access. The Brief’s read: the first licensees will be the firms already holding MiCA-style compliance infrastructure, which favours EU-authorised operators with Ukrainian books.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟩 Bullish Licensed EU-authorised service providers with Ukrainian client books (sector) — the regime rewards authorisation.

🔴 Bearish Unlicensed offshore platforms serving Ukrainian residents (sector) — authorisation and reporting raise the cost of access.

⚪ Neutral Listed Ukrainian equities — no direct listed exposure identified to this bill.

📋 What To Do — Before 1 July
TraderTreat the swap exemption as a possible tax-timing factor only once the second-reading text is final.
CounselCompare the comparison table against the current draft to see whether the securities commission or the NBU holds custody oversight.
ComplianceFlag Ukrainian-resident client accounts for annual reporting readiness under the draft.
FounderIf you plan a Ukrainian entry, prepare an authorisation file that maps to the MiCA-style requirements now.

Commission Opens MiCA Infringement Case Against Spain as Portugal Receives Reasoned Opinion

“[paraphrased] The Commission opened a MiCA infringement case against Spain and sent Portugal a reasoned opinion.” European Commission, as summarised by OneSafe, 24 Nov 2026 [paraphrased — verbatim not located]

According to a 24 November compilation that cites the Commission, on 19 November the Commission opened an infringement case against Spain over MiCA and sent Portugal a reasoned opinion. [Unconfirmed — single secondary source; verify against the Commission’s infringement register before relying on this.] The step follows the end of MiCA’s transitional period on 1 July 2026, after which ESMA said unlicensed crypto services in the EU would breach EU law.

The register counts cited in the same compilation are 17 crypto-asset service providers for Spain and 2 for Portugal on ESMA’s interim register as of 20 November. The same source notes that Tether’s USDT does not appear on the e-money token register. The 8 January 2027 stablecoin remediation deadline, already covered on 23 November, is the nearer date to watch.

📎 Historical Precedent: ESMA’s 17 April 2026 statement on the end of MiCA’s transitional periods set the enforcement expectation that national authorities now act on.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Infringement proceedings run in stages, and the reasoned-opinion stage is the last before referral to the Court of Justice of the EU. The Brief’s read: a referral is a multi-year path, so the near-term exposure for Spanish and Portuguese providers is supervisory, not a fine. Practitioners should read the 8 January deadline as the operative date for client-facing stablecoin changes.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Spanish and Portuguese providers already on the ESMA register gain a licensing moat while unregistered rivals face forced exits. Stablecoin distributors that have not moved away from non-MiCA tokens face the most direct commercial pressure. The Brief’s read: the register gap, not the infringement itself, is what will shift client flows.
