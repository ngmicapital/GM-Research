Alpha — Volume Profile & Point of Control

Thursday · 14 May 2026 · Sydney, Australia · Issue #20 11-min read
TL;DR

Volume Profile flips the volume chart on its side and shows where trade actually happened — the Point of Control (POC) is the single price where the most contracts changed hands, and it acts as the market's gravitational centre. The practical takeaway: in a balanced market, price rotates around the POC like a magnet; in a trending market, the POC migrates with the new value area, and the prior POC becomes a "naked" magnet that gets revisited ~80% of the time within ten sessions. Trade to the POC in balance, through low-volume nodes in discovery, and back to the naked POC when nothing else is in the way.

§01
What It Is
Horizontal volume · the market's fair-price ledger
Volume Profile is a horizontal histogram of volume traded at each price — not over time. The price with the fattest bar is the Point of Control, where the most business got done.

Standard volume bars sit on the time axis: they tell you when volume printed but not where. Volume Profile rotates the analysis 90 degrees and answers the more useful question: at what prices did trade actually clear? Each horizontal bar is the total contracts traded at that price during the chosen window. The longest bar is the Point of Control (POC) — the most accepted price, where the most buyers and sellers agreed to do business.

The tool was built by J. Peter Steidlmayer at the Chicago Board of Trade in the late 1970s, originally as the time-based Market Profile; volume-weighted derivatives followed. The framing he wanted to give traders was philosophical, not statistical: markets aren't drifting randomly, they are conducting a continuous two-sided auction, and the histogram is the running record of where that auction has spent its time. James Dalton's Markets in Profile (2007) is the canonical extension — the framework most institutional desks still teach against.

The reason this matters more than raw volume: a market that's accepted a price (high volume there) is structurally different from one that's rejected it (thin volume). High-volume zones are fair value — price will return to them, churn there, and treat them as gravitational. Low-volume zones are rejected — price moves through them fast in both directions because there's nothing structural to slow it down. The chart is a map of where supply and demand have actually been settled, not a guess about where they might go.

In crypto, the volume profile is the missing structural layer above the order book. The book shows you what's resting right now; the profile shows you what's been filled over the relevant window. One is intent; the other is fact. Both matter, but the profile is what tells you which levels carry historical conviction — and which were just air.

§02
Mechanics
How the histogram is built · value area · profile shapes
Volume gets bucketed by price (not time): the bucket with the most volume is the POC, and 70% of the volume around it bounds the Value Area.

At the calculation level, the platform pulls lower-timeframe bars (1-minute is typical for daily profiles), assigns each bar's volume to a price bucket, and aggregates. The bucket with the largest sum is the POC. The platform then expands one bucket up, one bucket down, alternately adding whichever side has more volume, until the running sum equals 70% of total volume. The outer edges of that expansion are VAH (top) and VAL (bottom). Everything inside is "value"; everything outside is "above value" or "below value".

Bucketing: volume_at_price[p] = Σ volume of all trades printed at p Point of Control: POC = price p* where volume_at_price[p*] = max(volume_at_price) Value Area expansion (70% rule): start with {p*}; running_sum = volume_at_price[p*] while running_sum < 0.70 × total_volume: compare volume of two buckets above current top vs two buckets below current bottom add the heavier pair to the value area VAH = max price in set; VAL = min price in set Naked POC (NPOC): A POC from a prior session where no subsequent session has yet traded back to that price → ~80% historical revisit rate within 10 sessions
Note: The 70% default comes from market profile convention (roughly one standard deviation of a normal distribution). Some platforms expand bucket-by-bucket rather than in pairs; this changes VAH/VAL slightly but not the POC. The volume-profile variants you'll see on TradingView differ mainly in which window they aggregate over: Session, Fixed Range (FRVP), Visible Range (VRVP), or Anchored from a chosen event.

Once the histogram is built, two structural readings dominate. The first is the shape of the profile. A symmetric, bell-shaped "D" profile means the market spent the session rotating around a single accepted price — that's balance, and rotation trades back to POC are the high-probability play. A "P" profile (heavy top, thin bottom) means buyers pressed up and then balanced — a short squeeze with acceptance on the highs. A "b" profile is the opposite: long liquidation followed by balance on the lows. A "double distribution" with two fat nodes means the market migrated from one accepted price to another during the session — discovery in action.

The second reading is value area migration. James Dalton in Markets in Profile frames this as the cleanest read of trend: compare today's VA to yesterday's. Higher VAH and higher VAL = healthy uptrend (value is migrating up). Lower VAH and lower VAL = healthy downtrend. Overlapping value areas = continued balance. Value area drift is what separates a real trend from a noisy chop — when value won't move, neither will conviction.

The third element — and where the concept earns most of its edge — is the naked POC. When a session's POC is never retested in subsequent sessions, the level sits "naked" on the chart. Across futures markets the documented revisit rate is approximately 80% within ten trading sessions. In crypto's 24/7 tape the cadence is similar but compresses — naked POCs from the prior week typically get visited within days. That's the magnet trade.

§04
In Practice
Regime first · then the three setups · with a worked example
The one rule: identify whether the market is in balance or discovery before picking the setup — the same level fires opposite trades in the two regimes.
1

Regime Before Setup — Balance vs Discovery

Compare today's value area to yesterday's. Overlapping VAs = balance (rotation regime — fade VAH/VAL, target POC). Migrating VAs = discovery (trend regime — fade the prior POC on retest, target the next HVN in the direction of travel). The same POC is a magnet in one regime and a rejection in the other. Get the regime right or every setup is wrong.

2

Three Setups, Three Tape Reads

Rotation (balance): price tags VAH or VAL, prints rejection with order-flow confirmation (CVD divergence, absorption), target POC. Breakout (discovery): price closes outside VA on volume, retests the broken edge, target the prior HVN beyond. Naked POC fill (any regime): no fresh high-volume node between price and the naked POC, scale in as price approaches, target the POC itself. Don't trade the level — trade the setup the level enables.

3

Stops Behind the Profile Structure, Not the Price

Tight stops below VAL or above VAH get harvested by anyone running stop-cluster targeting (see May 2 — Stop Clusters). Park stops behind the next structural level: behind the prior session's POC, on the far side of the HVN, or outside the prior week's value area. The cost is wider risk; the benefit is not getting wicked out of a thesis that's still valid.

4

Use Multiple Timeframes — Composite vs Session

The session profile (today's auction) is for execution. The composite profile (last 1–4 weeks, FRVP) is for context: where are the multi-session HVNs and naked POCs? When a session POC aligns with a composite POC, that level becomes structural. When today's POC is a thin pin floating inside a composite LVN, it's noise — expect price to leave it quickly.

Worked Example — BTC Rotation Inside the Weekly Value Area
ContextBTC range-bound $77.6K–$82K for the past two weeks. Composite POC sits at ~$80,000. Weekly profile is symmetric "D" — pure balance, no migration.
SetupPrice taps VAH zone $81.5K on the 4h, prints a long upper wick and a rejection candle. CVD divergence on the same bar (price HH, CVD LH).
EntryShort on the close of the rejection candle at ~$81,420. Confluence: VAH + CVD divergence + 200-day MA acting as ceiling ($82,228).
StopAbove the 200-day MA and prior swing high: $82,420. Risk = ~$1,000 (1.2%).
Target 1Composite POC at $80,000 — partial close ($1,420 reward; ~1.4R).
Target 2VAL zone $78,200 — runner. Full reward if reached: ~3.2R.
Invalidation4h close above $82,228 with rising volume → migration to new value above. Cover and reassess; balance has resolved up.

Timeframe fit: Composite/weekly profile for bias, daily for value-area migration read, 4h/1h for the setup, 5m–15m for execution. Never use a session profile alone — without composite context you're trading noise.

The framework's architects and current practitioners
From Steidlmayer's original auction-discovery framing to current practitioners drilling the POC playbook — the same idea, sixty years apart.

"The market is a continuously unfolding process of discovery. Price is not value in itself, but the market's best guess at a given moment."

J. Peter Steidlmayer · Markets and Market Logic · 1986 · The original framing of value-as-discovery, from the inventor of Market Profile

"If the Value Area resembles a bell and the VAs of several trading days practically coincide, the market is balanced — and trend movements start from a balance."

James F. Dalton · Markets in Profile · 2007 · Wiley

"Point of Control by itself is a very, very strong level. If price does not respect it and just shoots past it, that is very, very important information."

Trader Dale · "What to do when POC fails" · trader-dale.com · May 2026

"Volume shows where market participants did business. Steady two-way trade signals acceptance; thin participation signals rejection."

"Whenever the tape shows a poor response to effort — heavy volume with little price movement — the market is meeting with supply or demand stronger than the move itself."

§07
Common Traps
Five ways traders mishandle volume profile
The single biggest mistake: trading a POC level without first establishing whether the market is in balance or discovery — same level, opposite trades.
1
Trading the Level Without the Regime

The POC is a magnet in balance and a rejection in discovery. Traders see "price approaching POC" and reflexively look for a fade — but if the value area is migrating, the POC is the last acceptance zone before continuation, not a turning point. Read the value area migration first; pick the setup second.

2
Using Session-Only Profiles Without Composite Context

A session POC inside a composite LVN is structurally weak — there's no multi-day acceptance behind it. Most beginner setups use the default session profile and miss that the level is sitting in air. Always overlay a Fixed Range or Visible Range profile across the last 1–4 weeks to find the real structural POCs.

3
Treating Developing vs Final Value Areas as Equivalent

The developing value area shifts as the session unfolds — a 09:00 VAH is not the same level as the 16:00 VAH. Trading developing levels means trading a moving target. Use developing VAH/VAL only for live read; size and stops should reference yesterday's completed value area, which is fixed.

4
Confusing TradingView Variants

TradingView ships at least five Volume Profile flavours — Session, Session HD, Visible Range, Fixed Range, Anchored. They aggregate over different windows and produce different POCs. The most-cited level in retail charts (Session VP) is the least structural; FRVP across a defined consolidation is usually the more useful read. Know which one you're looking at.

5
Assuming Naked POC Magnets Are Free Money

The ~80% revisit-within-10-sessions stat is a futures-era heuristic. Reality on crypto: naked POCs do fill at high rates, but the path matters more than the destination. A naked POC can take weeks to fill, can fill on a violent overshoot, and during strong trends will sit unfilled for months. Use it as a target bias, not as a standalone entry signal. Anyone selling "naked POC scalping" as a strategy is mis-selling — the level is real, but it doesn't tell you when.
