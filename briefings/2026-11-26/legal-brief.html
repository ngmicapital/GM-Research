Update Ukraine's Rada Passes Crypto Market Law With 18% Profit Tax and Licensing Duties

[paraphrased] Lawmaker Yaroslav Zheleznyak said the law should bring 15 billion hryvnia (about $332 million) a year into the state treasury. Yaroslav Zheleznyak, Lawmaker — Telegram statement reported by Bloomberg, 25 Nov 2026

Ukraine's parliament approved the crypto market bill on 25 November by 271 votes. It taxes net annual gains from crypto trading at 18% personal income tax plus a 5% military levy, while swaps between virtual assets fall outside the charge.

Crypto bought before enactment faces a transitional rate of 5% income tax plus 5% military levy on sale. Virtual asset service providers must obtain licences and keep client assets segregated, and the securities regulator and central bank share supervisory duties. The bill still needs presidential signature, with implementation scheduled for 1 July 2027.

📎 Historical Precedent: The EU's MiCA licensing and client-segregation template, whose transitional period ended on 1 July 2026, is the closest analogue for Kyiv's licence-plus-segregation model.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Tax is the gateway here: licensing and segregation duties arrive alongside a net-gains computation that counsel will need to model client by client. Licensed platforms gain a compliant route into the market from 2027, while unlicensed offshore venues face the sharpest pressure to exit or license. The Brief's read: Ukraine is borrowing MiCA's architecture for licensing and writing its own tax stack on top, which makes the 2027 start date the real compliance deadline.

🔄 Contrarian Take

A licence requirement that bites only from July 2027 gives offshore platforms a long runway, and Ukrainian users may drift toward peer-to-peer channels that the tax regime cannot observe. The Brief's read: the rate is only as good as the reporting, and enforcement will depend on exchange-side data that the bill's timeline defers.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Licensed VASPs that can meet segregation and capital requirements gain a regulated home for Ukrainian retail flow, while offshore exchanges lose share unless they license. Listed exposure is thin, so the relevant read is for compliant global venues rather than Ukrainian-listed names. The Brief's read: watch Coinbase (COIN) and other compliant platforms for any Ukraine entry announcements.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟩 Bullish COIN — compliant platforms gain a licensed route into a market pricing a 2027 start.

🔴 Bearish Unlicensed offshore venues serving Ukrainian clients — no listed proxy, but flows migrate away from them.

⚪ Neutral Ukrainian sovereign exposure — the revenue target is small against the war-budget gap.

📋 What To Do — Before 1 July

CounselTrack presidential signature and the final text, and confirm how the 5% transitional rate applies to pre-enactment holdings.

Update CFTC Final Rule Settles Event-Contract Reporting Status Left Open by Conflicting Reports

[paraphrased] CoinDesk reports that fully collateralised event contracts receive a tailored data-reporting category and relief from heavier requirements. CoinDesk, 23 Nov 2026 — reporting on the CFTC final rule

The CFTC issued the final rule on event-contract data reporting on Monday, 23 November, which resolves the question left open by the 24 November reports that disagreed on whether it was final. The rule was first proposed in June. Fully collateralised contracts now sit in their own reporting category and are exempt from the heavier obligations that apply to other event contracts.

Practitioners should treat this as settled reporting infrastructure, not a ruling on the merits. It does not decide whether states can police these contracts, a question still in litigation after the Fourth Circuit affirmed denial of Kalshi's preliminary injunction against Maryland on 17 November.

📎 Historical Precedent: The Fourth Circuit's Maryland ruling of 17 November 2026 is the most recent appellate read on state enforcement against Kalshi-style contracts.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Troutman Pepper Locke's 19 November digest frames the Kalshi fight as a contest over CFTC preemption against state gaming law, and the final rule strengthens the federal regulatory record without deciding that question. The Brief's read: reporting clarity helps designated contract markets answer examiners, but it does nothing to stop states from enforcing gambling law against retail users.

🔄 Contrarian Take

Utah's court filing, covered in the 20 November issue, argues the CFTC cannot upend a state-court loss. The Brief's read: that argument is weaker against a procedural reporting rule; the preemption risk sits in the substantive event-contract stance, not in reporting mechanics.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Designated contract markets and clearinghouses with event-contract books absorb the reporting change first. Coinbase joined a proposed amicus supporting the CFTC on 9 November, so its interests track the federal position. The Brief's read: CME Group (CME) sees marginal cost change, while state-licensed sportsbooks carry the enforcement risk.

📊 Portfolio Impact

⚪ Neutral CME — reporting-cost change is marginal to its derivatives franchise.

Senate Democrats Press SEC to Rescind Regulation Crypto Assets and Tokenized-Stock Exemption

[paraphrased] Four Senate Democrats asked SEC Chair Paul Atkins to withdraw both the proposed Regulation Crypto Assets and the innovation exemption for onchain tokenized-stock trading. Sens. Warren, Van Hollen, Smith and Reed — letters to SEC Chair Paul Atkins, 25 Nov 2026

Two letters dated 25 November, sent by Warren, Van Hollen, Smith and Reed, target both pillars of the SEC's Project Crypto agenda. One goes after Regulation Crypto Assets, proposed on 18 August with comments closed on 20 October. The other goes after the innovation exemption that permits onchain trading of tokenized stocks.

The letters carry no legal force, and Chair Atkins can finalise the rule without responding to them. The operative risk therefore sits in the final-rule calendar rather than in the correspondence, and the real signal is the oversight agenda the Democratic majority will take up in January.

📎 Historical Precedent: The SEC has issued a run of staff no-action letters for crypto projects, including Molecule, DoubleZero, Fuse and Canton, that deliver relief without rulemaking (The Block, 24 Nov 2026).
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Counsel should read the letters as a map of the next oversight agenda, since they attack the rule and the exemption together, which is how a future Congress would approach unwinding them. CryptoTicker's November update confirms Democrats take control of both chambers on 3 January 2027. The Brief's read: the letters have no effect today, but they give future hearings a ready document trail.

🔄 Contrarian Take

Supporters of Project Crypto argue that the exemption and the proposed rule give issuers certainty without waiting for statute, which is the point of the agenda. The Brief's read: that certainty is only as durable as the next Commission, because staff-level relief can be withdrawn without a rulemaking.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Tokenized-equity entrants carry the most exposure, and the OKXICE launch of around-the-clock tokenized stock and ETF trading on 23 November relied on the temporary exemption (Troutman Pepper Locke, 19 Nov 2026). The Brief's read: onchain brokers that have built products on the exemption face a reversal risk that custodians and market makers will price in.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟩 Bullish COIN — the rule and exemption underpin its onchain-equity thesis if they survive.

🔴 Bearish Offshore tokenized-stock venues serving US users — private, no listed proxy, most exposed to rescission.

⚪ Neutral HOOD — no direct exposure identified in today's sources.

📋 What To Do — Before 1 July
TraderSize any tokenized-stock positions against the risk that the innovation exemption is withdrawn.
CounselTrack the SEC docket for a final Regulation Crypto Assets rule rather than treating the letters as a decision point.
ComplianceConfirm that onchain equity access stays within the stated scope of the exemption.
FounderStage a fallback path to registered securities treatment in case the exemption is rescinded.

Strategic Bitcoin Reserve Bill Clears House Financial Services Committee With 20-Year Sale Ban

[paraphrased] The committee approved a bill to create a strategic bitcoin reserve for the US government, with a bar on sales for at least 20 years. House Financial Services Committee, as reported by Solid Intel, 25 Nov 2026

The House Financial Services Committee approved a bill that would create a strategic bitcoin reserve for the federal government. The measure would prohibit sales of reserve bitcoin for at least 20 years, and it can now proceed to a full House vote. [Unconfirmed — sources conflict on floor timing; verify against the committee record.]

Committee passage is a long way from enactment. With the CLARITY Act stalled after the 49–50 cloture failure on 15 September, floor time before the lame-duck session ends is scarce, and the bill would need Senate action after that.

📎 Historical Precedent: The March 2025 executive order establishing a Strategic Bitcoin Reserve and a digital asset stockpile set the policy frame this bill would convert into statute.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Codifying the reserve would turn an executive policy into a statute with a 20-year lock, which matters for custody arrangements and for any accounting treatment of government holdings. The Brief's read: the custody and disposal language will matter more than the headline, and counsel should read the text for who holds the keys and what triggers a permitted sale.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Bitcoin treasury companies and institutional custodians gain policy signalling, and Strategy (MSTR) is the most-watched listed proxy. The Brief's read: the sale ban binds government holdings only, so it does not directly lock up private supply.
