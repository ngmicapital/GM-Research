4 ⚖ Regulatory & Legal Radar

The active crypto bill is dead until the new Congress convenes, so the live regulatory channel for today is agency rulemaking and enforcement, and neither moves BTC this week. The CLARITY Act failed a procedural vote in the Senate on September 15 by 49 to 50, and the bill is generally expected to need reintroduction in January 2027. Price action will be driven by the Fed, not by Washington.

Active Legislation

  • CLARITY Act (H.R. 3633): failed Senate cloture 49 to 50 on September 15. The House and Senate texts differ, and both chambers must pass identical language before it reaches the president.
  • Stablecoin yield: the central sticking point. The Senate text bars passive interest on payment stablecoins while allowing activity-based rewards. Banks say the carve-out leaves room for exchanges to pay yield.
  • GENIUS Act rulemaking: stablecoin rules are moving through agency rulemaking, not Congress.
  • Post-midterm math: reported control changes after the November 3 midterms raise the odds of a different committee landscape in 2027.

Today's Decision Points

  • No floor votes today: US markets are closed for Thanksgiving, so there is no legislative catalyst on the calendar.
  • SEC crypto framework: Regulation Crypto Assets and the tokenised securities exemptions are the agency track to watch.
  • CFTC market-structure rulemaking: the CFTC opened its own rulemaking in October, which is the route for spot digital-commodity oversight.
  • December 9 FOMC: the decision that matters most for BTC, and the one traders should be pricing today, not the crypto bill.
Legal overhang in the AI chip complex: the SEC probe into Nvidia's accounting, a California AG subpoena served November 19, and reported Commerce Department rules tightening chip exports to Southeast Asia all sit in this radar. These are the enforcement items that can move semis faster than any bill.
Regulatory: The stablecoin-yield fight and the SEC probe are the two live legal items. Neither is a catalyst for BTC this week. Treat any headline about a Senate revival as noise until the 2027 Congress sets a calendar.
5 🤖 AI & Semiconductor Watch

The AI capex cycle is still growing, but the market has stopped paying for it: spending is accelerating while the multiple is fatiguing, and that gap is where the risk sits. Nvidia's last reported quarter showed revenue of $115.8B, and management guided to $130B, yet the stock fell 14% on November 20 to $125.53 and lost roughly 23% that week, its worst since 2008. Revenue is not the problem. Governance and the accounting probe are the problem, and the forward multiple has compressed to around 8x.

Key Movers & Data

  • NVDA: closed at $125.53 on Nov 20 after a 14% drop on the Reuters report of an SEC accounting probe. Down about 47% from the October record close of $239.24 at the lows.
  • NVDA Wednesday: down about 3.9% on the day while the broad index recovered.
  • 50-day and 200-day moving averages for NVDA and INTC: not retrieved in this run EST. Treat trend calls as provisional until the moving averages are confirmed.
  • INTC and SPY/QQQ snapshot: not retrieved in this run EST.

Broader AI Landscape

  • Philadelphia Semiconductor Index: reported down 7.9% overnight and about 40% below its June peak.
  • AI power and infrastructure: Bloom Energy fell about 26% in a week and Vertiv about 18%, so the selloff reaches beyond chips.
  • Anthropic: the public debut traded below its IPO price, a sign that new AI listings are being marked down with the group.
  • Chip export policy: reported H200 export restrictions to China and a congressional claim of more than 500 procurement attempts keep policy risk live.
Learning note: multiple compression. A stock can keep growing revenue and still fall, because the price investors pay per dollar of earnings shrinks. When a stock trades at a single-digit forward multiple after a 40%-plus drawdown, the market is pricing a credibility discount, not a growth collapse. In Wyckoff terms, a climactic volume drop followed by a failed bounce is what a distribution-to-markdown handoff looks like. The signal to wait for is a higher low on volume, not a low P/E.
AI & Semis: The capex cycle is intact, but the trade has moved from buying the spend to demanding proof of governance and accounting. Stay underweight the AI leaders until the SEC scope is known. The first credible positive is a higher low in NVDA on volume, not a valuation argument.

7 🌎 Geopolitical Calendar

Oil-supply and trade-policy shocks, not a new war, are the geopolitical story most likely to move prices this week, and both push in the inflation direction that keeps the Fed hiking. The three alerts below are the live items; the rest of the background backdrop did not move the tape today.

🏴 Strait of Hormuz and Houthi strikes on energy infrastructure (Active)
Status: Reports of further attacks on shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and a Houthi strike on a Saudi refinery. WTI rose about 3% to roughly $85, Brent about $90.
Transmission Mechanism: Higher crude feeds headline inflation, supports energy equities, and keeps the Fed hawkish. That is a headwind for long-duration assets, including BTC, and a tailwind for the Dow's energy weight.
Reversal Signal: A safe reopening of shipping lanes, or WTI closing back below $80, would remove the inflation leg and free the Fed to pause in its guidance.
🏴 New US tariff proposals on trading partners (Active)
Status: A Section 301 investigation and tariff proposals covering 14 to 16 trading partners, with reported rates of up to 35% on some economies. Still a proposal, not enacted.
Transmission Mechanism: Tariffs raise import prices and feed core goods inflation. A stronger dollar on rate differentials compounds the pressure on BTC and gold, and it hurts exporters and the Hang Seng.
Reversal Signal: A formal delay, a narrowing of the target list, or a softer December inflation print would reduce the tariff channel to a headline.
🏴 Reported cyber incident at US Treasury financial systems (Active)
Status: A reported cyberattack on FinCEN systems, with unconfirmed scope. It was among the morning headlines on Wednesday and did not drive the close.
Transmission Mechanism: Operational risk in Treasury systems can widen the liquidity premium on Treasuries and add a risk-off impulse. It would matter more if it coincided with a yield spike.
Reversal Signal: Official confirmation of a minor, contained incident, with no disruption to settlement, would close the risk.
Geopolitical: The oil channel is the one to trade. Long crude and energy exposure is the hedge against the inflation leg; stay cautious on BTC and tech while the Hormuz headlines continue. Watch the WTI close and the 10-year yield as the confirmation pair.
