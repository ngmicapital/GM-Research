Alpha — Liquidity Voids & Air Pockets

Sunday · 3 May 2026 · Sydney, Australia · Issue #11 10-min read
TL;DR

A liquidity void is a stretch of the orderbook (or the chart it leaves behind) where almost no resting orders sit between two levels — when price runs into one, it doesn't fill ladder-step by ladder-step, it skips. An "air pocket" is the same idea framed dynamically: orders evaporate just as price arrives, so a small market order produces an outsized move. The practical edge is not that voids predict direction — they don't — it's that they tell you how price will travel through a region: fast, slippery, and likely to mean-revert later. Trade accordingly: smaller size, wider stops, no chasing, and remember the empty space because price often comes back to retest it.

§01
What It Is
Voids vs air pockets · what's actually missing
A liquidity void is a region of the chart where price travelled with almost no resting orders to slow it; an air pocket is the moment those orders disappear right as price arrives.

Most candles you look at are the output of a dense orderbook: passive bids and offers stacked at every tick, and price walking up and down that ladder one rung at a time as aggressors cross the spread. A liquidity void is what happens when the ladder has missing rungs. Price does not gracefully step through — it jumps. The candle you see in hindsight has a long body, a small wick, and almost no volume relative to its range. There was nobody home.

The two terms get used almost interchangeably, but the framing is slightly different. Liquidity void is a chart-level description: a printed region of price you can point to after the fact, often a one-directional impulse with thin participation. Air pocket is a tape-level description: the live moment when bids or offers vanish as price approaches them — orders pulled by market makers, spoofed walls yanked, institutional liquidity stepping back into a news event. Voids are the scar; air pockets are the wound being made.

This is also the cleanest place to draw the line from Auction Market Theory: markets exist to facilitate trade, and price moves only as far as it needs to to find counterparties. A void is, by definition, a region where the auction did not facilitate trade — price travelled, but no real two-sided negotiation happened there. The auction left an inefficiency on the tape. Markets tend to revisit those inefficiencies later, not because of any mystical magnet effect, but because trade still hasn't happened there and the system hasn't finished its job.

Note the explicit distinction from Fair Value Gaps (FVGs): an FVG is a 3-bar imbalance where the wicks of bar 1 and bar 3 don't overlap. FVGs can form on healthy participation — strong, decisive moves with real volume. A liquidity void is specifically the thin case: low volume relative to the candle's range. All voids look like impulses; not all impulses are voids. Volume separates the two.

§02
Mechanics
How depth disappears · what price does next
Voids form when resting orders cancel faster than aggressors can fill them — the book hollows out, and the next market order travels through multiple price levels before finding size.

At the orderbook level the mechanic is precise. A trade only executes when an aggressive order finds a passive one to match against. If the bid stack at $79,000 is 50 BTC and the next material bid is at $78,400, then a single 80 BTC market sell will eat the $79,000 bid and continue down — printing fills at $78,990, $78,950, $78,800, $78,500 — until it hits the next real layer at $78,400. Six hundred dollars of price travel for one order. That intermediate range is a liquidity void by construction: the book had nothing in it.

Why does the book end up that way? Several reasons that show up regularly on crypto venues:

Where voids come from: 1. Cancel-then-trade — market makers pull bids/offers as volatility rises (they don't want to be picked off) 2. Pre-event withdrawal — orders disappear ahead of CPI, FOMC, ETF flow data, large unlocks 3. Spoof collapse — fake walls cancel the moment price seriously approaches 4. Post-breakout vacuum — when a level breaks, no new resting size has materialised above it yet 5. Off-hours thinning — APAC overnight, weekends, holiday closes — base liquidity is structurally lower 6. Cascade selling — liquidations market-sell, MMs widen, bids cancel, more liquidations fire
Notes: 40–70% of visible limit orders on major exchanges never execute — they get cancelled before being filled. That high cancel rate is itself a feature: it means the displayed book is an unreliable map of what's actually committed. Voids are partly a measurement problem — you see the bid until you don't.

Once a void opens, the dynamics that follow are well-known. Price accelerates: the same 80 BTC order that would have moved price by $50 in a normal book moves it by $600 in a thin one. Slippage explodes: market orders fill at progressively worse prices as they walk the depleted book. Stop runs are violent: any stop cluster sitting inside a void becomes a magnet for cascade liquidations because each triggered stop is itself a market order driving deeper into the empty zone.

And then, crucially, price tends to come back. Not always immediately, and not always all the way — but the auction-theory logic holds: the void is unfinished business. Resting orders eventually return to that price region, the spread tightens, real two-sided trade happens, and the void "fills." Crypto traders track CME bitcoin gaps obsessively for exactly this reason — empirically, 80–90% of CME gaps eventually fill, even if the timing is unpredictable.

§04
In Practice
How to read voids · how to trade them · how not to die in them
The one rule: never enter into an active void — wait for it to print, watch the retest, take the trade on confirmation as the void fills, not on the impulse that creates it.
1

Identify the Void by Volume, Not Just Shape

Long-bodied impulse candles are everywhere; voids are the subset where volume is materially below the recent baseline. Compare the candle's volume to its 20-bar average — a true void is typically <60% of average. If volume was high, it's a real impulse, not a void; trade it as momentum, not as a return zone.

2

Mark the Zone, Then Forget It Until Price Returns

Drawing a horizontal box across the body of the void candle gives you a zone to watch. Don't pre-position into it — voids fill on unpredictable timelines, sometimes hours, sometimes weeks. Set an alert at the void's far edge and let the chart do the work. The trade sets up only when price re-enters the zone with rising volume.

3

Cut Size When the Heatmap Shows Thin Conditions

If you trade Bookmap, Coinglass heatmap, or any orderbook visualiser: when wide stretches of the chart are dark/cool-coloured, you are operating inside a void in real time. Standard tactics — reduce position size by 50%+, widen stops to absorb slippage, never use stop-market orders inside thin zones (use stop-limits or mental stops triggered by structure). Trading thin books with normal size is how accounts blow up on weekends.

4

Pair the Void With a Structural Anchor

Voids fill, but they don't all fill the same way — some get reclaimed cleanly, others get sliced through on the retest as thin liquidity offers no resistance the second time either. Filter for the ones that have an additional reason to hold: a prior swing high/low at the void's edge, a VWAP, a value-area boundary, a known stop cluster. Void + structure is a trade; void alone is a watch.

Worked Example — BTC Long on Void Retest
SetupBTC prints a Saturday flush from $80,000 → $77,000 on visibly thin weekend volume — the candles between $79,000 and $77,500 are oversized and show ~40% of the prior 24h average volume.
Mark the zoneLiquidity void zone defined as $77,500 → $79,000 (the body of the impulse candles).
WaitTwo sessions of consolidation between $77,200 and $77,800. No entry yet.
TriggerMonday Asia open: BTC pushes through $77,800 with rising volume and 5m closes inside the void. Long entry on confirmation candle close at ~$78,050.
StopBelow the consolidation low at $77,150. Risk = $900/BTC (~1.15%).
Target 1Mid-void at $78,250 — take 1/3 off. Move stop to entry.
Target 2Far edge of void at $79,000 — take another 1/3.
RunnerTrail final third with a 5m structure stop, looking for void to slice through cleanly toward $80,000.
Invalidation5m close back below $77,500 with declining volume — void may slice through downward instead. Exit immediately.

Timeframe fit: 1h–4h to identify the void zone, 5m–15m to time the retest entry. Daily voids are real but slow — the rest can sit empty for weeks. CME bitcoin gaps are the canonical multi-day version; intraday weekend voids fill within sessions.

BTC's Saturday Air Pocket — $80K → $77K, $850M Liquidated

Late Saturday 25 April 2026, BTC was rejected at the $80,000 psychological level it had been pounding on for two weeks. As US equity markets were closed and APAC traders were thin, market makers pulled bids across the $79,000–$77,500 stretch. The next several hours were the textbook expression of a void filling in real time: a single 4h candle displaced price ~3.5% on visibly thinner volume than the days around it, and the cascade triggered ~$850 million of leveraged long liquidations across major exchanges with nearly 200,000 traders blown out.

This is not a 2-3% pullback in a deep weekday book. It's the same mechanic QCP Capital and CoinDesk have been flagging all year: bitcoin is increasingly behaving as a split system — deep and efficient during US weekday hours when ETF arbitrage runs, brittle and high-risk on weekends when institutional liquidity steps back. Thin books amplified what should have been a routine rejection into a cascading liquidation. Reflexive loop: void → liquidations → makers widen further → more void → more liquidations. CME's planned 29 May 2026 launch of 24/7 crypto futures should partially fix this, by letting the arbitrage mechanism run continuously. Until then, the weekend air-pocket regime is structural, not random.

Quotes
From Livermore's "line of least resistance" to Bookmap's modern thin-book heuristic
The same idea — markets travel fast where there is nothing to slow them — has been written about for a century, by Livermore, Wyckoff, and the modern orderflow community alike.

"There is what is called the line of least resistance. Pay attention to it — it pays you to study it. Markets often travel furthest along it because there is nothing in the way."

Jesse Livermore · via Reminiscences of a Stock Operator (Lefèvre, 1923) · The original framing of the idea that became the modern liquidity void.

"A liquidity void is what's left behind when [participation steps aside] — an area on the chart where price traded with very little volume, leaving a 'hole' in market participation. The market often wants to revisit and fill in that area once participation returns."

Zeiierman · TradingView · "Liquidity Voids: Where Price Runs Through Empty Space" · 2026 · source

"A thin order book doesn't tell you where price will go — but it does tell you how it will move: quickly, unpredictably, and with higher risk."

Bookmap · "What Happens When Book Liquidity Disappears" · 2025 · source

§07
Common Traps
Five ways traders lose money in voids — and one honest reframe
The single biggest mistake: chasing the impulse through a void. The candle that prints the void looks like a breakout — it isn't, it's a vacuum. Wait for the retest.
1
Chasing the Impulse Candle

The breakout candle that prints a void is the worst place on the chart to enter. You are buying at the top of an empty zone with no resting bids beneath you — slippage is high, stops are wide, and the next 30 minutes typically retrace much of the move as the auction works back into the void. Wait for the candle to close, mark the zone, take the retest.

2
Putting Stops Inside the Void

A stop-loss inside a void is a stop the market makers can hunt for almost free — there's no liquidity between price and your stop to slow the move. Stops belong beyond the void, anchored to structure (the prior swing low/high or a level outside the empty zone). If that risk is too wide for your size, your size is too big.

3
Treating Every Long-Bodied Candle as a Void

Voids are defined by thin volume relative to range, not by how big the body looks. A high-volume impulse candle with a long body is real momentum — that move was paid for by aggressors and the price is likely to hold. Misclassifying a momentum candle as a void leads to fading strength on retest and giving back capital to the trend you should have been with.

4
Assuming Voids Always Fill

The empirical "voids fill" stat (e.g. 80–90% of CME bitcoin gaps) is helpful and overstated at the same time. Some voids are completed by the market trading through them in the opposite direction; others sit unfilled for months while structure shifts; a few never fill at all because the market regime changed underneath them. Treat the fill as a probabilistic tendency, not a destiny — and never bet the size implied by certainty.

5
The "Liquidity Void" Buzzword Hype

Honest callout: large parts of "liquidity void" content on YouTube and X are repackaged Fair Value Gap / ICT material with new branding, treating any 3-bar wick imbalance as a void without ever measuring volume. That isn't a void — that's a wick gap, and they behave differently. Treat any source that doesn't reference volume in its definition with scepticism. The concept is real and useful; the marketing layer on top has gotten thick.
