4 ⚖️ Regulatory & Legal Radar

The GENIUS Act implementation clock is ticking — July 18 deadline for final regulations is the story. With the GENIUS Act signed into law (July 2025), the focus has shifted from legislation to implementation. Treasury, FDIC, and FinCEN have each issued proposed rules ahead of the July 2026 regulatory deadline. Meanwhile, Senator Reed introduced the Foreign Stablecoin Transparency Act (S.3907) targeting foreign issuers like Tether — the next battleground is whether offshore stablecoins can escape US audit requirements. The CLARITY Act (market structure) roundtable is scheduled for May, keeping crypto market structure reform on the front burner.

📜 Active Legislation & Implementation
  • GENIUS Act (signed July 2025) — Final regulations due July 18, 2026. Treasury/FDIC/FinCEN proposed rules published. 100% reserve backing for payment stablecoins required.
  • CLARITY Act — May roundtable scheduled. Crypto market structure bill. Senate Banking Committee markup still pending.
  • SEC-CFTC Joint Interpretation (Mar 17, 2026) — 68-page guidance, 5-category token taxonomy. Binding. MOU signed March 11 for coordination.
  • Foreign Stablecoin Transparency Act (S.3907) — Sen. Reed targeting Tether and other foreign stablecoin issuers. Closing audit loopholes in GENIUS Act.

7 🌐 Geopolitical Calendar
🚨 STRAIT OF HORMUZ CRISIS — CONDITIONAL CEASEFIRE, STRAIT STILL BLOCKED
Status: Conditional ceasefire in place (extended until talks conclude), but the Strait remains effectively closed — shipping at ~5% of pre-conflict levels. The “dual blockade” persists: US blockading Iranian ports since April 13, Iran restricting tanker passage since Feb 28. Pakistan-mediated talks are “stalled” per Al Jazeera. Iran has ~2 weeks of oil storage capacity left before being forced to cut production.

Transmission Mechanism: 20% of global oil transited Hormuz pre-conflict. The IEA calls this the “largest supply disruption in the history of the global oil market.” The price impact: Brent averaged $113/bbl in Q1 but has pulled back toward $108 on ceasefire hopes. The chain: oil prices → energy CPI → Fed on hold (96% June) → yield curve anchored → airlines/shipping margin-compressed. Exxon CEO warned the market hasn’t absorbed the full impact yet. A sea drone struck the tanker Sonangol Namibe 800km from the Strait — the conflict’s geographic footprint is expanding.

Reversal: Iran running out of storage (2 weeks) forces a production cut, which ironically could de-escalate by removing Iran’s leverage. A sustained diplomatic opening via Pakistan backchannel or US naval escort corridor would collapse crude $15–20 in a session. Watch for any Iran storage/production news as the near-term catalyst.
🚨 US TARIFF REGIME — SECTION 122 TEMPORARY DUTIES
Status: After the Supreme Court struck down IEEPA broad tariffs, the administration imposed Section 122 temporary 15% global import duties. Core inflation at ~3% with the full tariff pass-through yet to materialise. Importers absorbed the initial hit; consumers and exporters are next in line.

Transmission Mechanism: Tariff overhang compounds the oil-driven inflation shock. Stagflationary pressure: higher import costs + higher energy costs = compressed consumer spending power. Companies front-loading imports ahead of potential escalation temporarily inflates inventory and trade data. The tariff + oil combination is why the Fed dissented 8-4 — the policy mix is pulling growth and inflation in opposite directions.

Reversal: Trade deal announcement or Congressional override of Section 122 (unlikely). Midterm election calculus — voter sensitivity to prices — may force de-escalation in H2.
🚨 IMF GROWTH DOWNGRADE — 3.1% GLOBAL, CONTINGENT ON SHORT WAR
Status: April WEO cut 2026 global growth to 3.1% with headline inflation at 4.4%. The baseline assumes a short-lived Iran conflict — which is now in its third month with no resolution timeline.

Transmission Mechanism: If conflict extends beyond Q2, the 3.1% baseline breaks down. Sustained $120+ oil pushes inflation above 5% and growth below 2.5% — stagflation. Central banks globally face the impossible choice between fighting inflation and supporting growth. The EIA forecasts Brent averaging $113 this quarter before falling to $80 by year-end — but that requires Hormuz reopening.

Reversal: Rapid Hormuz de-escalation + coordinated SPR releases. The 2-week Iran storage deadline could force the issue.
Hormuz remains the single highest-impact geopolitical variable. The new wrinkle: Iran is running out of oil storage in ~2 weeks, which could force a production cut and paradoxically create a de-escalation opening. If crude falls below $100 sustainably, the entire stagflation narrative unwinds and the Fed gets room to signal cuts. Long energy and defence as hedges; watch Iran storage capacity as the near-term reversal catalyst.
