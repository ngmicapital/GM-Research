[Launch][Benchmark]

MiniMax M2.7 — Self-Evolving Open Source, SWE-Pro 56%, Released April 12

MiniMax released M2.7 on April 12, 2026 — open source, under a permissive licence, with a context window of 204.8K tokens. Pricing: $0.30/$1.20 per 1M input/output tokens. The headline capability: M2.7 is the first model to publish autonomous self-improvement at scale — it ran 100+ rounds of scaffold optimisation without human intervention, lifting benchmark performance by approximately 30%. The mechanism: the model proposes, evaluates, and implements improvements to its own inference-time reasoning scaffolds, treating self-optimisation as an agent task rather than a training-time process. [Sources: MiniMax.io, MarkTechPost, VentureBeat, WaveSpeedAI — confirmed across 4 sources]

Benchmark highlights: SWE-Pro 56.22% Terminal Bench 2 57.0% PinchBench 86.2% SWE Multilingual 76.5 GDPval-AA ELO 1495. On PinchBench, M2.7 lands just behind GLM-5 and GPT-5.4 (both 86.4%) — effectively at parity with the commercial frontier on this benchmark. GDPval-AA ELO of 1495 is the highest published score among open-source models.

The "self-evolving" framing warrants scrutiny: what MiniMax demonstrated is scaffold optimisation — the model improves its prompting and reasoning structure, not its weights. It is not rewriting its own training data or changing base model parameters. The distinction matters. Scaffold optimisation is a powerful and reproducible technique; "evolving AI" is a marketing framing. [Source: analyst interpretation of published methodology]

Claude Ecosystem

Claude Mythos Preview found thousands of zero-days across every major OS and browser — and Anthropic is deliberately not releasing it publicly, which tells you exactly how capable it is and how seriously they're treating the risk.
[Update][Use Case]

Claude Mythos Preview + Project Glasswing — Frontier Cybersecurity AI, Gated to 50 Orgs

Announced April 7, 2026. Anthropic unveiled Claude Mythos Preview, described as a "step change" over Claude Opus 4.6 — a general-purpose model that is "strikingly capable" at computer security tasks. The headline demonstration: Mythos autonomously identified and exploited CVE-2026-4747, a 17-year-old remote code execution vulnerability in FreeBSD that allows any unauthenticated attacker on the internet to gain root access to a machine running NFS. The model found it without guidance. More broadly, Anthropic used Mythos to identify thousands of previously unknown zero-day vulnerabilities across every major operating system and every major web browser, along with other critical infrastructure software. [Sources: TechCrunch, Fortune, CNBC, The Hacker News, Anthropic, InfQ, Simon Willison — confirmed across 7 sources]

Project Glasswing is the controlled deployment framework: approximately 50 organisations have access, including AWS, Apple, Broadcom, Cisco, CrowdStrike, Google, JPMorganChase, Microsoft, and Nvidia — a who's-who of critical software infrastructure. These partners are using Mythos to scan and patch their own systems, and to audit open-source code. The findings are being shared across participants. Anthropic explicitly stated it does not plan to make Mythos Preview generally available — the restriction is deliberate, citing the dual-use risk: the same model that finds vulnerabilities for defenders can find them for attackers.

The alignment risk report published alongside Mythos (April 7) describes it as triggering new internal safety protocols. Anthropic's framing: the model is operating at a capability level where the lab needs to gate access not for commercial reasons, but because broader availability would constitute a genuine security risk. This is the clearest public acknowledgment by any frontier lab that a model is too capable to release freely.

⚡ Implication: Mythos is not in dc's workflow. But two things matter: (1) Mythos reveals the capability tier above Claude Opus 4.6 — it's a qualitative jump, not an incremental improvement; (2) if/when Mythos becomes available for non-security tasks, its agentic capabilities will redefine what automated research and analysis can do. Watch the Project Glasswing access list — when JPMorganChase and similar non-tech firms are using Mythos for financial analysis (not just security), it'll be announced.

Research Radar

Google's PaperOrchestra achieves 84% simulated CVPR acceptance — AI is now writing its own research papers well enough to fool reviewers, raising the question of what "published research" means when the author is an agent.
[Research]

PaperOrchestra — Google Multi-Agent Framework, 84% Simulated CVPR Acceptance, 81% ICLR

Google Research · arXiv (April 2026) · Presented at ICLR 2026

One-line finding: A multi-agent AI framework (PaperOrchestra) converts unstructured pre-writing materials — notes, hypotheses, literature fragments — into submission-ready AI research papers including literature reviews and figures, achieving 84% simulated acceptance at CVPR and 81% at ICLR in independent evaluation.

Method: PaperOrchestra uses a pipeline of specialised agents: a literature review agent (queries and synthesises related work), a hypothesis structuring agent (formalises research questions), a writing agent (produces LaTeX-formatted sections), and a visual agent (generates charts and diagrams). The system was evaluated against actual CVPR and ICLR acceptance criteria by a blind review simulation. [Source: DevFlokers AI paper digest April 2026 — [Unconfirmed — single summarisation source; primary arXiv paper not independently fetched]]

Why it matters in 6 months: Paired with SakanaAI's AI Scientist-v2 (covered Issue #18 — first AI-generated paper to pass peer review), PaperOrchestra represents the second major data point that the academic research pipeline is being automated at submission quality. The recursive implication: AI models can now generate research that improves AI models, creating a closed loop that accelerates capability development faster than any human research team can match. Conference verification mechanisms will need to evolve quickly — watermarking, contribution audits, or AI-detection requirements are likely to become mandatory at top venues within 12 months.

[Research]

TurboQuant — Google, ICLR 2026 — Reducing Memory Overhead in Vector Quantisation

Google Research · ICLR 2026 Vienna (April 24–28)

One-line finding: TurboQuant reduces the memory overhead of vector quantisation in large models — the technique that compresses model weights without full precision, enabling larger models to run on smaller hardware.

Why it matters in 6 months: Quantisation is the primary reason open-weight models like Gemma 4 and MiniMax M2.7 can run on consumer hardware. Any improvement to quantisation efficiency directly expands where frontier-quality models can be deployed — on-device, in edge infrastructure, on DePIN compute nodes.
