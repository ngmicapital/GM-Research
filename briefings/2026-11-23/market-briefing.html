Stay short the rally in risk until the 10-year and the dollar both roll over together. A DXY close back under 103 with the 10Y still below 5% would be the first sign the tightening impulse is fading; until then, the Fed is setting the tone and I am not fighting it.

4 ⚖ Regulatory & Legal Radar
EST

Bearish lean on crypto legislation: the CLARITY Act is stalled with no Senate vote on the calendar, so near-term regulatory risk runs through enforcement and agency action, not statute. The live regulatory story is the SEC probe into Nvidia, which matters for the AI trade more than for crypto.

Active Legislation

  • Digital Asset Market CLARITY Act: the House passed it in July 2025 by 294 to 134. The Senate Banking Committee advanced its version in May and reported a substitute in June, which was merged with the Agriculture text in July.
  • Senate procedural failure: a motion to proceed failed 49 to 50 on 15 September, well short of the 60 votes needed. Senator Lummis has called the bill dead, though a revival procedurally remains possible.
  • Lame-duck reality: the 15 November Senate schedule omitted any CLARITY vote. Former White House crypto official Patrick Witt sees low odds for a new bill this year and expects agency rulemaking to shape market structure instead.

Today’s Decision Points

  • No Senate floor vote is scheduled. Any sudden schedule change would be a headline surprise, not a base case.
  • SEC civil probe into Nvidia: reported as an accounting and disclosure inquiry, focused on how revenue is reported by geography and on the Megaspeed dealings. Investigations are not findings and many close without action.
  • Commerce export controls: reported tighter chip export rules to Southeast Asia add to the scrutiny of diverted shipments to China.

Regulatory risk for crypto is a slow grind through agency rulemaking, which means no legislative catalyst to chase this week. The sharper regulatory risk is the Nvidia disclosure probe, and a finding or a broader document request would hit the AI trade harder than any crypto headline.

Broader AI Landscape

  • Anthropic debut: the IPO swung from a 17% gain on Thursday to a sharp early drop on Friday, which is a live test of appetite for AI valuations.
  • Circular financing: questions about AI financing deals and revenue quality are now part of the Nvidia probe scope, which widens the discount on the whole AI complex.
  • Earnings still strong: the market is pricing a credibility problem, not a demand collapse. That is a different and slower unwind.
Why a revenue-by-geography probe matters. Companies choose how to tag where a sale happened. If a region drops out of the breakdown, investors can no longer trace where chips ultimately ship, which is exactly the question regulators ask when they suspect diversion. Nvidia’s 2025 change from billing address to headquarters country removed Singapore as a separately reported market. The probe is about the disclosure rather than the demand, and that distinction matters for how long the discount lasts.

The AI trade needs a clean earnings and disclosure story to re-rate, and it does not have one this week. I would treat the Nvidia rebound as a fade until the probe scope is clear. The capex cycle is intact on the reported numbers; the multiple is what is broken.

7 🌎 Geopolitical Calendar

Oil is trading the Iran de-escalation headlines rather than the tanker incidents, and that is the mispricing: the Strait risk premium is still there, and the supply surplus story has not yet arrived in the physical market. I would not chase WTI lower into the OPEC+ meeting without a clean supply signal.

🏴 Hormuz tanker incidents and US–Iran talks (Active)
Status: Analysts counted about one tanker incident per day in November, yet crude shipments through the Strait have returned to roughly pre-war levels and WTI is on a third straight weekly decline. No formal US–Iran agreement has been announced.
Transmission Mechanism: Lower oil eases headline inflation and gives the Fed room to slow the hike path, which is a rate-relief channel for crypto and gold, but a surplus that keeps oil under $85 also deflates energy equities.
Reversal Signal: A confirmed attack that halts tanker transit for more than a day, or a formal agreement that removes the risk premium in a way that does not lift supply, would change the read.
🏴 Houthi strikes on Riyadh (Active)
Status: Missile attacks on Saudi Arabia’s capital on 20 November lifted Brent and WTI intraday, though prices faded into the weekly close. Analysts expect Brent to trade in an $89 to $96 range this week.
Transmission Mechanism: The Saudi supply risk is the upside tail for crude and for the energy overweight. A spike in crude would push breakevens and the dollar in different directions, which is messy for the crypto read.
Reversal Signal: Strikes on Saudi export infrastructure, or a move in Brent above the $96 top of the range, would flip oil from a fading premium to a live supply shock.
🏴 Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries (Active)
Status: Drone attacks have cut Russian refinery crude processing by about 45% since late October, according to Reuters reporting. Analysts flag possible further falls in Russian output as export infrastructure is hit.
Transmission Mechanism: The impact lands on refined products and diesel margins before crude, which is a second-order inflation channel that keeps central banks hawkish for longer.
Reversal Signal: A ceasefire or a restart of refinery throughput would ease product cracks and remove a hawkish input from the Fed narrative.

Geopolitics is a headline risk for oil this week, not a trend driver. The trend is supply surplus and the Fed. I would only buy oil on a confirmed supply shock, and I would sell the rally into the 29 November OPEC+ meeting if it fails above the high of the range.
