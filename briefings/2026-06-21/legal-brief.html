ESMA Pivots From Writing MiCA to Enforcing It as the July 1 CASP Cliff Nears

"2025 was a pivotal year for Europe's capital markets, with momentum shifting from policy ambition to concrete action." Verena Ross, ESMA Chair — 2025 Annual Report, 17 June 2026

ESMA published its 2025 Annual Report on 17 June, framing a year in which the authority moved "from policy ambition to concrete action" — the drafting phase of MiCA's rulebook is done, and supervision begins now. The report lands exactly 10 days before MiCA's EU-wide transitional period expires on 1 July 2026, after which any firm serving EU clients without a CASP authorisation is in breach of EU law.

This threads the countdown we flagged on 17 June, when only ~194 CASPs had been licensed across the bloc. ESMA's earlier statements (4 December 2025 and 17 April 2026) set the enforcement posture: national competent authorities (NCAs) are expected to act against firms still operating unauthorised, last-minute applications should face heightened scrutiny, and unauthorised entities must have credible, immediately executable wind-down plans in place by the deadline.

The report also highlights ESMA's MiCA market-abuse guidelines and supervisory-convergence work — signalling that the full supervisory architecture, not just the authorisation gate, is going live alongside the deadline.

📎 Historical Precedent: MiFID II's January 2018 go-live similarly turned a multi-year drafting phase into hard supervisory deadlines, leaving late applicants stranded in authorisation backlogs.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Enforcement risk now sits with NCAs, not ESMA centrally, so exposure is uneven by member state — but firms relying on a national transitional regime past 1 July face cease-and-desist orders and frozen client-asset access (ESMA statements, 4 Dec 2025 / 17 Apr 2026; Hogan Lovells peer-review note). The Brief's read: the bigger 2H risk is not the deadline itself but ESMA's stated intent to scrutinise last-minute filings — a firm that files on 30 June is signalling it will be operating unauthorised on 2 July.

🔄 Contrarian Take

The "hard stop" may be soft in practice: NCAs have wildly uneven supervisory capacity, and a pile-up of pending applications could produce de facto forbearance for firms with a credible application already in train. The Brief's read: forbearance buys process, not a licence — clients and counterparties will still treat an unlicensed venue as un-bankable from 1 July regardless of NCA backlog.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Winners are venues that locked in MiCA/MiFID licences early — Coinbase (COIN), Kraken and OKX's MiFID-compliant EU build-out — which inherit the order flow of any peer that goes dark. Losers are offshore-anchored exchanges serving EU retail without a local entity; Binance's reported Greek MiCA rejection (flagged 18 June) is the live test case. The Brief's read: EU market share consolidates toward the already-licensed over July.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟩 Bullish COIN, licensed EU-domiciled CASPs and EURC/USDC issuers (CRCL) that already clear MiCA — they absorb displaced flow.

🔴 Bearish Offshore venues serving EU retail without a CASP entity — wind-down risk and lost EU revenue from 1 July.

⚪ Neutral Non-EU-facing global majors — exposure is limited to the EU client book, not the franchise.

📋 What To Do — before 1 July
TraderConfirm your EU venue is on ESMA's CASP register before 1 July; pre-stage withdrawals from any platform without a licence.
CounselMap which group entity holds (or has filed for) the CASP licence and brief the board on cease-and-desist and client-asset-freeze exposure.
ComplianceIf unlicensed at the deadline, have the wind-down plan executable, not drafted — ESMA expects immediate execution.
FounderGeo-fence EU clients now if authorisation will not land by 1 July; serving them unlicensed is the breach.

Update House Signals It Will Move Fast on CLARITY if the Senate Acts Before Recess

"In order for the CLARITY Act to pass in 2026, it probably needs to get through the Senate by the end of July — preferably in June." Brian Gardner, Chief Washington Policy Strategist, Stifel

On 18 June, Rep. Dusty Johnson (R-SD), chair of the House Agriculture Committee's digital-assets subcommittee, said the House would act swiftly on the CLARITY Act if the Senate takes up the market-structure bill before the August recess — a commitment to compress the House timeline to near-zero so the only remaining constraint is Senate floor time.

This picks up the "do or die" window flagged on 17 June. The bill cleared the House 294–134 in July 2025; the Senate Banking Committee advanced its version 15–9 on 14 May (joined by Democrats Gallego of Arizona and Alsobrooks of Maryland) and it has sat on the Senate Legislative Calendar as No. 423 since 1 June. It still needs roughly seven Democratic votes to break the 60-vote threshold, with an unresolved ethics provision on officials' crypto ties hanging over the count.

White House crypto adviser Patrick Witt has publicly targeted 4 July; Stifel and JPMorgan are colder, with JPMorgan reportedly pegging passage odds below 50%. The binding constraint is Senate floor scheduling — not House willingness.

📎 Historical Precedent: FIT21 cleared the House in 2024 with bipartisan votes, then died in the Senate for want of floor time — the exact failure mode CLARITY is now racing.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Johnson's signal removes the bicameral-sequencing excuse: if the Senate passes a clean text, the House conforms rather than conferences, which is what makes a pre-recess signing mathematically possible (House Ag Committee). The Brief's read: the live blocker is the ethics carve-out, not market-structure substance — whoever controls that language controls the seven swing Democrats, and therefore the bill.

🔄 Contrarian Take

JPMorgan and Stifel both see the window closing, and a sub-50% base rate is the honest read of an unscheduled bill needing seven crossover votes. The Brief's read: the optimistic path is not a standalone floor vote but attachment to a must-pass vehicle later this year — possible, but that trades the 4 July headline for a quieter, contingent enactment.

🏗️ Industry Implications

CLARITY's CFTC-centric market-structure regime is the spot-market clarity Coinbase (COIN), Kraken and the broader US exchange complex have lobbied for; its ban on passive stablecoin yield directly hits Coinbase's USDC rewards and reshapes the USDC/RLUSD contest (Circle, Ripple). The Brief's read: passage is a re-rating event for US-listed crypto equities; failure resets the catalyst to 2027.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟩 Bullish COIN and US-listed exchange/infrastructure names on any confirmed Senate floor date.

🔴 Bearish Passive stablecoin-yield models (USDC rewards) if the yield ban survives to enactment.

⚪ Neutral BTC/ETH spot — market structure is a US-equity catalyst more than a token-price one near term.

Brussels Asks Whether MiCA Still Works — 10 Days Before Its Hardest Deadline Bites

The Commission is examining whether MiCA "remains fit for purpose" as traditional financial institutions and asset managers move into crypto-assets, tokenised assets and DLT-based markets. [paraphrased] European Commission — Targeted Consultation on the Review of MiCA, 20 June 2026 framing

The European Commission opened a targeted consultation on the review of MiCA on 20 May, with comments due 31 August 2026 and a report to the Parliament and Council due by 30 June 2027. The review reaches across scope and definitions, stablecoin rules, CASP authorisation, DeFi, staking and NFTs.

The timing is the story: Brussels is interrogating whether the rulebook is fit for purpose in the same fortnight it forces unlicensed firms out under it (Story #1). Skadden's "Fit for Purpose?" note and Freshfields read the review as an early signal that a MiCA 2.0 will widen scope — into DeFi and staking — rather than loosen the existing regime.

📎 Historical Precedent: MiFID's post-implementation review produced MiFID II — a broader, stricter successor — not a rollback; reviews of EU financial frameworks tend to expand them.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

A "fit for purpose" review opened pre-enforcement is the Commission front-running the gaps MiCA already concedes — DeFi, staking, NFTs sit largely outside the current text (Skadden; Freshfields). The Brief's read: firms should respond to the consultation as a rule-shaping opportunity, not a box-tick — the DeFi and staking perimeter being drawn now is the one they will live under for the next decade.

🏗️ Industry Implications

The named driver is institutional entry — BlackRock, asset managers and tokenisation desks pushing into DLT markets that MiCA did not contemplate. The Brief's read: incumbents with compliance scale will lobby to pull DeFi and staking inside the perimeter, raising the barrier for protocol-native players who currently sit outside it.

Bipartisan House Bill Would Rebuild a Federal Crypto-Crime Task Force a Year After DOJ Killed the NCET

"Last year, Americans lost more than $11 billion to crypto theft and scams, and right now, victims have nowhere to turn." Rep. Josh Gottheimer (D-NJ)

On 11 June, Reps. Lance Gooden (R-TX) and Josh Gottheimer (D-NJ) introduced the Federal Cryptocurrency Theft Enforcement and Coordination Act, which would stand up a DOJ-housed task force to coordinate DOJ, DHS, Treasury (including FinCEN) and the FBI on crypto hacks, exploits and fraud, and to support victims through a single federal point of contact.

It cuts against the trajectory of Deputy AG Todd Blanche's April 2025 "Ending Regulation by Prosecution" memo, which disbanded the National Cryptocurrency Enforcement Team (NCET). The measure has not cleared committee, and its odds this session are uncertain.

📎 Historical Precedent: The NCET (2021–2025) was DOJ's prior centralised crypto-crime unit; this bill would legislate a successor rather than leave it to executive discretion.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

The bill draws the line the Trump DOJ has been drawing informally: industry-level "regulation by prosecution" is off, but victim-facing theft and fraud enforcement is on — and Congress wants the latter codified so it survives the next administration (CoinDesk; PYMNTS). The Brief's read: a statutory task force is harder to disband than an executive memo created one, so the real significance is durability, not new powers.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Exchanges and custodians (Coinbase, Kraken) gain a single federal counterpart for incident response, and blockchain-intelligence firms — Chainalysis, TRM Labs, Elliptic — are the natural vendors to a coordinated tracing mandate. The Brief's read: if it advances, expect the task force to lean heavily on private analytics, expanding that procurement pipeline.
