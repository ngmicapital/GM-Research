Microprice: the weighted mid that tells you where the book wants to lean

The mid-price shows where the market is quoted. The microprice shows which side of that quote is under more pressure. When you care about the next few seconds or minutes, that difference matters.

Microprice is the weighted mid that shifts toward the thinner side of the book before the quote itself moves. Today’s BTC order book was ask-heavy, so microprice was a better read than the flat mid.

§01 — What It Is

Microprice asks a better question than the mid: not “where are the quotes?” but “which quote is more likely to get hit next?”

The normal mid-price is just the midpoint between best bid and best ask. That is fine for a static snapshot, but it ignores where the resting size is concentrated.

Microprice adjusts the midpoint toward the side of the book that is thinner and therefore easier to move through. If the ask is much thinner than the bid, the fair short-term price is closer to the ask because less buying pressure is needed to push the market up. If the bid is thin, the microprice leans down.

Classic source: Stoikov’s “The Micro-Price” frames it as a fair price conditional on the state of the order book.

§02 — Why It Matters

The mid can stay frozen while the next move is already being telegraphed by queue imbalance.

This is why traders who care about execution, tape reading, or very short-horizon continuation look at book imbalance and microprice instead of the printed mid alone. A stable quote can be fake calm. If one side is losing queue support, the next move is often baked in before the visible bid/ask changes.

The edge: microprice is not a prediction of the whole day. It is a short-horizon pressure gauge. Use it for timing and context, not for replacing higher-timeframe structure.

The academic support is consistent: queue imbalance carries information about the next tick, and microprice is one clean way to express that information in price terms.

§03 — The Formula

You weight each best quote by the size standing on the opposite side.
microprice = (ask_price × bid_size + bid_price × ask_size) / (bid_size + ask_size)

That “opposite side” weighting is the key. Bigger bid size means more support under price, so the fair price shifts upward toward the ask. Bigger ask size means more overhead supply, so the fair price shifts downward toward the bid.

Bid heavy
Microprice sits above the mid.
Ask heavy
Microprice sits below the mid.
Balanced book
Microprice and mid are nearly the same.

§04 — How To Read It

Do not ask “is microprice up or down?” Ask “is it diverging from the mid, and is that divergence growing?”

The clean read has three steps. First, note the mid. Second, calculate or infer the imbalance at the best bid and ask. Third, watch whether microprice keeps leaning the same way while price stalls. That is the tell that passive liquidity is losing the fight.

Best use cases: confirming a sweep, deciding whether to join a breakout or fade it, and improving passive execution timing when the quote looks balanced but the queue is not.

Current-trader read: microstructure practitioners increasingly treat microprice as a way to translate raw queue imbalance into a more intuitive “fair next price” signal.

§06 — Common Traps

Microprice is powerful, but traders overhype it when they forget latency, spoofing, and timeframe.

Trap 1: treating it like a swing-trading signal. It decays fast.

Trap 2: ignoring fake size. If the queue is spoofed or constantly canceled, the signal degrades.

Trap 3: using only level-1 data in a market where depth two or three levels back matters more.

Overhyped claim to reject: “microprice tells you the next move with certainty.” It does not. It improves your odds at a very short horizon; it does not abolish uncertainty.

§07 — Trader Checklist

Before you act on microprice, force it through a basic sanity filter.
  1. Is the market a tight-spread, high-liquidity environment where queue changes actually mean something?
  2. Is microprice diverging from the mid, or are they effectively identical?
  3. Is the divergence confirmed by taker aggression, not contradicted by it?
  4. Are you inside a meaningful structure level, or reacting to noise in the middle of nowhere?
  5. Is your holding period short enough for microprice to matter?
