Alpha - Microprice

Wednesday, 24 June 2026 · Sydney · Issue 81 · 7 min read
TL;DR

Microprice is the midpoint adjusted by queue imbalance, so it leans toward the side of the book most likely to get hit next. When the printed mid says “balanced” but the resting size is lopsided, microprice is usually the cleaner read for the next tick.

§01
What It Is
Fair value with queue pressure
Microprice answers a simple question: if the best bid and best ask are not equally defended, where is the true short-horizon fair value actually leaning?

The quoted midpoint assumes both sides of the book matter equally. Real books are rarely that symmetric. If the bid queue is thin and the ask queue is heavy, the next aggressive seller is more likely to push price lower, so the “real” fair value is already leaning below the printed mid.

§02
Mechanics
Weight the price by opposite-side size
The smaller the bid queue relative to the ask queue, the more microprice shifts down; the smaller the ask queue, the more it shifts up.
microprice = (ask × bid_size + bid × ask_size) / (bid_size + ask_size)

The weighting looks backwards at first glance, but that is the point. A large ask queue gives the bid more informational weight because the offer is harder to lift. A thin ask queue gives the ask more urgency because it can disappear or get swept quickly.

What you are estimating is not “where value should be in theory,” but “which side gets exhausted first if the current flow persists.” That makes microprice most useful for execution, scalps, passive quoting, and deciding whether a flat midpoint is actually neutral.

§03
Visual
Why the midpoint can lie
Same bid/ask prices, different queue sizes: the midpoint does not change, but the microprice does.
If the offer is thinner than the bid, the fair value is already leaning above the simple mid because the ask is easier to lift.
§04
In Practice
Execution and short-horizon read
Use microprice when you care about whether to quote, cross, or wait one more beat.
1. If you are working a passive buy, a microprice below mid says your current queue is more likely to get run over than filled safely.
2. If price is flat but microprice keeps leaning one way while aggressive flow agrees, the next break is usually not random.
3. Do not use it alone in thin books. Pair it with trade classification, sweep activity, and whether size reloads after being hit.

§07
Common Traps
Where traders misuse it
The biggest mistake is treating microprice as prophecy instead of a conditional read on queue fragility.
Trap 1. Using stale book data. If your depth snapshot lags, microprice becomes historical decoration.
Trap 2. Ignoring reloads. A thin ask that instantly replenishes is not actually weak.
Trap 3. Overhyping it. Microprice is excellent for next-tick bias, not for inventing a 4-hour swing thesis from one queue snapshot.
