Alpha — Open Drive & Open-Type Auctions

Friday · 22 May 2026 · Sydney, Australia · Issue #28 12-min read
TL;DR

Open-Type Auctions is Dalton's taxonomy of the four ways a trading day begins — Open-Drive, Open-Test-Drive, Open-Rejection-Reverse, and Open-Auction — ranked by the confidence of the participants who set the tone. The practical edge: the first 30 minutes of a session are not noise to wait out; they are a probabilistic readout of who is in control and what kind of day to expect. Knowing the open type lets you size up on trend days and stay small on rotation days — most of the work is done before the Initial Balance closes.

§01
What It Is
Dalton's four open archetypes · confidence as the ranking axis
An open type is the price-action signature of the first minutes of a session — a one-glance readout of how confident the dominant participants are about the direction of the day.

James Dalton, in Mind Over Markets and later Markets in Profile, formalised what Pete Steidlmayer's Market Profile work had been pointing at since the 1980s: the open is not a single tick on a chart — it is an auction with a personality. That personality is set in the first 5–30 minutes by who shows up willing to pay (or accept) the spread immediately. The faster and more one-sided that initial commitment, the more reliable the day-type forecast that follows.

Dalton sorts those personalities into four archetypes, ranked by descending confidence. Open-Drive is the highest-conviction open: "Highest confidence opening which is caused by participants who have made their trade decisions before the opening." The market opens and immediately auctions in one direction, never trading back through the opening price. Open-Test-Drive opens, tests beyond a known reference (yesterday's high/low, value-area edge) to make sure no business exists there, then reverses and drives. Open-Rejection-Reverse opens, trades in one direction, then meets enough opposite size to reverse back through the opening range — initial extremes hold only about half the time. Open-Auction is conviction-less: price oscillates inside the open range with no committed side, in-range or out-of-range. (Dalton, Mind Over Markets summary)

Why this matters for crypto: even though BTC trades 24/7 with no canonical "open", the framework applies cleanly to every regime-defining auction window — the weekly open (Mon 00:00 UTC), the CME futures Sunday-night open, the US cash equity open (9:30 ET), the Asian session open (Tokyo 09:00 JST), and the daily candle (00:00 UTC). Each is an auction with a personality. Sydney-based, that puts the Asian open at ~10:00 AEST and the US open at ~23:30 AEST — your two highest-information windows.

The reason the framework holds: it is not really about time; it is about commitment. The open is when participants who have been deciding overnight finally have to act. The shape of their action — aggressive and one-sided, hesitant then aggressive, indecisive and reversed, or absent altogether — is a forensic readout of their conviction. Confidence at the open predicts day type. That is the whole game.

If you only remember one thing The open is a confidence readout, not a time. Read the first 30 minutes for who is committed and which side they are committed to — the day's range, structure, and trend probability are encoded right there.
§02
Mechanics
Classification rules · confidence ranking · day-type linkage
Classification uses three measurements: distance from the opening price, whether price trades back through the open, and how that move resolves relative to a known reference level.

The classification is mechanical. You need three things in the first 15–30 minutes: the opening price (O), the initial directional thrust, and the relationship of subsequent price to O and to a known reference (prior-day high/low, prior value area edge, weekly open). From those three you can place the open into one of four buckets within minutes.

Classification logic (first 15–30 min after open at O): IF price drives directionally AND never returns through O → OPEN-DRIVE IF price tests a reference (PDH/PDL/VAH/VAL), rejects, reverses through O → OPEN-TEST-DRIVE IF price moves one direction, then reverses back through O on opposing size → OPEN-REJECTION-REVERSE IF price oscillates inside open range with no committed side → OPEN-AUCTION Confidence ranking (Dalton): Open-Drive (highest) > Open-Test-Drive > Open-Rejection-Reverse > Open-Auction (lowest) Reference levels for crypto open classification: • PDH/PDL — prior day's high / low • VAH/VAL — prior day's value area edges (70% volume zone) • WO — weekly open price (Monday 00:00 UTC) • ON-H/ON-L — overnight session high / low
Note: In crypto the "open" is whichever session boundary you anchor to. Most institutional desks classify against the CME futures Sunday open or the daily UTC reset. Retail-leaning traders often anchor to the Monday weekly open. Pick one and stay consistent within a system.

Each open type maps probabilistically to a day type. Open-Drive overwhelmingly precedes trend days — the kind of day where the Initial Balance is broken in the direction of the drive and never recovered. Open-Test-Drive typically leads to trending days after the initial fake-out — the test confirms no opposing business exists, so the drive that follows has nothing in its way. Open-Rejection-Reverse often produces neutral or normal-variation days — initial direction was wrong, but the reversal itself defines structure. Open-Auction tends toward balancing days with rotation inside value, narrow range, and low realised volatility.

The mechanic underneath all four is the same: the Initial Balance is being built by participants whose conviction is being measured by their willingness to keep paying the spread. An Open-Drive shows continuous one-sided spread-paying. An Open-Test-Drive shows that conviction after a brief look. An Open-Rejection-Reverse shows the initial conviction was met by stronger opposing conviction. An Open-Auction shows no real conviction either way.

A practical statistical anchor: trend days are less than 10% of all sessions on most major instruments — which is exactly why correctly identifying the Open-Drive matters so much. (ATAS, Open Drive guide) When you spot one, the asymmetry is yours to take. When you mistake an Open-Rejection-Reverse for an Open-Drive, you pay for the lesson with stops on the wrong side of the day.

§04
In Practice
Pre-open checklist · classification process · worked entry
The one rule: classify the open in writing within 30 minutes of the auction window — if you can't put it in one of four buckets by then, the day is an Open-Auction and you trade rotation, not trend.
1

Mark Reference Levels Before the Open

Before any session boundary you intend to trade — Asian open, US open, Sunday CME open, Monday weekly open — mark prior-day high (PDH), prior-day low (PDL), VAH/VAL, and the relevant reference open (weekly or daily). Without these you cannot classify a Test-Drive or distinguish in-range from out-of-range Auction. The classification is mechanical only if the levels are pre-marked.

2

Classify Within 30 Minutes — In Writing

At the open, watch the first 15–30 minutes and write down which of the four it is. "BTC weekly open — Drive long, never touched O, ran 1.2% in 18 min." That single sentence is the trade plan. If you cannot classify it cleanly, default to Open-Auction and trade rotation only. Indecision is itself the signal.

3

Size by Open Type, Not by Setup Quality

Open-Drive days deserve full size — the asymmetry is rare and the rest of the day usually moves in one direction. Open-Test-Drive deserves full size after the test fails. Open-Rejection-Reverse deserves half size — the initial extreme holds only ~50% of the time. Open-Auction deserves a quarter or no trade — chop costs more than it pays. The same chart pattern is a different trade depending on which open type wraps it.

4

Stop Behind the Most Reliable Extreme

On Open-Drive: stop just beyond the opening price (price never returned through O — if it does, the Drive is invalid). On Open-Test-Drive: stop just beyond the test extreme. On Open-Rejection-Reverse: stop just beyond the rejected extreme. On Open-Auction: stop just outside IB. Each open type has a structurally meaningful invalidation level — using a fixed % stop ignores the information the open is giving you.

Worked Example — BTC US-Open Drive Long
Pre-open setupBTC closed Asia session at $77,420. PDH = $78,010. PDL = $76,750. VAH = $77,810. Weekly open (Mon) = $77,200. Marking these on the chart before 23:30 AEST.
The open (23:30 AEST / 09:30 ET)BTC opens at $77,520 and immediately drives. First 5 candles are all green with no overlap below the open. By 23:48 AEST price is at $78,050 — through PDH, never traded below $77,520.
ClassificationOpen-Drive long. Highest-confidence open. Initial extreme = O = $77,520 — the most reliable session low.
EntryWait for first pullback to the IB midpoint (~$77,780) — enter long at $77,790. Drive-day pullbacks are shallow; don't wait for deep retraces that won't come.
StopBelow O at $77,490 — invalidates the Drive classification. Risk = $300/BTC (~0.38%).
Target2× IB range projection from the high of IB. If IB high = $78,100, IB low = $77,520, range = $580 → target = $78,680. R:R = ~3.0.
InvalidationPrice closes back through O at $77,520. Drive is no longer a Drive; trade thesis is dead.

Timeframe fit: 1m–5m to classify the open in the first 15–30 minutes. 15m to manage the trade through the rest of the IB hour. 1h–4h for higher-timeframe bias filter (don't take Drive longs against a 4h downtrend; the Drive is more likely a failed bounce). Best venues to apply: CME Sunday open, US cash open (09:30 ET), Asian open (Tokyo 09:00 JST), weekly open (Mon 00:00 UTC), daily candle (00:00 UTC).

● Pattern Replay · Memorable Open-Drives

What an Open-Drive Looks Like When It Fires

The cleanest crypto-native Open-Drives come around CME-handoff windows and macro-event opens. Two recent ones worth memorising as templates:

August 5, 2024 — Sunday CME open (post-yen-carry unwind). BTC opened the CME Sunday session sharply lower vs Friday's close and drove down for the entire Asian and London handoff without printing back through the Sunday open. Textbook Open-Drive short — IB extreme (the high) was set in the first 5 minutes and held all session. Anyone who classified the open correctly was on the right side of a multi-percent move with the cleanest stop available all year (above the Sunday open).

November 6, 2024 — US cash open (post-election). S&P futures and BTC both opened sharply higher and drove for the entire US session without returning through the cash open. BTC moved from ~$69,500 at the open to ~$74,000 by close. The Drive classification was obvious by 09:45 ET — the first 15 candles never overlapped the opening price. Sized correctly, that was a 6%+ day on the right side of the auction with the stop below the cash open.

PatternOne-sided drive, no return through O for ≥30 minutes
ConfirmationFirst 5–10 candles all close on the drive side · no candle wicks back through O
StopJust beyond O — Drive invalidates if O is broken
SizingFull position — Drives are the highest-confidence open and reward conviction
Frequency realityTrend days < 10% of sessions (Dalton/ATAS) — rare and worth waiting for
Read Open-Drives are the asymmetric trade of the framework. They are rare — most sessions are Auction — but when they fire, the stop is structurally tight and the target is the day's full range. Misidentify them and you stand aside on the cleanest day of the month. Identify them and one trade can carry the week.

§07
Common Traps
Five ways traders misread the open
The single biggest mistake: classifying every fast move off the open as an Open-Drive — most fast moves are Open-Rejection-Reverses that look like Drives until they reverse.
1
Calling Every Fast Move a Drive

A Drive requires that price never trades back through the opening price. A fast move that touches O even once is not a Drive — it is the start of an Open-Rejection-Reverse. Waiting the full 15–30 minutes before classifying costs you a few ticks of entry but saves you the cost of being sized up on a fake.

2
Ignoring Higher-Timeframe Context

An Open-Drive long inside a confirmed 4h downtrend is statistically much more likely to be a violent failed bounce that turns into an Open-Rejection-Reverse than a genuine trend-day Drive. The open type is a session-level signal — the higher-timeframe regime gates whether to act on it at full size or reduce.

3
Forcing Trades on Open-Auction Days

Open-Auction days are the most common day type and the worst for directional trading. Most P&L damage in this framework comes from trying to force a Drive read onto an Auction. If by 30 minutes after the open the price has touched the opening price three or more times, you are in an Auction — stand aside or trade rotation back to the midpoint, never both sides of a breakout.

4
Using Random Stops Instead of Structural Stops

Each open type has its own structurally meaningful invalidation level — O for Drive, the test extreme for Test-Drive, the rejected extreme for Rej-Reverse, the IB extreme for Auction. Using a fixed % or fixed dollar stop ignores the information the open is giving you and ensures you get stopped on noise inside the structurally valid level.

5
Pretending 24/7 Crypto Has "One Open"

There is no single canonical open in crypto. The framework works against any auction boundary you anchor to — CME Sunday open, US cash open, Asian open, daily UTC reset, weekly open — but you must pick one and apply it consistently. Mixing references mid-week (classifying against US open on Mondays but daily UTC on Tuesdays) destroys the statistical signal. Choose the boundary that matches your trading hours and stick with it. Honest read: the framework is more powerful in regulated, session-bound markets where the open is sharp; in crypto it is still useful, but the edges are softer because the auction handoff is more diffuse.
