Alpha — Effort vs Result

Thursday · 11 June 2026 · Sydney, Australia · Issue #46 11 min read
TL;DR

Effort vs Result is Wyckoff's third law: volume is the effort, price progress is the result, and your entire edge lives in the bars where the two disagree. When huge volume fails to move price, someone large is taking the other side — that absorption, not the trend, is the signal worth trading. Right now it is live: BTC is holding 61.7K with below-average down-volume while macro throws everything bearish at it, the textbook effort-without-result divergence.

§01
What It Is
Wyckoff's third law
Volume is the effort; the price move it produces is the result — and the trade is in the bars where big effort yields a small result, or a small effort yields a big one.

Stop reading every candle as a verdict and start reading it as a transaction. A bar is effort spent (the volume) buying a result (the price progress). Wyckoff's third law says those two should be proportional: real conviction shows up as volume and as the price travelling. When they line up, the move is genuine. When they don't, you've found the most useful object on the chart — an anomaly.

The framing is deliberately physical. Anna Coulling calls it Newton's third law for markets: every action has an equal and opposite reaction, so the price action above must reflect the volume action below. If a bar prints the highest volume of the session and barely closes off its low, that effort went somewhere — it was absorbed by a passive party sitting on the other side. The crowd supplied the aggression; a larger operator quietly supplied the liquidity. Price didn't move because someone made sure it didn't.

This is the law beneath methods you already know. VSA is Tom Williams' bar-by-bar codification of effort vs result. Delta divergence is the same idea read through aggressor-side volume instead of total volume. Absorption is effort-vs-result at a single price level on the book. Learn the law and those tools stop being separate tricks — they're three lenses on one question: did the effort buy what it should have?

If you only remember one thing Effort and result agreeing tells you nothing new — it just confirms the trend. The money is in the disagreement: maximum effort that fails to produce a result is the market telling you who is really in control.
§02
The Mechanics
Reading effort against result
Measure effort as volume, measure result as spread plus where the bar closed, then classify the relationship — four readings cover almost every case.

The mistake beginners make is treating "result" as direction only. Result has two components: how far price travelled (the spread, high to low) and where it finished (the close location). A wide bar that closes back in the middle of its range is not the same result as a wide bar that closes on its extreme — the first is rejection, the second is follow-through. You have to read both.

EFFORT = volume on the bar (participation, conviction)
RESULT = price spread × close location (how far, and did it hold)

HARMONY high effort → wide spread, close on the extreme  ✓ trend continues
DIVERGENCE high effort → narrow spread / close rejected    ✗ absorption — size is taking it
DIVERGENCE low effort → wide spread, fast move            ✗ no participation, prone to snap back
There is no fixed threshold for "high" — effort is always relative to the bar's own recent average (e.g. volume vs its 20-bar mean).

Walk it through. In a healthy downtrend, each leg down comes on rising volume and wide spreads closing near the lows — harmony, supply in control, keep your shorts. Then a bar prints the largest volume yet but the spread narrows and it closes off the low: that is maximum effort with no result. The selling didn't stop because sellers vanished — it stopped because someone with size stood there and bought every contract thrown at them. That is stopping action, and it is the first evidence the trend is being defended against.

The inverse matters too. A wide bullish bar on collapsing volume looks like a breakout but is a hollow one — nobody is participating, so there's no fuel behind it. These "move on no effort" bars are fade candidates, not chase candidates; they typically retrace because the result wasn't paid for. [Sources differ] on naming — VSA calls the low-volume tests "no supply" and "no demand" bars; the underlying read is identical.

§03
The Picture
Harmony, divergence, absorption
One sequence tells the whole story: a genuine downtrend, a climactic effort bar that produces no result, then a quiet no-supply test before demand takes over.
A downtrend in harmony (rising effort, falling result) runs into a climactic bar where the largest volume of the move produces no new downside — the prior low is undercut and reclaimed. That is absorption. The quiet, low-volume bars that follow (no supply) confirm sellers are exhausted before demand takes price back up.
§04
In Practice
Trading the divergence
Don't trade the absorption bar — trade the follow-through that fails to extend it; the anomaly is the alert, the next bar is the trigger.
1

Hunt anomalies, not every candle

You are not grading all 200 bars on screen. You are waiting for the one where effort and result disagree at a level that matters — a high-volume bar that won't extend the trend, or a fast bar nobody participated in. Everything else is harmony, which only confirms what you already know.

2

Result is spread and close location

High volume with a wide bar that closes on its low is a genuine result — stay short. The same volume with a narrow bar that closes back in range is no-result — that's your absorption. The close location is what separates a continuation from a reversal.

3

Wait for the failure to extend

One high-volume narrow bar is a hypothesis. It becomes a signal only when the next bar can't make a new low (or new high) and starts closing the other way. Absorption with no follow-through is just noise; absorption confirmed by a refusal to extend is a trade.

4

Weight it by context

Effort vs result at a fresh range low, on a swept liquidity pocket, with stops piled beneath, is worth ten times the same read in the mid-range. Read the law where prior structure and resting liquidity make the absorption meaningful.

Worked example — illustrative, keyed to current BTC levels

From Wyckoff to the modern desk
The law is a century old and its modern interpreters say the same thing — the disagreement between volume and price is the message.

“Volume represents effort while price represents result. Without effort there can be no result.”

Richard D. Wyckoff · The Third Law (Effort vs Result) · Wyckoff Stock Market Institute · c.1930s · wyckoffsmi.com

§07
Common Traps
Where it breaks
The single biggest mistake: calling absorption on one bar and entering before price has refused to extend it.
1
Trading the anomaly bar instead of the failure

A high-volume narrow bar is a hypothesis, not an entry. Wait for the follow-through bar to fail to make a new extreme. Front-running absorption is how you get run over by the flush that actually had a result.

2
Ignoring close location

Volume plus a narrow range means nothing on its own. A bar that closes on its low is result; one that closes back inside is no-result. Same volume, opposite signal — the close is the whole tell.

3
Trusting dirty crypto volume

Wash trading, fragmented venues, and spot-vs-perp double counting make "high volume" far noisier than in equities. Anchor your effort read to one clean reference — a single major venue or a properly aggregated feed — not a random exchange's print.

4
Reading a hollow move as strength

A wide bar on collapsing volume is weakness wearing a breakout costume. The result wasn't paid for, so it usually snaps back. Don't chase wide bars that nobody participated in.

5
The "smart-money absorption" repackaging (overhyped)

Effort vs result gets resold constantly as a proprietary "institutional absorption" indicator with a fresh coat of paint. It's a 90-year-old volume heuristic, not a secret algorithm. The edge isn't the label or the indicator — it's reading close location in context and waiting for confirmation. Anyone selling you the signal as magic is selling the wrapper.
