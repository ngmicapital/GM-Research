Driving Thesis

A 2025 pilot RCT published in GeroScience (Espinoza et al.) is the first human trial to directly measure autophagic flux — the actual throughput of cellular recycling machinery — during a fasting-mimicking diet. Thirty healthy adults completed an 8-day trial with a single 5-day ProLon FMD cycle. The standard ProLon protocol produced a detectable rise in LC3B-II/LC3B-I ratio (the canonical autophagy biomarker) in peripheral blood mononuclear cells, with the effect persisting two days post-intervention; metabolic improvements included fasting glucose −13 mg/dL, HOMA-IR −1.5 units, and BHB +1.0 mmol/L. Separately, secondary analysis of a multi-cycle trial showed 3 FMD cycles associated with 2.5 years of biological age reduction. On the training side, a January 2026 systematic review of 20 trials (400+ participants) in BMJ Open Sport & Exercise Medicine confirms accentuated eccentric loading (AEL) at 110–120% of concentric 1RM delivers 9–22% maximal strength gains and 4–11% jump performance improvements — without requiring additional hypertrophy stimulus beyond conventional training.

The supplements picture also sharpened: a 2026 meta-analysis of 20 studies found collagen peptides significantly improve bone mineral density at the femoral neck and spine (SMD 0.46–1.74), with virtually no heterogeneity when combined with calcium and vitamin D (I²=0%). Astaxanthin's neuroprotection case strengthened with a February 2026 Frontiers review mapping its multi-pathway action — Nrf2/ARE antioxidant signalling, BDNF/TrkB-CREB synaptic plasticity, and NF-κB inflammatory suppression — though clinical evidence in healthy adults remains small-n and short-term. The POLYCAD trial (n=187 elderly CAD patients) testing 24 mg/day spermidine for 48 weeks is completing final visits in August 2026 — the first high-dose human data on spermidine's cardiac and lean-mass outcomes.

Section 01
Longevity & Performance Science

The central question in autophagy research has always been: does any dietary intervention actually switch on cellular recycling in living humans, not just in rodent models? A 2025 GeroScience pilot RCT now offers the first direct answer. Thirty healthy adults completed a single 5-day ProLon fasting-mimicking diet cycle; autophagic flux was measured in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) using the LC3B-II/LC3B-I protein ratio — the gold-standard readout of autophagosome throughput — assayed with and without chloroquine blockade ex vivo. The standard ProLon diet produced a measurable increase in autophagic flux that persisted two days after refeeding. Separately, secondary analysis of a multi-cycle RCT linked three 5-day FMD cycles across several months to a median 2.5-year reduction in biological age independent of weight loss, with improvements in insulin resistance, hepatic fat, and immune-cell composition.

First human RCT confirms FMD activates autophagic flux — and the effect outlasts the fast
Human RCT
Espinoza et al. — GeroScience, 2025

Design: 30 healthy adults randomised to ProLon FMD, a modified FMD2 formulation, or normal eating for 8 days (single 5-day cycle embedded). Autophagic flux measured in PBMCs via LC3B-II/LC3B-I with chloroquine challenge at 4 time points.

Key outcomes (ProLon arm): Autophagic flux elevated during and two days post-fast. Body weight −1.7 kg vs +0.2 kg control. Fasting glucose −13 mg/dL. Fasting insulin −5.0 mIU/L. HOMA-IR −1.5 units. β-hydroxybutyrate +1.0 mmol/L (confirming metabolic switch to fat oxidation). The modified FMD2 produced transient autophagy effects during fasting only; control showed none.

Mechanism: mTORC1 suppression via amino-acid restriction and ketone elevation activates ULK1, initiating phagophore formation. The ProLon macronutrient composition (low-protein, low-carb, specific caloric ramp) is designed to maximise this suppression window without triggering acute stress adaptation that would blunt the signal.

Caveat: n=30, pilot study, single cycle. Authors note findings are preliminary and cannot yet establish whether repeated cycles produce disease-relevant outcomes. But it is the first human data measuring actual flux — not merely upstream pathway markers.

Three FMD cycles linked to 2.5-year biological age reduction — independent of weight loss
Human RCT (secondary analysis)
Brandhorst et al. — Nature Communications, 2024 / USC Longevity Institute, 2025

Secondary analysis of a multi-site RCT: participants completing 3 ProLon cycles showed a median −2.5 years in biological age (epigenetic clocks + metabolic composite). Changes included reduced hepatic fat, lower insulin resistance, and an improved lymphoid-to-myeloid ratio — a marker of immune aging that tracks T-cell and NK-cell abundance relative to pro-inflammatory myeloid cells. The biological age reduction held after controlling for weight change, suggesting the caloric architecture — not simply the caloric deficit — drives the effect.

⚡ Implication: One 5-day ProLon cycle per month is the Valter Longo lab's recommended frequency for healthy adults; the data now support this as a verified autophagy-induction protocol, not just a theoretical one. If you run a PPL split or any periodised training, schedule the FMD cycle during a planned deload week — amino-acid restriction during a high-volume block suppresses mTOR when you need it active for muscle protein synthesis.
Section 02
Training & Performance

Accentuated eccentric loading (AEL) exploits a fundamental asymmetry in muscle physiology: a muscle can resist more force eccentrically (lengthening) than it can produce concentrically (shortening). AEL protocols use this by applying a load at 110–120% of concentric 1RM during the lowering phase — via weight releasers, band-assisted setups, or a training partner manually adding resistance — while returning to a manageable concentric load. A January 2026 systematic review synthesised 20 trials across lower-body training, covering strength, power, speed, and hypertrophy outcomes in predominantly trained athletes.

AEL at 110–120% 1RM: 9–22% strength gains and better jump performance — but not extra hypertrophy
Systematic Review
PMC 2026 — Accentuated Eccentric Loading Review (20 trials, 400+ participants)

Strength: Chronic AEL produced maximal strength gains of 9–22% across protocols, with moderate-to-large effect sizes, consistently matching or exceeding traditional resistance training. The eccentric-phase neural drive and tendon mechanical stimulus appear to drive gains independent of hypertrophy.

Jump performance: Vertical jump improved 4–11% with chronic AEL. Acute AEL (a single session) produced post-activation potentiation (PAP) effects — transient performance spikes — suggesting utility as a warm-up protocol before power sessions.

Speed and change-of-direction: One study reported up to 4% sprint time reduction, but reviewers rated this evidence insufficient to draw conclusions. Transfer to sport-specific speed remains unproven.

Hypertrophy: Here AEL offers no advantage — muscle cross-sectional area increased 10–15% in both AEL and conventional training groups at 8–10 weeks. If hypertrophy is the primary goal, standard progressive overload is equally effective and simpler to programme.

Practical protocol: Use weight releasers (adding 10–30% body mass) or band-assisted eccentrics. Keep eccentric tempo controlled (3–4 sec). Programme AEL for squat, Romanian deadlift, and Nordic curl — exercises with a clear eccentric-phase identity. 2–3 sets of 4–6 reps at 110–120% is the dose used across reviewed trials.

Use AEL for
  • Maximal strength blocks (periodisation phase targeting 1RM)
  • Plyometric and jump-performance programmes
  • Breaking strength plateaus on compound lifts
  • Post-activation potentiation warm-up before power output sessions
Don't expect AEL to deliver
  • Greater hypertrophy than conventional training
  • Reliable speed/COD transfer (evidence base too thin)
  • Benefit without controlled eccentric tempo (uncontrolled lowering loses the stimulus)
  • Easy programming — requires spotters, bands, or releasing mechanisms
⚡ Implication: For a PPL programme targeting both strength and hypertrophy, AEL fits best in the Leg/Pull days during a dedicated strength phase (4–6 weeks), not a volume hypertrophy block. Pair it with conventional progressive overload for the concentric sets rather than replacing the whole session.
Section 03
Supplements & Nutrition
Collagen peptides + Ca + D3: consistent bone mineral density gains with near-zero heterogeneity
Meta-analysis (20 studies)
Frontiers in Nutrition, 2026

Findings: 20 studies (17 RCTs) across 10 countries, primarily postmenopausal women aged 20–61. Collagen peptides alone improved femoral neck BMD (SMD 0.46) and spine BMD (SMD 0.58–1.74 across trials; heterogeneity I²=80.1% alone). When combined with calcium and vitamin D, heterogeneity collapsed to I²=0% and effect sizes stabilised at SMD 0.40–0.56 — a strong signal for synergistic action. Authors conclude collagen peptides are viable as adjunct therapy for osteopenia/osteoporosis management.

Mechanism: Collagen peptides supply hydroxyproline-proline dipeptides absorbed intact; these stimulate osteoblast differentiation and reduce osteoclast activity. The Ca+D3 combination adds the mineralisation substrate and vitamin D receptor signalling needed to actually deposit the new collagen matrix as bone.

Dose from trials: 5–15 g/day specific collagen peptides (type I, hydrolysed), typically morning, alongside calcium carbonate/citrate and 800–2000 IU vitamin D3. Evidence strongest in postmenopausal women and osteopenic adults; data in trained healthy males is sparse.

Evidence rating: Meta-analysis — meaningful signal for at-risk populations; reasonable to add for bone maintenance even in healthy adults given the safety profile.

Astaxanthin: multi-pathway neuroprotection confirmed in review — clinical evidence still limited
Narrative Review
Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience, February 2026

Mechanism summary: Astaxanthin (a marine xanthophyll carotenoid from Haematococcus pluvialis) crosses the blood-brain barrier — unlike most carotenoids — and neutralises reactive oxygen species in both aqueous and lipid-soluble compartments simultaneously. Four named pathways confirmed active in preclinical models: (1) Nrf2/ARE upregulation of antioxidant enzymes; (2) NF-κB suppression reducing IL-6 and TNF-α; (3) BDNF/TrkB-CREB enhancement of synaptic plasticity and neurogenesis; (4) PI3K/Akt pro-survival signalling. Also enhances autophagy and proteasomal clearance of misfolded proteins.

Human RCT data: Four small RCTs (n=207 total, middle-aged to older adults): 9 mg/day × 12 weeks showed significant improvements in memory domains and verbal memory in two Japanese trials. A combined astaxanthin + sesamin protocol showed psychomotor and processing-speed gains. A 2026 clinical trial from University of North Alabama (results posted May 2026) tested cognitive effects in recreationally active females post-mental fatigue.

Current rating: Promising preclinical mechanism base; human evidence is small-n, short-duration, and heterogeneous. Dose signal: 6–12 mg/day. Worth tracking but not yet a Tier 1 recommendation for healthy adults. Expert Review

POLYCAD: first high-dose spermidine trial in humans completing August 2026 — 24 mg/day × 48 weeks
Phase II RCT (in progress)
PMC 2025 — POLYCAD Protocol

Design: 187 elderly patients (≥65, established coronary artery disease) randomised to spermidine 24 mg/day or placebo for 48 weeks. Four co-primary endpoints: (1) LV mass via cardiac MRI; (2) peak VO2 via cardiopulmonary exercise test; (3) appendicular lean mass via DEXA; (4) hsCRP. This dose is 3–10× higher than most commercial supplements.

Status: Recruitment closed August 2025; final study visits expected August 2026. This will be the first RCT-grade evidence for high-dose spermidine's effect on cardiac remodelling and lean mass in humans — the endpoints most relevant to healthy-aging practitioners.

Current evidence base: Epidemiological associations support dietary spermidine intake correlating with cardiovascular longevity; animal studies show lifespan extension via autophagy induction. Human outcomes data at therapeutic doses has been absent until now. Awaiting Results
