Section 01
Longevity & Performance Science

Three converging signals this cycle: (1) the rate of epigenetic aging, not its absolute level, predicts who dies earlier — which means a one-time biological age test tells you less than two tests five years apart; (2) the largest human rapamycin trial ever is now running, betting that mTOR inhibition at 8 mg/week can push back the frailty transition; (3) metformin’s exercise-blunting effect is now quantified precisely enough that the calculus has shifted — for anyone doing serious resistance training, the lean mass cost likely exceeds the longevity signal.

The practical implication: track the slope, not the snapshot. DunedinPACE is the clock to adopt for longitudinal self-monitoring. And if you’re debating rapamycin, the Arizona trial’s IL-6 and frailty endpoints should produce meaningful data within 18 months.

Epigenetic Clock Acceleration Predicts Mortality Independent of Age — Nature Aging, 2026
Longitudinal Cohort

Kuo et al. tracked 699 adults from the InCHIANTI cohort (Chianti region, Italy) across 24 years, collecting DNA methylation samples at three timepoints (1998, 2007, 2013). The key variable was not biological age at any single measurement but the velocity at which epigenetic clocks were advancing — calculated as year-over-year rate of change.

Finding: Faster acceleration in epigenetic clocks was strongly linked to higher all-cause mortality, independent of chronological age at study entry. Biological aging did not progress uniformly; for participants with the steepest acceleration curves, the clock sped up further as they aged.

Practical implication: A single biological age test is insufficient. The clinically actionable tools in 2026 are DunedinPACE (measures current rate of aging) and GrimAge (predicts all-cause mortality risk). Lifestyle factors confirmed to slow DunedinPACE: physical activity, healthier diet. Accelerants: smoking, high BMI, elevated glucose, poor blood pressure control (per a parallel December 2025 multi-cohort study).

Source: Kuo et al. · Nature Aging · DOI 10.1038/s43587-026-01066-6 · InCHIANTI Cohort, Italy
University of Arizona Rapamycin Trial — 720 Participants, Now Underway
Human RCT

The largest human rapamycin longevity trial in history is actively enrolling. Led by Dr. Bonnie LaFleur (University of Arizona, Biostatistics), 720 participants aged 65+ are receiving 8 mg/week sirolimus (brand: Rapamune) for 2 years, with a third year of follow-up.

Dosing rationale: 8 mg/week was selected based on pharmacokinetic data showing measurable mTOR pathway engagement while staying below transplant-level immunosuppression. The intermittent weekly protocol is designed to suppress mTORC1 while sparing mTORC2 — avoiding the metabolic side effects (insulin resistance, immune compromise) seen with continuous high dosing.

Primary endpoints: Transition from pre-frail to frail state; IL-6 reduction. Secondary: epigenetic clocks (including DunedinPACE), lung function, joint health, immune profiling via spectral flow cytometry. The preliminary pharmacokinetic run in 50 participants confirmed target engagement and safety.

Context: The PEARL trial (2025, 48-week, 5 mg or 10 mg/week) found no significant reduction in visceral fat (primary outcome) but established useful safety data and some secondary signals.

Source: Healthspan Research · University of Arizona Trial Overview · 2026
Metformin Blunts Resistance Training Gains: Lean Mass Suppression Now Quantified
Human RCT

Multiple controlled studies have now converged on the same finding. In resistance-trained participants, placebo gained 1.95% lean mass versus 0.41% for metformin users over comparable training periods. Thigh muscle mass: 3.90% gain (placebo) vs. 0.45% (metformin). The mechanism is metformin’s inhibition of skeletal muscle mitochondrial respiration and cardiorespiratory fitness adaptations.

Peter Attia stopped taking metformin based on this evidence, noting that for non-diabetic individuals doing regular exercise, the exercise-blunting effect outweighs the putative longevity benefit. The TAME trial (Targeting Aging with Metformin) continues, but its primary population is adults not optimizing for exercise performance.

Source: Peter Attia MD · peterattiamd.com · Metformin and Exercise
⚡ Implication: Run DunedinPACE longitudinally, not once. For rapamycin: the Arizona trial’s 720-person size and epigenetic endpoints should finally provide a signal worth acting on — watch for interim data in late 2027. For metformin: if you train seriously (PPL or equivalent), the lean mass cost is now too well-quantified to ignore. The drug may still be appropriate for sedentary or insulin-resistant individuals.
Section 02
Training & Performance

Two findings this cycle: (1) cluster sets vs traditional sets produce equivalent hypertrophy when volume and proximity-to-failure are matched — meaning set structure is less important than total effort; and (2) the timing advantage of early time-restricted eating is smaller than claimed — a 2026 Science Translational Medicine RCT found no difference in insulin sensitivity between early and late eating windows when calories were equated.

Cluster Sets vs. Traditional Sets: Similar Hypertrophy When Volume and Effort Are Matched — PMC12174233, 2026
Human RCT

An 8-week study in resistance-trained adults directly compared cluster sets (sets split with intra-set rest) against traditional sets (continuous reps to the same RPE), matched for total volume load. Muscle thickness and lean tissue mass increased similarly in both groups.

Mechanism: The primary driver of hypertrophy is cumulative mechanical tension near failure, not the temporal distribution of that tension within a set. Cluster sets may reduce metabolic fatigue and allow higher force output per rep, but this does not translate to superior hypertrophy when the rep-effort envelope is matched.

PPL application: On heavy compound movements (squat, deadlift, bench, row), cluster sets can be useful for managing fatigue on later sets without compromising hypertrophy. They’re a fatigue-management tool, not a hypertrophy accelerator.

Source: PMC12174233 · 2026 · Cluster Sets and Traditional Sets · Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research
⚠ Hype Watch — Time-Restricted Eating and Circadian Timing

The claim: Eating in an early window (8am–4pm) activates circadian metabolic advantages beyond simple calorie restriction — improving insulin sensitivity, fat oxidation, and metabolic flexibility through alignment with circadian tissue clocks.

The 2026 data: A randomized crossover RCT published in Science Translational Medicine (2026) compared early TRE (8:00–16:00) vs. late TRE (13:00–21:00) in 31 women with overweight/obesity under isocaloric conditions. Insulin sensitivity did not differ between conditions. Internal circadian clocks shifted as expected, but this did not translate to measurable metabolic benefit beyond the phase shift itself.

Bottom line: TRE works, primarily through energy deficit. Early windows are still worth adopting for insulin signaling context (meal timing around training, AM light exposure, cortisol rhythms), but don’t expect them to outperform an equivalent late-window deficit. The circadian timing hypothesis is real but weaker than marketed.

Integrate Now
  • Use cluster sets on compound lifts (3–4 main sets) to preserve force output on sets 3 and 4 without sacrificing hypertrophy
  • TRE: maintain an eating window that ends before 8pm; the exact start time matters less than consistency
  • Prioritise proximity-to-failure over set structure novelty
Caveats
  • Cluster sets require precise intra-set rest tracking — app or timer essential
  • TRE circadian benefit in isocaloric conditions is small; energy deficit remains the primary driver
  • Women’s hormonal cycles may interact differently with TRE timing — 2026 RCT was all-female
⚡ Implication: For a PPL split, cluster sets are a legitimate load-management technique on heavy days — not a hypertrophy hack. TRE timing: pick a window you can sustain; early is preferable for context with morning training, but don’t expect timing alone to move the metabolic needle.
Section 03
Supplements & Nutrition

Ergothioneine is the most interesting emerging longevity micronutrient right now — and it differs from typical antioxidant supplements in a mechanistically important way. The body has a dedicated transporter (OCTN1, encoded by SLC22A4) that actively concentrates ergothioneine in mitochondria-rich and oxidatively stressed tissues. This specificity — unique among amino acid derivatives — suggests it’s not a generic antioxidant mop but a regulated cellular defence molecule. The human evidence base is still observational, but the Swedish cohort data is large enough to warrant serious attention.

Ergothioneine: Dedicated Cellular Transporter, SIRT1/SIRT6 Activation, 21% Cardiovascular Mortality Reduction — ★★ Evidence
Observational Cohort + Mech

What it is: Ergothioneine is a dietary amino acid synthesised exclusively by fungi (mushrooms) and some bacteria. Humans cannot synthesise it — dietary intake (mushrooms, black beans, fermented grains) or supplementation is the only source. The body actively concentrates it via the OCTN1 transporter into tissues with high oxidative stress (mitochondria-rich, UV-exposed, immune cells).

Mechanisms confirmed in cell/animal models (Protein & Cell, Oxford Academic, 2024):

1. KEAP1–NRF2 pathway: dose-dependent upregulation of downstream antioxidant genes (catalase, SOD, heme oxygenase-1). Unlike general antioxidants, selectively scavenges hydroxyl radicals (most destructive ROS) while leaving regulatory ROS signalling intact.
2. Sirtuin activation: upregulates SIRT1 and SIRT6 — the same longevity-associated deacetylases targeted by NAD+ precursors (NMN, NR).
3. Mitochondrial DNA protection: reduces ROS production at the mitochondrial membrane; protects mtDNA from oxidative damage.
4. Epigenetic modulation: proposed effects on DNA/histone methylation via SAM availability and iron cofactor availability for demethylase enzymes.

Human observational evidence: A Swedish prospective cohort of 3,236 participants followed for a median 21.4 years found 21% lower cardiovascular mortality per 1 standard deviation increase in plasma ergothioneine levels. Causality unestablished — higher ergothioneine may track with mushroom intake and other healthy diet patterns.

Human RCT evidence: A 2025 Japanese RCT (n not specified in available abstract) found 20 mg/day ergothioneine for 4 weeks significantly improved sleep quality in healthy adults. No RCT on mortality endpoints exists yet.

Practical protocol: 20–30 mg/day ergothioneine (available via MitoPrime or equivalent standardised extract). Alternatively: 100 g cooked oyster mushrooms ≈ 1–4 mg; meaningful dietary intake requires consistent daily mushroom consumption at high volumes. Evidence rating: ★★ (mechanistic evidence strong; human observational evidence compelling; no mortality RCT yet).

Source: Liu et al. · Protein & Cell, Oxford Academic · 2024 · “Ergothioneine and its congeners: anti-ageing mechanisms and pharmacophore biosynthesis” · Longevity.Technology · Swedish Cohort Coverage
Protocol Upgrade
  • Add 100 g/day oyster or shiitake mushrooms for dietary ergothioneine
  • Supplement at 20–30 mg/day if dietary intake is inconsistent
  • Combine with NAD+ precursors: SIRT1/SIRT6 pathways overlap, potential synergy
  • Consider tracking plasma ergothioneine at next blood panel (not yet standard but available via specialist labs)
Caveats
  • No RCT demonstrating mortality or healthspan benefit in humans yet
  • Swedish cohort observational: confounded by overall dietary quality (mushroom eaters may have healthier patterns overall)
  • Optimal dose for mTOR/SIRT pathway engagement in humans: unknown
  • Most supplements lack standardised assay for actual ergothioneine content — verify COA
⚡ Implication: Ergothioneine sits in the same tier as NMN/NR — mechanistically compelling, observationally supported, awaiting a definitive RCT. At ★★ it’s worth adding at low cost (daily mushrooms) but not worth high-dose supplementation until the RCT evidence matures. The SIRT1/SIRT6 overlap with NAD+ precursors makes it an interesting pairing to investigate, though synergy is speculative.
