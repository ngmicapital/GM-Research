Alpha — Value Area

Thursday · June 4, 2026 · Sydney, Australia · Issue #41 11 min read
TL;DR

The Value Area is the price band that held roughly 70% of a session's volume — bounded by the VAH on top and the VAL on the bottom, with the POC inside — and it marks where buyers and sellers agreed on fair value. Trade it simply: price rotating inside value mean-reverts toward the POC, price accepted outside value trends, and the value edge that gets rejected becomes your highest-conviction fade.

§01
What It Is
The market's agreed-upon fair price band
The Value Area is the price range that absorbed ~70% of the period's volume — the zone where the auction found agreement, with everything outside it priced as too expensive or too cheap.

Forget the textbook framing for a second. Markets are an auction, and an auction only continues where both sides are willing to transact. The Value Area is the slice of the price range where that agreement actually happened — the central ~70% of all volume traded. Inside it, buyers and sellers were comfortable doing business. Outside it, price moved too far for the crowd and got rejected.

It's defined by three lines. The POC (Point of Control) is the single price that did the most volume — the fairest of the fair, the magnet. The VAH (Value Area High) caps the band; the VAL (Value Area Low) floors it. Everything above VAH and below VAL — roughly 15% of volume on each side — is the tail: prices the market sampled briefly and walked away from.

This is the layer above raw Volume Profile and POC. The POC is one line; the Value Area is the distribution around it. That distinction is the whole game — a single high-volume level tells you where the magnet is, but VAH and VAL tell you the edges of balance, and the edges are where decisions get made.

POChighest-volume price — the mean / magnet

If you only remember one thing The Value Area answers one question — where is the market comfortable? Price inside value is balance; price accepted outside value is a trend; price rejected at the edge is your trade.
§02
Mechanics
How the 70% band is actually built
Start at the POC, then grow outward by repeatedly adding whichever adjacent row — above or below — traded more volume, until you've captured 70% of the total.

The Value Area isn't drawn equidistant from the POC. It's grown by an iterative row-pairing expansion, the method TradingView and most platforms use. You build it volume-greedily, not symmetrically, which is why a real value area is almost always lopsided.

VA target = 0.70 × Σ(volume of every price row)
1. Seed the VA with the POC row; subtract its volume from the target.
2. Look at the next row above and the next row below the current VA.
3. Add whichever of the two traded more volume; subtract it from the target.
4. Repeat the comparison from the new frontier until cumulative volume ≥ target.
5. VAH = highest row included  ·  VAL = lowest row included.
Tie-break: take the row closer to the POC; if equidistant, take the upper row.

Why 70%? Because it approximates one standard deviation of a normal distribution. A true ±1σ captures 68.2% of activity; the round 70% is the convention Steidlmayer and Dalton settled on. The logic: if price is normally distributed around fair value, the central 1σ is the zone the auction actually "accepts," and the tails beyond are statistical excess — the prices that got tested and rejected.

One nuance worth flagging: there are two ways to count "activity," and they don't always agree.

§03
The Picture
Anatomy of value, and how it migrates
A volume profile is a sideways bell curve — fat in the middle (value, POC) and thin at the ends (tails); when those bells stack lower session over session, that's a downtrend.
Anatomy of a value area: a sideways volume distribution. The fat middle is value (VAH→VAL, ~70%), the POC is the heaviest-volume row, and the thin ends are the tails the market rejected. Price spends most of its life inside the band, rotating around the POC.
Value migration is the higher-timeframe signal. Overlapping value areas = balance/range. Higher value session-over-session = uptrend. Lower value, like BTC right now, = downtrend — the auction is repricing fair value lower.
§04
In Practice
Fade inside value, follow outside it
The one rule: inside value, fade the edges toward the POC; outside value, only trade in the direction the market has accepted — never fight a value breakout that holds.
1

Inside value → fade the edges to the POC

When price is rotating inside the prior value area, the market is in balance. Long at the VAL, short at the VAH, both targeting the POC. Stops sit just beyond the opposite extreme — if balance breaks, you're out cheap.

2

Open outside value → read the first hour

Open above VAH and hold = buyers in control; buy pullbacks to VAH, don't fade. Open above VAH then fall back inside = rejection — the day's strongest mean-reversion short, targeting the POC then VAL. The mirror image applies below VAL.

3

The 80% rule rotation

Price opens outside value, trades back inside, and holds for two consecutive 30-min bars = acceptance. Historically ~80% odds the session then fills the entire value area, VAH to VAL. A clean rotation with a built-in target — but only in balanced regimes.

4

Hunt naked value as targets

A prior VAH or VAL that price hasn't retested since it printed is a naked / virgin level — an unfinished piece of business that acts as a magnet. An untouched VAL below current price is the logical objective once today's value breaks down.

§07
Common Traps
Where value-area trading goes wrong
The biggest mistake: treating VAH/VAL as automatic fade buttons. They're decision points — and in a trend, the edge breaks and keeps running.
1
Fading every edge mechanically

VAH and VAL are decision points, not auto-reverse levels. In a trend day the edge breaks and accelerates — fading it bleeds you. Wait for the market to show acceptance or rejection before committing size.

2
Using the wrong profile window

A 24/7 crypto "daily" value area is not a futures-pit RTH session. Match the window to your thesis — session, weekly composite, or a fixed-range from the last swing — and stay consistent. Switching anchors mid-trade is how you talk yourself into anything.

3
Confusing "inside value" with "safe"

Price inside the prior value area means balance, not support. The POC is a magnet, not a floor — in a downtrend it offers no defence, and value simply keeps migrating down through it. Ask "is value moving?" before you assume mean-reversion.

4
The "80% rule = 80% win rate" myth

Overhyped on trading X. The 80% figure is a conditional probability of filling the value area after two-bar acceptance — not your trade's win rate, and not edge by itself. It degrades hard in trending regimes, and standalone backtests outside balanced markets are unimpressive. Use it as a context filter, with a real stop, never as a system.
