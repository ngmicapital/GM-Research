MASPO — joint prompt optimisation for multi-agent LLM systems

Research Workflow +2.90% avg across 6 benchmarks

MASPO is an iterative framework for joint prompt optimisation in LLM-based multi-agent systems, demonstrating +2.90% average accuracy improvement across six diverse benchmarks. The core idea: in multi-agent systems, optimising each agent's prompt independently produces locally-optimal but globally-suboptimal prompts because the agents' behaviours interact; MASPO co-optimises the prompts jointly with respect to the system's overall objective.

⚡ Implication: Directly relevant for the Multi-agent orchestration spike (§02, §06). If the spike reaches the point of "the research agent's prompt and the writer agent's prompt are each producing decent work but the system output is worse than the monolithic skill," MASPO is the right intervention to try before adding more agents. MASPO — joint prompt optimisation, +2.90% across six benchmarks ⚡

Research Workflow

One-line finding: in LLM-based multi-agent systems, optimising each agent's prompt independently is a local optimum; jointly co-optimising the prompts against the system's overall objective yields +2.90% average accuracy across six diverse benchmarks. Why it matters in 6 months: the multi-agent architecture is rapidly becoming the production pattern for any non-trivial agentic workload (see TradingAgents resurgence in Issue #43, Anthropic Multiagent Orchestration GA today, mattpocock/skills displacing single-skill repos), and per-agent prompt-engineering will become a bottleneck the same way per-model fine-tuning was in 2024. MASPO is the methodology that becomes routine when teams hit the bottleneck.

HumanNet — egocentric human video as a robot-data substitute

Research

Why it matters in 6 months: robot fleet capture has been the rate-limiting step on robotics-foundation-model scaling — every hour of robot data costs 100×+ more than an hour of phone-camera video. 🔬 Plain-English Explainer — TPUs vs GPUs, and why Anthropic just bet $200B on the cheaper option

A GPU (graphics processing unit) is a chip originally built for video games — it's good at running lots of parallel math operations at once, which turns out to be exactly what AI needs. NVIDIA built the dominant GPU business around this. A TPU (tensor processing unit) is a chip Google designed from scratch specifically for AI math — no legacy graphics gear, no general-purpose compromises. Think of it like the difference between a sports car (GPU — fast at many things) and a Formula 1 car (TPU — fastest at one specific kind of racing, useless for everything else).

For AI inference (the part where the model actually answers your question — not training it), TPUs are now reportedly 40–50% cheaper per token than comparable NVIDIA GPUs at the same model quality. The trade-off: TPUs only exist on Google Cloud, so committing to them is committing to Google's data centres as the supply. Anthropic just signed a $200B / 5-year deal for 5 gigawatts of TPU capacity — that's the supply-side answer to "Claude has 80× more demand than last year and the math has to keep working."

For traders, this matters because the cost curve is the deepest moat in AI commercialisation. Whoever has the cheapest inference per unit of model quality wins gross-margin-per-token, and gross-margin compounds into pricing power, into reinvestment, into the next model. Anthropic just locked in the cost-leader position for at least the next two years — which is the structural reason Claude API prices have not gone up despite a 6-month run of model frontier improvements. For the AI-DePIN crypto thesis (AKT, RENDER, TAO), TPUs are a different game — decentralised compute networks compete primarily against the marginal NVIDIA-GPU rental market (cloud providers, hyperscalers spilling demand), not against centrally-owned TPU pools. Akash and Render are still structurally bid because not all inference workloads can move to TPUs (open-source models, regulatory requirements for non-Google compute, latency-sensitive edge inference) — and those workloads still need cheaper-than-AWS GPU supply, which is where the DePIN networks have a real product.
