Alpha — Sweep Orders & Sweeping the Book

9 June 2026 · Sydney, Australia · Issue 44 8 min
TL;DR

A sweep order is an aggressive market order (or burst of orders) that consumes every resting limit at successive price levels, clearing the book in a single violent motion. Sweeps reveal urgent institutional intent — the aggressor needed size now, price be damned. Reading sweeps correctly tells you who just showed their hand, where the next magnet is, and whether the move has legs or is already exhausted.

§01
What It Is
Definition & taxonomy
A sweep order eats through multiple price levels in one motion — it is the orderbook's equivalent of a wrecking ball. Where a normal market order hopes to fill at or near the touch, a sweep plans to consume depth.

In microstructure terms, sweeping the book means submitting an order (or an algorithm that fires a rapid succession of child orders) sized large enough to exhaust all resting liquidity at the best price and continue filling at progressively worse levels until the order is complete. The price "sweeps" upward through the ask stack, or downward through the bid stack, leaving a trail of filled orders and a noticeably thinner book in its wake.

On US equities exchanges, the canonical form is the Intermarket Sweep Order (ISO) — a Reg NMS order type explicitly exempt from the "trade-through" rule, allowing it to execute simultaneously across multiple venues at multiple prices without waiting for best-price routing. In crypto, which has no Reg NMS equivalent, the same economics play out via algorithmic "taker" orders fired at speed across a single venue's depth ladder, or across multiple CEX/DEX venues simultaneously.

The important distinction: a sweep is a deliberate choice. The trader or institution chose market impact over price improvement. That urgency is signal.

Key insight Normal institutional execution minimises market impact — TWAP, VWAP, iceberg, dark pools. A sweep is the opposite: maximum speed, maximum impact. When someone sweeps, the cost of impact was judged acceptable. Ask yourself: why were they in that much of a hurry?
§02
Mechanics
How the book gets cleared, level by level
A sweep consumes the ask (or bid) stack sequentially. The speed — microseconds in equities, milliseconds in crypto — means the aggressor is paying a known slippage premium. Track the cumulative filled volume at each level and the residual depth after the sweep ends.

Step-by-step anatomy of an ask-side sweep (buy sweep):

  1. Best ask is, say, $105,000 with 2.0 BTC resting. A 10 BTC market buy fires.
  2. The first 2.0 BTC fill at $105,000 exhausts that level — it disappears from the book.
  3. The next 3.5 BTC resting at $105,050 gets consumed. Level cleared.
  4. 1.8 BTC at $105,100 — gone.
  5. …continuing until all 10 BTC are filled, ending perhaps at $105,340.
  6. The book now has a gap (thin depth) between $105,340 and the next cluster of asks.

The resulting footprint on the tape: a sequence of large aggressive prints at ascending prices, all executed within milliseconds. On a footprint chart each row lights up in sequence — a clear visual "ladder" ascending the ask side.

In crypto perpetual futures the mechanics are identical but with added nuance: the sweep can simultaneously trigger liquidations — forced market orders from levered longs or shorts whose margin ran out — which in turn feed the sweep with more aggressive flow and compound the move.

§04
In Practice
Reading & trading the sweep signal
Don't chase the sweep candle — you're already late. Instead read what the sweep reveals: is the book thin enough to continue? Did any absorption appear? Is delta confirming? Then position into the aftermath, not the flash.
1

Identify the Sweep in Real Time

On a footprint chart, a sweep is a column of ascending ask-side prints within a single candle — each cell shows bid × ask volume and the ask side "stacks" green from bottom to top with large numbers. On a time-and-sales feed it appears as a burst of aggressive fills at consecutive prices within under a second. Size threshold: generally the sweep needs to be at least 3–5× normal candle volume to classify as institutional.

2

Assess Book Depth After the Sweep

Immediately check the updated order book. If the next ask cluster above the swept zone is thin (<30% of pre-sweep depth), price has a "vacuum" to fill — continuation likely. If a wall of fresh sell limit orders materialises within 2–3 seconds, market makers are refilling aggressively; the sweep may have exhausted itself. This "depth replenishment rate" is the single most important post-sweep variable.

3

Confirm with Cumulative Volume Delta

After a buy sweep, CVD should spike sharply upward — more aggressive buying volume than selling for that window. If CVD diverges (price ticked up but CVD went flat or fell), the sweep was absorbed by passive sellers who were not visible on the lit book. That's a bearish divergence and a potential fade setup. Convergence (CVD up, price up) = confirmation.

4

Watch for the "Sweep & Reverse" Pattern

A sweep that fails to hold — price sweeps through a level, then pulls back and closes below the swept zone within 1–3 candles — is called a sweep-and-reverse or a "fakeout sweep." This often indicates the sweep was engineered to collect stop-loss orders below a support level (sell-side liquidity sweep) before the real direction resumes upward. The reversal candle closing back inside the range is the entry signal.

Worked Example — BTC Perpetual, 5-Minute Chart
SetupBTC ranging $104,800–$105,200 for 3 hours. Clean equal highs at $105,200 visible on the chart — textbook stop cluster above.
Sweep Candle09:42 UTC: single 5-min candle prints $105,180 → $105,640 in 8 seconds. Footprint shows 312 BTC on ask side, 18× average candle volume. CVD surges +290 BTC.
Post-Sweep BookAsks at $105,640–$105,900: only 45 BTC resting. Pre-sweep comparable depth was ~200 BTC. Depth ratio = 22% → vacuum conditions.
EntryLong entry on first 5-min close above $105,200 (the prior high), ~$105,260. Stop below the sweep candle open at $105,150.
TargetNext visible ask cluster on heatmap at $106,400 (the prior month's VPOC). Risk/reward ~10:1.
OutcomePrice reached $106,380 over the next 40 minutes. No re-test of entry before the move.

Timeframe note: Sweeps are most visible and actionable on 1m–15m charts with a footprint overlay. On higher timeframes (4H+) they compress into a single wick and lose granularity. Keep a footprint or time-and-sales window dedicated to sweep detection even when your primary chart is higher timeframe.

The $60K Liquidity Sweep: $1.57B Flushed in One Candle

In the first week of June 2026, BTC printed one of the cleaner sweep-and-reverse setups of the year. Equal lows at ~$60,200 had been accumulating retail stop-loss orders for nearly two weeks. On June 4, a wave of algorithmic selling swept price to an intraday low of $59,743 on Bitstamp — just enough to collect the stops and the forced liquidations of leveraged longs — before price recovered above $60,800 within the same 4-hour candle.

The tell: the $59,743 candle closed back above $60,200, reclaiming the level it had swept. The sweep zone became support. CVD data showed aggressive buying immediately after the liquidation cascade completed — institutional size entering on the fill of retail stops.

§06
Voices
Practitioners on sweep mechanics
The sweep has been a feature of lit markets since open-outcry pits. From pit traders watching the broker run the stops to today's algo watchers monitoring liquidation cascades — the same intent persists.

"When you see a sweep order hit, that is someone who needed to own those contracts right then. They could not wait for price to come to them, they could not slice it quietly over the session. Whatever they knew — or thought they knew — was time-sensitive. Respect that urgency."

— Trader Dale, Order Flow: Trading Setups

"A sweep is a liquidity destruction device. It doesn't just move price — it removes the book depth that would have slowed the next move. The aftermath is often more important than the event itself. An empty shelf means the next buyer or seller has no resistance."

— Bookmap Research, Order Flow Strategies (2025)

"Sweeps in crypto are particularly violent because liquidations act as synthetic sweeps — forced market orders that compound the original aggression. One whale sweep can trigger a cascade of automatic liquidations, each one a new aggressive taker adding to the momentum."

— Ed Silantyev, Order Flow Analysis of Cryptocurrency Markets (Medium)

"Unlike US equities subject to anti-spoofing policies, there are no equal regulatory counterparts in cryptocurrency markets. Order flow may carry relatively lower information in crypto than in equities — which is why trade flow imbalance, not order flow imbalance, better explains contemporaneous price changes in BTC."

— ScienceDirect, Order flow and cryptocurrency returns (2026)
§07
Common Traps
Where sweep traders go wrong
The sweep candle is the most exciting thing on your screen — and the most dangerous time to trade. Most sweep traps come from acting on the candle itself rather than on what the book looks like after.
1
Chasing the Sweep Candle

Entering a market order the moment you see the sweep spike means you're buying the highest prices of the candle. The sweep is over — you're paying the premium. Wait for the first pullback or re-test of the swept zone before entry. The setup is in the aftermath, not the flash.

2
Ignoring Book Replenishment

Not all sweeps leave a vacuum. High-frequency market makers can replenish depth within 100–200ms in liquid markets. If you see the book refilling rapidly at higher prices with large size, the sweep may have been absorbed. Check the depth chart 2–5 seconds after the sweep — if walls are already rebuilding, skip the trade.

3
Confusing a Sweep with a Fake Sweep

Spoofing algos occasionally post and cancel large orders to create the visual impression of a sweep. On genuine sweeps, time-and-sales will show actual fills at multiple price levels — not just order book changes. Always confirm with real prints in the tape, not just DOM flickering.

4
Over-sizing After a Sweep-and-Reverse

The sweep-and-reverse (fakeout) is seductive because the stop location is tight — the sweep low is clear. But these setups often have wide initial price swings before the recovery trend begins. Use half normal size on sweep-and-reverse entries and scale in only once the sweep zone confirms as support (two candle closes above it).

5
Assuming Every Sweep is Directional

Liquidation cascade sweeps in crypto (the June 4 BTC example) are often engineered exits, not new entries. A sweep caused by cascading liquidations may simply clear over-leverage without attracting fresh institutional buyers. Confirm institutional re-accumulation with CVD turning positive after the liquidation flush — not just during it.

○
Honest Framing: Sweeps Are Rare, High-Quality Signals

On any given day you might see 2–4 genuine sweeps across all BTC/ETH markets. Most are not tradeable (wrong context, bad location relative to structure). Resist the urge to classify every fast move as a sweep. A real sweep is distinguished by multi-level sequential fills on T&S at 3–5× normal volume — anything less is just volatility.
