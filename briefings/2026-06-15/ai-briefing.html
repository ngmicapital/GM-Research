§01 Model Landscape

The launch wave that ran through early June has paused — nothing new has shipped at the frontier since Fable 5 and Mythos 5 on June 9, and SWE-bench Pro is replacing Verified as the score that actually separates models.

The frontier has gone quiet — and that is the story

Cadence

Nine days after Anthropic shipped Fable 5 and Mythos 5 (June 9), the frontier board is unchanged. No US lab has launched a new top-tier model since; the cadence that produced five frontier releases in nine days back in May has cooled into a consolidation phase. The practical implication is the most useful kind: the model you picked last week is still the right one, and there is nothing newer to chase this morning.

Gemini 3.5 Pro — still preview-only, GA slipping deeper into June

Pending

Google's frontier-tier Gemini 3.5 Pro — 2M-token context, a "Deep Think" reasoning mode, the model that inherits the old Gemini Ultra use cases — remains in limited Vertex preview. Sundar Pichai promised GA "next month" on the I/O stage on May 19; mid-June, that month is nearly gone and there is still no general-availability date. Until it ships, Gemini 3.5 Flash (GA since May 19) is the only 3.5-series model you can actually call. This is the one pending launch with real positioning weight — watch the Vertex release notes.

SWE-bench Pro is becoming the benchmark that matters

Benchmark

The Verified leaderboard has compressed into the high-90s — Mythos 5 95.5%, Fable 5 95.0%, Opus 4.8 88.6% — to the point that it no longer separates frontier models. Scale AI's SWE-bench Pro (1,865 tasks across 41 repos in Python, Go, TypeScript and JavaScript) is where the spread reopened: Fable 5 leads at 80.3%, +11.1 points over Opus 4.8, and every model drops roughly 23 points moving from Verified to Pro.

When you compare coding models from here, use the Pro number. Verified is saturated — a 95.0 vs 95.5 gap tells you nothing about which model survives a hard, realistic codebase; the ~23-point Verified→Pro haircut is where real-world reliability lives.

§02 Claude Ecosystem

Two operational changes hit Anthropic's platform today — and the bigger story is a US government order that has pulled Anthropic's most capable models offline for everyone outside the US.

⚡ Today: the original Claude 4.0 models retire

Deprecation

From today, claude-opus-4-20250514 and claude-sonnet-4-20250514 — the original 4.0 releases, deprecated back on April 14 — reach end-of-life on the Claude API, Claude Platform on AWS and Microsoft Foundry. Requests to those IDs now return errors. Anthropic's recommended replacements are claude-opus-4-8 and claude-sonnet-4-6. If you maintain anything pinned to a claude-*-4-0 ID, it breaks today, not gracefully degrades.

The wider retirement schedule

Today is one step in a rolling cleanup: claude-opus-4-1-20250805 retires August 5 (→ Opus 4.8), and the restricted claude-mythos-preview retires June 30 in favour of claude-mythos-5. Note too that temperature, top_p and top_k now 400-error on Opus 4.7 and later — sampling is prompt-driven on the current top line.

⚡ Update: a US order takes Anthropic's top models offline abroad

National security

Reported June 14 by Semafor, Axios and Al Jazeera: the US government issued an order barring foreign access to Anthropic's most advanced (Mythos-class) models — reportedly issued partly over suspicion that a China-linked group had accessed a new Anthropic model. Senior Anthropic technical staff are in Washington this week to meet White House officials and try to get the models back online. [reported — the order itself is not public]

For a Sydney desk this is not abstract. If it holds, the Mythos/Fable-class tier is off-limits to you and Opus 4.8 becomes the practical ceiling for non-US users. This is an escalation of the earlier Glasswing / Pentagon-access dispute — but where that was about expansion plans, this is models actually pulled. Highest-impact item in today's brief; see Watchlist.

Claude Code: 2.1.174, Fable 5 access, Safe Mode

Tooling

Claude Code is at 2.1.174 (June 12). Recent additions: Fable 5 access (2.1.170), a --safe-mode flag and CLAUDE_CODE_SAFE_MODE env var that boot with all customizations off for troubleshooting, a fallbackModel setting (up to three models tried in order on overload), per-component /usage token breakdowns, and the enforceAvailableModels managed setting. Opus 4.8 is the default. Incremental — but turn on the per-component /usage view now, because billing is about to get more granular (see §06).

§04 Open-Source Pulse

No new open-weight frontier dropped in the last 24 hours — the story is consolidation, and the most interesting release on the board is still an agent that trains models, not another chat checkpoint.

The open-weight frontier is consolidating, not advancing

State of play

Quiet 24 hours on open weights. The production open stack is settled rather than shifting: Qwen 3 235B-A22B remains the all-round open leader for reasoning and coding, DeepSeek R1 still leads on deep math, Llama 4 Scout holds the long-context crown (10M tokens), and Gemma 3 27B / Phi-4 14B remain the easiest serious models to self-host on consumer GPUs. If you are choosing an open model today, none of those answers changed overnight.

ml-intern — the open agent that ships models, not chats

Agent

Hugging Face's open-source ml-intern stays the structurally interesting release: an agent that reads papers, trains models and ships them end-to-end through the HF ecosystem, rather than another chat-tuned checkpoint. It is the clearest open expression of the "agent that does ML engineering" pattern — worth a look if you want to automate post-training, not just inference. Still early, but it is the open answer to the closed agentic-coding tools.

Carry: Moonshot's Kimi K2.7-Code is the freshest open coding model

Carry

Last issue's open drop — Moonshot's Kimi K2.7-Code (coding-agentic, 256K context, MIT-style licence) — is the most recent open coding model worth benchmarking against the proprietary leaders. No new numbers today; flagged so it stays on the eval shortlist alongside Qwen 3 and MiniMax M3.

IPO pipeline

§06 Workflow & Tools

The single most important workflow change today is financial: programmatic Claude usage — including scheduled tasks exactly like this one — now bills from a separate, capped credit pool.

⚡ Today: the Claude Agent SDK billing split goes live

Billing

From today, programmatic Claude usage no longer draws on your subscription's chat pool. The Agent SDK, the claude -p headless command, Claude Code GitHub Actions and any third-party app built on the Agent SDK now consume a separate monthly credit — roughly $20 on Pro, $100 on Max 5x, $200 on Max 20x — billed at standard API list rates. Once it is gone, automated requests stop unless you have manually enabled overflow billing, and unused credit does not roll over. Interactive use (chat, Claude Code in the terminal, Cowork) is unaffected.

Why it matters here: this briefing runs as a scheduled task on the Agent SDK path. From today it draws the separate credit, not the chat pool. The practical move is two-fold — (1) claim the credit (eligible users got an email), and (2) audit every headless/CI job so nothing critical halts mid-month when the pool empties.

MCP keeps standardising — AWS MCP Server hits GA

MCP

AWS's MCP Server reached general availability (updated June 5); the protocol now spans 200+ server implementations and crossed roughly 97M monthly SDK downloads. OpenAI deprecated its proprietary Assistants API in favour of MCP, and Google's ADK, LangGraph, CrewAI and Microsoft's Agent Framework all support it. The takeaway is boring and important: MCP is now the default integration layer, so tools you build against it stay portable across runtimes.

§07 Research Radar

Two papers worth tracking point at the same weak spot the new agent tooling is racing past: long-horizon planning, and the reinforcement learning that is meant to fix it.

Why Reasoning Fails to Plan (arXiv 2601.22311)

Planning

A planning-centric analysis of long-horizon decision-making in LLM agents: strong step-level reasoning does not translate into reliable multi-step plans, and the paper isolates where the breakdown happens. Directly relevant now that Claude Code ships nested sub-agents — recursive orchestration only pays off if the planner above it actually holds a coherent long-horizon plan.

Rethinking Agentic Reinforcement Learning in LLMs (arXiv 2604.27859)

Agentic RL

A survey of the emerging methods in agentic RL — how reward and exploration are being reframed when the "environment" is tool use and multi-step tasks rather than single-turn outputs. Read it as the map of how labs intend to close the planning gap above. Worth tracking, not yet actionable.
