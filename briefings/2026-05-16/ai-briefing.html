§07 — Research Radar

Two papers worth tracking — MIT's "Embedded Language Flows" (ELF) claims a continuous diffusion language model that competes on quality with ~10x fewer training tokens, and MolmoAct2 open-action reasoning for robotics builds the bridge between language models and motor-control pipelines.

ELF — Embedded Language Flows (MIT) ⚡

Research

MIT's Embedded Language Flows (ELF) proposes a continuous diffusion language model paradigm based on Flow Matching, achieving competitive generative quality on tasks like machine translation and summarization while requiring ~10x fewer training tokens and fewer inference steps than autoregressive baselines. The finding sits in the "diffusion language model" lineage that has been compounding since LLaDA in late 2024 and the various flow-matching work in 2025.

⚡ Why it matters in 6 months: If the 10x-fewer-tokens efficiency claim survives reproduction, the open-source frontier (DeepSeek, Qwen, GLM) plus university labs gain a real shot at frontier-tier capability without frontier-tier compute. The asymmetric upside is for the open-source community more than the labs — the closed labs already have the compute. Track: which open-source model adopts a flow-matching backbone first and posts an MMLU / SWE-bench number.

MolmoAct2 — open-action reasoning for robotics

Research

MolmoAct2 presents an open-action reasoning model for robotics, building on the prior MolmoAct work with specialised vision-language backbones, new datasets, open-weight action tokenisers, and adaptive reasoning for reduced latency. The combination of open weights + action-token output + VL backbone is the closest open-source approach yet to a Physical Intelligence-style robotics foundation model.

⚡ Why it matters in 6 months: Embodied / robotics AI is the segment where the open-source frontier has lagged hardest. MolmoAct2 narrows the gap. For dc's stack this is monitor-only — no immediate use — but the broader signal is that "physical intelligence" stops being a one-lab story (Pi, Tesla, 1X) and starts to look like a multi-actor frontier the way LLMs did in 2023.
