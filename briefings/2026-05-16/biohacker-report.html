Longevity & Performance Science

The lysosomal HSC story from Mount Sinai is the most important longevity finding this cycle. It's not abstract: it identifies exactly where the aging immune system breaks down (lysosomes become hyperacidic, damaged, and overactive in blood stem cells), pinpoints the repair mechanism (vacuolar ATPase inhibition), and demonstrates that correcting this dysfunction restores regenerative capacity eightfold in old mice. The cGAS-STING pathway dampening is critical — chronic low-grade immune activation driven by mitochondrial DNA leaking into the cytoplasm is one of the dominant mechanisms behind inflammaging. Fixing lysosomes fixes that signal.

The CAG-170 findings from Cambridge represent a different kind of landmark: not a treatment, but a map. Across 11,000 people in 39 countries, a single bacterial group consistently separates health from disease across conditions that look unrelated on the surface — IBD, obesity, CFS, rheumatoid arthritis, Parkinson's. That consistency suggests CAG-170 is not a marker of any single disease pathway, but of microbiome resilience itself.

Mount Sinai Reverses Blood Stem Cell Aging by Repairing Lysosomal Dysfunction — 8x Restoration of Regenerative Capacity in Mice
[Mechanistic]

Researchers at the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai published findings in Cell Stem Cell (2025; 32:12) showing that aged hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs) undergo a specific and reversible failure mode: their lysosomes — the cell's recycling and waste-clearing organelles — become hyperacidic, depleted, damaged, and abnormally overactivated. This lysosomal dysfunction drives two downstream harms: it impairs HSC regenerative capacity, and it activates the cGAS-STING inflammatory signaling pathway by allowing mitochondrial DNA to leak from damaged lysosomes into the cytoplasm.

The intervention: administering a vacuolar ATPase (V-ATPase) inhibitor ex vivo to calm lysosomal hyperactivation. Old stem cells treated this way showed restored metabolic function, improved epigenomic state, reduced inflammatory output, and — critically — more than an eightfold increase in blood-forming capacity when returned to living animals. The treated cells produced balanced immune populations and renewed their regenerative self-renewal capacity. The team is now investigating whether lysosomal dysfunction connects normal HSC aging to leukemic stem cell development. Human trial timelines are not yet announced.

The practical frame: this mechanism is directly relevant to inflammaging — the chronic, low-level immune activation that accelerates age-related disease across cardiovascular, metabolic, and neurodegenerative domains. Exercise, caloric restriction, and intermittent fasting are all known to support lysosomal clearance. This study explains part of why.

Source: ScienceDaily · May 12, 2026 · Cell Stem Cell, 2025; 32(12) · Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai
CAG-170 Gut Bacteria: A Cross-Disease Health Biomarker Identified Across 39 Countries and 13 Conditions
[Observational]

A global study led by University of Cambridge researchers and published in Cell Host & Microbe (February 2026) analyzed over 11,000 gut microbiome samples from individuals across 39 countries, comparing healthy participants to those with 13 diseases including Crohn's disease, colorectal cancer, Parkinson's disease, multiple sclerosis, obesity, and chronic fatigue syndrome. The finding: a single bacterial group, CAG-170, appears consistently at higher levels in healthy individuals and lower levels across virtually every disease state studied.

CAG-170 has not yet been cultured in a laboratory — it's identified only through genetic sequencing. Its functional profile is compelling: it produces substantial quantities of Vitamin B12 and enzymes that decompose carbohydrates, sugars, and dietary fibers. Lower CAG-170 levels correlate with dysbiosis states linked to IBS, rheumatoid arthritis, and mood disorders. The researchers' position is that CAG-170 could serve as a health indicator for microbiome status, and that targeted probiotics to restore its levels represent a more specific next-generation approach than current broad-spectrum probiotic products.

This is an observational study — association is not causation, and CAG-170 hasn't been tested as a therapeutic target yet. But the cross-disease consistency across 39 countries is a strong signal. The existing diet-based strategy to support CAG-170 abundance is inferred from its carbohydrate-metabolizing function: dietary fiber and fermented foods are the logical intervention while targeted probiotics are developed.

Source: University of Cambridge · Cell Host & Microbe, February 2026
Longevity Research Pivots from Single-Target Fixes to Preserving Biological Coordination — 2026 Berlin World Congress
[Expert Opinion]

The 2nd World Congress on Targeting Longevity (Berlin, April 2026) produced a clear scientific consensus shift: aging is increasingly understood not as a sequence of isolated molecular defects, but as a progressive loss of coordination between biological systems. The mechanisms highlighted — mitochondrial signaling influencing inflammation, microbiota–brain interactions shaping aging trajectories, metabolic environments regulating tissue repair — are not separate problems with separate solutions. They're coupled.

The implication for individual longevity protocols: stacking isolated supplements targeting isolated pathways (NAD+, mTOR, telomeres, etc.) without attending to system-level coordination may be less effective than the simpler coherent interventions: consistent training, diet quality, sleep architecture, and social engagement, all of which simultaneously support multiple coordinated systems. Single-target optimization makes more sense in disease treatment. Prevention requires coordination maintenance.

Source: EurekAlert! · Targeting Longevity World Congress 2026 · World Mitochondria Society / International Society of Microbiota

For dc's PPL context: consistent training volume is not optional. For the gut, prioritize fiber and fermented foods now — CAG-170 targeted probiotics are coming but aren't available yet.

Training & Performance

VO2max is having its research moment. A network meta-analysis published in PMC in 2026 evaluated 51 studies across 1,261 athletes comparing HIIT, sprint interval training (SIT), and repeated sprint training (RST). The finding is unambiguous: long-interval HIIT — specifically intervals of 2 minutes or more at 90–95% of maximum heart rate — produces significantly greater VO2max gains than SIT or RST. The Norwegian 4×4 protocol (four 4-minute intervals at 90–95% HRmax with 3-minute active recovery) remains the benchmark for good reason: it accumulates the most time at high aerobic strain per session. For dc's PPL rotation, this is the one cardio format with the clearest outcome data.

Long-Interval HIIT Produces the Largest VO2max Gains — Network Meta-Analysis of 51 Studies Settles the Protocol Debate
[Meta]

A comprehensive network meta-analysis published in PMC (search completed April 2025, published 2026, covering 51 eligible studies, 1,261 athletes) directly compared high-intensity interval training (HIIT), sprint interval training (SIT), and repeated sprint training (RST) for VO2max improvement. The results: HIIT significantly outperformed both SIT and RST for VO2max gains when interval duration was long (≥2 minutes) and total high-intensity volume was sufficient (≥15 minutes per session) over a meaningful duration (4–12 weeks).

The mechanism: longer intervals keep the athlete at VO2max stimulus for more cumulative time per session than maximal sprint efforts, which are too brief to sustain peak oxygen uptake. The classic Norwegian 4×4 protocol (4 minutes at 90–95% HRmax, 3-minute active recovery, repeated 4 times) exemplifies optimal parameters. Effect sizes in well-controlled studies show 5–10% VO2max improvement in 6–8 weeks with consistent application. University students and recreationally active adults show similar response curves to trained athletes at this protocol, suggesting it's effective across fitness levels.

One practical caveat for PPL programming: long-interval HIIT is muscularly taxing, particularly for legs. Scheduling it on non-leg days or as a session add-on, rather than same-day as heavy squats/deadlifts, reduces interference and recovery cost.

Source: PMC12218014 · Network meta-analysis, 51 studies, 1,261 athletes
What to Change
  • Add one long-interval HIIT session per week (4×4 Norwegian protocol, 90–95% HRmax)
  • Place HIIT on non-leg days or at least 6 hours post-leg session
  • Track VO2max improvements over 6-week cycles as a training output metric
What NOT to Change
  • PPL frequency and progressive overload remain the primary hypertrophy driver — don't cut volume to add cardio
  • Zone 2 base has its own role (fat oxidation, mitochondrial density) — HIIT doesn't replace it, it adds to it
  • Sprint/RST work for sports performance context is still valid — the meta-analysis is VO2max specific
⚡ Implication: One 4×4 HIIT session per week at 90–95% HRmax on a non-leg day is the single highest-yield addition for VO2max, which is the strongest independent predictor of all-cause mortality in the literature. Add it now.

Supplements & Nutrition

Two distinct supplement stories this cycle. First: a network meta-analysis has ranked collagen protein above whey for both fat-free mass and strength in resistance-trained adults — a finding that contradicts years of practitioner consensus and demands a careful read before acting on it. The effect size difference is striking (SMD 0.94 vs 0.16 for FFM), but the researchers themselves flag that the estimate rests on relatively few studies and mostly indirect comparisons. This is a signal, not a directive. Second: MUSC's Cell Reports study formally separates EPA from DHA in the context of brain repair — a distinction that most commercial fish oil products don't make, and that directly affects how to dose if you have any history of repeated mild head impacts.

Collagen Outranks Whey in Resistance Training Network Meta-Analysis — SMD 0.94 vs 0.16 for Fat-Free Mass
[Meta]

A 2026 network meta-analysis (PMC12862422) compared protein-based dietary supplements combined with resistance training for increasing fat-free mass (FFM) and strength in healthy adults. The ranking: collagen protein showed the highest effect for both outcomes — SMD of 0.94 (approximately 3.20 kg FFM gain) vs whey's SMD of 0.16 (approximately 0.54 kg). For strength, collagen also led: SMD 0.41 (~6.05 kg) vs whey's 0.15 (~2.21 kg). Plant-based proteins (soy, pea) showed no statistically significant effects over placebo in this analysis.

Critical caveats from the authors: "These estimates were informed by relatively few studies and were largely derived from indirect comparisons." The collagen result could reflect specific study populations (older adults where collagen has a separate joint/connective tissue benefit that indirectly supports training), incomplete control for total protein intake, or genuine superiority of collagen amino acid profile for specific anabolic signaling. The hydroxyproline content of collagen protein and its effect on connective tissue synthesis (tendons, ligaments) is mechanistically plausible as an indirect hypertrophy contributor in compound movement programs like PPL.

The practical position: don't swap out whey entirely based on one meta-analysis. Do consider adding hydrolyzed collagen (10–15g/day, ideally with vitamin C to support synthesis) as a complementary supplement, particularly given PPL's heavy compound movement load on connective tissue.

Source: PMC12862422 · Network meta-analysis · 2026
EPA — Not DHA — Impairs Brain Repair in Repeated Mild Head Injury: MUSC Cell Reports 2026
[Mechanistic]

Researchers at the Medical University of South Carolina published a study in Cell Reports (2026) examining fish oil supplementation in mice exposed to repeated mild traumatic brain injuries (TBI), alongside analysis of human brain microvascular endothelial cells and postmortem CTE brain tissue. The key finding: EPA specifically impairs vascular repair in the brain following repeated impacts, while DHA does not produce the same effect.

The mechanism: EPA, unlike DHA, suppresses genes supporting vascular stability and repair, weakens blood vessel structural integrity in the brain's microvasculature, and contributes to tau protein accumulation — a hallmark of CTE. DHA integrates beneficially into neuronal membranes and doesn't follow this pathway. Lead researcher Onder Albayram, Ph.D. (MUSC) emphasizes this applies specifically to "repeated mild traumatic brain injuries" — not a blanket indictment of EPA for healthy individuals. But the finding is specific and mechanistically detailed enough to take seriously.

The practical implication is narrow but important: anyone with a history of contact sports, combat sports, falls, or occupational head impact risk should consider switching to DHA-dominant omega-3 formulations and monitoring EPA intake. Standard fish oil capsules typically contain roughly equal EPA and DHA without distinction.

Source: ScienceDaily · April 2026 · Cell Reports 2026 · Medical University of South Carolina
Magnesium Bisglycinate (250mg + 1,523mg Glycine) Shows Modest but Real Sleep Improvement in 4-Week RCT
[RCT]

A randomized placebo-controlled trial published in PMC (PMC12412596) tested magnesium bisglycinate supplementation in 155 German adults (mean age 41.5, 80% female, BMI 25.4) with self-reported primary insomnia symptoms. The intervention: two capsules providing 250mg elemental magnesium and 1,523mg glycine in bisglycinate form, taken 30–60 minutes before bed, for 28 days.

Primary outcome was the Insomnia Severity Index (ISI). Results: the magnesium group showed significantly greater ISI reduction vs placebo (−3.9 [95% CI: −5.8 to −2.0] vs −2.3 [95% CI: −4.1 to −0.4], p = 0.049). The effect size is small (Cohen's d = 0.2), and secondary psychological measures didn't differentiate significantly. The authors conclude: supplementation alone unlikely eliminates insomnia, but the effect is real and this is cleaner evidence than most magnesium sleep claims rest on.

The mechanism is dual: magnesium potentiates GABA-A receptor activity (reducing neuronal excitability), and glycine at these doses independently lowers core body temperature, which is a separate sleep-onset facilitator. This combination is more plausible than magnesium alone.

Source: PMC12412596 · RCT · 155 adults · Germany · 2026
New Data — Consider Acting
  • Magnesium bisglycinate 250mg + glycine (from bisglycinate form) 30–60 min before bed — first solid RCT on this specific formulation
  • Shift toward DHA-dominant omega-3 if any contact sport / head impact history — most fish oil doesn't separate EPA:DHA clearly
  • Hydrolyzed collagen 10–15g/day + Vitamin C — worth trialling as complement to existing protein intake, especially for connective tissue under PPL load
Carry-Forward — Stable Positions
  • Total protein ≥1.6g/kg/day remains the primary hypertrophy nutritional lever — supplement timing and type are secondary
  • Omega-3 index (EPA+DHA ≥8% of red blood cell membrane) remains a meaningful CVD risk predictor independent of TBI context
  • Fiber and fermented foods: primary dietary lever for microbiome health while CAG-170 targeted probiotics remain in development
⚠ Hype Watch

Generic fish oil as a universal brain health supplement: The MUSC Cell Reports study makes clear that EPA and DHA have different effects in brain recovery contexts, and that high-dose EPA supplementation may be actively harmful following repeated mild head injuries. Standard fish oil marketing does not make this distinction — most products are formulated as a combined EPA+DHA ratio without distinguishing their independent effects. Anyone supplementing omega-3s for brain health specifically should check their product's EPA:DHA ratio.

Collagen as a "better than whey" protein replacement: The 2026 network meta-analysis finding is interesting but preliminary. The researchers explicitly note few direct comparison studies and reliance on indirect comparisons. Collagen has a different amino acid profile (low leucine, high glycine/proline/hydroxyproline) from whey, which has superior leucine content for mTORC1-mediated muscle protein synthesis. These may serve different roles — structural/connective tissue vs. myofibrillar — rather than being interchangeable.

⚡ Implication: Add magnesium bisglycinate (250mg + glycine) nightly. Audit your fish oil formulation — if you have any head impact history, switch to DHA-dominant. Don't replace whey with collagen, but consider adding 10–15g hydrolyzed collagen for connective tissue support under PPL load.
