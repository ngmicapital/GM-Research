4 ⚖️ Regulatory & Legal Radar

CLARITY Act markup Thursday May 21 is the single most important crypto regulatory date of 2026. The Senate Banking Committee released a 309-page draft on May 12. It formalises the SEC/CFTC split: decentralised networks → CFTC commodity jurisdiction; team-controlled tokens → SEC securities until they mature. Senators Tillis and Alsobrooks proposed a compromise on May 1 to restrict crypto firms from offering deposit-like yields. This is not a kill provision — it's a guardrail. The GENIUS Act stablecoin framework is meanwhile being operationalised by Treasury/OCC with 2026 compliance deadlines. And the Kelp DAO $292M exploit is a reminder that bridge security is still the open wound of cross-chain infrastructure.

📜 Active Legislation & Guidance
  • CLARITY Act — 309-page Senate draft released May 12. Markup Thursday May 21. SEC/CFTC jurisdiction split. Decentralised networks → commodity. 100+ firms lobbying passage.
  • GENIUS Act (Stablecoins) — Treasury/OCC implementing. Licensing, capital, custody, AML deadlines due H1 2026. Payment stablecoin infrastructure being built now.
  • SEC-CFTC Joint Release (Mar 17) — 68-page interpretive guidance, 5-category token taxonomy. Operational and binding.
  • SEC DeFi Relief (Apr 13) — UI providers operate without broker registration. DEX front-ends given runway.

5 🤖 AI & Semiconductor Watch

The NVDA China approval is a permission slip, not a purchase order — but the market doesn't care about that distinction today. The US approved ~10 Chinese companies to buy up to 75,000 H200 chips each through NVDA or authorized distributors. The approved firms include Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance, and JD.com. The complication: Beijing directed those firms not to buy, pushing domestic chip supply chain development instead. No H200s have been delivered to Chinese buyers. But NVDA's stock ripped 4.4% to $235 anyway, pushing market cap to $5.7T — because the market is pricing in eventual execution when Beijing's posture shifts. Learn Something: Why Beijing Is Blocking Its Own Companies From Buying NVDA Chips
It's not just geopolitics — it's industrial policy. China's government wants Alibaba, Tencent, and ByteDance to buy from Huawei's Ascend 910B and domestic suppliers to accelerate China's own chip stack. If Chinese tech giants buy H200s, they reduce the domestic demand that would otherwise force-grow Chinese alternatives. Beijing is essentially using its corporate giants as captive customers to build a domestic AI chip industry. The near-term cost: slower AI deployment in China. The 5-year goal: chip sovereignty. For NVDA investors, this means the China market isn't just blocked by US export controls — it's also being blocked by Beijing itself.
NVDA at $5.7T is pricing in eventual China execution. The H200 approval is the permission; Beijing reversing its directive is the revenue event. Long NVDA for the AI supercycle; the China optionality is a free call option at current prices. Watch Rubin memory supply and 30Y yield trajectory as the dual risks to the thesis.
