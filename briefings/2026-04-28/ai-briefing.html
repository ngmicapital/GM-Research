Model Landscape

No new frontier model in the past 24 hours — focus today is on the follow-through from last week's DeepSeek V4 and Sony Project Ace papers.

DeepSeek V4 — efficient-inference paper now public, 73% FLOP reduction validated ⚡

UpdateResearch1M contextHybrid Attention

Covered in Issue #29 when the V4-Pro/V4-Flash preview dropped at 1/6th Opus 4.7 cost; now the underlying paper is public. What changed: DeepSeek published the architecture detail behind the cost claim — Hybrid Attention Architecture (HAA) plus a redesigned KV-cache layout reduce single-token inference FLOPs by up to 73% and KV cache usage by up to 90% versus V3.2. The cost figure isn't marketing; it's mechanical.

⚡ Implication: If V4-Flash is genuinely 90% lower KV-cache than V3.2, batch inference economics for any agent loop with long-running context (multi-turn coding sessions, Telegram-style ambient agents) shift hard toward open-weight models for non-flagship tasks. Worth a one-day spike to benchmark V4-Flash against Haiku 4.5 on your transcript-intelligence pipeline before next week.

Sony Project Ace — first robot to defeat elite human pros at table tennis, Nature cover

ResearchUse CaseVLA + RL19.6 m/s return velocity

Sony AI's autonomous table-tennis robot beat both elite human players and one of two professional players in December 2025 trials, then defeated all three professional opponents at least once in March 2026 follow-up matches. The paper, "Outplaying Elite Table Tennis Players with an Autonomous Robot," landed on the cover of Nature (April 23).

⚡ Implication: First commonly-played competitive sport defeated by a real-world robot — a Lee Sedol moment for physical AI. The training pipeline (VLA + RL with sim-to-real transfer on a narrow physical task) is the template that Sereact, Figure, and the rest of the VLA-with-world-model cohort will be benchmarked against. Don't trade on the news; do note that "robotics is the AI frontier of 2026" just got a much harder citation behind it.

Research Radar

The DeepSeek V4 inference-efficiency paper is the most under-discussed result of the week — it's the receipts behind the cost claim, and the architecture is not what most people expected.

DeepSeek-V4 — Hybrid Attention Architecture, 73% FLOP reduction, 90% KV-cache reduction

ResearcharXiv April 20261M token context

Authors: DeepSeek-AI team. One-line finding: by replacing the standard self-attention block with a hybrid mechanism that mixes a sparse global pattern with a learned local pattern, V4 keeps near-frontier benchmark performance at one-sixth the inference cost of Opus 4.7 — and the cost reduction is mechanical, not just batch-economics. Why it matters in 6 months: if HAA generalises, the open-weight cost-per-token gap to closed frontier models compresses faster than the capabilities gap, which inverts the economics of agent loops that don't need flagship reasoning.

⚡ Implication: The technique is open. Expect Mistral, Qwen, and the Llama-Stack derivative cohort to ship HAA-equivalent attention variants by July. For dc, the relevant question is whether V4-Flash at $0.14/$0.28 per million tokens is worth a benchmark spike against Haiku 4.5 ($0.20/$1.00) for the cheaper end of the briefings pipeline.

Technicals

Inference economics shifted twice this week: at the chip layer (TPU 8i, MTIA next-gen) and at the architecture layer (HAA) — the cost of running a model just dropped on both axes simultaneously.

Google TPU 8i SRAM-heavy inference chip — broader context behind Issue #31's coverage ⚡

UpdateSRAM-heavy80% perf gain

Covered in Issue #31 at announcement; what changed: Google confirmed both TPU 8t (training) and TPU 8i (inference) will be GA later this year, with the 8i's 384MB on-chip SRAM targeting agentic workloads where KV-cache locality matters more than raw FLOPs. Pair with DeepSeek V4's HAA: if your model halves its KV-cache and your chip triples its on-chip SRAM, the inference cost curve for long-context agents bends meaningfully — not 10%, more like 3–5×.

⚡ Implication: Multiplicative effect. Anthropic, OpenAI and DeepSeek all publish models that get cheaper to serve faster than the chip cycle alone would predict. Inference-cost-per-million-tokens is on a steeper decline curve than benchmark-quality is on an improvement curve, which is the most important second-order effect in the AI economy this year.

Meta–Broadcom MTIA next-gen partnership — multi-generation inference accelerator roadmap

LaunchMTIACustom silicon

Announced April 14: Meta and Broadcom committed to co-developing multiple generations of MTIA (Meta Training and Inference Accelerator) — Meta's purpose-built inference and recommendation chip. The strategic signal: Meta is not just buying Nvidia GPUs and TPUs at volume, it's building independent silicon roadmap, which removes a top-three buyer from the Nvidia bid stack on a multi-year horizon.

⚡ Implication: Less direct than TPU 8i but worth tracking — the four hyperscalers (Google TPU, Amazon Trainium/Inferentia, Microsoft Maia, Meta MTIA) have all now committed to silicon independence. Nvidia's TAM is still growing in absolute terms but its share of new spend is declining. → Market Briefing for any NVDA-adjacent positioning.

🔬 Plain-English Explainer — why "73% FLOP reduction" + "384MB SRAM" matters even if you've never trained a model

Think of an LLM serving a long conversation like a chef remembering every ingredient ever asked for. The longer the conversation, the more memory the chef needs to keep on the bench (this is the "KV cache"). Two things just changed at once: (1) the chef got smarter about which ingredients to keep on the bench versus the pantry — DeepSeek's new architecture cuts the bench-space needed by 90%; (2) the kitchen got a much larger bench — Google's new TPU 8i ships with 3× the on-chip "fast memory" of the prior generation. The practical result: running an AI agent that holds a long context (a coding session, a transcript pipeline, an ambient Telegram bot) gets cheaper by something like 3–5× over the next year — not 10–20% like a normal hardware cycle. For traders, this matters because it accelerates the wedge between "AI tokens with real compute demand" (TAO subnets, RENDER GPU jobs, Akash leases) and "AI tokens that just have AI in the name." Cheaper inference means more agent traffic, which means more demand for verifiable distributed compute. The DePIN sector outperforming traditional DeFi by 45% YTD already reflects this — and it's the second-order trade behind the chip and architecture news.
