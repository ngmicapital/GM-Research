That's why AMD rallied 15% in sympathy — the market is re-rating the entire chip stack, not just the training leaders.

7 🌐 Geopolitical Calendar
🚨 STRAIT OF HORMUZ CRISIS — ACTIVE
Status: Dual blockade since April 13. US blockading Iranian ports; Iran restricting tanker passage through the Strait since Feb 28 following US-Israeli air strikes.

Transmission Mechanism: 20% of global oil transits Hormuz. The blockade has removed 10.1 mb/d from global supply (largest disruption in history per IEA). This flows directly into energy prices (Brent +55% since war began), then into CPI via fuel and transport costs, then into Fed policy (83% hold), then into yield curve (10Y grinding to 4.31%). The second-order effect: airlines, shipping, and consumer discretionary get margin-compressed while energy equities and defence names rally.

Reversal: Iran briefly reopened the Strait on April 17 (crude fell 10% intraday) before re-imposing controls within hours. A sustained diplomatic opening or US naval escort corridor would collapse crude $15–20 in a session. Watch for any US-Iran backchannel signals.
🚨 US TARIFF REGIME SHIFT — ONGOING
Status: Supreme Court struck down IEEPA broad tariffs. Administration responded with Section 122 temporary 15% global import duty. New Section 301 investigations opened in March.

Transmission Mechanism: The tariff overhang keeps import prices elevated, compounding the oil-driven inflation shock. The combination pressures Fed rate cuts and weighs on consumer spending. Supply chain repricing is ongoing — companies are front-loading imports ahead of potential escalation, which temporarily inflates inventory and trade data.

Reversal: Congressional override of Section 122 authority (unlikely with current majority) or trade deal announcement. Watch the midterm election calculus — voter sensitivity to energy/food prices may force de-escalation.
🚨 IMF GLOBAL GROWTH DOWNGRADE — APRIL 2026
Status: IMF cut 2026 global growth to 3.1% with headline inflation at 4.4% in its April World Economic Outlook. Reference forecast assumes a short-lived Iran conflict.

Transmission Mechanism: If the conflict extends beyond Q2, the IMF's optimistic 3.1% baseline breaks down. A sustained $120+ oil scenario pushes inflation to 5%+ and growth below 2.5% — stagflation territory. Central banks globally would be forced into a choice between fighting inflation and supporting growth, which historically means volatility spikes across all asset classes.

Reversal: Rapid Hormuz de-escalation + coordinated SPR releases. Without that, the IMF baseline looks too optimistic.
Hormuz is the single highest-impact geopolitical risk on markets right now. Every $10/bbl move in crude shifts the macro calculus. Long energy and defence as hedges; watch for US-Iran backchannel signals as the potential reversal catalyst.
