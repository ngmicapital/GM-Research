4 ⚖ Regulatory & Legal Radar
EST

No US crypto market-structure law arrives before the new year, so today’s regulatory risk is about enforcement and rates, not about Congress. The CLARITY Act has stalled, the 3 November midterms have reset the committee map, and the next real decision point is the Fed. I would not trade a legislative headline as a catalyst this week.

Active Legislation

  • CLARITY Act (H.R. 3633): the House passed the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act in July 2025 by 294 to 134.
  • Senate Banking: the committee advanced the bill 15–9 on 14 May 2026, and the amended text was reported to the full Senate on 1 June.
  • Floor vote: the Senate failed cloture on 15 September 2026 by 49–50, well short of the 60 votes needed. Passage this Congress is widely viewed as unlikely.
  • Procedural life: Senator Lummis called the bill dead, but Senator Tillis’s vote change preserves a path to revive it. The lame-duck session is the earliest realistic window and is unlikely.

Today’s Decision Points

  • Fed, 8–9 December: the decision point that matters for crypto. A 25bp hike is priced at about 95%. The 50bp tail was about 8% on 16 November.
  • Hong Kong crypto licensing bill: reported to be heading for tabling before the end of 2026. A headline risk, not a price driver today.
  • Nvidia legal overhang: California subpoenaed the company over China-related chip disclosures, and a 16 November US chip-access rule added to the uncertainty.
  • Nvidia class action: the lead-plaintiff deadline is 8 January 2027, so expect filings, not verdicts, this month.
Regulatory call: treat legislation as dead for this year and price any crypto-policy headline as noise unless it changes the Fed path. The Nvidia subpoena and export-rule risk is the live legal story for AI names, and it adds to the multiple compression rather than to the earnings risk.
5 🤖 AI & Semiconductor Watch
EST

The AI capex cycle is still accelerating at the top line, but the stock market has stopped paying for it. The multiple is compressing on leadership risk, so this is a fatiguing equity trade even as the fundamentals broaden. Nvidia’s fiscal third-quarter revenue of $115.8B was up 103% year on year and ahead of guidance, and the company guided fourth-quarter revenue to about $130B. The problem is what sits around the numbers: the CEO transition, the China exclusion from guidance, and a sharp de-rating.

Key Movers & Data

  • NVDA: closed at $145.98 on 19 November, down 3.3%. The stock is about 36% below its 6 October intraday record of 243.37 and at the lower end of its post-November range.
  • Moving averages: the 50-day and 200-day MAs were not retrieved from TrueNorth this run EST.
  • Analyst action: Melius cut NVDA to Hold with a price target of 185 from 315 on 12 November, while UBS kept a Buy rating and called the pullback a buying opportunity.
  • INTC: not retrieved this run, so I am not making a call on it today.

Broader AI Landscape

  • Demand: management said the company is sold out for the next 12 to 18 months, and it called the Vera Rubin ramp an explosive start.
  • Capital return: a $150B buyback addition takes the total authorisation to about $227B.
  • China: guidance assumes no China data-centre compute revenue, and the company stopped licensed China chip sales.
  • Sentiment: Anthropic’s IPO gave an early boost, but the rally did not hold.
Learn: why a beat can fall. A company can beat earnings and still lose value when the market is repricing its future multiple rather than its current profit. That happens when the reported quarter is strong but the risk around the next one rises, through leadership change, customer concentration, or policy. In that setting, the stock responds to positioning and the rate backdrop, not to the headline beat. Watch for the reverse: a stock that rises on bad news is usually a sign the selling is exhausted.
AI call: keep the capex thesis intact and the stock thesis underweight until the multiple stabilises. The next confirmation would be NVDA reclaiming the post-earnings range with the 10-year yield falling. A failure at the November lows would show the market is pricing the end of the cycle, not just a rough patch.

7 🌎 Geopolitical Calendar

Oil is now a bargaining chip, not a risk premium: Brent has fallen from a wartime peak near $125, but the Iran strike talk and Hormuz shipping risk can still lift it in a day. The transmission runs through inflation and the Fed, so every geopolitical headline is really a rate headline.

🏴 US–Iran strike risk (Active)
Status: reports say the US is weighing additional military strikes on Iran, with Israel urging a four-to-seven-day campaign. WTI rose above $84 at one point, then steadied around $83 on 19–20 November, near its lowest since late August.
Transmission Mechanism: a strike headline lifts crude, which lifts the inflation expectations that feed the Fed hike path. That pushes the 10-year and DXY higher and hits BTC and high-multiple equities together.
Reversal Signal: a credible diplomatic track or a US–Iran memorandum that holds, with oil falling back under $80. That would ease the inflation read and take pressure off the 50bp tail.
🏴 Strait of Hormuz shipping risk (Active)
Status: shipping risk around the Strait remains high, which has kept Brent elevated even as supply recovers. Brent traded below $92 on 12 November and was back near $96 on 13 November on Houthi strikes on Saudi pumping stations.
Transmission Mechanism: a tanker incident or a Houthi escalation pushes Brent above the mid-$90s, which feeds headline inflation and reinforces the hawkish Fed path. Energy names benefit, rate-sensitive equities and crypto do not.
Reversal Signal: confirmed resumption of Gulf export volumes and a sustained fall in Brent toward the low $80s would show the supply story is winning.
🏴 Russia–Ukraine talks and refinery attacks (Active)
Status: peace efforts are advancing on paper, but the key differences remain. Ukrainian attacks on Russian refineries and tankers continue to limit Russian export capacity.
Transmission Mechanism: a breakdown in talks keeps Russian crude restricted and supports the oil floor, which keeps energy inflation elevated. A genuine settlement is a disinflation impulse for Europe and, at the margin, the US.
Reversal Signal: a signed framework with a timetable for sanctions relief on Russian crude would take a bid out of oil and ease the inflation side of the Fed debate.
Geopolitical call: the oil path is the input that decides whether the December hike stays at 25bp. Treat Iran and Hormuz as the live risks, and treat Russia–Ukraine as a slower-moving background variable. I would not add risk on a geopolitical de-escalation headline until hike odds start to ease as well.
