Alpha — Cumulative Volume Delta (CVD)

Friday, 20 November 2026 · Sydney, Australia · Issue 58 11 min read
TL;DR

Cumulative Volume Delta (CVD) is the running total of aggressive buying minus aggressive selling, built from trades that cross the spread, and it matters because it shows which side was paying up to move price. The practical takeaway: read the slope of CVD against the swing structure of price, not its absolute value. When price makes a new high or low that CVD fails to confirm, you have a warning that the move is running on passive flow or fading aggression. Treat that warning as context and wait for a level and a trigger before acting. On the current tape, BTC is down about 13% over seven days with Fear and Greed in the 10 to 12 range, so the question is whether sellers are still the aggressors or are running out of fuel.

§01
What It Is
The running tally of who was paying up
CVD is the running total of aggressive buy volume minus aggressive sell volume, plotted as a single line that shows which side has been crossing the spread.

Cumulative Volume Delta is the running total of aggressive buying minus aggressive selling. Every market order that lifts the offer counts as buying, every market order that hits the bid counts as selling, and the net of each bar is added to a line that never resets inside the window you choose. The line is the position of the aggressors. Price tells you where the market ended up; CVD tells you which side had to cross the spread to get it there.

The reason it earns a place on the chart is that volume alone is blind to direction. Two hours can print identical total volume and opposite CVD: one where buyers lift offers all session, one where sellers keep hitting bids into a flat tape. CVD separates those cases because it only counts orders that took liquidity. Passive limit fills never move it, which is both its strength and its blind spot, and the reason it is strongest when read next to what price is doing.

For a trader who already reads the tape, CVD is not an oscillator to be traded in isolation. It is a test of whether the flow agrees with the move. This builds on Passive vs Aggressive Flow (19 Nov 2026 issue), because the aggressive-versus-passive split is the raw material CVD accumulates. If that split is unclear, go back to that issue first.

§02
Mechanics
From each bar's delta to one running line
Each bar's delta is added to the previous total, so the line only moves when a market order takes liquidity, and limit fills leave it untouched.

The mechanic runs in three steps, and each one matters for how you read the output.

Step 1, classify each trade. Exchanges stream every execution with a price, a size and an aggressor side. A trade that prints at the ask was initiated by a buyer lifting offers. A trade that prints at the bid was initiated by a seller hitting bids. Limit orders resting on the book are not classified until they are hit.

Step 2, net each bar. For each candle or time slice, total the buy-initiated volume and subtract the sell-initiated volume. A positive bar means buyers dominated the window; a negative bar means sellers did. A single bar is noisy, which is why the next step exists.

Step 3, accumulate. Add each bar's delta to the previous CVD value. The line rises while buyers keep dominating and falls while sellers do. Its slope is the information. Its absolute level depends entirely on where you started the sum, so two traders with different anchors will see different numbers and the same shape.

Deltan = Buy-initiated volumen − Sell-initiated volumen
CVDn = CVDn−1 + Deltan
Anchor CVD0 at a session open or a fixed start. Read the slope and divergence against price, never the raw number.

Because only aggressive orders count, a rising price on falling CVD means the advance is being filled by passive liquidity rather than by people paying the spread. That is the mechanical basis of divergence, and it is also why CVD cannot tell you about the passive side directly; you infer it from price not responding.

The moment the price line steps up and the CVD line steps down, you are looking at a move that has stopped being paid for by the people who used to pay for it.

§04
In Practice
THREE WAYS TO USE THE LINE
Divergence is a warning, not a trigger: only act on CVD disagreement when it sits at a level you already had marked and price shows a reaction.
1

Trust the disagreement, not the direction

CVD rarely tells you where price goes next. Its value is in the moments when price and the flow stop agreeing at a level that already matters to you. Start with the level, then ask whether the flow confirms it when price gets there.

2

Anchor the line on purpose

A session-anchored CVD resets when the day changes, which can hide a multi-day distribution. Use a fixed lookback for swings and a session anchor for intraday work, and never compare two charts with different anchors as if they were the same line.

3

Cross-check the venue before you trust the slope

A CVD divergence on one exchange can be a local dislocation rather than a market-wide shift. Before acting, check whether the same shape appears on at least one other large venue, and note any gap in the window.

§07
Common Traps
THE LINE SEES ONLY THE IMPATIENT SIDE
Treating CVD as a standalone buy or sell signal, or reading the absolute number instead of the slope and the divergence against price.
1
Treating divergence as a trade

A CVD divergence is a warning that the move is losing its funding, not an entry. Wait for a level, a rejection and a trigger, and let the flow be the confirmation rather than the reason.

2
Reading the absolute number

The CVD value depends on the anchor you chose, so the raw level means nothing across charts. Read the slope and whether the line made a higher or lower high against price.

3
Forgetting the impatient side only

CVD sees the aggressors and nothing else. A flat price on falling CVD can mean sellers are being absorbed, or it can mean the passive side has simply not been tested yet. Check the book and the reaction before deciding which.

4
Trusting one venue

Exchange CVD can diverge from the others for a full session before price catches up. Cross-check at least one other large venue before you call a divergence market-wide.

!
Overhyped: the divergence-only system

Vendor content often sells CVD divergence as a standalone edge. The honest read is that it is a useful context tool with no published, independent evidence of a standalone edge in the material I reviewed. If a course promises a win rate from divergence alone, treat that as marketing.
