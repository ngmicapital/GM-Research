Alpha — Naked POC

Sunday · 24 May 2026 · Sydney, Australia · Issue #30 11-min read
TL;DR

A Naked POC is a prior session's Point of Control that price has not yet returned to — a high-volume price the market once agreed was fair and then walked away from without retesting. Naked POCs behave like overhead gaps: the auction tends to revisit them, often within ten sessions, because the participants who positioned around that level are still waiting for confirmation. Mark every naked daily, weekly, and monthly POC on the chart, then use them as gravity targets, not standalone entries — the magnet decides the destination; structure decides the trade.

§01
What It Is
Unfinished auctions · the magnet on the chart you can't see until you mark it
A Naked POC is the most-traded price of a prior session that price has not revisited since — an untested fair-value level the auction has agreed to come back and audit.

Every trading session produces one Point of Control: the single price at which the most volume changed hands. It marks the centre of the day's value — the price both sides treated as fair for the longest stretch of time. Most of the time, the next session pokes back through that POC and the level is reabsorbed into the broader profile. But sometimes price moves away cleanly and never returns. That orphaned POC — a fair-value level that was never re-auctioned — is a Naked POC (also called Virgin POC, NPOC, or NVPOC; the terms are interchangeable).

The premise behind why these levels matter is auction-mechanic, not technical: a Point of Control is, by definition, the price where the market reached its strongest consensus on value. When price leaves that consensus without revisiting it, there is unfinished business. As AMS Trading Group puts it: "the market has not gone back to see if this still represents value." The auction is incomplete. Sooner or later the market will return to ask the question again — and the answer (acceptance or rejection) will produce a tradable move.

This is why naked POCs behave like price gaps. Both represent missing two-way trade. Both tend to fill. The difference: a price gap is a hole in the candle structure that's obvious to every retail trader. A naked POC is a hole in the volume structure that's invisible unless you have a profile drawn — which is precisely why it stays magnetic. Most chart-watchers can't see it; the auction can. Adam at Trading Riot frames the underlying logic cleanly: "the market is an ongoing auction looking to facilitate trade by alternating between balanced and imbalanced periods." An untested POC is balance left mid-question.

In crypto you typically track three layers in parallel: daily naked POCs (5–10 sessions back, shortest-cycle magnets), weekly naked POCs (multi-week structural targets), and monthly/quarterly naked POCs (deep-cycle reference levels that mark major regime pivots). The cluster of all three sitting near each other is where the highest-conviction trades are built — three magnets aligned at the same price is not three signals, it is one very heavy signal.

§02
Mechanics
How they form · why they pull · time-decay and the magnet hierarchy
A POC turns naked the moment the next session closes without trading back through it; it stays naked until a future candle's range covers that price — and the longer it stays naked, the heavier the pull.

The formation rule is mechanical. At the close of every session you build a volume profile of that day, identify the single price with the highest volume bar (the POC), and store it as a horizontal line. Each subsequent day, every POC line is checked: if any candle of the new session covers (trades through) that price, the POC is marked as tested and removed from the naked list. If the session closes without touching it, the POC remains naked and is carried forward.

Marking rule: POC(t) := price with max volume in session t naked(t) := { POC(s) : s < t, AND no session s' ∈ (s, t] has high(s') ≥ POC(s) ≥ low(s') } Magnet probability (practitioner heuristic): P(test within N sessions) ≈ 80% for N = 10 → source: Chart Champions / Buildix dataset; no peer-reviewed validation → treat as directional, not predictive — sample bias and timeframe selection are real Distance filter (crypto): trade-relevant if |price − naked_POC| / price < 2-3% → further-away NPOCs exist but have lower near-term magnetism
The 80%-within-10-sessions figure comes from practitioner-shared datasets, not academic research. It is broadly consistent with the gap-fill statistics traders quote for traditional markets but should be treated as a working heuristic — not a backtested edge. The mechanism (unfinished auctions get retested) is robust; the exact probability is approximate.

The reason naked POCs pull is structural, not psychological. Every POC is, by construction, the level at which the largest cohort of size was transacted that session. Institutional executions concentrate around these levels because algorithms are programmed to participate where liquidity is thickest. When price leaves without retesting, those positions are still open at unsettled value — participants positioned around the POC have an inventory question (was that price still fair?) that only a retest can answer. Algorithms running responsive logic at known volume nodes will route orders back toward the level. Discretionary traders watching profile do the same. The magnet is not myth — it is the recursive expectation of liquidity meeting unfinished trade.

The magnet has a hierarchy. Not all naked POCs are equal. The order of strength typically runs:

TimeframeMagnetismTypical hold time before test
Monthly / Quarterly POCStrongest — anchor-level pivotsWeeks to months; high probability of eventual test
Weekly POCStrong — swing-level magnets1–4 weeks typical
Daily POCModerate — most relevant 5–10 sessions out2–10 sessions; decays sharply past that
Session / 4H POCWeak — intraday onlySame session or next, then loses relevance

Profile thickness matters as much as recency. A POC bar that towers above its neighbours represents stronger consensus and therefore a stronger magnet. A POC that is only marginally taller than the surrounding bars — typical in fast-trend sessions — is a weak magnet. The Buildix guide states the filter directly: "the thicker the POC bar, the stronger the magnet." In practice this means filtering out POCs from low-volume holiday sessions or thin overnight ranges — they qualify as "naked" but they do not pull.

Time-decay is real but loose. A daily naked POC from three sessions ago carries weight; from twenty sessions ago, it has been displaced by enough new structure that the magnet is mostly gone. Weekly and monthly POCs decay much more slowly — a monthly naked POC from six months back can still resolve when price finally retraces into its zone. The GoCharting guide recommends tracking 5–10 recent sessions; deeper history matters only for the higher-timeframe layer.

§04
In Practice
Mark the levels · filter the noise · trade the response, not the arrival
The one rule: a naked POC is a target, not an entry. The trade is the auction's reaction to the level — never the touch itself.
1

Mark Three Layers, Carry Them Forward

Every Sunday night (or session-open in your timezone), refresh the chart: mark each daily naked POC from the past 10 sessions, the weekly naked POCs from the past 6 weeks, and the monthly/quarterly naked POCs from the past 6 months. Three layers, three colours. Most platforms auto-extend POC lines forward — Chart Champions, TradingView volume-profile add-ons, and ATAS all support this. The discipline is in maintaining the list, not in the indicator.

2

Filter by Thickness and Cluster

Not every naked POC matters. A POC from a thin holiday session or an overnight low-volume range qualifies as naked but has no magnetic mass. Keep only POCs from normal-volume or high-volume sessions — POCs whose profile bar towered above its neighbours. Then look for clusters: where two or three naked POCs from different timeframes sit within a fraction of a percent of each other, the magnet is amplified. A daily NPOC sitting on top of a weekly NPOC is the highest-conviction level on the chart.

3

Use Naked POCs as Targets, Not Entries

The single most common mistake. Naked POCs are destinations — they tell you where price is likely heading, not where to climb in. If BTC sits $1,500 below a clear NPOC and the broader structure is bullish, the NPOC is your take-profit zone for an existing long. If price arrives at the NPOC and stalls, then you have a setup: short the rejection with the NPOC as the line in the sand. Entering at the touch — without waiting for a response — turns a high-probability target into a coin-flip trade.

4

Combine with Auction Context — HVN, LVN, IB

A naked POC that sits in the middle of a high-volume node is a stronger reversal candidate than one sitting in thin air. A naked POC that lines up with the Initial Balance high or low, or with quarterly VWAP, becomes a confluence level worth real size. CVD divergence into the touch (Alpha #2) and aggressive perp flow against passive spot (Alpha #13) are the order-flow tells that confirm the rejection. The level is the where; the order flow is the when; the structural overlay is the why this size.

Worked Example — Short the NPOC Test
SetupBTC trading at $75,958. A naked daily POC from 6 sessions ago sits at $76,220 (also short-liq wall, per Coinglass — Morning Edge 24/5).
ConfluenceNPOC $76,220 + the upper end of the prior week's value area + the visible short-liq cluster $76,220-$76,390 = three magnets within 0.2%.
Plan A (target)Already long from $74,800 → take profit into $76,220 zone. Use the cluster as a partial-out level, not a hold-through.
Plan B (fade entry)If price wicks above $76,220 and 1H closes back below, short at market with stop at $76,500 (above the wick). Target: $74,740 (Wyckoff Spring / prior week NPOC). R = $280, target = $1,200, ~4:1 R:R.
Invalidation (Plan B)1H candle accepts above $76,400 (closes inside and the next one holds). At that point the NPOC has been absorbed — the squeeze trigger is live, exit immediately.
Sizing notePlan B is smaller size than the underlying long position. Fading magnets at the squeeze trigger is a counter-trend reaction trade, not a trend trade — half-size at most.

Timeframe fit: Daily / 4H for mapping NPOCs; 1H / 15m for the response and entry trigger; 5m only for executions inside the trigger candle. Higher-timeframe NPOCs (weekly, monthly) drive the bias; lower-timeframe profile is for execution. Never use a 5m developing POC as a magnet — it migrates too fast to be one.

Quotes
Auction-theory classicists and current practitioners on unfinished business
The same insight runs from Wyckoff in 1910 through Dalton in the 2000s to current crypto-native profile traders: unfinished auctions ask the question again.

"The market exists for one purpose only: to facilitate trade. To accomplish this, the market continues to auction until trade can no longer be facilitated. When old activity has not been reauctioned, the market remembers."

James F. Dalton · Markets in Profile: Profiting from the Auction Process · 2007 · The foundational text on Market Profile and untested value

"The market is an ongoing auction looking to facilitate trade by alternating between balanced and imbalanced periods. Aggressive participants are willing to pay the spread to get filled immediately — and prices return to where size has not yet had its question answered."

Adam · Trading Riot · Auction Market Theory · Feb 2026 · source

"A Naked or Virgin VPOC is the price that saw the most acceptance by both buyers and sellers, but the market has not gone back to see if this still represents value."

AMS Trading Group · Knowledge Base · NVPOC definition · source

"Whenever the tape shows little response to effort — heavy volume and little movement — the experienced tape reader recognises that the market is meeting demand or supply stronger than the move itself."

Richard D. Wyckoff · Studies in Tape Reading · 1910 · The first English-language statement of effort/result and unfinished business at volume nodes

"Traders can go further and note where the POC of the week, month, quarter sits, see if any of these levels are 'virgin' levels — they will provide a large inflection point in the market, especially if they haven't been traded in a very long time and if they are clustered together in a key region."

TRADEPRO Academy · Volume Profile Inflection Points · source
§07
Common Traps
Five ways the naked POC framework gets misused
The biggest mistake: treating every untested POC as a guaranteed magnet — most of the failures come from acting on weak NPOCs with no context.
1
Entering at the Touch Instead of Trading the Response

The most expensive mistake. Price arriving at a naked POC is the setup, not the trade. The trade is what price does after the touch — accept above, reject below, or chop sideways. Entering blind at the level is a coin flip masquerading as a strategy. Always wait for the wick-and-close-back signature or a clear acceptance candle before committing risk.

2
Marking Every POC Without Filtering for Quality

A POC from a low-volume holiday session, a thin Asia overnight, or a profile where the POC bar barely rises above its neighbours is a "naked POC" technically — but it has almost no magnetic mass. Charts pollute fast if you mark them all. Only carry forward POCs from normal-or-higher volume sessions with a visibly dominant POC bar. The thickness of the bar at formation predicts the strength of the magnet.

3
Ignoring Time-Decay on Daily NPOCs

A daily naked POC from three sessions back is live structure. A daily NPOC from twenty sessions back has been displaced by enough new value that the magnet is mostly gone — price will eventually trade through it, but not because it pulled there. Treat the 5–10 session window as the practical relevance band for daily NPOCs. Weekly and monthly NPOCs decay much more slowly and warrant carrying for months.

4
Using NPOCs in Isolation Without Higher-Timeframe Context

A 5m naked POC in a strong trending session is not a reversal level — it is a level price will blow through. NPOCs work when the higher-timeframe regime is balanced or rotating, where there is genuine "go back and audit value" behaviour. Inside parabolic legs, NPOCs get walked past without reaction. Use them in ranges and transitions; expect them to fail in trending markets.

5
The "80% Revisit" Statistic is a Heuristic, Not a Backtest

The widely-quoted figure that ~80% of naked POCs get revisited within 10 sessions originates from practitioner-shared datasets (Chart Champions, Buildix) with no peer-reviewed validation. The directional claim — that NPOCs tend to be revisited — is robust and matches the gap-fill intuition. The precise probability is approximate and will vary by asset, timeframe, and regime. Anyone selling you a "naked POC bot" that bakes in 80% win-rate math is selling you a story, not an edge. Use the framework as a mapping tool and a target-generator; never as a standalone signal.
