TideGS — 3D Gaussian Splatting with 1B+ primitives on a single GPU ⚡

Research HuggingFace trending: #1 weekend slot Scale: 1B+ primitives on single GPU Method: SSD-CPU-GPU hierarchy + block-virtualization

New for Issue #54 per HuggingFace papers trending: TideGS enables training 3D Gaussian Splatting with over one billion primitives on a single GPU by managing parameters across an SSD-CPU-GPU hierarchy through block-virtualization, an asynchronous pipeline, and differential streaming techniques. The structural innovation is the SSD-as-extended-VRAM pattern: rather than requiring multi-GPU rigs to fit 1B+ Gaussian primitives in GPU memory, TideGS treats the SSD as a hot-swappable cold tier, the CPU as a warm tier, and the GPU as the active tier — with block-virtualization to manage the address translation and an asynchronous pipeline to hide the streaming latency. Differential streaming further reduces the SSD ↔ CPU bandwidth requirement by only re-streaming the deltas that change per training step. The single-GPU milestone is the structurally important read: 3D-scene-AI workloads no longer require multi-GPU rigs for frontier-tier work.

⚡ Why it matters in 6 months: The SSD-CPU-GPU hierarchy is a generalisable architectural pattern beyond 3D Gaussian Splatting. If TideGS-style hierarchical-memory training becomes the default for parameter-heavy workloads, the inference + training cost curves for large-context-window models flatten further — which compounds the §08 architecture-of-2026 thesis. The cross-reference to §03 holds: another marginal-compute-economics inflection that structurally pressures the DePIN compute basket (AKT / RNDR) on a multi-quarter horizon. For dc's stack: not directly actionable today, but the block-virtualization + differential-streaming pattern is the kind of architectural template that future briefings-stack visualization pipelines could borrow from when scaling beyond single-process limits.
