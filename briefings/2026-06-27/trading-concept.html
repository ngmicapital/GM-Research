Alpha — VWAP & Anchored VWAP

Saturday · 27 June 2026 · Sydney, Australia · Issue #61 11 min read
TL;DR

VWAP is the volume-weighted average price — cumulative value traded divided by cumulative volume — and it is the single most structurally significant line on an intraday chart because the execution algos at every desk are programmed to beat it, which makes price gravitate to, react at, and respect it. Trade with the side of VWAP price holds, fade the ±2σ band extremes back toward the mean, and drop a fresh anchored VWAP off every major swing to read who is actually offside.

§01
What It Is
The volume-weighted fair-value line
VWAP is cumulative value traded divided by cumulative volume — the average price weighted by size, not time, and the benchmark institutional execution is graded against.

VWAP is not an indicator you overlay for decoration. It is the one line on the chart that has real money mechanically defending it. Every trade contributes its price multiplied by its size to a running total; divide that total value by the total volume and you get the price at which the average unit actually changed hands. A 500-BTC print at $60,000 bends the line hard; a 0.01-BTC print barely moves it. That weighting-by-size is the entire difference from a moving average, which counts every bar equally no matter how thin.

It matters because institutions benchmark large orders against the session VWAP. A buy filled below VWAP is a good fill; above it, a poor one — and that scorecard drives the VWAP execution algorithms at every brokerage. The line is self-reinforcing: price respects VWAP because the biggest participants are programmed to execute around it, and they execute around it because price respects it. The first desk to benchmark against it was James Elkins at Abel Noser in 1984, executing for the Ford Motor pension fund; it was formalised in a 1988 Journal of Finance paper. Four decades later it runs under most of the algorithmic volume you trade against.

Brian Shannon, the modern anchored-VWAP authority, frames it the way a Wyckoff trader already thinks: "Volume is the cause and price is the effect." VWAP is simply the running tally of that cause — the price where the day's effort actually accumulated.

§02
Mechanics
From every print to one weighted line
Multiply each print's price by its size, keep a running sum of that value and of volume, divide one by the other — then wrap the result in standard-deviation bands to map where price is statistically stretched.

The calculation is a cumulative ratio, recomputed on every bar from the anchor (session open or a chosen event) forward. It never looks back over a fixed window the way a 20-period EMA does — it accumulates from the anchor to now.

VWAP = Σ ( Pj × Qj ) / Σ ( Qj )
Pj = typical price of bar j, usually (High + Low + Close) / 3 · Qj = volume of bar j · sums run cumulatively from the anchor.

The bands are the second half of the tool. Compute the volume-weighted standard deviation of price around VWAP and plot it as an envelope:

Bandk = VWAP ± k·σvw   (k = 1, 2, 3)
±1σ ≈ the "Value Zone" where price spends ~68% of the session · ±2σ ≈ ~95% · ±3σ = statistical anomaly, where mean reversion becomes the base case.

One mechanical quirk drives most VWAP behaviour: the line stiffens as the session ages. Early on, each new print is large relative to the tiny cumulative volume, so VWAP swings freely. By late session the cumulative denominator is huge, so new trades barely move it — late-day "rejections" off VWAP are partly an artefact of that inertia. Crypto's 00:00 UTC reset re-sensitises the line each day; that is why session VWAP is an intraday tool, while multi-day context needs an anchored VWAP instead.

§04
In Practice
Trade the side, fade the band, anchor the swing
The one rule: align with the side of VWAP price holds — long above a rising VWAP, short below a falling one — and only fade VWAP at the ±2σ/±3σ extremes, never in the middle.
1

Trade the side, not the touch

Above a rising VWAP, buyers own the session; below a falling one, sellers do. Don't fight the side until price reclaims or loses the line on a volume expansion — that flip, not a casual touch, is the signal.

2

Buy the pullback that holds

In an uptrend, the first pullback that tags VWAP (or −1σ) and reclaims is the continuation long; stop below the band, trail against VWAP. The mirror works for shorts under a falling VWAP.

3

Fade only the extreme

A stretch to ±2σ/±3σ on fading volume with a rejection candle reverts toward the mean — the "institutional reversion" trade. Target VWAP itself; the band edge is the entry, not the destination.

4

Anchor the catalyst

Drop an anchored VWAP on the swing high/low, the CPI/FOMC bar, or the breakout candle. Where it sits versus price tells you whether longs or shorts from that event are offside and likely to defend or capitulate.

§07
Common Traps
Where VWAP gets people chopped
The single biggest mistake: treating VWAP as a magic line in a balanced, low-volume tape — it only carries an edge when real volume is defending it.
1
Trading VWAP in chop

VWAP has an edge only when relative volume is high and price is trending off the line. In a low-volume, balanced range it is just the middle of the noise — buying every touch gets you chopped. Require a volume expansion before trusting a reclaim or loss.

2
Reading a stiff late-session line

By late session the cumulative volume is enormous, so new prints barely move VWAP — a "rejection" near the close is half optical illusion. Weight late-day VWAP signals less; in crypto, respect the 00:00 UTC reset that re-sensitises the line.

3
Cherry-picking the anchor

Anchored VWAP only works if the anchor is a real, objective event — a swing high/low, a CPI bar, a breakout. Shannon himself calls anchor choice "the most subjective part of our analysis." Anchor to events anyone can point to, not whatever makes the line "work" in hindsight.

4
Confusing VWAP with a moving average

Session VWAP resets; a 20-EMA does not. Dragging a single day's VWAP across multiple sessions produces garbage. For multi-day context use an anchored VWAP from a meaningful pivot, not a stretched intraday line.

5
Overhyped: VWAP bands as a crystal ball

The ±2σ/±3σ "statistical reversion" framing borrows the language of a normal distribution, but crypto returns are fat-tailed. A 3σ stretch inside a trending squeeze keeps going and stops out every mean-reversion fader. The bands map where price is stretched, not where it must turn — use them as a context filter, not a signal.
