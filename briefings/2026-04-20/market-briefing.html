5 ⚖️ Regulatory & Legal Radar
The SEC just made DeFi front-ends legal. The Commission issued a policy statement clarifying that software interfaces allowing users to execute securities transactions through self-hosted wallets won't trigger broker registration requirements. This is an interim step — valid for five years unless the Commission acts — but it's the clearest green light yet for decentralised trading interfaces under US securities law.
New: DeFi Front-End Exemption

What changed: SEC staff found that broker regulations won't apply to software interfaces that enable self-hosted wallet transactions. This means DEX front-ends like Uniswap, Jupiter, and others won't need to register as brokers — for now.

Caveat: This is a staff statement, not a rule. It sunsets in 5 years. But it represents the Atkins SEC's posture: enforce fraud, accommodate innovation.

📚 Learn Something: AI Inference vs Training Economics
When people talk about AI compute, they usually mean training — the massive upfront cost of teaching a model. GPT-4-class models cost $50-100M+ to train. But the bigger long-term market is inference: the cost of actually running the model every time someone asks it a question. Training happens once (or periodically); inference happens billions of times per day across every user. OpenAI reportedly spends more on inference than training now. This matters for investors because training favours the absolute fastest GPUs (NVIDIA's moat), while inference favours efficiency — smaller models, distillation, and custom ASICs that do one thing well. The Rubin R100's claim of "90% lower inference token costs" is NVIDIA's attempt to own both sides of the market before custom chips eat the inference pie.
The 11-day streak will end — the question is whether it ends on profit-taking or on a macro shock. Into Tuesday's ceasefire deadline, trimming NVDA exposure is prudent risk management even if the fundamental thesis is intact. The inference economics shift is the longer-term story that justifies re-entry on any pullback.
