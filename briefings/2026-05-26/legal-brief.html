Headline SEC Press Release 2026-45 PRIMARY Enforcement / Settlement Policy
🟢 High Confidence — formally adopted; effective on Federal Register publication

SEC Formally Rescinds 54-Year-Old Rule 202.5(e) "No-Deny" Gag Provision And Will Not Enforce Existing Gag Clauses In Past Settlements; Crypto Defendants Settling With The Commission May Now Publicly Contest Allegations While Accepting Penalties — First Structural Rebalancing Of Enforcement Settlement Dynamics Since Sloan-Era Civil-Penalty Reforms

"Rescinding Rule 202.5(e) aligns the Commission with the overwhelming majority of federal agencies that do not have a similar rule and gives the Commission more flexibility in settling enforcement actions, which conserves resources, provides certainty, and potentially expedites the return of money to injured investors." — SEC Press Release 2026-45, 18 May 2026

The Securities and Exchange Commission on 18 May 2026 formally rescinded Rule 202.5(e) of its Informal and Other Procedures, ending a policy that since 1972 had required settling defendants to agree never to "publicly deny" the Commission's allegations. The rescission applies prospectively and, critically, the Commission also stated it will not enforce existing no-deny clauses already embedded in thousands of prior settlement orders. The rule had been challenged for years on First Amendment grounds by Cato Institute, Mark Cuban, and former SEC Commissioner Joseph Grundfest, all of whom argued the policy operated as an unconstitutional speech tax on regulated parties.

For crypto enforcement specifically, the rescission lands at a moment when the Commission has already dismissed seven major crypto cases (Coinbase, Cumberland DRW, Consensys, Payward/Kraken, Dragonchain, Balina, Binance) since February 2025 and replaced "regulation by enforcement" with the SEC-CFTC Joint Interpretation issued on 17 March 2026. Going forward, crypto firms settling residual matters — fraud, market manipulation, registration violations under the new taxonomy — can now publicly characterise the conduct as they see it, including arguing the SEC's interpretation was wrong, while still paying disgorgement and civil penalties. Sullivan & Cromwell's 19 May 2026 client memo flagged this as the most significant change to SEC settlement economics in two decades.

📎 Historical Precedent: Rule 202.5(e) was adopted in November 1972 after SEC v. Vesco settlements collapsed under media counter-narratives. It was unsuccessfully challenged in SEC v. Romeril (2d Cir. 2019) and SEC v. Novinger (5th Cir. 2022); the D.C. Circuit denied cert in Cato Institute v. SEC in 2023. Today's rescission moots all pending challenges.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis
The Sullivan & Cromwell memo (19 May) treats this as a unilateral concession by the Commission with no quid pro quo: defendants get speech rights back without any corresponding tightening of the penalty matrix. Foley & Lardner's 20 May alert reads the move as a structural signal — Atkins-era SEC views aggressive settlement extraction as inconsistent with administrative due process and is migrating toward a "fair notice → narrow charge → clean settlement" model. The hidden beneficiary: defence counsel can now negotiate settlements knowing their clients retain reputational tools, which weakens the SEC's leverage to extract over-inclusive admissions of conduct. Whistleblower-driven cases against still-active crypto fraud (Ponzi, market manipulation, fake-issuer offerings) will continue, but the public narrative around those cases is no longer the Commission's to dictate.
🔄 Contrarian Take
Regulatory Oversight's 19 May commentary warns the rescission could backfire: settling defendants who publicly deny allegations may face renewed civil suits from private plaintiffs newly able to cite the contradiction. Investor-protection advocates including Better Markets argue the change creates a "settlement asymmetry" where the SEC absorbs reputational cost while the defendant absorbs only the cheque, eroding deterrence in fraud cases. The Brief's read: the deterrence concern is real for retail-investor fraud cases but irrelevant for the SEC's crypto book, which is shrinking by design — this rescission is best understood as the procedural counterpart to the Joint Interpretation.
🏗️ Industry Implications
Per Davis Polk's 20 May Crypto Regulation Hub update, the immediate beneficiaries are firms still inside open SEC matters under the prior Commission — including residual claims against Justin Sun (Tron), and the ongoing Binance follow-on individual-officer suits. Coinbase, which has its interlocutory Second Circuit appeal of SEC v. Coinbase stayed, retains optionality to settle on commercially better terms. The Brief's read: watch for a wave of crypto-firm settlements in Q3 2026 with simultaneous "we did nothing wrong" press releases — that pattern would be unimaginable under the old rule and is now the default. Insurance carriers writing D&O coverage for crypto founders should reprice based on the new public-defence option.
📊 Portfolio Impact

🟢 Bullish COIN, GLXY, MSTR — reduced reputational tail-risk from any residual settlements; cleaner public narrative for institutional onboarding.

🔴 Bearish No direct losers, but plaintiff-side securities-class-action firms (e.g. Robbins Geller, Pomerantz) may see deal-flow shifts as SEC settlements become noisier evidentiary records.

⚪ Neutral CRCL, HOOD — already past their SEC matters; minimal incremental benefit.

High Federal Reserve Board (other20260520a.htm) PRIMARY Banking / Payment Rails
🟡 Medium-High Confidence — proposed rule out for 60-day comment; final form may shift

Federal Reserve Board Proposes New "Payment Account" Framework On 20 May 2026 — Legally Eligible Nonbank Institutions Including Crypto Firms Get Direct Access To Fedwire, FedNow, National Settlement Service With USD 1 Billion Overnight Cap, No Discount Window And No Interest On Balances; Tier 3 Master Account Decisions Paused Through 31 December 2026

"The Federal Reserve Board today requested public comment on a proposal to establish a 'payment account,' which legally eligible financial institutions could use for the specific purpose of clearing and settling their payments." — Federal Reserve Board press release, 20 May 2026 (other20260520a.htm)

The Federal Reserve Board on 20 May 2026 issued a proposal — one day after President Trump's 19 May Executive Order on "Integrating Financial Technology Innovation Into Regulatory Frameworks" — that would establish a new account class designated "payment account" available to non-federally-insured financial institutions seeking direct settlement access. The proposed account excludes intraday credit, discount window access, interest on reserves, and any deposit-taking function. Holders would only access the Fedwire Funds Service, FedNow, the National Settlement Service, and free-of-payment Fedwire Securities transfers. An overnight balance cap calibrated to each holder's payment activity tops out at USD 1 billion — a deliberate increase from the December 2025 Request for Information's USD 500 million ceiling.

This is the structural compromise the crypto-industry has been seeking since Custodia Bank's 2023 master-account litigation collapsed. By creating a Tier 3-style "skinny" account that bypasses the depository-institution charter requirement embedded in Section 19 of the Federal Reserve Act, the Board acknowledges that GENIUS Act-permitted payment stablecoin issuers, OCC-chartered crypto trust banks (Anchorage, Paxos, Protego, Bridge/Stripe, Crypto.com, Ripple, Circle, BitGo) and qualifying non-bank fintechs need clearing-rail access without the full bank-holding-company regulatory perimeter. The Board simultaneously instructed regional Reserve Banks to pause all new Tier 3 master-account decisions until policy is finalised, which means existing applicants face a procedural freeze through year-end. Comment period closes 60 days after Federal Register publication.

📎 Historical Precedent: Custodia Bank v. Federal Reserve (10th Cir. 2024) held that the Fed has discretion to deny master accounts. The "skinny" account concept first surfaced in Vice Chair Brainard's 2022 speech on payment-system access; today's proposal operationalises it for the first time.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis
Sullivan & Cromwell's 21 May client alert and Wilson Sonsini's "term sheet for industry input" analysis both flag that the proposal is narrower than crypto-industry advocates wanted on three dimensions: no FX leg, no overnight investment of balances, and an explicit BSA/AML/sanctions information-provision requirement that Reserve Banks may impose at will. Cravath's view (cited in Bloomberg coverage 20 May): the account is functionally a settlement utility, not a master account substitute — firms still need a chartered banking partner for credit, balance-sheet, or interest-bearing functions. The USD 1 billion overnight cap was the most-contested figure in industry comment letters and represents a clear concession to issuers like Circle and Ripple whose stablecoin reserves move daily volumes well above prior limits.
🔄 Contrarian Take
Duke FinReg Blog (Lev Menand, Hilary Allen, 21 May 2026) argues the OCC charter + Fed payment account stack constructs a parallel "stablecoin bank" without statutory authorisation, creating systemic risk without FDIC backstop or Federal Reserve lender-of-last-resort. The Brief's read: the academic critique correctly identifies a structural fragility but understates the Trump administration's revealed preference — the Fed is being repurposed as a settlement utility, not a backstop, and that is a deliberate policy choice the courts are unlikely to second-guess absent a statutory challenge.
🏗️ Industry Implications
Direct beneficiaries: Circle (CRCL), Ripple, Anchorage Digital, BitGo, Paxos, Bridge (Stripe), Crypto.com — every OCC-chartered crypto trust bank now has a credible path to direct Fed settlement. Coinbase Custody, which sits inside a New York DFS limited-purpose trust company, faces a harder route. Per Coinbase Institute's 21 May post, the rule indirectly favours stablecoin issuers over exchanges by giving issuers settlement primacy. Per a16z crypto's commentary on X, the missing element is permitted FX activity — until that lands, USD-pegged stablecoins remain easier to operationalise on these rails than non-USD or basket stablecoins. The Brief's read: the second-order winners are stablecoin payment processors (BVNK, Bridge, Conduit) that can now build directly atop the issuer-Fed stack without a sponsor bank.
📊 Portfolio Impact

🟢 Bullish CRCL, COIN, HOOD, MA, V — direct rail access compresses settlement spreads; payment-network incumbents benefit from cleaner crypto-rail interoperability.

🔴 Bearish Correspondent banking franchises (Cross River, Customers Bancorp's CBIT) — disintermediated by direct Fed access for the largest crypto firms.

⚪ Neutral Money-center banks (JPM, BAC) — too large to be affected near-term; revenue impact <5bps of payment-services line.

SEC Indefinitely Delays Tokenized Stocks "Innovation Exemption" After NYSE / Nasdaq / Issuer Pushback On Third-Party Token Provision; Commissioner Peirce's 21–22 May Statement Confirms Exemption Will Cover Only Digital Representations Of Real Underlying Equities, Synthetic Crypto-Stocks Explicitly Excluded — Coinbase Off 4.4% On 22 May Tape

"I appreciate the interest in — but not the hyperbole about — the contemplated innovation exemption for the onchain trading of tokenized NMS stock. Keep in mind: I've always expected that it'd be limited in scope & would facilitate trading only of digital representations of the same underlying equity securities available in secondary markets today." — SEC Commissioner Hester Peirce, public statement 21–22 May 2026

Bloomberg reported on 22 May 2026 that the SEC has shelved its planned release of a Regulation Crypto "innovation exemption" for tokenised securities, which had been expected this week (week of 18 May). The principal sticking point: a provision that would have permitted trading of "third-party tokens" — digital representations of corporate shares created without the explicit knowledge or approval of the underlying corporation. Public-company representatives, NYSE and Nasdaq listing teams, and former SEC Acting Director Amanda Fischer raised operational concerns about dividend administration, proxy voting, and shareholder-tracking systems if unauthorised tokenisations proliferate across decentralised networks.

Commissioner Hester Peirce — the most consistent advocate for tokenisation inside the Commission — moved publicly on 21–22 May to temper expectations, clarifying that the exemption was always intended to be narrow and limited to actual tokenised equities representing real shares of publicly traded companies recorded on blockchain. The framework is now indefinitely paused; no replacement timeline has been announced. The market reaction was immediate: COIN fell ~4.4% on 22 May, BTC declined ~2.75% to ~USD 75,253, ETH dropped ~3.4%.

📎 Historical Precedent: The "third-party token" debate mirrors the 2018-19 dispute over crypto ETPs where regulated wrappers were issued without underlying-issuer consent. The SEC's resolution then — only spot ETPs with consent and surveillance-sharing agreements — is the template Peirce's clarification points back to.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis
Morrison Foerster's tokenisation team (per 12 May 2026 client alert, updated 23 May) reads the delay as a course correction, not a withdrawal: the SEC is still committed to producing an exemption, but only after consultation with NYSE, Nasdaq, the Council of Institutional Investors, and the Society for Corporate Governance. The Block's 23 May coverage notes that Peirce's statement effectively rules out the "synthetic" model that platforms like Backed Finance, Securitize, and Robinhood-EU have built in offshore jurisdictions — those will not obtain the innovation exemption. The Brief's read: the delay is a Pyrrhic win for the listing exchanges; they preserve their distribution monopoly for another 6-9 months, but the directionality is clear and the structural reform will land.
🔄 Contrarian Take
Per Galaxy Research's Alex Thorn on X (22 May), the SEC delay is bullish in the medium term because a narrower, well-engineered exemption is more durable than a sweeping one that invites D.C. Circuit challenge. By contrast, Bitwise's Matt Hougan argues the delay materially impairs the bullish "everything onchain by 2027" thesis used to justify 30x revenue multiples on tokenisation infrastructure plays. The Brief's read: both views can be right — short-term re-rating downside for tokenisation pure-plays, but no permanent damage to the structural thesis if the SEC produces a workable framework in 2H 2026.
🏗️ Industry Implications
Per Morgan Stanley's 23 May digital-assets desk note, the delay disproportionately hits Coinbase (COIN), Robinhood (HOOD), and Securitize, all of which had built tokenised-equity roadmaps assuming an early-summer exemption. BlackRock (BLK) — whose 8 May SEC filings for the tokenised BSTBL Treasury fund and BRSRV stablecoin reserve vehicle remain pending — is less affected because Treasuries do not implicate the third-party-token problem. Backed Finance and Ondo Finance, which have been pre-positioning tokenised US equities in offshore markets, lose the runway they had to migrate onshore. The Brief's read: BlackRock's strategic position is enhanced because Treasuries-first tokenisation now reaches market 6-9 months before tokenised equities; the company captures first-mover advantage in the most regulated asset class.

CFTC Division Of Enforcement Issues 19 May Cooperation Advisory Codifying Self-Reporting, Cooperation And Remediation Credit Framework — Mirrors DOJ's 2023 Corporate Enforcement Policy And Operationalises Selig-Era "Knowing Violation" Enforcement Threshold For Digital Asset Cases

"The CFTC's Division of Enforcement will not recommend an enforcement action against a registrant or non-registrant that voluntarily and timely self-reports misconduct, fully cooperates, and remediates appropriately." [paraphrased — Advisory 9234-26] — CFTC Staff Advisory 9234-26 on Cooperation in Enforcement Matters, 19 May 2026

The CFTC's Division of Enforcement on 19 May 2026 issued Staff Advisory 9234-26 setting out a formal framework for crediting voluntary self-disclosure, cooperation, and remediation in enforcement matters. The advisory codifies what has been the de-facto practice since Chairman Selig's January 2026 confirmation: the CFTC's enforcement staff will not proceed with purely technical regulatory violations in the digital-assets space absent a knowing violation of law, and will substantially reduce or decline penalties for entities that come forward voluntarily.

The framework borrows heavily from DOJ's 2023 Corporate Enforcement and Voluntary Self-Disclosure Policy and the SEC's parallel cooperation framework. For crypto firms, it crystallises Chairman Selig's stated intent to align CFTC enforcement with the post-Joint-Interpretation regulatory architecture — meaning that exchanges, DCMs, swap dealers, and intermediaries handling digital-asset derivatives now have a clear cooperation pathway that did not formally exist before 19 May. Issued the same day as the CFTC's federal preemption suit against Minnesota (Story 9233-26) and one day after Chairman Selig announced DJ Hennes as Director of the Market Participants Division (9232-26), the advisory completes the structural reset of CFTC enforcement under the new Commission.

📎 Historical Precedent: DOJ's January 2023 Corporate Enforcement and Voluntary Self-Disclosure Policy and SEC's 2010 Cooperation Initiative established the template; CFTC's prior 2017 Cooperation Advisory was rescinded de facto during the Behnam era and is now formally replaced.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis
Per Cleary Gottlieb's 22 May client alert and Cooley's commentary on the related Phantom no-action letter, the cooperation advisory completes a three-piece reform: (i) the March SEC-CFTC Joint Interpretation narrows substantive enforcement scope, (ii) the Phantom letter narrows registration-trigger scope for self-custodial wallets, and (iii) the 19 May Cooperation Advisory narrows penalty exposure for the remaining enforcement universe. Davis Polk's 22 May Crypto Regulation Hub update notes the advisory's significant practical implication: pre-enforcement counsel can now run cost-benefit analyses on self-reporting that simply weren't possible before, materially changing the incentives for firms that discover internal compliance failures.
🔄 Contrarian Take
Better Markets' Dennis Kelleher (per 22 May Reuters comment) argues the framework effectively immunises sophisticated repeat-offenders who can afford the legal architecture to self-report tactically. The Brief's read: the criticism applies more to the original DOJ template than to the CFTC's narrower implementation; the digital-asset universe is small enough that staff retains discretion to refuse cooperation credit for serial offenders.
🏗️ Industry Implications
Direct beneficiaries: any CFTC-registered DCM (CME, Coinbase Derivatives, Cboe, Kalshi, Polymarket-QCX), every swap dealer with crypto exposure, and the universe of FCMs handling crypto derivatives margin. Per Sidley's Data Matters Privacy Blog (23 May), the advisory should also benefit non-registrants increasingly drawn into CFTC enforcement via the Joint Interpretation's clarified jurisdiction over digital commodities. The Brief's read: the second-order winner is the compliance-tech market — Chainalysis, Elliptic, TRM Labs all stand to gain from increased demand for self-reporting infrastructure and remediation documentation.
📊 Portfolio Impact

🟢 Bullish CME, ICE, COIN — registered DCM franchises see compressed enforcement-risk premium; insurance pricing should tighten for crypto exchange D&O.

🔴 Bearish Pure-play crypto enforcement-defence law firms — fewer enforcement actions, faster resolutions, lower fees per matter.

UK HM Treasury Cryptoasset Statutory Instrument Consultation Closed 22 May 2026 — Draft Carves Qualifying Stablecoins Out Of "Dealing" And "Arranging Deals" Regulated Activities To Eliminate Dual Crypto/Payments Authorisation Burden; Custody And Lending Activities Remain In Scope; Q2 2026 Payment Services Reform Consultation Next

"As part of the government's upcoming payment services reforms, the government intends to rationalise the dealing and payments perimeters for stablecoins." — HM Treasury Policy Note on Draft SI Amending Cryptoassets Regulations 2026 (gov.uk), April 2026

HM Treasury's consultation on the draft Statutory Instrument amending the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) Regulations 2026 closed on 22 May 2026. The headline measure: UK qualifying stablecoins (UKQS) used for payments are carved out of the regulated activities of "dealing as principal or agent in qualifying cryptoassets" and "arranging deals in qualifying cryptoassets." The carve-out eliminates the dual crypto-and-payments authorisation requirement that would otherwise apply to UK payment firms handling stablecoin transactions.

Critical exclusions: UKQS lending and borrowing remain inside the regulated perimeter, allowing the FCA to police consumer credit risk. Custody activities and arranging-the-safeguarding of UKQS remain inside scope as cryptoasset custody permissions. Under the Payments Forward Plan (HM Treasury, Bank of England, FCA, PSR), the government will consult on a broader payment-services framework in Q2 2026, with formal integration of stablecoins into UK payments law as the structural goal.

📎 Historical Precedent: The UK's 2017 Payment Services Regulations (PSRs) and the 2020 e-money perimeter both used carve-outs as transitional mechanisms; HMT's approach here borrows directly from that template.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis
Per Herbert Smith Freehills Kramer's 20 May client alert and Linklaters' financial-regulation post (22 May), the carve-out is materially friendlier to UK payment-firm growth than equivalent EU MiCA provisions, which require full CASP authorisation for any payments use of stablecoins. Freshfields' Technology Quotient blog (22 May) emphasises that the UK is deliberately positioning to attract stablecoin-issuance and stablecoin-payments business that finds MiCA too prescriptive. The Brief's read: the UK is racing to land its full framework before the EU MiCA 1 July transitional deadline closes; if successful, expect material capacity migration from Paris/Frankfurt/Dublin to London in 2H 2026.
🏗️ Industry Implications
Direct beneficiaries: any UK-headquartered payment firm with stablecoin ambitions (Revolut, Wise, GoCardless, Volt, BVNK) plus stablecoin issuers considering UK authorisation (Circle, Ripple's RLUSD).
