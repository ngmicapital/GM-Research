Section 01
Longevity & Performance Science

Cardiorespiratory fitness is the most powerful single lever in longevity medicine — and the most consistently underused. A 24-year follow-up of 4,137 apparently healthy U.S. adults (Imboden et al., published ACC 2025) quantified the mortality dose-response with granular precision: each 1-MET increase in VO2max reduces all-cause mortality by 11.6%, cardiovascular mortality by 16.1%, and cancer mortality by 14.0%. The most striking finding is at the lower end of the curve: simply moving from the bottom fitness quartile to the next one is associated with a 50% reduction in all-cause mortality — a return that dwarfs most pharmaceutical interventions. The highest-fit cohort averaged VO2max of 49.8 ± 8.8 ml/kg/min in men and 35.9 ± 8.9 ml/kg/min in women. These are the targets to track.

VO2max as longevity medicine: 50% mortality reduction from one fitness quartile jump
Observational/Cohort
Imboden et al. · ACC · 2025

n=4,137, mean follow-up 24.2 years. Every MET increment cuts CVD mortality 16.1%. Top-quartile men averaged VO2max 49.8 ml/kg/min; women 35.9 ml/kg/min. The dose-response does not plateau in the studied range — more fitness, less death. Protocol implication: 2–3 true VO2max-building sessions per week (intervals at 90–95% max HR for 4–6 × 4 min), not just zone 2 base.

Sauna 3–4×/week at 80°C+: 40% lower all-cause mortality in Finnish 20-year cohort
Observational/Cohort
Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine · 2025

The Finnish sauna cohort remains the landmark dose-response dataset for heat stress. Cardiovascular mechanism: sauna induces heat shock proteins (HSPs) that protect endothelial cells, improves nitric oxide bioavailability, reduces arterial stiffness, and lowers resting blood pressure. The physiological load mirrors moderate aerobic exercise — heart rate rises to 100–150 bpm, cardiac output increases, and core temperature reaches 38.5–39°C. Evidence supports 3–4 sessions/week, 15–20 min each, at 80–100°C as the protocol bracket where mortality benefit is clearest. Avoid immediately post-resistance training (attenuates mTOR-driven protein synthesis); morning or evening, 3+ hours post-lifting.

⚡ Implication: VO2max testing (treadmill or cycle ergometer protocol) should be a routine annual biomarker — more predictive than a standard lipid panel. If you're not doing at least two high-intensity aerobic sessions per week, you're leaving the largest mortality lever unpulled. Target: men above 45 ml/kg/min by age 40, above 40 by age 50. Women: above 35 ml/kg/min by age 40. Add a sauna protocol around it — the heat stress compounds the cardiovascular adaptation.
Section 02
Training & Performance

The most actionable training insight of the last 18 months is deceptively simple: muscle growth is maximised when you apply load in the lengthened position of the muscle — not through full range of motion, and not in the shortened position. Eccentric contractions at long muscle lengths generate greater mechanical tension per motor unit recruited, produce more sarcomere addition (longitudinal hypertrophy), and cause greater muscle damage signalling at lower metabolic cost than concentric-dominant work. A 2025 meta-analysis across 43 RCTs confirmed eccentric training elicits greater hypertrophy than concentric-matched protocols, with the greatest effect sizes at slow (2–4 s) eccentric tempo in the stretched position. The practical re-design: replace cable flies with incline dumbbell flyes (chest), replace standing curls with incline curls (biceps), swap leg press with deficit split squats (quads/glutes), and use Romanian deadlifts rather than stiff-leg.

Lengthened-position eccentric training: more hypertrophy, less metabolic cost
Meta-analysis
PMC · 2025 · Eccentric training meta-analysis

43 RCTs reviewed. Eccentric training produced significantly greater hypertrophy than concentric-matched loads (SMD ~0.4–0.6 depending on population). Effect concentrated in: slow eccentric tempo (≥3 s), exercises with load at full stretch (incline, deficit, lengthened ROM), and higher intensities (≥70% 1RM eccentric). Older adults showed the largest relative benefit — eccentric overload preserves muscle mass with lower cardiovascular demand, making it an especially high-value protocol for those 50+. For a PPL split: every push, pull, and leg session should have at least one lengthened-position emphasis movement.

Integrate Now
  • Incline dumbbell curl — peak bicep tension at full stretch
  • Deficit Bulgarian split squat — glute/quad load at maximum hip flexion
  • Romanian deadlift — hamstring under tension throughout
  • Incline dumbbell fly — pec stretch with external load
  • 3–4 s eccentric tempo on every rep of these movements
Caveats
  • DOMS is significantly higher for 1–2 weeks when switching to lengthened emphasis — reduce volume by ~30% initially
  • Evidence base is clear for hypertrophy; strength and power outcomes are more mixed
  • CNS fatigue may accumulate — not a protocol for every exercise in every session
  • Joint health consideration: full stretch under load requires good mobility baseline
Section 03
Supplements & Nutrition

Two supplement/nutrition stories dominate this cycle: the omega-3 dose-response finding that flips sign at high doses (important nuance largely absent from mainstream coverage), and berberine's now-mechanistically-characterised GLP-1 stimulation via bitter taste receptors and gut microbiota — an accessible and cheap metabolic tool with a meaningful but overstated clinical evidence base.

Omega-3 EPA/DHA: the 750 mg ceiling — cardioprotective below, arrhythmogenic above
Narrative Review
Djuricic & Calder · Current Atherosclerosis Reports · 2025

Mechanistic picture: EPA and DHA reduce TG via enhanced hepatic fatty acid oxidation; serve as precursors for resolvins and protectins (inflammation resolvers, not just suppressors); modulate the gut microbiome Firmicutes:Bacteroidetes ratio; and improve endothelial NO production. The dose-response cliff: up to ~750 mg/day EPA+DHA, cohort data and RCTs show 10–18% reduced mortality risk, 18% lower coronary heart disease events, 17% reduced stroke (EPA), and 11–28% MI reduction in meta-analyses. Above 1.8–4 g/day: atrial fibrillation risk rises ~50% (REDUCE-IT, RESPECT-EPA). The REDUCE-IT/STRENGTH divergence (same dose range, opposite results) likely reflects a mineral oil placebo artefact in REDUCE-IT and formulation differences. Practical guidance: achieve 500–750 mg/day from oily fish (2–3 portions/week of mackerel, sardines, salmon) or a supplement in that range. High-dose fish oil for cardiovascular disease prevention is not supported unless in a cardiology-supervised setting monitoring AF risk.

Berberine: nature's GLP-1 secretagogue — real mechanism, overstated clinical data
Mechanistic/Preclinical
Liu et al. · Pharmaceuticals · 2025

Mechanism now well-characterised: berberine activates bitter taste receptor TAS2R38 in intestinal enteroendocrine cells → PLC signalling → GLP-1 secretion from L-cells. Concurrently, AMPK activation suppresses hepatic gluconeogenesis, promotes gut SCFA-producing bacteria (GPR41/43 → further GLP-1 induction), and reduces TNF-α/IL-6/IL-1β. Clinical signal: one double-blind RCT showed HbA1c reduction from 9.5% to 7.5% with berberine monotherapy in T2DM. However: most trials n<100, duration ≤12 weeks, and oral bioavailability is <1% — meaning effective tissue concentrations are hard to confirm. Evidence verdict: promising metabolic tool, not a pharmaceutical-grade GLP-1 agonist replacement. Protocol: 500 mg twice daily with meals; titrate up from 300 mg to reduce GI effects. Most useful in the pre-diabetic or metabolic syndrome context; unlikely to replicate the 15–20% body weight reduction of semaglutide.

ApoB: the 2026 ACC/AHA's preferred cardiovascular target — updated thresholds
Clinical Guideline
HCPLive · 2026 ACC/AHA Dyslipidemia Guidelines

The 2026 ACC/AHA Dyslipidemia Guideline elevates ApoB to a primary treatment target alongside LDL-C, reflecting its advantages: directly measured (not calculated like LDL), fasting-independent, accurate when TGs are elevated or in metabolically complex patients. ApoB counts every atherogenic particle — VLDL, IDL, LDL, and Lp(a) — making it a truer index of cardiovascular exposure. Longevity-medicine targets: <80 mg/dL for low-risk adults; <70 mg/dL with risk factors (family history, hypertension, smoking, metabolic syndrome); <60 mg/dL with existing CVD or diabetes. Standard lab ranges (usually <90–100 mg/dL) are population-normal, not longevity-optimal. Request ApoB on your next standard panel — most labs run it; it is rarely ordered by default.

Watchlist
1
Action
Book a VO2max test — set 12-month target
The evidence is unambiguous: VO2max is the most predictive single biomarker for longevity. Without a baseline number you cannot track the largest lever. Protocol: treadmill or cycle ergometer incremental test at a sports medicine lab or exercise physiology clinic. Target for men 35–45: reach >45 ml/kg/min within 12 months via 2× Norwegian 4×4 protocol/week. Signal to watch: resting HR trend, HRV trend, Garmin/Apple Watch VO2max estimate as a proxy between tests.
2
Action
Order ApoB on next blood panel
If your standard lipid panel doesn't include ApoB, request it explicitly — it costs ~$15–30 as an add-on and changes the clinical picture materially. Target: <80 mg/dL. If >90 mg/dL despite optimised diet and exercise, the 2026 guidelines now support earlier statin initiation using ApoB as the primary trigger. Review panel: also add Lp(a) once — it doesn't change with lifestyle and sets lifetime cardiovascular risk baseline.
3
New
Restructure one PPL session around lengthened-position emphasis
Pick one session this week and audit every exercise: does the heaviest load fall at the shortest or longest muscle length? Replace any shortened-position dominant movement with a lengthened equivalent. Start conservative — 30% volume reduction for the first 2 weeks to manage DOMS. Watch: are weights and pumps comparable or better? If yes, the protocol is working. Timeline: 6 weeks to assess hypertrophy signal via measurement or DEXA.
4
Carried
Sauna protocol: lock in 3× weekly at 80°C+
The dose-response data is clear at 3–4 sessions/week, 15–20 min. If access is available (gym sauna, private), this is the lowest-effort intervention with the strongest cardiovascular longevity signal outside of formal exercise. Timing constraint: avoid immediately post-resistance training — delay 3+ hours to avoid HSP-mediated attenuation of mTOR protein synthesis signalling. Signal: resting HR and blood pressure trend downward within 6–8 weeks of consistent use.
5
New
Trial berberine 500 mg ×2 daily (8-week window)
If metabolic markers (fasting glucose, HbA1c, lipids) are suboptimal, berberine is worth an 8-week trial. Mechanism is now well-characterised (TAS2R38 → GLP-1; AMPK activation). Start at 300 mg/day for week 1 to manage GI effects, titrate to 500 mg twice daily with meals. Track: fasting glucose, post-meal glucose if CGM available, HbA1c before/after. Do not combine with pharmaceutical GLP-1 agonists without medical supervision. Low bioavailability is a real limitation — some evidence phytosome formulations improve absorption.
