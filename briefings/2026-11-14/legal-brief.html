Update CLARITY Act Stalls Past the Midterms With No Senate Vote Scheduled

[paraphrased] Senator Cynthia Lummis, the Senate's chief Republican architect of the bill, treated the 15 September cloture defeat as fatal to the bill. Sen. Cynthia Lummis, as reported by FinTech Weekly, Sep 2026 [paraphrased]

The July 4 target for the bill has passed without a Senate vote. The 15 September cloture vote failed 49 to 50, eleven votes short of the 60 needed to open debate. Democrats won both chambers on 3 November, and no new vote has been set, which leaves the lame-duck session before the new Congress takes control on 3 January 2027 as the only realistic window.

Reporting treats the bill as stalled rather than dead. Senator Thom Tillis switched his vote to no so that he could file a motion to reconsider, which keeps a Senate procedural path open. The sticking points remain stablecoin rewards and ethics rules on officials' crypto income, not the CFTC and SEC jurisdictional split.

📎 Historical Precedent: The GENIUS Act cleared the Senate 68 to 30 and the House 308 to 122 before its July 2025 signing, showing that stablecoin legislation moved on bipartisan votes where market structure did not.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

AMINA Bank's September analysis argues that the binding constraint is now the GENIUS Act rather than CLARITY, because agency rules stand without the market-structure statute, although a future Commission could withdraw them. Issuer and custody programmes should be built around the GENIUS dates of 18 January 2027 and the 18 July 2028 unlicensed-stablecoin gate. The Brief's read: a lame-duck vote is possible but needs 60 votes, so treat CLARITY as an option rather than a planning assumption for 2027.

🔄 Contrarian Take

Cryptoticker's status update says the CLARITY Act formally lives on, and Tillis's motion to reconsider is the mechanism that keeps it there. The Brief's read: the procedural path exists on paper, but the Senate calendar shows no agreement for another vote, and the incoming majority changes the bargaining position in January, so revival before 3 January is a low-probability outcome.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Community banks objected to stablecoin rewards during the Senate fight, and the Senate text would bar passive interest on payment stablecoin balances while allowing activity-based rewards. Exchanges that pay rewards, including Coinbase, sit on the permitted side of that line, while issuers such as Circle carry the most direct exposure to a passive-yield ban. The Brief's read: banks gain from any delay, and reward-driven distribution models carry the most legislative risk.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟢 Bullish COIN — the Senate text's activity-based reward carve-out preserves the core economics of exchange reward programmes.

🔴 Bearish CRCL — a passive-yield ban would squeeze distribution economics for payment stablecoins, even though GENIUS already restricts issuer interest.

⚪ Neutral BK — custody and settlement demand is largely unchanged whichever path the bill takes.

TraderPrice any CLARITY-dependent crypto equity or token position as a lame-duck option, not a base-case catalyst.
CounselMap reward-program terms against the Senate text's activity-based carve-out and flag any passive-interest feature.
ComplianceConfirm that stablecoin reward and yield features comply with the GENIUS Act's interest prohibition today.
FounderPlan product roadmaps around GENIUS rules and avoid designs that depend on a market-structure statute passing in 2026.

Coinbase Trust Charter Clears Final Approval as Senate Demands Records

[paraphrased] Coinbase says its approved national trust company gives its custody business a single federal regulatory framework. Coinbase statement, as reported by Chaincatcher, 14 Nov 2026 [paraphrased]

Coinbase's national trust company received final approval to open on 9 November, after the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency granted preliminary conditional approval in April. Congressional investigators now want the records behind the trust charter decisions, including how regulators justified them, how crypto firms handled compliance, and what dealings those firms had with people tied to the Trump administration.

The approvals sit alongside CFTC Letter 26-30, which was reported on 13 November and lets any derivatives clearing organisation use a state-regulated trust company as if it were a settlement bank for daily settlements. Taken together, the two moves show federal agencies building a route for crypto custody into clearing and settlement, while Congress examines who is getting through that route.

📎 Historical Precedent: The CFTC previously withdrew a staff advisory on accepting virtual currencies into customer segregation once stablecoins and tokenized collateral had developed, the last time the agency reset its digital-asset custody guidance.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Trust charters now carry a records and conflicts overlay as well as a licensing one. Firms pursuing OCC or state trust status should prepare documentation of their compliance programmes and of any dealings with government-linked persons before a congressional request arrives. The CFTC letter treats a trust company as a settlement bank for DCO purposes, which matters for clearing members. The Brief's read: the no-action letter is the more durable signal, because it reaches any DCO rather than one named firm.

🔄 Contrarian Take

Troutman Pepper Locke's November newsletter reports that state regulators and attorneys general asked the SEC to withdraw Regulation Crypto Assets, and that four state-regulator associations asked Treasury to protect state-approved stablecoin issuers. Their concern is that federal charters and federal rules displace state supervision. The Brief's read: the CFTC relief depends on state trust companies, so state-federal friction is a risk to the pathway, not a reason to avoid it.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Coinbase gains a federal custody framework that can compete with bank-only custodians, and state-chartered trust companies gain a possible settlement role at clearing houses. Clearing members that rely on bank settlement accounts may face new counterparty choices. The Brief's read: federally chartered custodians gain share relative to bank-only incumbents, and the congressional records request is a cost that falls on the firms, not the regulators.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟢 Bullish COIN — federal trust status deepens the custody business that just received final approval.

🔴 Bearish BK — bank custody incumbents lose share if trust companies win settlement roles at clearing houses.

⚪ Neutral CME — clearing volume is unaffected, but the set of firms that can serve as settlement counterparties is widening.

OKX and ICE Set 23 November Launch for Tokenized Securities Venue

[paraphrased] OKX and ICE say they have confirmed 70 assets from issuers, including Coinbase, Circle and Robinhood, together with 59 exchange-traded funds. OKX and ICE announcement, as reported by The Block, 11 Nov 2026 [paraphrased]

OKX and ICE plan to launch their tokenized securities venue on 23 November, operating under the SEC's five-year innovation exemption that debuted in September. The joint venture, which is a partnership between OKX and the parent of the NYSE, has confirmed 70 assets from issuers, along with 59 exchange-traded funds.

The opt-out record is the more telling detail. In a 5 November filing, 67 companies had opted out of having tokenized versions of their stock traded on the venue, including Meta Platforms. The exemption lets the venue operate without registering as a traditional exchange, but issuer consent is becoming the practical limit on what gets listed.

📎 Historical Precedent: Securitize tokenized its own NYSE-listed stock on day one in July, an earlier test of how far existing securities law reaches into on-chain trading.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

The innovation exemption is doing work that the stalled CLARITY Act would otherwise have done, so tokenized equity trading now runs on SEC relief that a future Commission can narrow. Issuer counsel should record whether each tokenized listing was opted into, opted out of, or left unaddressed. The Brief's read: the opt-outs matter more than the launch date, because they show that issuer consent, not venue registration, decides which equities go on-chain.

🔄 Contrarian Take

The Block reports that dozens of issuers have objected to tokenized versions of their stock trading on the venue. The counter-argument from the venue side is that 70 assets are already confirmed, so objectors are a minority. The Brief's read: the two counts are not comparable, since 70 counts assets and 67 counts companies, so the opt-out filing is the better guide to issuer sentiment.

🏗️ Industry Implications

ICE, the NYSE parent, gains a listed-securities channel on-chain, while OKX extends its tokenized footprint. Brokerage and custody incumbents face competition for tokenized equity order flow as issuers choose venues. The Brief's read: venues that can onboard issuers quickly win, and transfer-agent-dependent models lose share.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟢 Bullish ICE — the venue adds a tokenized listing channel tied to the NYSE parent.

🔴 Bearish BK — custody and settlement fees face pressure if tokenized equity settles on-chain.

⚪ Neutral HOOD — Robinhood appears among confirmed issuer assets, with no direct revenue effect disclosed in the announcement.

📋 What To Do — Before 3 January 2027
TraderCheck whether any tokenized equity you hold appears on the confirmed list before the 23 November launch.
CounselConfirm whether each issuer client opted out and document the decision in writing.
ComplianceAdd venue-listing status and opt-out status to tokenized asset due diligence.
FounderDecide whether to pursue issuer status on the venue or wait until the exemption's scope is tested.

Blumenthal Letters Seek Investigative Records From Binance, Tether and World Liberty

[paraphrased] Senator Richard Blumenthal asked Binance, Tether, World Liberty Financial and others to produce investigative records by 4 January 2027. Sen. Richard Blumenthal, as reported in KuCoin Daily Market Report, 9 Nov 2026 [paraphrased; Unconfirmed, check the Senate press release]

Senator Richard Blumenthal has written to Binance, Tether, World Liberty Financial and other entities requesting investigative records by 4 January 2027. The inquiries concern illicit use of crypto assets and crypto businesses linked to Trump family interests. [Unconfirmed — sources conflict on the letter list; verify against the Senate release before citing.]

The letters follow the FinCEN order against MEXC, so congressional oversight now targets both illicit finance and political conflicts. A letter is not a subpoena, which makes compliance voluntary today, but the documents produced could become public in hearings once the new majority takes control.

📎 Historical Precedent: The CLARITY Act stall shows Congress can scrutinise crypto firms without passing a statute, which is the same pattern as the Senate trust-charter inquiry covered above.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Recipients should preserve documents now and map what a January request would cover, especially Trump-linked dealings and illicit-finance controls. The Brief's read: a voluntary request this early is often the precursor to subpoena-backed demands, and the timing points to the new majority in January rather than the current Congress.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Binance, Tether and World Liberty Financial each take on reputational and compliance cost, and exchanges that rely on US banking relationships may see heavier due diligence from correspondent banks. The Brief's read: the issuers with the most political exposure face the greatest cost of delay.

ESMA Tells MiCA Platforms to Drop Non-Compliant Stablecoins Within Three Months

[paraphrased] ESMA considers that MiCA-authorised crypto-asset service providers should stop serving non-MiCA-compliant stablecoins to EU clients across all MiCA services. ESMA Opinion, 8 Oct 2026, as summarised by Sidley, Nov 2026 [paraphrased]

On 8 October 2026 ESMA issued an opinion telling national supervisors that MiCA-licensed firms should stop offering services tied to stablecoins that do not comply with MiCA. The opinion covers exchange, order execution, placing, portfolio management and advice, and asks firms to clear residual exposures within three months.

Counting from 8 October, the three-month window lands around 8 January 2027, which is our calculation. That falls before the GENIUS Act effective date of 18 January 2027, so the EU and US stablecoin compliance clocks now overlap in the same quarter.

📎 Historical Precedent: ESMA's January 2025 public statement on stablecoins was an earlier warning to the same market, but the 2026 opinion sets a hard clearance timetable.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Any EU desk that uses non-MiCA stablecoins for pairs or settlement needs a migration plan before early January. The Brief's read: the opinion is supervisory rather than a regulation, but national competent authorities are likely to treat it as the expected standard when they examine licensed firms.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Stablecoin liquidity on EU venues is likely to shift toward MiCA-compliant tokens, and issuers without MiCA authorisation lose EU distribution. The Brief's read: MiCA-authorised issuers and their licensed venues gain share quickly once a hard deadline is set.
