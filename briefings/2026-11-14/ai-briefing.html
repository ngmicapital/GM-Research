Google put Gemini 4 Hydrogen into production on 13 November with flat long-context pricing and automatic switching between Hydrogen and Carbon inside Antigravity, which makes it the day's biggest model move. Anthropic's IPO is the follow-on story: the roadshow began on 10 November with a $70 to $78 price range, pricing is pencilled in for 18 November, and Nasdaq trading under ANT for 19 November. Act on two things: first, test Gemini 4 Hydrogen against your current coding-agent spend, because the flat $1.50 input and $7.50 output rate across the full 1M-token window changes the cost maths for long-context work; second, put the 18 and 19 November Anthropic IPO dates on your calendar and read the final price when it lands, since it will set the reference point for every AI-lab valuation debate that follows. Model Landscape

Google's Gemini 4 Hydrogen is the day's headline model: cheaper long-context access, plus a router that flips between Hydrogen and Carbon inside a single session.

Gemini 4 Hydrogen goes live across Google's surfaces

Google released Hydrogen on 13 November across AI Studio, the Gemini API, Gemini for business, the Gemini app and Search. The pricing is the number to notice: $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens, applied flat across the full 1 million-token context window rather than stepping up past a threshold. For teams that have been trimming prompts to avoid long-context surcharges, that removes a design constraint. Access to the Antigravity coding environment requires a paid Google AI Pro or Ultra plan.

Routing is the real product idea. Inside Antigravity, an orchestrator swaps between Carbon and Hydrogen mid-session, so a cheaper model handles routine steps and the heavier one is reserved for hard ones. Google reports near-Carbon task success on Terminal-Bench 4.0 with 24% less quota consumed and completion 19% faster. Those are Google's own figures, and quota consumption does not translate directly into dollar savings, so treat the economics as a hypothesis to test on your own workload.

Thinking Machines puts Inkling 2 Small under Apache 2.0

Thinking Machines made the weights for Inkling 2 Small downloadable on Hugging Face under Apache 2.0, so developers can modify and self-host it. The largest checkpoints still need serious GPU memory, which keeps the practical audience narrower than the licence suggests.

IBM previews small Granite 4.1 models

IBM released experimental 3B and 8B previews of Granite 4.1. The notable design choice is explicit structure in the prompt: reserved delimiters separate instructions, data and requirements, and an execution block binds each instruction to its inputs. It is a preview, so treat it as a pattern to borrow for prompt scaffolding rather than a production dependency.

Claude Ecosystem

Anthropic's IPO has moved from rumour to a dated calendar, and its disclosed financials are now the main thing to read.

⚡ Update: IPO price band set, pricing on 18 November

This is a follow-up to Thursday's roadshow opening. The roadshow launched on 10 November with an expected price of $70 to $78 per share for 825 million Class A shares, of which 801,351,354 are being sold by Anthropic and the balance by existing stockholders. Pricing is expected on 18 November and trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under ANT on 19 November. Anthropic will not receive proceeds from the shares sold by stockholders. The final price is not yet fixed in the preliminary terms, so the range is the only public anchor.

What is new today. Seven of Anthropic's major suppliers are reported to be buying $26 billion of shares through a private placement tied to the IPO. That is a supply-chain commitment that also doubles as a vote of confidence, and it means the offering size could be larger than the base 825 million shares suggests.

Financials in the prospectus

The company reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of about $16.96 billion, roughly twelve times the year-earlier level. Reporting from SiliconANGLE put the quarter at about $17 billion of revenue alongside a $536 million operating loss, which is the number a roadshow audience will test against the 35% long-term adjusted operating margin target. Goldman Sachs, one of the lead underwriters, is reported to estimate about $52 billion of revenue for the full year. Treat these as reported figures, not audited results.

Claude product moves this week

Anthropic put Claude Sheets and Whiteboards into beta. Separately, Claude Data switches on by default for Enterprise customers on 19 November, the same day the stock is expected to begin trading. Enterprise admins should decide before then whether they want that default.

Decentralized AI

Decentralized AI is shipping plumbing rather than demand headlines this week: payment credits, a chain migration and burn-based tokenomics dominate.

Bittensor opens subnet payments and adds post-quantum signatures

The Openτensor Foundation introduced Gamma Tokens on 11 November, which are wallet-based credits for paying subnet services. Each subnet has to opt in, so adoption depends on subnet operators rather than users. On 6 November the foundation also added three post-quantum signing options covering transfers, staking and validator actions, but those protections only take effect after a mainnet upgrade. TAO traded around $206.62 on 12 November, down about 5.7% over 24 hours in a CoinMarketCap snapshot; treat that as a secondary data point, not the story.

Akash votes to move AKT to Solana

On 2 November, Akash stakers approved Proposal 346, which moves AKT to Solana as a one-to-one SPL token. The marketplace itself has not moved yet, so the vote is a commitment rather than a completed migration. The compute marketplace is still the part that matters for demand, and that is the piece to watch.

io.net and Render: burns as the design pattern

A governance research report published on 10 November says Akash, io.net and Render have all converged on burn-linked token designs in 2026. io.net has committed to burning up to 12 million IO over its first year of the Incentive Dynamic Engine, and it is described as burning more than half of post-payout revenue. The same report argues that foundations still control emissions and treasury parameters, which is the governance critique to keep in mind. Render's foundation dashboard shows more than 82 million cumulative frames and about 5,600 nodes, and a third-party estimate puts AI workloads at 35% to 40% of activity. Grayscale's Decentralized AI Fund cut its RENDER weighting to 15.60% as of 22 October, from 21.59% in August.

Open-Source Pulse

The open-weight leaderboard is still led by Qwen, with DeepSeek and Moonshot close behind and a notable Apache 2.0 entrant from Ricursive.

Qwen3.8-27B keeps the download crown

Daily trending digests from 11 November still rank Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B first, with roughly 19,600 likes and about 7.2 million downloads. Those are auto-generated community digests, so use them as a directional read on attention rather than an audited ranking.

Video models lead by downloads

Lightricks' LTX-2.5 was reported as the most-downloaded model in the 9 November digest, with about 2.66 million downloads and roughly 11,000 likes. Its single-file diffusion design is the reason developers cite for picking it over multi-checkpoint setups.

Kimi K3.1 Pro and Ripple-65B-Preview

Moonshot's Kimi K3.1 Pro appeared on the 11 November trending list, which is a sign that the Kimi line is still gathering developer attention after K3. Ricursive Intelligence's Ripple-65B-Preview, released under Apache 2.0, sits at around 490 likes. It is a preview, and the download numbers are modest, so it is worth bookmarking rather than benchmarking yet.

Watch the uncensored fine-tune wave. Several of the trending entries are abliterated or "heretic" variants of DeepSeek and Qwen models, and some have large download counts. These are a community-driven trend with real usage, and they raise governance questions for anyone redistributing or deploying them, but the safety-filter stripping is the community's doing rather than the base labs'.

Deals & Regulation

The deal flow is dominated by Anthropic's IPO mechanics, a supplier-backed private placement and an OpenAI listing that is starting to attract strategic investors.

⚡ Update: Strategic investors line up around the IPOs

Reuters reported that TSMC plans to invest $500 million in OpenAI Group PBC's IPO. Alongside Anthropic's $26 billion supplier placement, the pattern is that the infrastructure providers the labs depend on are buying into the listings. That is a commercial relationship as much as an investment, and it ties the valuation of each lab to its own supply chain.

Single-source report, unconfirmed. One daily AI roundup reported that Jensen Huang has resigned as Nvidia's chief executive. I could not verify this against a primary Nvidia filing or a second independent outlet, so treat it as an unconfirmed claim until Nvidia or a major financial wire confirms it.

Schwab moves from read-only AI to agentic tools

Charles Schwab is rolling out its own Model Context Protocol server, and reporting describes it as a move from read-only AI to agentic AI that can carry out tasks and trades. The MCP server is the sanctioned route for clients who want to use their own model, while Schwab's in-house agent remains the default front door. The launch leaves out registered investment advisers for now, and reporting suggests it weakens Anthropic's position as Schwab's exclusive LLM provider.

Workflow & Tools

Agent tooling this week is about standard connectors and governed access: MCP servers, MCP Apps and SDK releases.

Schwab and Truman extend MCP into regulated and physical settings

Schwab's MCP server, covered above for its commercial angle, is the highest-profile enterprise MCP launch of the week. Secure Passage also announced MCP server capabilities for Truman, its identity-driven physical-security platform, aimed at exposing multi-vendor access-control systems through one interface. Both launches point the same way: MCP is becoming the default connector between agents and systems that were never designed for AI access.

MCP Apps rolling out across plans

The MCP Apps extension, described as the open extension to the Model Context Protocol, is rolling out on all plans in the latest agent-tools roundup from 11 November. For builders, that means UI-bearing MCP servers can reach users on lower tiers, not only on enterprise deployments.

Microsoft Agent Framework keeps shipping

The Microsoft Agent Framework repository logged release 1.28.0 on 6 November, with the Claude agent package updated to require Claude Agent SDK 0.2.140 or later for custom-tool servers. A pre-release of the Anthropic connector followed on 7 November. If you run Microsoft and Claude side by side, pin the SDK floor before the next build.

Practical step. Audit any agent that touches money or accounts for the difference between read-only and agentic tool access, since the Schwab launch shows where mainstream firms are heading.

Research Radar

Two papers this week are worth tracking: one on when agents should write to memory, one on binding agent behaviour to declared intent.

When Should Agents Remember? Falsification-Gated Self-Evolution

This paper, listed on 10 November, proposes that agents should store a lesson in memory only after it has been checked as reusable and safe. The motivation is that memory can make agents worse at tasks they would otherwise solve. It is a useful counterweight to the default assumption that more memory is always better, and it is directly relevant to anyone building long-running coding or research agents.

Intent-Aware Protection for AI Agents

Submitted on 12 November, this arXiv paper argues that current defences mostly detect prompt injection or harmful output, and miss actions that stay inside granted permissions but break the agent's assigned purpose. The authors propose treating trusted intent as a first-class security object. Their abstract reports a seven-point gain in protection success over DataFilter at comparable benign utility. That is a preprint result, so verify it against the full paper before relying on it.
