Section 01

Longevity & Performance Science

Position: The most important longevity finding this cycle is not a new drug — it's a sleep microstructure marker you already have data on if you wear an Oura. Slow-wave sleep spindle coupling quality predicts amyloid-beta burden in older adults, independent of total sleep time. Separately, the senolytic field is maturing: the first longitudinal DQF (Dasatinib + Quercetin + Fisetin) human trial is reporting, and the epigenetic age signal is directionally positive even if not yet conclusive. These are not the same story. One is immediately actionable; the other warrants watchlisting, not early adoption.
RCT ⚡ Deep Sleep Spindle Coupling Linked to Amyloid-Beta — Scientific Reports April 2026

A pooled analysis from three clinical trials (47 older adults, mean age 70.5) published in Scientific Reports (April 2026) found that slow-wave–spindle coupling quality is selectively linked to plasma amyloid-beta levels — a key Alzheimer's biomarker. Critically: younger-brain individuals show spindles following the slow-wave peak (correct hierarchy). In aging brains, spindles shift to precede the slow-wave peak — a breakdown in hierarchical coupling associated with higher amyloid-beta burden, medial frontal cortex atrophy, and greater longitudinal memory decline.

The implication is sharp: total sleep duration is not the primary neurological protection variable. Spindle-slow-wave coupling quality determines whether the glymphatic system is clearing amyloid efficiently during sleep. You can log 8 hours and still accumulate neurological risk if SWS quality is poor. The Oura ring's "deep sleep" and HRV overnight metrics are imperfect proxies — but they're the best accessible signal for tracking whether your SWS architecture is functioning.

What improves spindle-SWS coupling: Consistent sleep timing (circadian alignment), avoiding alcohol before bed (alcohol selectively fragments SWS), vigorous exercise ≥6 hours before sleep, magnesium glycinate, and reducing late-day light exposure. Acoustic stimulation devices (closed-loop neurofeedback) are the emerging clinical intervention — early Alzheimer's pilot data suggests feasibility.
Observational Senolytics: DQF Longitudinal Study — Dasatinib + Quercetin + Fisetin on Epigenetic Age

The Aging journal's longitudinal DQF study followed 19 participants through a 6-month Dasatinib + Quercetin (DQ) protocol, then added Fisetin for a second cohort phase. Finding: adding Fisetin produced non-significant increases in epigenetic age acceleration — interpreted as a potential mitigating effect on DQ's epigenetic impact. Fisetin alone showed strong senolytic activity in animal models (the most potent flavonoid of 10 tested), with mice showing reduced senescence markers across multiple tissues and extended healthspan.

The honest read: animal data on Fisetin is compelling; human data is in early-stage, n=19, with epigenetic clocks as proxy endpoints. Dasatinib is a prescription cancer drug — the off-label use as a senolytic carries risk. Quercetin (1,000mg) and Fisetin (1,000–1,500mg, pulsed 2 consecutive days/month) are the low-risk OTC components. The clinical trial NCT07025226 is enrolling for a sequential senolytic treatment study — this is the one to watch.

Stack note: Fisetin + Quercetin pulsed monthly (not daily) is the low-risk biohacking entry point. Daily dosing misses the senolytic mechanism — senolytics work by clearing zombie cells in brief high-dose pulses, not via chronic supplementation.
⚡ Implication: Check your Oura "Deep Sleep" minutes and HRV trend over the last 30 nights — if trending down, address sleep timing and alcohol habits before adding any supplement intervention. SWS quality is the highest-leverage neuroprotection variable that doesn't require a prescription.
Section 02

Training & Performance

Position: VO2max is the longevity biomarker that most people in a serious PPL program have never actually measured. The data is unambiguous: each MET increment in cardiorespiratory fitness reduces all-cause mortality by 11.6%, CVD mortality by 16.1%, and cancer mortality by 14.0%. Moving from "low normal" to "above upper limit of normal" CRF adds 4.9 years of mean life expectancy. These aren't marginal improvements — they're larger than most pharmaceutical interventions. If you lift 4–5x per week and haven't tested VO2max in the last 12 months, you're flying blind on the most important biomarker.
Meta Observational ⚡ VO2max: The Most Powerful Longevity Biomarker — And Most People Haven't Measured It

The evidence base here is four decades deep. A 46-year follow-up study in JACC (2018) established that midlife CRF benefits extend well into later life, with no plateau at high fitness levels. A 2025 British Journal of Sports Medicine study confirmed VO2max outperforms BMI as a cardiovascular mortality predictor. The American College of Cardiology (2025) challenged the long-held U-shaped hypothesis — new analysis suggests elite-volume training is not harmful for mortality, and the highest-fit individuals live longest without ceiling.

The quantification: each 1 ml/(kg·min) increase in VO2max = 45 additional days of longevity. Low normal CRF = 2.1 years extra life expectancy vs. below-normal. High normal = 2.9 years. Above upper limit of normal = 4.9 years. Individuals with the lowest VO2max had a 4× higher mortality risk than those with the highest. Smartwatch VO2max estimates (Garmin, Apple Watch) have become increasingly validated as longitudinal tracking tools — not gold-standard, but directionally useful for trend monitoring.

Zone 2 vs. HIIT for VO2max improvement: Long Zone 2 (60–70% HRmax) builds the mitochondrial base and is the primary lever for raising VO2max over 12–24 weeks. 4×4 Norwegian HIIT intervals (4 minutes at 85–95% HRmax, 4 reps, 2×/week) produces faster VO2max gains acutely. The evidence-based protocol: 3–4× Zone 2 per week + 1–2× HIIT. PPL users typically under-invest in cardiorespiratory training — the VO2max-longevity return on 30 minutes of Zone 2, 3× per week, is larger than almost any supplement stack.
Mechanistic Circadian Alignment for Training Recovery — AHA Scientific Statement 2026

The American Heart Association's 2026 scientific statement on circadian health and cardiometabolic disease formalized what has been building in the literature: misaligned circadian rhythms drive inflammation, insulin resistance, and elevated cortisol — all of which impair both training adaptation and recovery. The relevant mechanism for PPL users: late-night training (after 9pm) combined with artificial light exposure delays melatonin onset, fragments SWS, and blunts growth hormone secretion (which peaks in the first SWS episode, typically within 90 minutes of sleep onset).

The 2026 circadian light modeling research confirms: bright morning light (ideally outdoor, ≥10 minutes before 9am) anchors the circadian clock and reduces sleep onset latency. Evening blue-light exposure (screens, bright indoor light) pushes the clock later — delaying not just sleep onset but SWS timing and GH release. This is mechanistically relevant for anyone doing late PPL sessions.

What to Change

  • Add 3× Zone 2 sessions (30–45 min) to weekly PPL schedule — even light cycling/walking counts
  • Schedule at least one 4×4 HIIT interval session per week
  • Get morning outdoor light within 30 minutes of waking
  • Shift training sessions before 8pm if possible to protect SWS

What NOT to Change

  • PPL split structure — resistance training is non-negotiable; cardio supplements it
  • Training intensity — don't reduce load to accommodate cardio, manage scheduling instead
  • Post-workout nutrition timing — protein + leucine within 2h window remains solid
  • Deload frequency — don't skip deloads for cardio accumulation
⚡ Implication: Book a VO2max test in the next 30 days — most sports medicine clinics offer a CPET (Cardiopulmonary Exercise Test) for ~$200–400 AUD. Your VO2max tier (below normal / low normal / high normal / above normal) is the most important piece of longevity data most people in a PPL program haven't collected.
Section 03

Supplements & Nutrition

Position: Three fresh data points this cycle — none are repeats of recent issues. Creatine's most interesting 2026 story is cognitive, not muscular. Leucine threshold research reframes protein dosing precision for PPL users over 30. And the Finnish sauna cohort data, updated through 2026, has moved sauna from "promising wellness habit" to an intervention with a 63% sudden cardiac death reduction at high frequency. These are three separate and non-overlapping additions to a well-running stack.

New Data This Cycle

RCT Creatine — Cognitive Protection Under Sleep Deprivation. April 2026 (NutraIngredients / Scientific Reports): a single dose of 0.35 g/kg creatine significantly reduced cognitive deterioration during sleep deprivation — pronounced effects on logic, language, and psychomotor vigilance (PVT). Female participants showed greater benefit on executive tasks. A separate pilot (n=20 Alzheimer's patients) showed 8 weeks at 20g/day improved total cognition, working memory, and oral reading recognition, with 11% increase in brain creatine concentration. The muscle mechanism (PCr replenishment) applies equally to brain energy metabolism — this is not a new compound, it's a new application context.

Observational Sauna — Dose-Response Cardiac Protection. Finnish KIHD cohort (2,315 men, 20.7-year follow-up): 4–7 sauna sessions/week = 63% lower sudden cardiac death risk and 50% lower fatal CVD risk versus once weekly. The mechanism mirrors moderate aerobic exercise: core temperature rise → heart rate elevation → vasodilation → heat shock protein production → vascular adaptation. A 2024 follow-up found frequent sauna counteracts elevated blood pressure on mortality risk — additive protective effect on top of hypertension.

Carry-Forward & Protocol Notes

Meta Leucine Threshold — Precision Dosing for PPL Users. Systematic reviews confirm MPS (muscle protein synthesis) is maximally activated at ~2–3g leucine per meal (~20–30g high-quality protein). Adding more leucine beyond this threshold in younger adults does not further stimulate MPS — the response is saturable. Critical age nuance: in adults 45+, the leucine threshold is higher — older muscle is less sensitive to leucine, requiring 3–4g per meal (40g+ high-quality protein) to reach equivalent MPS activation. If you're distributing protein across 4–5 meals, the question is not total daily protein but whether each serving crosses the leucine threshold. Low-leucine fillers (collagen-heavy powders, plant blends with incomplete EAA profiles) don't count.

Creatine standard protocol unchanged: 3–5g/day monohydrate, no loading required. New finding supports acute higher doses (0.35 g/kg) on cognitively demanding days — roughly 28g for an 80kg person as a one-off performance dose under sleep deprivation. Not a daily protocol; a targeted tool.

Hype Watch — Sauna Overclaiming: The Finnish cohort data is robust but context-specific: Finnish dry sauna, 80–100°C, 15–30 minutes per session. IR (infrared) sauna reaches lower temperatures (45–60°C) and may not replicate the same cardiovascular stress response. The heat shock protein and vasodilation mechanism likely requires the full thermal challenge. Claims that 10-minute IR sessions at 50°C produce equivalent benefits to 20-minute traditional sauna at 90°C are not supported by the literature. If you're using IR, prioritise longer sessions and understand you're working with a different (probably smaller) stimulus.
⚡ Implication: Audit each protein serving against the leucine threshold — if you're over 35, target 40g+ per meal from high-quality complete protein sources (whey, eggs, meat, fish). Add sauna to your weekly PPL recovery protocol — target 4+ sessions/week. Standard creatine (3–5g/day) continues; consider an acute 0.35 g/kg dose on high-stress cognitive work days or when running on short sleep.

Oura's proprietary health LLM and Fountain Life's cross-wearable AI integration are the first signs of health AI moving from generic chatbot to personalised biomarker interpretation. The question is whether they actually surface actionable insights or just repackage data you already see.

Early access; watch for independent validation of insight quality versus dashboard aggregation.

Stack implication: If using both Oura (sleep, HRV) and Garmin (VO2max estimates), Fountain Life's integration is worth trialling. The VO2max tracking in context of sleep quality — both now available in one view — is the most useful combined signal for training load management.

01
VO2max Baseline Test Action Required

The longevity case for measuring VO2max is now unambiguous — 4.9-year life expectancy differential between "low normal" and "above normal" CRF. Book a CPET (Cardiopulmonary Exercise Test) at a sports medicine clinic in the next 30 days. The result anchors whether your current cardiorespiratory training is sufficient or whether you need structured Zone 2 + HIIT additions to your PPL week.

02
Rapamycin UT Health San Antonio Multi-Phase Trial Carried Forward

Launched April 2026: daily vs. intermittent rapamycin, 84 older adults, 6-month treatment + 6-month follow-up. The definitive dosing study that will establish the optimal anti-aging dose and immunosuppression threshold. Readout expected late 2026 / early 2027. Do not adjust any rapamycin protocol until this data is available.

03
Senolytic Personal Protocol — Fisetin + Quercetin Pulsed Monthly New

The DQF longitudinal data and Fisetin animal model literature are strong enough to consider the low-risk OTC entry point: Fisetin 1,000–1,500mg + Quercetin 1,000mg for 2 consecutive days, once monthly. This is the pulse senolytic protocol — not daily supplementation. Clinical trial NCT07025226 is the one to watch for human efficacy confirmation on zombie-cell clearance.

04
Testosterone Panel — Total T, Free T, SHBG Baseline Carried Forward

From issue #044: the 2026 Cureus synthesis confirms most borderline-low T cases haven't exhausted natural interventions before TRT. If no panel in the last 12 months, run total T, free T, and SHBG. This remains the starting line for whether lifestyle or clinical intervention is the right next step.

05
Urolithin A Brain Health Trial (n=650) — 2026 Readout Carried Forward

The largest UA human trial to date (Mitopure, brain health endpoints) is reporting in 2026.
