7 🌐 Geopolitical Calendar
🚨 STRAIT OF HORMUZ DUAL BLOCKADE — WEEK 9, ESCALATING
Status: Traffic through the Strait at 5% of pre-war levels. Only 154 vessels crossed in all of March vs. ~3,000 normally. Iran controls passage via territorial water routing past Larak Island, charging $1M+ per ship in tolls. US blockade of Iranian ports since April 13 has created a "dual blockade" with 38+ vessels turned away. The Strait has been effectively closed since February 28, when US-Israeli air strikes began.

Transmission Mechanism: 20% of global oil transits Hormuz. The IEA calls this the "largest supply disruption in the history of the global oil market" — exceeding 10 mb/d of lost capacity. Brent crude has gained for 8 consecutive sessions to $114. The flow chain: Hormuz closure → crude surge (Brent +55% since war began) → fuel/transport cost spike (Australia fuel +32.8% m/m) → headline CPI acceleration → central bank paralysis (Fed holds, RBA may hike again) → yield curve steepening → equity sector divergence (energy/defence up, airlines/consumer down).

Reversal: Iran proposed reopening Hormuz while deferring nuclear talks. The US has so far rejected this framing — Washington wants a comprehensive deal. Watch for any US-Iran backchannel signals. Trump discussed the proposal with aides on April 27. A sustained reopening would collapse crude $15–20 in a session, triggering a violent rotation out of energy and into rate-sensitive growth/tech.
🚨 FED LEADERSHIP TRANSITION — MAY 15
Status: Powell confirmed he will step down as Chair on May 15 but remain on the Board "for a period of time to be determined." Kevin Warsh's nomination as successor is advancing. Yesterday's 8-4 dissent — the most fractured FOMC in 34 years — suggests the transition will be contentious.

Transmission Mechanism: Fed Chair transitions historically create 2-4 weeks of elevated volatility in rates and FX. Warsh is perceived as more hawkish than Powell on inflation but potentially more accommodating on financial conditions. The uncertainty premium is compounded by the unprecedented 4-way dissent. Markets will need to re-price the Fed reaction function under new leadership, which affects the entire rate curve, mortgage rates, equity valuations, and crypto's macro sensitivity.

Reversal: A smooth transition with Warsh adopting Powell's "data-dependent" framework would calm markets quickly. The risk is Warsh signalling a material policy shift at his first press conference (likely late June FOMC).
🚨 US TARIFF REGIME — SECTION 122 ONGOING
Status: After the Supreme Court struck down IEEPA broad tariffs in January, the administration imposed a temporary 15% global import duty under Section 122. EU retaliated with a 47% reduction in steel import quotas. Trade tensions simmering beneath the oil crisis headlines.

Transmission Mechanism: Tariffs compound the oil-driven inflation shock by raising import prices. The dual pressure (energy + tariffs) is why headline CPI is running hotter than core in every major economy. Companies are front-loading imports ahead of potential escalation, temporarily inflating inventory and trade data. The real impact hits consumer prices with a 2-3 month lag.

Reversal: Congressional override of Section 122 (unlikely), or trade deal announcements. Midterm election calculus may force de-escalation as voter sensitivity to energy/food prices intensifies.
Hormuz is the macro variable that dominates all others. Every $10/bbl move in crude shifts the Fed calculus, the RBA decision, and the crypto risk premium. The Fed transition on May 15 adds a second layer of uncertainty. Position defensively: long energy/defence as hedges, and watch for the Iran counter-proposal response as the potential circuit-breaker.
