Alpha — Passive vs Aggressive Flow

Thursday, 19 November 2026 · Sydney, Australia · Issue TBC 11 min read
TL;DR

Passive vs aggressive flow is the split between resting limit orders that wait for a fill and market orders that cross the spread to take one, and it decides whether a move is absorbed or travels. Practical takeaway: when heavy aggression fails to move price, the passive side is winning, so wait for that absorber to fail before you trade the break. Builds on Absorption (Issue, 15 Nov 2026) and treats the split as the prerequisite for reading it.

§01
What It Is
Who is waiting and who is paying the spread
Passive flow is resting size that waits to be hit, aggressive flow is market orders that cross the spread to take it, and the outcome at each level tells you which one is in control.

Passive vs aggressive flow is the split that decides whether a trade moves price or gets absorbed by it. A passive participant posts a limit order and waits, so it supplies liquidity to the book. An aggressive participant sends a market order that crosses the spread and takes whatever is resting. Every executed contract has one of each, so the useful question is not “who bought” but “who had to pay up to get filled, and did the price survive it”.

This is the foundation under the rest of the orderflow family. It builds on Absorption (Issue, 15 Nov 2026), which covered the outcome side of this split, and it is the vocabulary you need before reading Spoofing or Stop Clusters. For a Wyckoff reader the mapping is direct: aggression is the effort you see on the tape, and passive size is the result that either shows up as a stall or fails to show up at all. When heavy aggressive selling produces a small price move, the resting bid is doing work the candle does not display. When the same selling produces a large move, the book was thin and the passive side stepped away.

AggressorThe side crossing the spread with a market order or a marketable limit.
MakerA resting limit order that waits to be hit or lifted.
Passive absorptionAggressive volume met by resting size while price makes little progress.
InitiativeAggression that pushes price into new ground instead of being met.
Remember If you only remember one thing: price travels when aggression wins a level, and it stalls when passive size wins that level. Judge the outcome, not the volume.
§02
Mechanics
One fill, two roles, one outcome
Each executed trade pairs one aggressor with one maker, so aggression shows up as volume and the maker's size shows up as the price that refuses to move.

The mechanic runs on one fill rule. Every trade pairs one aggressor with one resting order. The aggressor pays the spread to be done now, and the maker is compensated, through the spread and sometimes a rebate, for being willing to wait. What prints on the tape is the level the aggressor was prepared to reach, and what holds the price is the size the maker left behind.

Walk one level through the sequence:

  1. A resting bid sits at a price with a fixed size. It is passive and leaves no trace on the trade record until something touches it.
  2. A market sell arrives and consumes that size. It is aggressive, and it is recorded as a sell-side trade printing at the bid.
  3. If new bids replenish the level as fast as the sells consume it, price holds. The aggression is being absorbed and the trade record shows heavy selling with a flat print.
  4. If the resting size is exhausted, the aggressor moves to the next bid down. Price travels, and the move is initiative rather than absorption.
Aggression balance = Aggressive buy volume − Aggressive sell volume, measured per level
Price response = Δ price across the same window
A large negative balance with a flat price means passive bids are absorbing. A large balance with a large move means the book was thin, which is a different read from strong buyers.

Delta only counts the aggressor. A limit order that never gets hit does not register, so the passive side is always inferred from what happened to price. That is the whole reason the outcome matters more than the number.

Read the left panel as the absorption case. The aggressive sells are real and visible in the trade record, but the 62,500 bid is large enough to fill them, so the price does not follow the volume. Read the right panel as the failure case. The sells are identical, but the resting size has gone, so the aggressor walks through the empty level and the candle shows a move that the earlier volume appeared to foreshadow.

The two panels differ only in the passive side, which is the point of the diagram. A chart that shows you only price and total volume cannot distinguish them. Order flow tools separate the two by showing aggression per level and by showing how price responded to it.

§04
In Practice
READ THE OUTCOME, NOT THE VOLUME
Judge a level by the price response to aggression, not by how much volume printed there.
1

Read the outcome, not the volume

Heavy aggressive selling that fails to move price is the signature of a passive buyer doing the work. Log the level, the volume, and whether the next two bars closed beneath it.

2

Treat resting size as a live claim

A bid is information only while it fills. Watch whether the level is being replenished after each hit, because a bid that disappears between prints has already changed sides.

3

Measure the distance to the next absorber

When aggression wins a level, the next resting size becomes the target. Mark where the book thins, since that is where the travel tends to stop or accelerate.

§07
Common Traps
VOCABULARY IS NOT AN EDGE
Treating every high-volume wick as absorption, when the passive side has not yet been shown to hold.
1
Reading volume as aggression

Volume is two-sided, and every trade has one maker. High volume tells you the level was busy, not which side was in control. Fix: separate aggressor volume from total volume and check the price response.

2
Treating a resting bid as fixed support

Passive size can be pulled before it is hit, and a bid that vanishes between prints has already changed sides. Fix: keep a bid on watch only while it is being replenished, and cross-check against Spoofing and Iceberg behaviour.

3
Calling absorption before the failure

A stall is a candidate absorption, not a confirmed one. Wait for the absorber to fail to hold on a closed bar before you act on the break in the other direction.

4
Overhyped: this is textbook vocabulary, not an edge

Every exchange book has resting limit orders and market orders, and this split has been described for decades. The hype is in labelling every wick as absorption after the fact. Use it as a reading frame, and do not treat it as a signal unless the level, the follow-through and the failure all confirm it.
