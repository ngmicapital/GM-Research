Fourth Circuit Rules States Can Target Kalshi’s Prediction-Market Event Contracts

“Event contracts have traded openly in Louisiana for years, in compliance with the Commodity Exchange Act and CFTC regulations,” Michael Selig, Chairman, CFTC — CFTC announcement as reported by SBC Americas, 13 Nov 2026

On 17 November the Fourth Circuit held that states may go after prediction-market event contracts, a loss for Kalshi on sports contracts traded through a CFTC-registered platform. It is Kalshi’s third federal appellate defeat; the Third Circuit remains its only win across four appeals. The same report counts Louisiana, Arkansas, Ohio and Tennessee as states now pressing enforcement against CFTC-registered platforms, with Robinhood most exposed through its Kalshi contract volume.

The federal position is a preemption claim. The CFTC’s complaint argues that state consumer-protection rules are preempted as applied to event contracts on designated contract markets, and the Eighth Circuit has not yet ruled on that question. The Brief's read: this ruling cuts against the agency’s position but does not settle preemption nationally.

📎 Historical Precedent: Kalshi’s earlier Maryland appellate defeat, one of the prior federal losses Prediction News counts before this ruling.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

The dispute has moved from district courts to a circuit scorecard that now leans toward states, while the CFTC presses an exclusive-jurisdiction theory on designated contract markets (per the agency’s own complaint, as summarised in SBC Americas). Kalshi’s venue strategy now matters as much as its merits. The Brief's read: a split on preemption is the realistic route to Supreme Court review, and until then, state orders will drive product geography.

🔄 Contrarian Take

Kalshi CEO Tarek Mansour argues the platform has built “significant guardrails” and has caught people attempting to move markets (Axios, 17 Nov 2026). The Brief's read: guardrails answer a manipulation question, while this ruling turns on whether states may act at all, so the argument does not reach the issue decided.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Robinhood sits closest to the exposure because its prediction volume runs heavily through Kalshi contracts (Prediction News, 18 Nov 2026). Coinbase event contracts were among those a Louisiana state judge barred (Invezz, 15 Nov 2026). The Brief's read: geofencing costs fall first on distributors such as Robinhood and Coinbase, not on the designated contract markets themselves.

CFTC Suspends Kalshi’s Trump Mention Markets Pending a Manipulation Review

“Kalshi has not attempted to establish that the Contracts are non-manipulable.” CFTC order, as quoted by Axios — 13 Nov 2026

On 13 November the CFTC stayed certification of certain Kalshi mention markets, contracts that let traders bet on the words the President will say, and opened a proceeding on whether those contracts can be manipulated. Kalshi took the contracts down, and confirmed the withdrawal on 17 November.

This is not an outright ban; the agency has not declared the markets illegal. Kalshi must answer the certification question by 11 December 2026, and CEO Tarek Mansour has said publicly that the platform opposes the decision.

📎 Historical Precedent: the 2023 Kalshi–CFTC legal dispute, which also ran for months over the scope of federal event-contract oversight.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

The CFTC is using a certification review as its lever instead of a rule or a prohibition, which puts the evidentiary burden on the listing venue. The order turns on the CFTC’s finding that Kalshi has not shown the contracts are non-manipulable. The Brief's read: expect the certification record, rather than enforcement, to become the main tool against contracts whose outcomes an individual can influence.

🔄 Contrarian Take

Mansour says the platform has implemented “significant guardrails” and did catch a few people trying to move these markets (Axios, 17 Nov 2026). The Brief's read: a catch rate is evidence of surveillance, but the CFTC’s standard asks whether the contracts are structurally resistant, which is a different and harder showing.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Distributors that carry Kalshi mention contracts face withdrawal risk, and Robinhood is named as the most exposed through its Kalshi volume (Prediction News, 18 Nov 2026). The Brief's read: a manipulation-focused standard favours venues with strong surveillance over venues that simply list more contracts.

Senate’s Announced Votes Omit CLARITY Act as the Lame-Duck Window Opens

“clear rules of the road for digital assets” Tim Scott, Chairman, Senate Banking Committee — statement after the 15 Sep 2026 cloture vote, as reported by TokenPost

Following our 14 November coverage, this is a thread rather than a new vote. The bill failed a 49–50 cloture vote on 15 September, and no floor vote has followed. The new fact is the Senate’s announced schedule for the week of 16 November, which lists a judicial nomination and cloture on an amendment to separate legislation, but no CLARITY vote.

The outgoing Congress can still act in its lame-duck session, and Senator Tillis’s motion to reconsider keeps a procedural route open. Cloture requires 60 votes to begin debate, so any lame-duck floor time needs a bipartisan deal first.

📎 Historical Precedent: the House passed the bill on 17 July 2025 by 294 to 134, which remains the last full-chamber vote on the measure.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

The realistic path now runs through agency rulemaking rather than statute. Senate Banking Chairman Tim Scott has said the SEC and CFTC should set clear rules until Congress legislates. The Brief's read: with the 3 January 2027 window closing and no scheduled vote, agency positions will govern US crypto market structure for the foreseeable future.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Exchanges and token issuers planning US market-structure products will rely on SEC and CFTC rulemaking as the operative regime. The Brief's read: firms that deferred registration decisions to a statute now need agency-level positions, and Coinbase-style trust and custody structures gain importance while the bill stays stalled.

Ohio and Tennessee Join Louisiana and Arkansas in Pressing Prediction-Market Enforcement

“The CFTC said Louisiana is the 12th state it sued to defend its jurisdiction over derivatives.” CFTC statement, as reported by SBC Americas — 16 Nov 2026

Prediction News reports that state gambling authorities in Ohio and Tennessee are pressing enforcement against CFTC-registered platforms, alongside Louisiana and Arkansas. The report links the spread to Kalshi’s recent appellate losses.

The federal government’s own count has grown quickly. Louisiana is the twelfth state the CFTC has sued over derivatives jurisdiction, which means the federal-state map is widening faster than any single appeal can resolve.

📎 Historical Precedent: a Louisiana state judge ordered ten prediction-market operators to stop serving the state, the template other states are now following.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Multi-state enforcement against a single federally designated market creates a direct conflict. The CFTC has said exchanges may not cut off customers based on geography, while state orders require exactly that. The Brief's read: the conflict will be resolved in court state by state, and until then distributors carry the compliance risk.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Robinhood has the most at stake because its Kalshi contract volume is named as its exposure (Prediction News, 18 Nov 2026). The Brief's read: distributors will meet enforcement risk before the venues do, so their state-by-state product controls become the first line of defence.
