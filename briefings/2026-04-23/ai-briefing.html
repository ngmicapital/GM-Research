Fundamentals

What RAG Actually Is — And Why Deep Research Max Is Really "RAG Over Everything"

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is the pattern of injecting fresh, external information into a model's context window at query time, rather than relying on what the model memorised during training. The model doesn't "learn" the new data — it reads it, reasons over it, and discards it. Think of it as open-book versus closed-book exam: same student, different access to references.

A RAG system has three moving parts. First, the retriever: when you ask a question, it searches a corpus (documents, databases, the web) for the most relevant chunks, usually by converting text to vectors and finding nearest neighbours. Second, the context assembler: it stuffs those chunks into the prompt alongside your question. Third, the generator: the LLM produces an answer grounded in the retrieved passages. The classic failure mode is "retrieval says A, model confidently says B" — which happens when the retriever scores poorly or the model over-weights its training data.

Modern agentic research — Claude Research, Deep Research Max, and the Managed Agents harness — is RAG with one crucial twist: instead of retrieving once and answering, the agent runs a loop. Retrieve, reason, identify gaps, retrieve again, refine, eventually produce a synthesised report. "Extended test-time compute" in Google's Deep Research Max announcement is marketing language for "the loop runs more iterations."

The reason MCP matters so much here is that MCP is RAG's standardised retrieval layer for private data. Before MCP, every vendor rebuilt the connector plumbing for Notion, Google Drive, FactSet, internal databases. With MCP, the retriever becomes portable — the same agent can query FactSet at a fund and your notes folder on a weekend.

Why this matters for the AI landscape: RAG-plus-loop is eating the "chatbot with a web search button" category. The next 12 months' productisation wave is agents that know how to decompose a question, retrieve iteratively, and synthesise across sources — which is exactly what Deep Research Max demonstrates at Google scale. Understanding this loop is the difference between prompting a model and designing a research workflow.

Research Radar

Papers that become products in 6 months
Google's TurboQuant at ICLR 2026 compresses vector retrieval memory enough to put production-grade RAG on a laptop — enabler for everything in §03.
Research

TurboQuant (Google) — Vector Quantisation That Makes Local RAG Viable

Google Research presented TurboQuant at ICLR 2026 (Rio, this week). The algorithm combines PolarQuant (random rotation of data vectors before quantisation) and Quantized Johnson-Lindenstrauss (QJL) compression to cut the memory overhead of large-scale vector search without the recall collapse that usually follows aggressive quantisation.

⚡ Why it matters in 6 months: Every agentic research workflow — Deep Research Max, Claude Research, anything RAG-heavy — is bottlenecked by vector-store memory. If TurboQuant ships in open-source vector DBs (faiss, qdrant, pgvector), on-device RAG over your entire document history stops being a cloud-only feature. This is the plumbing that makes the §03 Fundamentals explainer actually cheap to deploy.
Research

Neuro-Symbolic VLA (Tufts, April 5) — Hybrid Reasoning for Robotics

Tufts researchers published a neuro-symbolic Visual-Language-Action system combining statistical pattern recognition with explicit symbolic reasoning. Addresses the brittleness of pure-neural VLA models on tasks that require rule-following rather than interpolation.

⚡ Why it matters in 6 months: Direct relevance to the Prometheus / physical-world AI thesis in §05. If neuro-symbolic hybrids become the default VLA architecture, the competitive moat for pure-neural robotics labs shrinks fast. Adjacent to anything you'd care about if the physical-AI category goes public.
