01 Key Ideas & Insights
Newsletter · Stratechery / Ben Thompson · 22 April 2026

Tim Cook's Impeccable Timing: A Succession Master Class

Ben Thompson used this week's column to reframe the Cook-to-Ternus handover (announced Monday, effective September 1) not as a routine CEO transition, but as one of the rarest artifacts in corporate history: a founder-successor who voluntarily steps down at a peak rather than being forced out at a trough.

The numbers Thompson cites are staggering. Under Cook's 15-year tenure, Apple revenue grew 303%, profit grew 354%, and market value expanded from $297B to $4T — a 1,251% increase. Cook inherited Apple roughly three years after its most important 0→1 product (the iPhone) and spent his entire tenure executing the 1→N phase of what Steve Jobs had architected.

The usual trap for successors to iconic founders is that the founder sticks around for 2, 3, 4 more years — and by the time they hand over, the only direction is down. Cook inherited early enough that the growth mandate was still live.

The deeper argument: succession quality is a function of when the handover happens, not just who receives the baton. Ternus (51, nearly Cook's age at his own ascent, also a lifelong Apple insider) inherits a company at a premium valuation with an AI roadmap still unresolved — giving him real 1→N work of his own. That's by design, not luck.

Newsletter · High Performance Founder / Dango · 21 April 2026

When the Mirror Lies: The 8-Biomarker Reality Check

Dan Martell's Tuesday newsletter opened with a case study worth dwelling on: Brian hit 15% body fat, his abs reappeared, people at his gym asked what he was doing — and his bloodwork came back showing a pre-diabetic HbA1c. The visual body and the metabolic body were telling opposite stories.

The frame Martell is pushing is substantive: healthy is a metabolic state measured in serum, not a phenotype photographed in a mirror. The 8-biomarker panel he uses with coaching clients — HbA1c, fasting insulin, HOMA-IR, hs-CRP, triglycerides, HDL, ApoB, and Vitamin D — systematically catches conditions years before they produce symptoms.

Most doctors call HbA1c under 5.7% "normal." Longevity-research targets are under 5.0–5.3%. The gap between clinical-normal and optimal is where a decade of quiet metabolic damage happens.

The most actionable insight in the piece is the primacy of ApoB — the count of harmful cholesterol-carrying particles — which leading longevity physicians now consider the single best predictor of cardiovascular disease. Standard LDL tests miss it; you can have a "normal" LDL with an elevated ApoB and an elevated trajectory toward atherosclerotic disease. Target: under 70 mg/dL.

Newsletter · School of Practical Philosophy Weekly Wisdom · 22 April 2026

The Borges Reframe: Happiness as Internal Infrastructure

The School of Practical Philosophy's Weekly Wisdom #020 anchored on a line from Jorge Luis Borges that bears repeating for its structural argument, not just its lyricism: "So plant your own gardens and decorate your own soul, instead of waiting for others to bring you flowers."

The operational claim behind the poetry is that happiness is a state-of-mind rather than a state-of-circumstances. Most people hold it dependent — contingent on outcomes, on how they're treated, on what they possess. The Borges reframe makes it independent: a cultivated internal infrastructure that persists across circumstance.

The key may be in the moment-by-moment process of blooming, and not in the flowers themselves.

This rhymes — unintentionally — with Mark Manson's "self-relationship as infrastructure" argument from earlier in the week: the external relationships (and external achievements) you try to extract meaning from can only reliably hold what you've already planted internally. Two different traditions, one recurring insight: outsourcing is the problem, not the solution.

02 Strategy & Practice
Practical · Leadership / Succession

The Cook Playbook: Designing for Handover From Day One

Thompson's Cook analysis yields a transferable playbook for any founder or leader thinking about eventual transition. The secret isn't who you pick — it's when you pick them, and what you leave undone for them.

  • Exit with a live growth mandate. A successor inheriting a flat or declining business is set up to fail. The best handovers happen when the incoming leader still has visible runway — a product category, a geography, an unsolved strategic question that gives them room to add their own 1→N chapter.
  • Promote from long-tenured insiders when the culture is the moat. Ternus has spent almost his entire career at Apple. For companies where culture and institutional knowledge are load-bearing, external hires carry transition risk that dwarfs any stylistic freshness they bring.
  • Announce early, transition at a fixed date. Cook's September 1 date was disclosed months in advance with Board unanimity. Ambiguity during transitions is the single largest destroyer of organisational momentum.
  • Keep the predecessor engaged, not in the way. Cook moving to Executive Chairman with a mandate focused on policymaker engagement keeps his unique value (regulatory relationships) available without giving him operational veto over Ternus.
Practical · Health / Longevity

The Annual Biomarker Request List

Most GPs won't order this panel unprompted. The cost for ordering these privately is modest relative to the downside of missing a 5-year-ahead signal. A once-a-year request with specific test names:

  • Ask for names, not ranges. HbA1c, fasting insulin, HOMA-IR, hs-CRP, triglycerides, HDL, ApoB, and 25-hydroxy Vitamin D. Your GP may push back on fasting insulin and ApoB — push back once; insist politely; these are the two that most often catch problems early.
  • Target optimal, not clinical-normal. Many "in range" results are sub-optimal by longevity standards. Know the optimal thresholds (ApoB <70, HbA1c <5.3, hs-CRP <1.0, fasting insulin <7) before you read the lab report.
  • Retest at 3 months after an intervention, not at 12. If an out-of-range marker triggers a lifestyle change, retest in 90 days. Most markers respond meaningfully within that window and the feedback loop is what produces compliance.
  • Do not wait for symptoms. The entire point of the panel is that symptoms are a lagging indicator — arterial damage, insulin resistance, and inflammation operate silently for years. You are testing for problems you cannot yet feel.
Practical · Mind / Cultivation

Planting vs. Waiting: The Daily Cultivation Audit

The Borges frame is easy to admire and hard to operationalise. The work, borrowing from the philosophical tradition it's drawn from, looks like this:

  • List what you're "waiting on." Most people can name 3–5 conditions they believe must be met before they'll be happy (a promotion, a resolved relationship, a fitness threshold, a financial milestone). Write them down. Ask whether your happiness has historically followed any of them for more than a few weeks.
  • Identify one internal capacity to cultivate weekly. Attention, gratitude, patience, curiosity, presence. Pick one. Commit to a specific practice — meditation, journaling, deliberate reading — that strengthens it, not instrumentally toward an outcome but as its own work.
  • Audit what drains vs. what cultivates. Scrolling, reactive consumption, status-monitoring — these are the inverse of cultivation. They decorate nothing; they deplete. Track a week honestly.
  • Divorce happiness from achievement tracking. Achievement metrics are a legitimate operational tool but a terrible happiness proxy. The two systems need different dashboards.

AI / Frontier Model

Claude Opus 4.7 — Self-Checking Agentic Workloads

Anthropic released Claude Opus 4.7 on April 16 — a meaningful bump over 4.5 with a new self-verification capability (the model can double-check its own work during long agentic runs), image resolution raised to 2576px / 3.75MP, and task budgets that let you specify a rough token target for a full agentic loop including thinking, tool calls, and final output. Pricing is $5 per million input / $25 per million output tokens, with up to 90% savings via prompt caching. For anyone running agents that iterate over long-horizon tasks, the task-budget and self-check features materially change cost predictability and output quality. Anthropic conceded the unreleased model codenamed Mythos still trails internally, suggesting another release is close.

Agent Framework

LangGraph Deep Agents — Planning + Subagents + Filesystem

LangChain's Deep Agents harness (MIT-licensed, released on top of LangGraph) gives developers a pre-built agent that can plan, spawn subagents, and leverage a filesystem for complex multi-step tasks — essentially a production-ready version of the architecture that most agent builders end up coding by hand. Used by Klarna, Replit, and Elastic in production. Gartner's projection that 40% of enterprise applications will feature task-specific AI agents by end-2026 (up from <5% in 2025) is increasingly consensus; the question has shifted from "if" to "which framework." LangGraph's lead is consolidating; AutoGen and CrewAI remain strong for multi-agent conversation and role-based teams respectively.

⛓️
DeFi Infrastructure

ETHGas — A Financial Marketplace for Ethereum Blockspace

Delphi Digital's latest research piece profiles ETHGas, a real-time infrastructure layer building a financial marketplace for Ethereum blockspace. Delphi's argument: Ethereum's scalability problem isn't purely about raw compute and throughput — a disproportionate share of blockspace is consumed by adversarial MEV activity (frontrunning, back-running, sandwich attacks) and the contention around valuable state transitions dominates marginal gas usage. ETHGas rethinks how blockspace is priced, structured, and allocated, creating markets for ordering and execution rather than leaving them to adversarial extraction. A useful primitive to understand if you hold any serious DeFi exposure — and a candidate standard if it wins adoption.

04 On the Horizon
AI

The AI-Era CEO Transition Wave

Cook's departure is the most visible of what will be a wave of incumbent-CEO handovers over the next 24 months. The AI strategic realignment is hard for leaders who built their companies on pre-AI assumptions; boards are increasingly willing to accelerate succession to bring in operators native to the new paradigm. Ternus is a transitional figure (lifer, hardware-first); the generation after him will look more like Replit's Amjad Masad or Anthropic's Dario Amodei. Watch for similar announcements at Microsoft's peer firms, the big-three consulting partnerships, and at least one of the major banks before year-end.

Health

Longevity Biomarker Testing Goes Mainstream

What was niche in 2022 (ApoB testing, fasting insulin, HOMA-IR) is becoming standard in high-performance circles and will be consumer-mainstream within 24 months. Direct-to-consumer lab services (Function Health, InsideTracker, BioHarmony) are pushing this, as are coaching businesses like Martell's. The regulatory and medical establishment is a lagging indicator — expect the gap between "what your doctor orders" and "what longevity-literate professionals order" to become a visible public-health conversation, especially as more concrete outcomes data accumulates. The infrastructure question: who builds the interpretation layer that turns raw biomarker reads into actionable protocol without requiring a concierge physician?

Finance

Blockspace as an Asset Class

ETHGas's pitch — that blockspace allocation should be priced and structured like other financial markets — is an early signal of a broader trend: execution rights, ordering, and MEV protection becoming tradeable primitives rather than emergent side-effects. If ETHGas (or a competitor) achieves meaningful adoption, expect derivatives, forwards, and institutional products on blockspace to follow. The regulatory treatment will be messy; the economic logic is clean. The teams building this infrastructure are also the ones best positioned when bridge and validator architectures get restructured post-April-exploit-wave.

Mind

The Independence Doctrine in Philosophy and Practice

A quiet thread running through multiple traditions this week — Borges via Practical Philosophy, Manson's self-relationship argument last week, the broader stoic revival — converges on the same operational claim: external conditions are volatile and uncontrollable; the reliable variable is the one you cultivate internally. This isn't new, but it's gaining cultural purchase against the backdrop of exhaustion with external-validation-seeking and the gradual visibility of attention-economy harms. Expect this to continue migrating from the therapy office into leadership development, education, and eventually into workplace and organisational design. The firms that figure out how to support employee self-cultivation (rather than just extraction) will find unusual retention advantages.
