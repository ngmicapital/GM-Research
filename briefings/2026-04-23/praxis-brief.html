Newsletter · Daily Stoic · 22 April 2026

What Survives Is Not Who He Was

Ryan Holiday framed Marcus Aurelius's Meditations this week with an insight that reorients how we read any journal, any diary, any private writing: the book that has shaped millions of readers was never written for publication. It survived by accident — fire, time, church censors, indifferent descendants could each have erased it. That it exists at all is a historical anomaly.

But Holiday's sharper observation is about what that survival distorts. Meditations is a record of Marcus wrestling with his worst tendencies — impatience, pride, grief, the seduction of power. It shows a man reminding himself of virtues he was still striving to embody, not a man who had mastered them. People reading it cold often mistake the medium for the message and conclude Stoicism is bleak, self-flagellating, joyless.

"In Meditations, we see only what he was struggling with, what he needed reminders of, what he was trying to cling to in a difficult moment. What was published obliterates the full and complex human life behind those words."

The deeper principle, beyond Marcus: our documentation captures our fights, not our wholeness. A journal of a good marriage looks mostly like a record of the hard days, because those are the days you need to write. The archive of a successful business reads as a catalogue of problems. If you only read someone's private notes, you can badly misread who they actually were. Marcus began his Meditations not with Stoic doctrine but with gratitude — a list of people who shaped him. The struggle diary came after the acknowledgment of joy.

Newsletter · School of Practical Philosophy Weekly Wisdom #020 · 21 April 2026

Plant Your Own Garden: On Happiness as Infrastructure

The School of Practical Philosophy opened this week's newsletter with a Jorge Luis Borges line that is deceptively simple but contains a full argument about the source of lasting wellbeing:

"So plant your own gardens and decorate your own soul, instead of waiting for others to bring you flowers." — Jorge Luis Borges, "After a While"

The newsletter's framing extends Borges into a practical diagnostic: most people outsource their happiness — treating it as a variable that depends on how others treat them, whether external circumstances align, whether a certain threshold of achievement has been crossed. This produces happiness that is perpetually conditional, perpetually deferrable.

The inquiry the newsletter poses is worth sitting with: Is your happiness based on a set of criteria that must be met, or is it a state of mind? Is it dependent, or independent? The Stoic echo here is clear — Epictetus distinguished sharply between what is "up to us" and what is not. Happiness that relies on the latter is not happiness at all; it's a scheduled delivery that may never arrive.

The practical implication isn't passive acceptance of circumstances. It's the recognition that the process of tending — to your character, your curiosity, your relationships, your craft — is where the wellbeing actually lives, not in any outcome the tending might produce.

Newsletter · Delphi Digital "Week in Focus" · 20 April 2026

DeFi's Real Enemy Is Now Human: The Off-Chain Attack Surface

Delphi Digital this week delivered the most important DeFi security analysis of 2026 so far. Twelve protocols drained in April's first twenty days. $600M+ gone — 3.7x the combined losses from all of Q1. Two exploits did almost all the damage, and neither required cracking a single line of smart contract code.

The Drift Protocol exploit ($285M, April 1) was pure social engineering. Lazarus Group — a North Korean state-sponsored team — spent six months running a reconnaissance and manipulation campaign against Drift's Security Council multisig signers. When they were ready, they drained the protocol in under 15 minutes. No zero-day. No novel cryptography. They manipulated the people who held the keys.

The Kelp DAO exploit ($293M, April 19) was a misconfigured LayerZero bridge — a forged cross-chain message released 116,500 rsETH tokens (~18% of circulating supply) to an attacker-controlled address. That rsETH immediately became collateral on Aave, generating a cascade: $196M in estimated bad debt for Aave V3, AAVE token down 18%, TVL falling from $26.4B to $20B as depositors exited. Aave's code was not compromised. It worked exactly as designed. The contagion arrived anyway.

"The attack surface has moved off-chain. Audits don't stop misconfigured bridges, phished admin keys, or multisig signers getting worked for six months. As AI sharpens reconnaissance and phishing at scale, the asymmetry only widens. Defenders need to be right every time. Attackers need one config file."

Delphi's framing is sobering: this is not a DeFi-is-dead story. It is a DeFi-has-a-different-enemy story. The ecosystem has spent years hardening contracts and still loses hundreds of millions to operational security failures. The next generation of DeFi security investment will need to be in human systems — key management, operational security culture, timelocked governance — not just audits.

Health Intelligence · High Performance Founder · 21 April 2026

The 8 Biomarkers Your Doctor Doesn't Order (But Should)

Dan (High Performance Founder) wrote the most practically useful health email of the week: a framework for understanding your metabolic and cardiovascular health that goes far beyond what a standard annual checkup captures. The central provocation — your doctor says "normal," longevity research disagrees — is not hyperbole. Standard reference ranges are set for median population health, not optimal performance or longevity.

The eight markers, with longevity-grade targets:

Marker What It Measures Optimal Target Flag
HbA1c Average blood sugar over 2–3 months <5.3% Key
Fasting Insulin Pancreatic workload / insulin resistance proxy <7 µIU/mL Rarely ordered
HOMA-IR Calculated insulin resistance score <1.0 Key
hs-CRP Chronic low-grade inflammation <1.0 mg/L Key
Triglycerides Carbohydrate / fat processing efficiency <80 mg/dL Standard
HDL Cholesterol Protective cholesterol (arterial clearing) >50 mg/dL Standard
ApoB Harmful particle count (plaque drivers) <70 mg/dL Rarely ordered
Vitamin D Immune / bone / anti-inflammatory function 40–60 ng/mL Key

The two markers flagged "Rarely ordered" deserve specific attention. Fasting Insulin reveals how hard your pancreas is working to maintain normal glucose — you can have completely normal fasting glucose and sky-high insulin, which means your body is compensating and won't do so indefinitely. Most doctors never order it unprompted. ApoB counts the actual number of harmful particles driving plaque into arterial walls; standard LDL tests only measure cholesterol concentration. Leading longevity physicians now consider ApoB the single best predictor of cardiovascular disease.

The operational move: take this list to your next appointment and request these tests by name. Especially: Fasting Insulin, HOMA-IR, and ApoB. Your waistline tells one story. Your blood tells the real one.

Strategy · Tim Ferriss Show · 21 April 2026

Brian Dean's Two Exits: The Architecture of a Non-Linear Path

Tim Ferriss released a case study episode this week featuring Brian Dean, founder of Backlinko and Exploding Topics — both acquired by Semrush, which was itself acquired by Adobe for $1.9 billion. The headline number obscures the more instructive story underneath.

Dean's path started in 2008 during the financial crisis: broke, directionless, eating canned beef stew in his dad's basement. He applied the 4-Hour Workweek framework not as a system but as permission to experiment cheaply. Geoarbitrage gave him the time margin to test ideas without needing them to work immediately. He built a content SEO framework (Backlinko) that became a reference site, then a trend research platform (Exploding Topics) that identified emerging topics before they went mainstream.

  • Test assumptions cheaply before committing. Dean's path wasn't planned — it was a series of small experiments where the cost of failure was kept low enough that the next experiment remained possible.
  • Build something that compounds without you. Both Backlinko and Exploding Topics were information businesses with durable, searchable content that accrued value over time. The model was muse-like: each piece of work kept generating return after the initial effort.
  • Plan for the void. Ferriss noted that Dean addressed "the chapter almost everyone skips" — what you actually do with freedom once you have it. Achieving the exit doesn't solve the question of meaning. That question requires separate work.
  • Exits are not endings. Semrush being acquired by Adobe for $1.9B means Dean's tools ended up in the hands of one of the world's largest creative software platforms — a compounding outcome neither Backlinko nor Exploding Topics could have engineered directly.
Operational Security · Delphi Digital

What Crypto Builders Must Do After April's OpSec Failure

The Drift and Kelp exploits carry directly applicable lessons for anyone building or investing in DeFi infrastructure. The checklist is not exhaustive, but these are the moves that would have prevented both exploits:

  • Multisig governance must have time-locks. Any protocol where a quorum of signers can execute a large transaction in under 24 hours without on-chain community visibility is one social engineering campaign away from catastrophe.
  • Cross-chain message verification needs minimum standards. The Kelp exploit exploited "flexible modular security" — a configuration that allowed a single message source to mint tokens. Cross-chain bridges should require multiple independent verifiers by default, not as an option.
  • Human threat modelling is now mandatory. Security audits cover code. They do not cover the 6-month LinkedIn and Telegram grooming campaign that preceded the Drift drain. Protocol teams need dedicated OPSEC for key personnel.
  • Aave survived as a case study. Aave's code worked correctly — it accepted valid-looking collateral and issued loans against it. The protocol's risk team is now reviewing oracle dependencies and collateral acceptance criteria. That review process is the right response: systematic, not panicked.

Longevity Protocol

The 8-Marker Blood Panel — Specific Tests to Request

From this week's High Performance Founder: the exact test names to request at your next doctor's appointment. Request explicitly: HbA1c, Fasting Insulin, HOMA-IR (calculated), hs-CRP, Triglycerides, HDL Cholesterol, ApoB, and 25-hydroxyvitamin D. Most doctors will not order Fasting Insulin, HOMA-IR, or ApoB unprompted. Bring the list by name. Retest Vitamin D after 90 days of supplementation to calibrate dose. This is the minimum viable longevity panel — each marker predicts disease 5–15 years before symptoms appear.

Kevin Warsh's confirmation hearing for Fed Chair opened April 21 with a disclosure that stopped the crypto Twitter feed: Warsh holds Solana and Polymarket among his personal assets. Morgan Stanley's Bitcoin ETF just pulled $100M in its first week. Charles Schwab is weeks away from launching retail BTC and ETH spot trading. Regulation

Post-Quantum Cryptography Becoming Urgent for Bitcoin

Bitcoin BIP 361 — "Post-Quantum Migration and Legacy Signature Sunset" — has been published. Zcash simultaneously released its post-quantum cryptography roadmap. Both documents reflect a growing recognition that quantum computing is moving from theoretical to engineering risk on a 5–10 year timeline. BIP 361 proposes a migration path for Bitcoin's signature scheme. The "Legacy Signature Sunset" framing is notable: it implies that at some future point, addresses secured by ECDSA — the current standard — would be considered insecure. The practical implication for large holders: the upgrade timeline matters and warrants monitoring, even if it's still years away.

Work / Performance

The 20% Capturing 75% of AI's Economic Gains

PwC's 2026 AI Performance Study found that three-quarters of AI's economic gains are being captured by just 20% of companies — and the distinguishing variable is not AI investment size but strategic intent. The leaders are using AI for growth, not just cost reduction. They are deploying AI in customer-facing and revenue-generating contexts rather than back-office efficiency. For individuals: the equivalent bifurcation is already visible in knowledge work. People using AI to extend their creative and analytical range are compounding faster than those using it to automate existing tasks. The gap between these groups is widening every quarter.

Mind / Philosophy

The Longer Arc of Stoic Practice

Holiday's observation about Meditations points toward a longer question worth sitting with: if the private writing we leave behind shows only our struggles, what does that mean for how we document our own lives? Most people's journals are records of problems, anxieties, and unresolved questions — not their joy, their relationships, or their moments of ease. This isn't a bug; it's why we write. But it suggests that the full picture of a life requires more than retrospection. The practice Marcus actually lived — beginning with gratitude, working toward virtue in daily friction — was more joyful than the book that survived him suggests.
