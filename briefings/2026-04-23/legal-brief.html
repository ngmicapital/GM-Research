CLARITY Act Senate Markup Imminent as Stablecoin Yield Compromise Reaches 99%

⚡ What this means: The CLARITY Act would permanently define which digital assets are "commodities" (CFTC-regulated, like BTC/ETH) vs "securities" (SEC-regulated), and set rules for stablecoins including whether they can pay interest. The last major sticking point — stablecoin yield — is nearly resolved: passive interest on holding stablecoins is banned; activity-tied rewards are permitted.
"We'd love to have reactions and everything else. It's not a rule as such but obviously we need to know how it [is] functioning." — SEC Chair Paul Atkins, on CLARITY Act market structure provisions submitted to OIRA [paraphrased — PYMNTS, April 7, 2026]

Senate Banking Committee Chairman Tim Scott is targeting a late-April markup of the CLARITY Act, with the stablecoin yield dispute 99% resolved. Under the emerging compromise, passive yield on simply holding USDC or USDT is prohibited; activity-based rewards tied to payments, transfers, or platform transactions are permitted. SEC, CFTC, and Treasury receive 12 months to define "activity-based" through formal rulemaking.

The White House Council of Economic Advisers published a 21-page analysis: a full yield ban saves banks $2.1 billion in displaced lending but costs consumers $800 million net. Coinbase and Stripe objected to the yield text; ABA and ICBA support it. XRP's commodity status would be permanently codified alongside 15 other assets including BTC, ETH, SOL, and ADA. Treasury Secretary Bessent urged passage with "robust developer protections" in a Wall Street Journal op-ed (April 8). A confirmed markup date moves markets.

📎 Historical Precedent
The Commodity Futures Modernization Act of 2000 drew the same commodity/security line for derivatives — the resulting definitional ambiguity generated decades of regulatory arbitrage and ultimately required Dodd-Frank to clean up.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis
The yield compromise directly disadvantages USDC — Circle's revenue model depends on yield-bearing appeal to institutional holders. RLUSD and payment-focused stablecoins benefit. The 12-month rulemaking clock for "activity-based rewards" means no legal clarity until 2027 at earliest, with litigation extending further. Compliance officers must begin mapping which existing reward programs qualify under the most conservative reading now — the interim ambiguity carries real enforcement risk.
🔄 Contrarian Take
The yield compromise is a political deal, not a legal one. "Activity-based" remains undefined in bill text — 12 months of rulemaking followed by 24 months of litigation means firms building stablecoin yield products on the compromise assumption may be building on sand. The real risk is Congress passes the bill, the definition fails notice-and-comment, and courts find the active/passive distinction unworkable.
🏗️ Industry Implications
Coinbase wins on market structure — CLARITY Act ends SEC's ability to reclassify BTC/ETH/XRP as securities, removing existential regulatory risk. Circle faces structural revenue compression from the yield ban, explaining why Coinbase and Ripple are reportedly bidding to acquire Circle. Traditional banks (ABA/ICBA) win if the yield ban holds — it prevents stablecoin competition for deposit balances.
📊 Portfolio Impact
Bullish: BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP (permanent commodity codification); Coinbase (COIN). Bearish: USDC/Circle (yield revenue compression); smaller stablecoin issuers. Neutral: DeFi protocols — 12-month rulemaking lag before any real clarity.
📋 What To Do

CounselDraft client memos now on the yield prohibition vs activity-based distinction; the 12-month rulemaking window creates compliance ambiguity you can help clients navigate before competitors do.
ComplianceMap any stablecoin yield or rewards programs against the active/passive distinction before markup finalizes — programs rewarding users for simply holding should be restructured to activity-linked triggers now.
FounderRestructure stablecoin reward features to tie payouts to platform activity (payments, transfers, volume) rather than passive holding — the ban is coming regardless of final language.

FCA Raids Eight Illegal Peer-to-Peer Crypto Trading Hubs in Coordinated London Operation

⚡ What this means: P2P crypto trading means individuals or businesses buying and selling crypto directly outside registered exchanges. In the UK, facilitating this commercially requires FCA registration — but zero P2P crypto traders hold that registration, making all commercial P2P activity illegal. The FCA just escalated from warnings to coordinated physical raids with criminal investigators.
"Unregistered peer-to-peer crypto traders operating in the U.K. are doing so illegally and pose a financial crime risk." — Steve Smart, FCA Executive Director of Enforcement (CoinDesk, April 22, 2026)

The FCA executed its first coordinated crackdown on illegal peer-to-peer crypto trading on April 22, raiding eight locations across London in a joint operation with HMRC and the South West Regional Organised Crime Unit (SWROCU). Cease-and-desist notices were issued at all eight sites; evidence was gathered for criminal investigations. Zero registered P2P crypto traders exist in the UK — commercial P2P activity has been uniformly illegal throughout the registration regime's operation.

Users of unregistered platforms have no access to the Financial Ombudsman Service or compensation schemes. The operation marks a shift from intelligence-gathering to active physical disruption of illegal crypto market infrastructure. HMRC's involvement signals a parallel financial crime and tax enforcement angle beyond registration violations alone.

📎 Historical Precedent
FCA's 2021–22 crackdown on illegal forex bucket shops followed the same pattern — intelligence phase, then coordinated physical raids once evidence was compiled. The FCA banned 144 firms and pursued criminal charges against six individuals.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis
This is the first enforcement test of the UK crypto registration regime against P2P operators. SWROCU involvement elevates this to organized crime territory under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002. Any firm facilitating informal crypto exchange, OTC desk, or P2P matching without FCA registration now faces criminal exposure — not just civil fines. The timing is deliberate: the FCA is signaling enforcement intent immediately ahead of the CP26/13 authorization regime, ensuring the market understands that pre-authorization operation is illegal, not merely irregular.
🔄 Contrarian Take
The zero registration rate for P2P operators isn't because they're all criminals — the FCA registration pathway for P2P models is genuinely unclear under current rules. Physical raids may push activity to Telegram OTC groups and decentralized protocols, making flows harder to monitor rather than eliminating them. The FCA may be winning this battle while making the surveillance problem harder.
🏗️ Industry Implications
Regulated UK exchanges (eToro, Kraken UK, Coinbase UK) benefit from forced consolidation of liquidity to licensed venues. Informal OTC desks serving UK clients from offshore jurisdictions now face increased scrutiny as HMRC tracks cross-border money flows. Chainalysis and TRM Labs will likely see increased FCA demand for blockchain intelligence to support ongoing criminal investigations.
📊 Portfolio Impact
Bullish: Regulated UK exchanges (liquidity consolidation); blockchain forensics firms (Chainalysis, TRM Labs). Bearish: Unregistered OTC/P2P operators; any platform facilitating UK P2P without registration. Neutral: DeFi protocols — not directly targeted.
📋 What To Do
TraderVerify any UK OTC or P2P desks you use at the FCA Financial Services Register — unregistered operators are now known enforcement targets and you have zero recourse if they are raided.
CounselReview whether any client operations touch informal crypto matching or P2P facilitation in the UK — the FCA's "commercial P2P facilitation" threshold is undefined and these raids show a broad enforcement interpretation.
ComplianceAudit any informal trading facilitation, OTC desks, or B2B crypto exchange services touching UK users against the FCA registration requirement — enforcement is live, not theoretical.

FinCEN and OFAC Propose Bank-Level AML and Sanctions Rules for US Stablecoin Issuers — Comment Deadline June 9

⚡ What this means: The GENIUS Act (enacted 2025) created a federal licensing regime for stablecoin issuers called "Permitted Payment Stablecoin Issuers" (PPSIs). Now FinCEN and OFAC are proposing the actual compliance rules — treating stablecoin companies like banks for anti-money laundering and sanctions screening for the first time under federal law. An NPRM is a formal draft rule; the June 9 comment deadline is the only opportunity to shape the final outcome before it binds the industry.
"The proposed rules would extend Bank Secrecy Act obligations to Permitted Payment Stablecoin Issuers, requiring formal sanctions compliance programs for the first time in the sector." — US Treasury press release SB0435 [paraphrased]

FinCEN and OFAC published a Notice of Proposed Rulemaking in the Federal Register (Vol. 91, No. 69, April 10, 2026) that would treat PPSIs as financial institutions under the Bank Secrecy Act. Requirements include full AML/CFT programs, suspicious activity reporting, and — for the first time under federal law — mandatory sanctions compliance programs for crypto issuers. FDIC separately approved a parallel April 7 rulemaking for FDIC-supervised stablecoin issuers. Comment deadline: June 9, 2026.

The NPRM's most consequential structural element: primary market interactions (PPSIs directly engaging with users) carry full BSA/OFAC obligations; secondary market interactions (smart-contract-only) face a lighter-touch framework. This creates differentiated compliance architecture for CeFi and DeFi engagement with stablecoins.

📎 Historical Precedent
FinCEN's 2013 guidance (FIN-2013-G001) first applied BSA to crypto money transmitters — this NPRM is the full formalization of that approach for GENIUS Act-licensed issuers, with mandatory OFAC sanctions compliance added for the first time.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis
The primary/secondary market distinction is the most consequential structural element. It creates a moat: CeFi issuers (Circle, Paxos) with direct user relationships face the full compliance architecture; DeFi protocols accessing stablecoins only via smart contracts get lighter-touch treatment. This design favors licensed incumbents over decentralized competitors. The June 9 comment deadline is the last lever to shape this framework — specifically the definition of "primary market interaction" and whether programmatic API interfaces qualify. Missing this deadline forfeits the opportunity to influence the most significant stablecoin compliance document since the GENIUS Act itself.
🔄 Contrarian Take
The primary/secondary distinction is legally fragile. Any stablecoin issuer whose contracts are callable by any smart contract wallet effectively has both primary and secondary exposure simultaneously. Regulators may collapse the distinction in the final rule, eliminating the DeFi carve-out. Firms building compliance architecture on the assumption of secondary market relief may face a rude awakening at finalization.
🏗️ Industry Implications
Circle and Paxos face the highest compliance burden — bank-level AML programs, dedicated compliance officers, real-time OFAC screening — but gain the highest regulatory moat against unregistered competition. Smaller stablecoin issuers unable to absorb these costs will exit or be acquired. Tether (USDT) remains outside US jurisdiction and avoids PPSI classification, maintaining structural advantage in offshore markets and DeFi pools.
📊 Portfolio Impact
Bullish: Circle, Paxos (compliance moat vs unregistered competition once final rule lands). Bearish: Smaller stablecoin issuers; DeFi protocols with ambiguous primary/secondary market classification. Neutral: Tether/USDT — operates outside US jurisdiction.
📋 What To Do
TraderCompliance costs will consolidate the stablecoin market — USDC and Paxos-backed stablecoins emerge structurally stronger; watch for smaller issuer exit announcements over the next 12 months as costs bite.
CounselFile comments by June 9 — the primary/secondary market distinction is the critical battleground and your clients' business models depend on how narrowly or broadly "primary market interaction" is defined.
ComplianceBegin gap analysis against proposed BSA/OFAC requirements now — PPSI designation creates immediate BSA exposure even before the final rule, and robust AML programs take 6–12 months to build correctly.
FounderIf issuing or planning to issue a stablecoin, PPSI compliance architecture is mandatory reading now — your primary vs secondary market interaction model determines your entire compliance cost structure.

Binance CCO in Exit Talks Amid Senior Compliance Departures and Internal Iran Sanctions Concerns

Multiple senior compliance staff are departing, and internal investigators who flagged Iran sanctions violations were reportedly dismissed afterward. Breaching a plea deal's monitorship obligations can trigger immediate DOJ prosecution of company executives — this is not a minor HR story.
"Changes are normal personnel flow and performance management" and "compliance commitment remains intact." — Binance spokesperson [paraphrased; company disputes Bloomberg's characterization of a compliance exodus]

Bloomberg reported April 6 that CCO Noah Perlman is in departure discussions with no successor identified. Named departures include Peter Van Logtenstein (global investigations unit lead) and Inga Petrauskaite (financial crime investigations team lead). More critically: internal compliance investigators who flagged potential Iran sanctions violations in February 2026 were subsequently dismissed — sources described this as retaliatory, a claim Binance denies.

Binance's 2023 guilty plea to violating the Bank Secrecy Act required compliance function rebuild under monitorship. The combination of CCO departure, senior team exits, and apparent retaliation against sanctions whistleblowers creates direct exposure to monitorship breach findings and potential DOJ re-engagement under the plea's breach provisions.

📎 Historical Precedent

⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

If the monitor concludes that the CCO departure and Iran-investigator dismissals reflect systemic backsliding, Binance faces plea breach findings — potentially triggering individual executive criminal charges under 18 U.S.C. § 1956 rather than company-level fines. 🟢 High — enacted legislation (Royal Assent April 8), official ASIC roadmap published April 20, 2026

ASIC Releases 18-Month Digital Assets Licensing Roadmap Following Royal Assent of DAF Act

⚡ What this means: Australia's Corporations Amendment (Digital Assets Framework) Act 2026 is now law — it requires crypto exchanges and custody providers to hold an Australian Financial Services Licence (AFSL). ASIC published the transition roadmap on April 20. Critical near-term risk: the existing regulatory shelter (ASIC INFO 225 no-action position) expires June 2026, creating a roughly 10-month compliance gap before the new AFSL regime formally starts April 2027.
ASIC outlined a three-phase roadmap: Phase 1 (Apr–Oct 2026) — industry roundtables and consultation; Phase 2 (Oct 2026–Apr 2027) — regulatory guide published, standards finalized; Phase 3 (Apr 2027 onward) — license applications accepted, full supervision begins. [paraphrased from ASIC.gov.au, April 20, 2026]

ASIC published its 18-month implementation roadmap on April 20, following the Corporations Amendment (Digital Assets Framework) Act 2026 receiving Royal Assent on April 8. The Act introduces two new AFSL-regulated financial product categories: "digital asset platforms" (DAPs) and "tokenised custody platforms" (TCPs). Full implementation commences April 9, 2027. Penalties: up to the greater of AUD $16.5 million, 3x benefit obtained, or 10% of annual turnover. Exemption: platforms holding less than AUD $5,000 per customer with under $10 million in annual transactions.

The most urgent near-term issue: ASIC's INFO 225 class no-action position — which currently shields unregistered platforms from enforcement — expires June 2026, creating a 10-month period without regulatory shelter before the AFSL regime begins. ASIC has not yet confirmed whether it will extend INFO 225 or issue transitional guidance. Offshore platforms serving Australian retail clients must urgently assess AFSL exposure under the Act's territorial scope.

📎 Historical Precedent
The FCA's crypto registration regime (launched 2021) saw over 90% of initial applicants rejected across 36 months — Australia is attempting a faster track via structured pre-consultation rather than a sudden application gate.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis
The June 2026 INFO 225 expiry is the key near-term risk. Any platform without a credible path to AFSL by April 2027 faces enforcement exposure in the interim 10-month gap — ASIC has not indicated it will extend the no-action position. The AFSL "efficient, honest, and fair" conduct standard combined with unfair contract term prohibitions imposes substantive obligations beyond registration. Offshore platforms serving Australian retail clients must assess AFSL exposure under the Act's broad territorial scope now, not after INFO 225 expires.
🏗️ Industry Implications
Australian domestic exchanges (Independent Reserve, BTC Markets, Swyftx) face the highest near-term compliance investment but gain regulatory certainty. Offshore platforms serving Australian retail must urgently assess AFSL exposure. Traditional financial institutions (ANZ, NAB) with tokenization programs benefit from new TCP product classification. The government's cited AUD $24 billion market opportunity signals premium regulatory intent — this is not a box-ticking exercise.

MEDIUM FCA · BCLP · Skadden PRIMARY UK REGULATION · PERIMETER

FCA CP26/13 Defines UK Crypto Authorization Perimeter — Seven Activities Regulated, Staking Split by Function

⚡ What this means: The FCA has published detailed guidance explaining exactly which crypto activities require FCA authorization under the UK's new regime (launching October 2027). The "perimeter" is the legal line between activities needing a license and those that don't. Technical staking (running validator nodes) doesn't require authorization; commercial staking-as-a-service does. The comment window closes June 3 — six weeks away.
"The FCA is consulting on guidance on the UK's future crypto regime, covering seven regulated activities including qualifying stablecoin issuance, safeguarding, trading platforms, dealing as principal and agent, and arranging staking." — FCA press release, April 15, 2026 [paraphrased]

The FCA published CP26/13 on April 15, proposing amendments to the Perimeter Guidance Manual (PERG) under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) Regulations 2026 (enacted February 2026). Seven regulated activities are addressed: qualifying stablecoin issuance; cryptoasset safeguarding/custody; operating a trading platform; dealing as principal; dealing as agent; arranging deals; and arranging staking. Technical staking services — operating validator nodes without user interaction — remain out of scope.

CP26/13 runs alongside CP26/4 (FCA Handbook application for crypto activities) and GC26/2 (Consumer Duty applied to crypto firms). The authorization gateway opens September 30, 2026; full regulation takes effect October 25, 2027. Comment deadline: June 3, 2026.

📎 Historical Precedent
The FCA's Project Innovate (2014) showed early engagement with perimeter guidance substantially improves authorization success rates — firms that wait for final rules face 6–12 month delays in the authorization queue and higher rejection rates.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis
The technical staking exclusion is the most consequential element for institutional infrastructure players — validator operators who don't interact directly with users avoid authorization. But "arranging staking" pulls in any commercial staking delegation or liquid staking token offering. EigenLayer operators and Lido with UK user bases need careful analysis against the PERG decision trees now. The Consumer Duty consultation (GC26/2) running in parallel adds retail conduct obligations on top of authorization requirements — the combined compliance burden by October 2027 is substantial, and firms engaging now in CP26/13 will shape the final PERG decision trees to their advantage.
🏗️ Industry Implications
UK-authorized platforms (eToro, Coinbase UK, Kraken UK) benefit from perimeter clarity — they know precisely which service lines require authorization. Staking infrastructure companies (EigenLayer, Lido operators) face new "arranging staking" exposure if they have UK user bases and need immediate legal analysis. Consumer Duty (GC26/2) running in parallel adds outcome-monitoring and fair treatment obligations across all regulated activities.
