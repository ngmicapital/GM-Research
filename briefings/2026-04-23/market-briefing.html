📚 Learn Something: AI Inference vs Training
Training is the upfront, compute-intensive process of building the model — Anthropic, OpenAI, or Google run millions of GPU-hours over weeks to learn from data. Inference is every single subsequent request: a user asks ChatGPT a question, the trained model runs forward once to answer. For investors, the economics diverge sharply. Training is lumpy, capital-heavy, and concentrated in a handful of frontier labs. Inference scales with every marginal user and every marginal token consumed — it's the recurring, annuity-like revenue stream. That's why custom ASICs (Google TPU, Amazon Trainium2/Inferentia, Microsoft Maia) target inference specifically: the workload is stable and predictable enough to justify purpose-built silicon, and the volumes make the unit economics of custom design pay off. NVDA dominates training on flexibility and ecosystem moat, but faces the most competitive pressure on inference — because that's where 10x the chips will ship long-term. When an analyst says "AI revenue is shifting from training to inference," they're telling you NVDA's market share at the margin is under attack even while total TAM grows.
The Mag 7 earnings week setup is binary: if MSFT/META/AMZN confirm $40B+ annualised AI capex each, semis grind higher and NVDA prints another breakout. If any of them guides cautious, the entire AI complex gets repriced. Own NVDA + TSM through the print, add AVGO/MRVL for inference diversification.
