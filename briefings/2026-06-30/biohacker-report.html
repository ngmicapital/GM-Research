Driving Thesis

A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis of 23 studies (1,074 non-diabetic participants across 11 countries) confirms CGM significantly reduces mean blood glucose in prediabetics (SMD −0.54, p=0.03) but fails to move BMI meaningfully (SMD −0.25, p=0.19) in any subgroup without an accompanying structured behavioral intervention — reframing the device as a biofeedback amplifier, not a passive weight-loss tool. Concurrently, a 2025 mechanistic review in the Journal of Oral Microbiology establishes that oral microbiome dysbiosis — specifically the enrichment of Porphyromonas gingivalis with age — triggers SASP (senescence-associated secretory phenotype) in immune dendritic cells, creating a systemic inflammatory loop that accelerates biological aging; a Nature Communications scoring model links each unit of oral biological-age gap to a 5% increase in all-cause mortality and a 5% increase in physical decline risk.

On the pharmacological frontier, the PEARL trial (rapamycin 5 mg/week, healthy adults 65–85, 48 weeks, double-blind RCT) confirmed immune function improvement with acceptable safety — but no human trial has yet demonstrated lifespan extension. Section 01
Longevity & Performance Science

Two distinct lines of research converged this cycle around a shared theme: the body's second-order feedback loops are more informative than first-order metrics. Continuous glucose monitoring gives glycaemic context that blood draws cannot — but its true signal is strongest where dysfunction already exists (prediabetes), not in optimized normals. Meanwhile, the oral cavity is emerging as an aging-clock organ: the composition of mouth bacteria now generates a biological-age score that predicts all-cause mortality as reliably as established biomarkers. The mechanism is SASP — a secretory cascade from senescent cells that oral pathogens can both trigger and amplify systemically. Both findings redirect practitioner focus from passive tracking to targeted intervention thresholds.

CGM in non-diabetics: biofeedback tool, not weight-loss device
Meta-analysis
Liao et al. · European Journal of Medical Research · 2026

A systematic review of 23 studies (7 RCTs, 16 observational/interventional designs) enrolling 1,074 non-diabetic participants from 11 countries found CGM significantly lowered mean blood glucose versus controls (SMD −0.54, 95% CI not reported, p=0.03), driven almost entirely by prediabetic subgroups. In healthy normoglycaemic participants, CGM demonstrated high feasibility in modifying short-term dietary behaviour but produced no significant reduction in BMI (SMD −0.25, p=0.19). The authors conclude CGM functions as a precision biofeedback amplifier — most effective when embedded in structured lifestyle programs rather than used as a standalone device. Behavioural adherence improved across all subgroups, suggesting the real-time feedback loop has motivational value even when the glycaemic benefit is marginal.

Mechanism: CGM surfaces postprandial glucose spikes invisible to HbA1c, enabling individuals to adjust food sequencing, exercise timing, and carbohydrate load — but without a concurrent intervention protocol, awareness alone does not translate to metabolic change in those already normoglycaemic.

Practical takeaway: If fasting glucose is below 5.6 mmol/L and HbA1c is below 5.7%, a 2–4 week CGM trial is most useful for identifying specific glucose-spiking foods rather than as an ongoing monitoring tool. In prediabetes (fasting 5.6–6.9 mmol/L), CGM-guided intervention has statistically meaningful glycaemic impact.

Oral microbiome as biological-age clock: the SASP loop
Observational/Cohort
Yue et al. · Journal of Oral Microbiology · 2025; updated 2026 SASP axis review

A 2025 review established that the oral microbiome changes in a predictable direction with age: Porphyromonas gingivalis enrichment, decline of protective commensals (Neisseria, Haemophilus), and — critically — induction of premature senescence in dendritic cells, impairing antigen-presenting function. The 2026 SASP-axis review builds on this: P. gingivalis activates the SASP cascade, releasing IL-6, IL-8, and MMP-family cytokines that propagate systemic low-grade inflammation (inflammaging). A Nature Communications scoring model using oral microbiome composition to estimate biological age found that a one-unit increase in the oral age gap (biological older than chronological) correlates with a 5% increase in all-cause mortality and a 5% increase in physical decline risk.

Practical takeaway: Mediterranean and plant-based diets reduce P. gingivalis abundance; oral probiotics (Lactobacillus reuteri, L. salivarius) significantly attenuate systemic inflammation caused by oral dysbiosis. Periodontal treatment reduces Alzheimer's disease-associated inflammatory burden in observational data.

⚡ Implication: CGM is now evidence-stratified: deploy it aggressively in prediabetes, use it diagnostically in normoglycaemics. The oral microbiome data makes a strong case for treating dental hygiene as a longevity intervention, not a cosmetic one — P. gingivalis is directly linked to neurodegeneration, cardiovascular disease, and accelerated cellular senescence via the SASP pathway.
Section 02
Training & Performance

Rapamycin is the most replicated life-extension compound in animal research — extending lifespan 10–25% in mice — yet its translation to humans remains incomplete. The PEARL trial is the most rigorous human data to date, and its findings are cautiously encouraging rather than definitive. The key mechanistic action — mTOR inhibition reducing cellular senescence markers — is now confirmed in human immune tissue, but lifespan extension as a primary endpoint has not been tested.

Rapamycin PEARL trial: immune benefit confirmed, longevity endpoint pending
Human RCT
Mannick et al. · Science Translational Medicine · 2024; Geroevidence summary 2026

The PEARL trial (Participatory Evaluation of Aging with Rapamycin for Longevity) enrolled healthy adults aged 65–85 in a 48-week, double-blind, placebo-controlled design using 5 mg/week low-dose rapamycin. The primary outcome — immune function markers — improved significantly versus placebo, with no serious adverse events attributed to the drug. A 2026 Aging Cell paper found low-dose rapamycin significantly reduced p21, a canonical marker of cellular senescence, in immune cells from older adults — providing a plausible mechanistic bridge between mTOR inhibition and slower biological aging. Three ongoing trials (PEARL-2, ERAP, TRITON) are expected to report through 2027–2029 with larger samples and harder endpoints.

Mechanism: mTOR (mechanistic target of rapamycin) is a master regulator of cellular growth, protein synthesis, and autophagy. Inhibiting it mimics caloric restriction signalling — reducing cellular debris accumulation, slowing senescence progression, and improving immune cell function in older adults. The trade-off: chronic rapamycin can impair mTORC2, raising concerns about metabolic side effects and immunosuppression at higher doses.

Current evidence tier: Moved from “Emerging” to “Moderate” in March 2026. Human lifespan extension: not demonstrated. Human immune and cellular-senescence benefit: demonstrated in one RCT. The gap between the animal data and human evidence remains the key uncertainty.

Senolytics: dasatinib + quercetin (D+Q) — where the human data stands in 2026
Human RCT
Geroevidence 2026 — Senolytic evidence review

Senolytics (drugs that selectively eliminate senescent cells) are the complementary approach to rapamycin's senescence-suppression. The D+Q combination has been tested in small human trials for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, diabetic kidney disease, and frailty — showing reductions in circulating senescence biomarkers (p16, p21, IL-6) in tissues. No large-scale longevity RCT exists. The 2026 evidence picture: mechanism-confirmed in humans, clinical-outcomes-pending. For a practitioner-level biohacker, intermittent D+Q protocols (3 days on, 1–3 months off) represent the highest-plausibility senolytic intervention currently available outside a trial.

⚡ Implication: If the PEARL-2 trial (expected 2027–2028) replicates immune benefit at the 5 mg/week dose with extended follow-up, rapamycin will become the most evidence-backed longevity pharmacological tool available to healthy adults. Until then, the risk/benefit calculation favours caution in the absence of a supervising physician — mTOR inhibition affects wound healing, infection response, and metabolic regulation.
Section 03
Supplements & Nutrition

The therapeutic peptide space in 2026 is characterised by a wide evidence gap between compounds with genuine FDA-approved phase 3 RCT data and the frequently self-administered grey-market peptides. The Frontiers in Aging 2026 review (PMC13095733) provides the most current hierarchy. The headline finding: GHK-Cu plasma levels decline measurably with age (200 ng/mL at 20 → 80 ng/mL at 60), BPC-157 human trials cap at n=58, and TB-500 has only Phase I safety data. Separately, a 2025 meta-analysis of DHEA supplementation in postmenopausal women (21 RCTs) quantifies a modest but real testosterone-raising effect.

Therapeutic peptides — 2026 evidence hierarchy (Frontiers in Aging review)
Narrative Review
Frontiers in Aging 2026 — Therapeutic peptides in gerontology: mechanisms and applications

A comprehensive 2026 review maps nine peptides across a four-tier evidence hierarchy. Tier 1 (FDA-approved, phase 3 data): Tirzepatide (dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist; HbA1c −2.4%, weight loss 15–21% at 72 weeks in >7,700 participants) and bremelanotide (melanocortin agonist). Tier 2 (small Russian clinical trials, animal data): Epitalon (telomerase activator; 12–24% lifespan extension in rodents; small retinitis pigmentosa trials) and Semax (BDNF +1.4-fold; stroke recovery data from Russian trials, no independent Western RCTs). Tier 3 (pilot studies only): BPC-157 (VEGFR2 activation; 58% sustained pain relief in knee pain, n=58 maximum; WADA-banned; no long-term efficacy RCT), CJC-1295 (GH 2–10× increase for 6+ days; Phase 2 discontinued after participant death). Tier 4 (in vitro/animal only): GHK-Cu (modulates 31% of human genes in vitro; plasma levels decline age 20→60 from 200 to 80 ng/mL; no human clinical trials), TB-500 (Phase I safety only), Ipamorelin (preclinical + limited human).

Practical read: GLP-1/GIP agonists like tirzepatide are the only longevity-adjacent peptides with hard human outcome data. The grey-market peptide stack (BPC-157, TB-500, GHK-Cu) operates almost entirely on preclinical mechanism — the risk/benefit calculation is genuinely unknown because no long-term safety data exist.

DHEA supplementation in aging women: meta-analysis of 21 RCTs
Meta-analysis
Diabetology & Metabolic Syndrome 2025 — DHEA meta-analysis, postmenopausal women, 21 RCTs

A 2025 meta-analysis of 21 RCTs evaluated DHEA supplementation's impact on serum testosterone and estradiol in postmenopausal women. Results supported a modest but statistically meaningful increase in testosterone concentrations (specific magnitude varies by dose and duration in subgroup analyses). DHEA, as the primary precursor to both androgens and estrogens, declines continuously after age 30 in both sexes. Anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties are well-characterised; cardiac aging attenuation is proposed in mechanistic research. The meta-analysis does not establish clinical outcomes (cognition, muscle mass, mortality) as primary endpoints — these remain underpowered in current literature.

⚠ Evidence reality check: BPC-157 & TB-500

Both compounds are among the most widely self-administered grey-market peptides in biohacking communities in 2026. The honest evidence picture: BPC-157's largest human trial enrolled 58 participants with no control group or placebo in the pain studies; TB-500 has only Phase I safety data. Both are WADA-banned. The mechanistic rationale (angiogenesis, tissue remodelling, gut-barrier repair) is legitimate and preclinically supported — the clinical translation has not been tested at adequate scale. Proceed with full awareness that personal experimentation here is genuinely early-stage.

⚡ Implication: GHK-Cu's measurable age-related plasma decline (200 → 80 ng/mL) and gene-regulatory breadth (modulates 31% of human genes in vitro) makes it one of the more scientifically coherent peptide candidates — but “interesting preclinical target” and “evidence-based intervention” are not the same thing. If exploring peptides, tirzepatide/GLP-1 pathway is currently the only one with hard human outcome data. For everything else: mechanism understood, risk profile unknown.
