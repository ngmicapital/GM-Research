CFTC Staff Let Bitcoin, Ether and Stablecoins Count Toward Derivatives Firms’ Operating Reserves

“Pleased to see CFTC staff continue to provide important clarity around crypto assets in US derivatives markets”Michael Selig, CFTC Chair — post on X, 23 Nov 2026 (as reported by The Block)

Staff at the Commodity Futures Trading Commission issued an advisory on 23 November that allows swap execution facilities, clearing organisations and designated contract markets to count bitcoin, ether and payment stablecoins toward the 12-month operating-cost requirement. Each asset takes a haircut before it counts.

The advisory creates no new legal obligations and is not enforceable. It also leaves crypto outside the separate liquidity test, because the CFTC does not treat these assets as cash or highly liquid securities under GAAP.

📎 Historical Precedent: CFTC staff letter 26-30 (5 Nov 2026) declined to recommend enforcement against a clearinghouse that settles through a trust company, another staff-level accommodation of the same kind.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Staff are widening the eligible collateral set step by step, from the trust-company settlement letter to this advisory. The Brief’s read: the haircut and the liquidity carve-out mean firms still need cash or Treasuries to pass the liquidity test, so the advisory eases the capital arithmetic at the margin rather than replacing it. A staff advisory can be withdrawn by the next staff, so codification by the Commission is the signal to watch.

🔄 Contrarian Take

The strongest counter is that a non-enforceable staff view gives a registrant no safe harbour if an examiner reads the haircut differently, and a firm that counts volatile assets toward a year of costs could be short in a drawdown. The CFTC’s own GAAP note, as reported by The Block, cuts the same way. The Brief’s read: the risk is real, but the haircut is the built-in control, so the test should be treated as a ceiling on crypto rather than a floor.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Coinbase, whose National Trust Charter took effect on 6 November, and crypto-native clearinghouses and exchanges gain the most, since they can hold their own crypto treasuries against reserve tests. Traditional FCMs holding cash-only reserves gain nothing from the change. The Brief’s read: expect firms to disclose reserve composition more explicitly in their next filings.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟩 Bullish COIN — a trust-charter timeline plus a clearer reserve path for its derivatives business.

🔴 Bearish Cash-only FCM balance sheets — the capital-rule edge they held over crypto-holding peers narrows.

⚪ Neutral BTC / ETH — haircut-discounted and excluded from liquidity, so direct price impact is limited.

CFTC Reportedly Finalises Event-Contract Reporting Rule, but Sources Disagree on Its Status

[paraphrased] The CFTC’s final rule sets up a reporting framework for certain event contracts.CFTC, as reported by The Block, 23 Nov 2026

The Block reported on Monday that the agency also published a final rule creating a reporting framework for certain event contracts. A KuCoin digest dated 24 November instead says final prediction-market rules were sent to the White House for review. [sources conflict] Both could be true if they cover different rules, but neither has been checked against the Federal Register.

The rule lands during a wider federal push against state limits. On 5 November the CFTC read designated contract markets’ impartial-access duties as requiring removal of domestic geographic barriers, and the federal government sued Louisiana and Arkansas to block state enforcement against CFTC-regulated exchanges. A KuCoin digest also reports CFTC chair Michael Selig saying on-chain platforms must register or partner with licensed exchanges to enter the US market; that remark is unverified.

📎 Historical Precedent: the CFTC’s 5 Nov 2026 interpretation of DCM impartial-access obligations, the closest prior step, which this rule would build on.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

The Commission is shifting from suing individual states toward rule-based and interpretive control of DCM access. Kelman Law’s Michael Handelsman notes that the 5 November document is interpretive guidance rather than a statute, and that any enforcement turns on the facts. The Brief’s read: a reporting rule is a disclosure regime and will not resolve federal preemption by itself; that question still sits with the courts.

🔄 Contrarian Take

The strongest counter is that states will argue a Commission interpretation cannot displace state gaming law until a court says so. The Brief’s read: the Commission is building a record, but until a court rules, state enforcement stays live and the guidance binds no one.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Coinbase, Kalshi, Payward, DRW, Jump Trading and Paradigm filed a proposed amicus brief supporting the CFTC on 9 November, so the capital is already aligned on the federal side. The Brief’s read: state-licensed sportsbook operators such as DraftKings face the mirror-image risk if federally accessible event contracts expand.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟩 Bullish COIN — backing the CFTC on DCM access gives it upside if federal preemption holds.

🔴 Bearish DKNG — state-licensed sportsbooks face competition from federally accessible event contracts.

⚪ Neutral Prediction-market platforms — the rule is reporting-only, so near-term operating effect is limited.

📋 What To Do — Before 1 July
TraderHold off on new event-contract positions in states that have active enforcement until the publication status is confirmed.
CounselPull the Federal Register entry for the event-contract rule and check whether it covers the same contract categories as the 5 November interpretation.
ComplianceLog each state enforcement action against your listed contracts and tag the ones the federal interpretation may affect.

South Korea Readies Digital Asset Basic Act With Exchange Licensing and a Shareholder Cap

[paraphrased] The Financial Services Commission will discuss the Digital Asset Basic Act before releasing its main provisions.Financial Services Commission, as reported by Yonhap and crypto.news, 22–23 Nov 2026

South Korea’s Financial Services Commission is set to take up the Digital Asset Basic Act through its Virtual Asset Committee, with a 3pm session on 24 November ahead of public release of the main provisions. Reports indicate the government draft is due for publication later this week.

The draft is expected to replace the current notification system with a licensing regime for crypto businesses, and to build a bank-centred issuance model for local stablecoins. The most contested element is a reported cap of 15% to 20% on major exchange shareholders’ stakes, aimed at concentration risk. After release, the draft is due to merge with lawmakers’ proposals before National Assembly review.

📎 Historical Precedent: the Virtual Asset User Protection Act, in force since 2024, was Korea’s first major crypto statute; this act would be the second.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Seoul is moving from notification to licensing, and a bank channel for won stablecoins pulls issuance toward incumbents. The Brief’s read: the shareholder cap is the clause most likely to be diluted in the National Assembly, while the tiered licensing is likely to survive. Domestic exchanges with concentrated holders will lobby hardest.

🔄 Contrarian Take

The strongest counter is that a stake cap could push foreign capital away from Korean venues and send listings offshore. The Brief’s read: the risk is real, but the bill is still a draft; the final cap level, rather than its existence, will decide the capital outcome.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Domestic exchange operators face the cap directly, while bank groups gain the won-stablecoin issuance channel if the bank-centred model survives. The Brief’s read: KB Financial and Shinhan are the natural beneficiaries of a bank-led issuance model, though no analyst note has priced this yet.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟩 Bullish 105560.KS (KB Financial) — bank-led won stablecoin issuance favours incumbents.

🔴 Bearish Concentrated-ownership Korean exchange operators — cap risk to control premiums.

⚪ Neutral BTC — a Korean regulatory headline with limited global price impact.

ECB Floats Replacing MiCA’s Bank-Deposit Floor for Stablecoin Reserves With Liquidity Rules

“minimum deposit requirements for stablecoins could be removed”European Central Bank, Financial Stability Review, 18 Nov 2026 — as quoted by Coindaro (p.78) [verbatim per secondary reading]

The ECB’s 18 November Financial Stability Review argues that MiCA’s minimum bank-deposit share for stablecoin reserves, 30% or 60% for significant tokens, could be swapped for liquidity requirements. The review is a proposal rather than a rule, and it welcomes the Commission’s planned MiCA review.

USDC’s reserves, liquidity and redemption arrangements came under direct EBA oversight from 12 November, and ESMA’s remediation deadline for non-MiCA stablecoins falls in early January 2027. Together these put reserve design under pressure from two directions.

📎 Historical Precedent: the MiCA e-money token regime, applicable since 30 June 2024, introduced the deposit floor now under review.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

A move from prescriptive deposit shares to liquidity tests would loosen bank-concentration rules but tighten what counts as liquid. The Brief’s read: issuers with bank-heavy reserves lose the compliance moat, while those holding short-dated government paper gain. The proposal still needs Commission text and a Parliament vote.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Circle, which appears among the authorised issuers on the ESMA interim register, stands to gain most from a liquidity-based test. Issuer counts differ: 25 per the interim register as of 2 November and 26 per CASP Tracker as of 18 November [sources conflict]. The Brief’s read: the count gap is a register-timing issue, not a dispute over substance.
