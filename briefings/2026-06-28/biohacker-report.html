Driving Thesis

A January 2026 Nature Metabolism RCT (65 healthy adults, 14 days) provided the first direct head-to-head comparison of NMN, NR, and nicotinamide: both NMN and NR approximately doubled circulating NAD+ levels while nicotinamide showed only a transient 4-hour spike. The mechanism is gut-mediated — bacteria convert both precursors to nicotinic acid, which drives sustained NAD+ elevation, and both precursors also raised short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) via microbiome modulation, adding a secondary anti-inflammatory signal. Nicotinamide, despite being cheaper and widely used, does neither.

On the structural side: a 30-RCT Frontiers meta-analysis (527 subjects, published 2026) found partial cold-water immersion (chest-down or lower-limb only) matches whole-body immersion for CK reduction (g = −0.24) and DOMS relief (g = −0.40) at 24h post-exercise — removing the logistical barrier of full submersion. Separately, an Oxford Nutrition Reviews meta-analysis of 23 RCTs (1,280 adults) quantified 16:8 time-restricted eating: fasting glucose drops modestly (SMD −0.25), HOMA-IR improves (SMD −0.16), HDL rises (SMD +0.15), but HbA1c is unchanged overall. Effects are meaningfully larger in males, physically active individuals, and protocols exceeding 6 months.

Section 01
Longevity & Performance Science

The dominant mechanistic story this cycle is NAD+ precursor routing via the gut microbiome. A January 2026 Nature Metabolism RCT settled a long-standing debate: NMN and NR both double circulating NAD+ not by direct cellular uptake but by bacterial conversion to nicotinic acid in the gut — a pathway nicotinamide (Nam) cannot access. This also explains why NMN and NR raise short-chain fatty acids and Nam does not: the precursors are reshaping gut ecology en route, not just topping up a cofactor. The implication for practitioners is that gut health is not a separate concern from NAD+ supplementation; they are the same system.

NMN and NR both double NAD+ via gut conversion — nicotinamide does neither Human RCT
Nature Metabolism · January 2026 · Yoshino et al.

65 healthy adults (mean age 34.7) received 1g NMN, 1g NR, or 0.5g Nam daily for 14 days in a randomised, placebo-controlled design — the first direct three-way human comparison. NMN and NR each produced ~2× NAD+ elevation sustained across the dosing period. Nam produced only an acute 4-hour spike with no sustained increase. Mechanistically, gut bacteria converted NMN and NR to nicotinic acid (NA), which drives robust NAD+ biosynthesis via the Preiss-Handler pathway. Both active precursors also elevated SCFAs — particularly via expansion of Enterocloster aldensis — a gut barrier and anti-inflammatory signal absent in the Nam arm. Practical dose confirmed: 1g/day NMN or NR; Nam cannot substitute at any dose tested.

NR accelerates NAD+ recovery and symptom resolution in long-COVID Human RCT
PMC · 2026 · Long-COVID NR RCT

A separate 2026 RCT tested NR supplementation in post-COVID patients with persistent fatigue and cognitive symptoms. NAD+ levels in participants were significantly depressed at baseline versus healthy controls. NR supplementation restored NAD+ toward normal ranges and was associated with statistically significant improvements in cognitive symptom scores and fatigue indices versus placebo. Effect on acute COVID illness was not examined — this was a recovery-phase intervention only. Notably reinforces the gut-to-NAD+ mechanism: participants with worse baseline gut dysbiosis showed attenuated response.

⚡ Implication: If you are running NMN or NR, the gut-conversion finding validates the supplement but also flags a dependency: poor gut health attenuates the response. Prioritising prebiotic fibre and avoiding antibiotics around supplementation cycles is no longer a soft recommendation — it is mechanistically justified. Nam-only products (often sold as "flush-free niacin" alternatives) do not replicate this pathway at standard doses.
Section 02
Training & Performance

Two structural updates this cycle for training practitioners: the optimal cold-water immersion protocol is now defined with body-region precision, and the interference-with-adaptation signal from CWI post-hypertrophy training is confirmed at effect-size level. These findings change how you sequence recovery modalities across a PPL week.

Partial cold-water immersion equals whole-body for DOMS and CK — and avoids explosive-power suppression Meta-analysis
Frontiers in Sports and Active Living · 2026 · Systematic review, 30 RCTs, n=527

This 2026 meta-analysis pooled 30 RCTs (527 subjects, age range 15.6–50.3 years) comparing whole-body versus partial immersion (lower-limb or chest-down) protocols across CK, DOMS, counter-movement jump (CMJ) height, and maximal strength. Key findings: CK reduction: g = −0.24 (p < 0.01); DOMS alleviation: g = −0.40 (p < 0.01); no statistically significant difference between whole-body and partial immersion on either marker. However, immediate post-exercise CWI significantly suppressed explosive power (CMJ: g = −0.94 for partial immersion immediately post-session), confirming the blunting effect on power output if bathing follows power training without a delay. The recommended protocol: 11–15°C, 10–15 min, targeting the trained limb or lower body, administered ≥2h post-session when explosive output the following day matters. Optimal timing for DOMS benefit is at 24h post-exercise, not immediately.

Integrate now
  • Partial CWI (chest-down or lower-limb bath) after leg day: logistically feasible, equally effective as full-body immersion
  • Temperature sweet spot 11–15°C — sufficient for CK/DOMS effect without cold-shock risk
  • Delay ≥2h post-session if training that day had explosive/power demands
  • Most effective at 24h post: scheduling a CWI session the morning after a hard lower-body day is mechanistically justified
Caveats
  • Maximal strength recovery: CWI showed no advantage over passive rest (g not significant)
  • Immediate post-hypertrophy CWI still likely attenuates anabolic signalling (consistent with prior literature not captured in this meta)
  • Female representation minimal (32 of 527 subjects) — apply findings cautiously across sexes
  • Effect sizes are small-to-moderate; CWI is a recovery aid, not a recovery solution
⚡ Implication: The practical unlock is operational: you do not need a full cold plunge tank or ice bath. A lower-limb soak in a wheelie bin or bathtub at 11–15°C for 12 minutes, scheduled the morning after a hard leg session, delivers the statistically confirmed effect. Skip CWI immediately after a sprint or Olympic-lift session — you will pay for it in next-day jump height.
Section 03
Supplements & Nutrition
16:8 time-restricted eating: real but modest glucose and HDL benefit, dose-response matters Meta-analysis
Nutrition Reviews · Oxford Academic · 2026 · 23 RCTs, n=1,280

The largest meta-analysis of 16:8-specific TRE to date pooled 23 RCTs covering 1,280 adults. Findings: fasting glucose SMD −0.25 (p=0.004); HOMA-IR SMD −0.16 (p=0.03); fasting insulin SMD −0.22 (p=0.04); HDL SMD +0.15 (p=0.04). HbA1c did not reach significance overall. Effect sizes are small but statistically robust across the pooled dataset. Three moderators amplify the signal: male sex (larger effect on glucose vs. females); physically active participants (HOMA-IR benefit ~2× larger); protocol duration >6 months (glucose benefit compounds). The HDL signal is particularly noteworthy — this is a hard cardiometabolic endpoint, not a surrogate. Meta-analysis

NAD+ precursor timing and gut-priming stack Mechanistic

Based on the January 2026 Nature Metabolism mechanism (gut bacteria → nicotinic acid → NAD+), a practical refinement emerges for NMN/NR dosing: morning administration on an empty stomach maximises gut-bacterial exposure time before a meal displaces transit kinetics. The SCFA co-benefit (gut barrier support) suggests co-timing with a prebiotic substrate — 5–10g inulin or resistant starch — amplifies the ecology shift. This is mechanistic reasoning from the established gut-conversion pathway, not a direct RCT of this co-stack. Mechanistic

⚠ Hype check: NAD+ IV infusions

A May 2026 NPR investigation found that NAD+ IV infusion clinics are charging $200–$1,000 per session with no published RCT evidence that IV delivery confers greater benefit than oral NMN/NR at 1g/day. The Nature Metabolism trial's oral protocol achieves ~2× NAD+ elevation — the same outcome IV clinics claim, at a fraction of the cost. Until a head-to-head IV vs. oral RCT exists, IV NAD+ is a premium with no confirmed premium benefit. Expert Review

Add partial CWI protocol to PPL recovery — leg-day morning-after soak
Equipment: wheelie bin or bathtub, 30–40L cold water (tap water ~15–18°C in winter, ice cubes to reach 11–15°C if needed), 12 minutes lower-body submersion. Schedule: morning after leg day (≥12h post-session). Avoid immediately post-session on power/explosive days. Track subjective DOMS (1–10) across 4 cycles to confirm personal response. Signal to escalate: no DOMS improvement after 4 weeks → check water temperature (must be <15°C).

Oxford meta-analysis shows the glucose and HOMA-IR signal compounds past the 6-month mark in active males. If you are already running 16:8 TRE, the data suggests staying the course rather than cycling off — the benefit appears cumulative, not plateau-limited at standard durations. Track fasting glucose quarterly as a personal signal. Combine with resistance training for the ~2× HOMA-IR amplification effect identified in the subgroup analysis.

Monitor: omega-3 / TBI interaction signal — EPA in repeat head-impact athletes
A 2026 Medical University of South Carolina study flagged EPA (the primary omega-3 in fish oil) as potentially interfering with vascular repair in individuals with repeated mild traumatic brain injuries — finding weakened blood vessel stability and disrupted healing signals. This is a single study and does not apply to most users. Watch for replication. Relevant population: contact sport athletes, those with concussion history. Signal to act: two independent replications → reassess high-dose EPA supplementation in that cohort specifically.

Carried
Track SCFA proxy markers if running NMN/NR — gut microbiome response window
The NMN/NR gut-conversion mechanism suggests short-chain fatty acid production is a co-benefit within the first 14 days of supplementation. Proxy signals for SCFA elevation: reduced post-meal bloating, improved stool consistency, reduced systemic inflammatory markers (hs-CRP if you track bloodwork). Not a hard biomarker — SCFA testing is not commercially accessible — but these proxies flag whether your gut is converting the precursor as the trial population did. Low-signal response at 30 days → revisit gut health fundamentals before escalating dose.
