01 Key Ideas & Insights
Newsletter · High Performance Founder

Eating as a Hormone Training System — Not a Willpower Problem

Dan Go (High Performance Founder) published a comprehensive breakdown of 20 evidence-backed nutrition heuristics this week. The underlying insight is more interesting than the list: most people frame diet as a willpower problem, but the science frames it as a hormone and signal-timing problem. The goal is to train your hunger hormones — ghrelin, GLP-1, leptin — not to override them.

"A predictable [meal] schedule trains your hunger hormones, so you stop fighting random cravings and start running on autopilot." — Dan Go

Three findings from this newsletter that aren't obvious:

  • The 40-chew study: Chewing each bite 40 times (vs 15) reduced calorie intake by ~12% in a controlled study, while also lowering ghrelin (hunger hormone) and increasing GLP-1 and CCK (satiety hormones). The mechanism is mechanical — more surface area contact triggers more gut signalling before food even reaches the stomach.
  • Hara hachi bu (the Okinawan 80% rule): Eating to 80% full and stopping is a documented practice in one of the world's longest-lived populations. The 20-minute satiety delay (the time for leptin to reach the brain) makes eating to 100% a systematic overshoot. The rule functions as a timing correction, not a calorie calculation.
  • Pre-meal protein loading: Drinking a protein shake 20–30 minutes before eating reduces appetite at the subsequent meal while contributing to daily protein targets. A two-for-one efficiency gain that solves both under-eating protein and over-eating at meals simultaneously.

Crypto's Infrastructure Phase: What Jamie Dimon's Warning Actually Signals

BitGo's weekly Market Pulse (covering March 29 – April 5) frames the current market moment as a structural transition — from "speculative tolerance to infrastructural integration." The signal set they point to: Charles Schwab launching spot BTC/ETH trading in Q2 2026 (managing $11.9T across 46M accounts), Bitcoin ETFs recording $1.3B in net inflows in March (breaking a four-month outflow streak), and Jamie Dimon telling JPMorgan to accelerate its blockchain initiatives because tokenization and stablecoins are "direct competitors to traditional banking."

"Jamie Dimon's warning that crypto rails threaten core banking revenue is less a concession than a signal that incumbent finance is taking the threat seriously." — BitGo Market Pulse

Dimon's move is the most analytically interesting one here. He's not saying crypto is good or bad — he's saying his bank will lose fee income on payments and asset management if they don't build on the same rails. The institutional infrastructure phase doesn't require a price rally to accelerate. It requires the threat of margin compression on existing products.

02 Strategy & Practice
Framework

Constraint-Based Systems vs. Willpower-Based Systems

The most durable personal operating systems eliminate decisions rather than optimising decisions. Dan Go's nutrition framework is a good illustration of this principle in action — almost all 20 of his heuristics work by removing the decision point, not by making the right decision easier:

  • Eat at fixed times → you never decide when to eat; the schedule decides
  • Stop eating 3–5 hours before bed → a binary rule, not a judgment call about what to eat late
  • 80/20 diet ratio → removes the guilt/restriction binary; 20% flex is pre-approved, so no willpower spent on it
  • Use smaller plates → environmental architecture does the work; no decision needed at the table

The same logic applies to trading, work routines, and any high-frequency decision context. The question isn't "how do I make the right choice more often?" — it's "which choices can I automate so I reserve decision capacity for the ones that actually require it?"

Market Strategy

Building When Others Are Watching the Chart

The BitGo newsletter closes with a line worth keeping: "The best time to build is when others are watching the chart." The context was crypto infrastructure, but the principle generalises. The companies that will define any emergent sector tend to be the ones that commit to infrastructure during the uncertainty phase — before the market has resolved whether the technology will persist. Schwab's Q2 spot BTC/ETH launch was probably decided 12–18 months ago, during a period of significant ETF outflows and macro headwinds. By the time the environment looked unambiguously good, the position would have been more expensive and more crowded.

"The foundation isn't being debated anymore, it's being built, quarter by quarter, regardless of price." — BitGo Market Pulse, April 8 2026
03 Tools & Resources
🥛
Supplement

Psyllium Husk — Satiety + LDL Reduction

Dan Go's most underrated recommendation. Psyllium husk (a soluble plant fiber) slows glucose absorption, feeds gut microbiome, and has been shown in studies to reduce LDL cholesterol by up to 25% at therapeutic doses. Cheap, widely available, and integrates into a morning routine as a teaspoon in yogurt or water. If LDL is rising and you want a first-line intervention before statins, this is worth trialling for 21 days with a lipid panel before and after.

🍵
Sleep Protocol

Sleep Tea Stack: Chamomile + Glycine + Electrolytes

The mechanism breakdown on this is worth knowing: chamomile contains apigenin, which binds GABA receptors to promote relaxation; glycine lowers core body temperature (the primary driver of deep sleep onset); electrolytes help cells retain water through the night, reducing the need to wake for urination. Each component addresses a different sleep disruption pathway. Timing: 2 hours before bed. This is a combinable stack, not an either/or.

🧬
Breakfast Protocol

3-Minute High-Protein Breakfast Formula

250g low-fat Greek yogurt + 1 scoop whey protein + 1 tsp psyllium husk + 100g frozen fruit. Output: ~300 calories, ~40–50g protein, ~10g fiber. The combination hits gut health (yogurt), fast-digesting muscle protein (whey), blood sugar regulation (psyllium), and micronutrients (fruit) in a single preparation that takes under 3 minutes. Prep the night before to remove the morning decision entirely.

Research Resource

BCG: "AI Will Reshape More Jobs Than It Replaces" (2026)

BCG's April 2026 report argues the dominant AI employment effect is task-level restructuring rather than role elimination. The practical implication for founders and solo operators: the leverage point is identifying which tasks within your role can be AI-augmented first, rather than waiting for a defined "AI role" to appear. The highest-leverage question is not "will AI replace me?" but "which 20% of my current tasks, if AI-assisted, would unlock the other 80%?"

Regulation

CLARITY Act Stablecoin Markup — Late April 2026

Revised CLARITY Act stablecoin yield language remains unreleased after industry and Senate staff meetings failed to produce a public draft. Banking Committee markup is still targeted for late April. The sticking point is whether interest-bearing stablecoins qualify as securities — a question with enormous implications for DeFi yield products. If resolved in favour of the industry's preferred framing, the stablecoin market could expand significantly into savings-adjacent products.

Work & AI

The Enterprise AI Transition — From Individual to Organisational Productivity

MIT Sloan's 2026 AI action items for decision-makers note that organisations have mostly taken an individual-level approach to generative AI (give each employee a tool, measure individual output). The next phase — enterprise-oriented AI use cases like product development facilitation or customer experience enrichment — is where the compound returns are. For founders: the question isn't "am I using AI personally?" but "have I redesigned any business process around AI?" Those are different interventions.

Finance

Drift Protocol $285M Exploit — DeFi Security Baseline Reset

Drift Protocol suffered a $285M exploit on Solana this past week — one of the largest DeFi breaches in recent history. The attack used social engineering over many months, including a $1M deposit to build trust before draining liquidity. The implication: DeFi security is still fundamentally an operational security problem, not just a smart contract audit problem. Multi-sig governance with social engineering vulnerability is the current attack surface to watch.
