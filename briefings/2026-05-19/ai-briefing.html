Predictive Maps of Multi-Agent Reasoning — successor-representation spectrum (May 12)

Research arXiv 2605.11453 Published: May 12, 2026 Method: spectral analysis of agent graphs

New for Issue #51 per arXiv cs.AI current listing: "Predictive Maps of Multi-Agent Reasoning: A Successor-Representation Spectrum for LLM Communication Topologies" published May 12, 2026. The paper addresses how practitioners deploying multi-agent LLM systems must choose between communication topologies (chain, star, mesh, and richer variants) and introduces a structural diagnostic based on successor representations from reinforcement-learning theory. The contribution is spectral: the paper connects eigenvalue spectra of agent-communication graphs to distinct failure modes (cascading hallucination in chain topologies, central-point-of-failure in star topologies, consensus drift in mesh topologies).

⚡ Why it matters in 6 months: The briefings-site stack is already a multi-agent system in practice — the orchestrator (Claude Code session) plus the spawned subagents (Explore, Plan, gsd-debugger, etc.) plus the MCP-tooled subagents (truenorth, coingecko, dune). The paper's diagnostic framework is one of the first to give multi-agent practitioners a quantitative tool for choosing topology — choose star for clear-ownership tasks, mesh for consensus-tasks, chain for sequential refinement. For dc's stack specifically, the orchestrator-spawn pattern is closer to star than mesh; the paper's read is that star topologies are most vulnerable to central-point-of-failure, which validates the discipline of writing self-contained subagent prompts (the orchestrator's instructions to subagent must survive even if the orchestrator's reasoning is wrong). The implications are practical, not theoretical.
