Watch Today: Any Iranian government response to Trump’s call-off. Any resumption of vessel transit data through Hormuz. Gulf state diplomatic activity. WTI futures in Asian trading (opened below $103) — if they hold there or fall further, the inflation relief trade accelerates.

Status: April’s meeting minutes drop Wednesday. This was Powell’s final meeting as Chair and the first with Warsh’s dissent patterns visible in the record. Markets will parse for: (1) Whether rate hike language appeared explicitly; (2) How the committee characterised energy CPI pass-through; (3) Any “looking through” language for transitory inflation.

Iran Context: The minutes were written when WTI was already above $100. The call-off news post-dates the minutes — meaning they will read more hawkish than current conditions warrant. Markets should expect a hawkish document but not trade it as a forward indicator given the oil price shift since April.

SocGen View: Rajappa noted Warsh’s structural conflict — he championed lower rates but the energy environment forces higher-for-longer. That conflict will be visible in the April minutes for the first time.
🚨 TRUMP–XI AFTERMATH — TARIFFS PERSIST
Status: The Beijing summit’s economic deliverables remain limited: 200 Boeing orders placed, trade councils formed, no semiconductor deal, no tariff reduction. Watch: Post-summit follow-up trade negotiations; any NVDA China commentary Wednesday.
The Iran call-off is the best geopolitical news in months but it is not a resolution — Trump has reversed course before, and Iran’s response to “serious negotiations” language is unknown. The fundamental structure — Hormuz still disrupted, IEA warning on inventory buffers, tariffs intact — has not changed. What has changed is the probability distribution: a diplomatic off-ramp now exists that was absent over the weekend. That is worth a 95-point ASX open and a 3% oil relief bid, but not a “crisis over” positioning.
