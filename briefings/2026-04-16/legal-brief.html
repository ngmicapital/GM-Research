CLARITY Act Markup Window Narrows — No Vote Scheduled for April 20 Week Despite White House Optimism

"Many issues that once felt unsolvable between negotiating camps have since been resolved quietly behind the scenes — including stronger protections against illicit finance in DeFi and other governance elements."
— Patrick Witt, Executive Director, White House Crypto Council, April 13, 2026 [paraphrased from CoinDesk]

The Senate returned from Easter recess on April 13 amid cautious optimism over the CLARITY Act (H.R. 3633 / Digital Asset Market Clarity Act of 2025), but the Senate Banking Committee's schedule for the week of April 20 contains no markup date — a procedural gap that narrows the window for passage before midterm-cycle paralysis sets in. Chairman Tim Scott has not announced a markup date. White House crypto adviser Patrick Witt, appearing at the Solana Summit, told attendees that outstanding sticking points have been resolved, specifically citing DeFi illicit-finance protections and Democratic demands that senior government officials — including President Trump — be barred from profiting from the crypto sector.

Senator Thom Tillis plans to release revised stablecoin yield draft language this week, a pre-markup technical step. The stablecoin yield compromise — prohibiting passive yield while permitting activity-based rewards tied to genuine payments and transfers — is holding, removing the obstacle that stalled the bill twice earlier in 2026. Coinbase CEO Brian Armstrong reversed his opposition, eliminating the largest remaining industry-side blocker. Ripple CEO Brad Garlinghouse now expects the bill to pass by end of May.

Senator Bernie Moreno has stated publicly that if the bill does not reach the Senate floor by May, it effectively dies for 2026, bleeding into the midterm cycle. After a successful committee markup, the bill still needs a full Senate floor vote at 60 votes, reconciliation with the Senate Agriculture Committee version, a House reconciliation conference, and presidential signature — four procedural steps that require weeks under the best conditions.

📎 Historical Precedent: The GENIUS Act passed the Senate on June 17, 2025 with 68 votes after a similar last-minute yield compromise; CLARITY is structurally more complex and has fewer weeks of runway.
⚖️ PRACTITIONER ANALYSIS

The CLARITY Act is the commodity-vs-security taxonomy legislation that operationalises the March 17 SEC-CFTC joint interpretation — without it, the interpretation lacks statutory force and can be reversed by the next administration. The bill's "functional decentralization" test for classifying tokens as digital commodities under CFTC jurisdiction is the specific provision that matters most for practitioners: a token that crosses the decentralisation threshold loses SEC jurisdiction permanently. The April 20 scheduling gap is a yellow flag, not a red flag — Senate Majority Leader Thune controls floor time and can schedule a markup at 48 hours notice. Who wins if it passes: Coinbase, Ripple, and Solana-ecosystem projects whose token status remains legally uncertain. Who loses: plaintiffs' lawyers holding active securities claims against token issuers.

🔄 CONTRARIAN TAKE

The "cautious optimism" narrative has preceded every prior CLARITY Act collapse. Four additional procedural steps after markup — each requiring Senate floor time, agriculture committee reconciliation, and a 60-vote threshold — means May passage requires a level of legislative discipline the 119th Congress has not yet demonstrated. The bill may pass, but June or July is more realistic than May.

🏗️ INDUSTRY IMPLICATIONS

Coinbase and Ripple gain the most regulatory certainty upon enactment: Coinbase's exchange business and Ripple's RLUSD stablecoin issuer position both rest on the commodity-vs-security line the bill draws. Circle benefits less directly — its stablecoin yield model is constrained by the passive-yield prohibition regardless. For institutional allocators in tokenised RWA products structured on Solana, Avalanche, or XRP Ledger, the bill's passage removes the residual securities-law overhang that currently increases legal-opinion costs for every issuance.

Bearish [USDC yield products, CEX yield accounts]: Passive yield prohibition eliminates a major retail acquisition channel for Circle and Coinbase Earn. Neutral [BTC, ETH]: Already classified; minimal additional upside from CLARITY passage.

Japan Reclassifies Crypto Under FIEA — Insider-Trading Bans, 10-Year Penalties, 20% Tax Aligned With Equities

"The cabinet-approved amendment elevates cryptocurrencies from payment instruments to financial instruments on par with stocks and bonds, with full securities-law conduct rules, mandatory annual disclosures, and insider-trading prohibitions effective from fiscal year 2027."
— FSA bill summary, April 10, 2026 [paraphrased; source: CoinDesk, CryptoBriefing]

Japan's cabinet approved an amendment to the Financial Instruments and Exchange Act (FIEA) on April 10, 2026, formally reclassifying crypto assets as financial instruments — a legal upgrade from the Payment Services Act (PSA) framework governing the sector since 2017. The bill, advanced by the Financial Services Agency, now moves to the National Diet for debate and final passage, with implementation targeted for fiscal year 2027. Japan becomes the most consequential G7 jurisdiction to formally place crypto at parity with equities in its regulatory architecture.

Key provisions: mandatory annual disclosures by crypto issuers, prohibition on insider trading based on non-public information, prison terms rising from 3 to 10 years and fines from ¥3 million (≈$19,800) to ¥10 million (≈$66,000) for operating without FSA registration, and a flat 20% capital gains tax aligned with the rate applied to stock-market profits — replacing the current progressive income-tax treatment that can reach 55% for high earners. The 20% rate is a substantial tax cut for Japanese retail traders.

📎 Historical Precedent: The EU's 2004 Markets in Financial Instruments Directive (MiFID) similarly reclassified derivative instruments as financial products; the resulting compliance wave took 3–4 years to fully absorb across the industry.
⚖️ PRACTITIONER ANALYSIS

Japan's FIEA reclassification is not merely symbolic — it triggers the entire securities law toolkit: insider-trading surveillance, mandatory disclosure obligations for issuers, and conduct-of-business rules for intermediaries. The 20% tax cut creates a powerful retail re-entry signal; Japanese retail accounts represent roughly 15–20% of global spot BTC trading volume on peak days. For exchanges operating Japan-licensed entities (Coincheck, bitFlyer, GMO Coin, Binance Japan), the compliance rebuild is significant: surveillance systems, issuer-disclosure processing, and insider-information handling require infrastructure that PSA firms never needed. Exchanges that cannot demonstrate FIEA-compliant infrastructure before the Diet passes the final bill face the risk of conditional or suspended licences from FY2027.

🔄 CONTRARIAN TAKE

Reclassification as a financial instrument increases regulatory compliance cost and may deter the mid-size token issuers and DeFi projects that currently use Japan's permissive PSA white-list system. Japan's FSA has never administered an insider-trading case involving tokens; building that enforcement capacity may take years, creating a credibility gap between the law-on-paper and enforcement reality that sophisticated market participants will exploit.

🏗️ INDUSTRY IMPLICATIONS

Binance Japan, Coincheck, bitFlyer, and SBI VC Trade face the heaviest compliance rebuilds. The 20% tax rate is a meaningful unlock for Japanese retail, historically the world's most price-sensitive spot-trading cohort; expect a re-entry rally in JPY-denominated spot pairs when the Diet passes the final bill. Issuers of tokens distributed to Japanese residents face new mandatory-disclosure obligations: projects that currently rely on the PSA whitelist system without formal annual reporting must restructure their Japan offering documents before FY2027.

Bearish [PSA whitelist tokens not meeting FIEA standards]: Smaller tokens that cannot satisfy mandatory-disclosure requirements may be delisted by Japan-licensed exchanges ahead of FY2027. Neutral [US-listed crypto ETFs]: Japan's reclassification creates no direct ETF arbitrage.

Banking Lobby Eyes Lawsuit Over OCC Crypto Trust Charters — BPI Questions Rulemaking Process as 11 Firms Race for Licences

"The OCC's grant of national trust bank charters to crypto firms without notice-and-comment rulemaking bypasses the fundamental process protections that exist to ensure major regulatory changes are subject to public scrutiny."
— Bank Policy Institute, reported March 2026 [paraphrased; source: The Block, Banking Dive]

The Bank Policy Institute — whose board includes JPMorgan Chase CEO Jamie Dimon, Goldman Sachs chief David Solomon, and Bank of America CEO Brian Moynihan — is weighing a lawsuit against the OCC over OCC Interpretive Letter 1176, which expanded national trust bank charter eligibility to crypto and fintech firms. The legal theory is administrative: that the OCC made a major regulatory change without the notice-and-comment rulemaking required under the Administrative Procedure Act, citing the major questions doctrine following the Supreme Court's 2022 West Virginia v. EPA ruling.

Eleven companies received conditional approvals or filed applications within an 83-day window ending March 5 — including Ripple, Circle, BitGo, Paxos, Coinbase, Crypto.com, Fidelity Digital Assets, Bridge, Protego, Morgan Stanley, and Payoneer. Coinbase received its conditional approval on April 2; Crypto.com was approved February 23. Revolut dropped its plan to acquire a U.S. lender and instead filed a de novo OCC/FDIC charter application on March 5. No lawsuit has been filed as of April 16, but reporting indicates an April deadline may have forced the BPI to a decision point.

📎 Historical Precedent: The BPI and ABA successfully used APA litigation to invalidate the OCC's "fintech charter" in 2021 (Lacewell v. OCC, 2d Cir.) — the same legal toolkit is available here if the lawsuit proceeds.
⚖️ PRACTITIONER ANALYSIS

The BPI's APA argument has real teeth: the 2021 Lacewell precedent from the 2nd Circuit held that the OCC exceeded its chartering authority for "special purpose national banks" without proper rulemaking, and the current conservative Supreme Court's major questions doctrine makes expansive agency action more vulnerable to challenge. If a lawsuit is filed and a court grants a preliminary injunction, conditional approvals already issued could be suspended — a material risk for Ripple, Circle, and Coinbase, all of whom have begun operational planning around the charter. The core question for counsel is whether OCC Interpretive Letter 1176 constitutes "major rulemaking" requiring formal APA procedures, or merely a clarification of existing authority. The OCC will argue the latter; BPI will argue the former.

🔄 CONTRARIAN TAKE

The BPI may be using the lawsuit threat as a negotiating chip rather than a genuine litigation strategy — the same playbook used in 2018–2021. A Trump-era OCC under Rodney Hood is unlikely to reverse the charter programme voluntarily, but the threat alone may be enough to extract concessions on capital requirements or examination standards that level the playing field.

🏗️ INDUSTRY IMPLICATIONS

Coinbase, Ripple, and Circle are the most exposed to an injunction: each has publicly announced strategic plans dependent on the trust-bank charter (Coinbase custody expansion, Ripple RLUSD settlement infrastructure, Circle's direct Fed access ambition). Morgan Stanley's application signals that traditional finance sees the charter as essential infrastructure for tokenised-asset custody. A successful BPI lawsuit would delay the entire crypto-banking convergence by two to four years while the OCC re-initiates formal rulemaking.

📊 PORTFOLIO IMPACT

Bullish [COIN, XRP — on lawsuit dismissal]: Charter survival is structurally bullish for Coinbase and Ripple's institutional custody and settlement ambitions. Bearish [USDC, COIN — on injunction]: Circle's direct Fed account access and Coinbase's custodial expansion are both contingent on the charter surviving legal challenge. Neutral [BTC, ETH spot]: No direct charter-market price link.

White House AI Framework Urges Congress to Preempt State AI Laws — DOJ Litigation Task Force Activates as 19 State Bills Pass in 2026

"The Framework recommends that Congress establish a single, minimally burdensome national standard rather than fifty discordant ones — precluding states from regulating AI model development or imposing liability on AI developers for third-party use."
— White House National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence, March 20, 2026 [paraphrased from Framework text; source: whitehouse.gov, Ropes & Gray, WilmerHale]

The White House released its National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence on March 20, 2026 — a legislative recommendation document, not an executive order — urging Congress to preempt state AI laws that "impose undue burdens" on AI development. The Framework is the Trump administration's formal statement that a single federal standard is preferable to the growing patchwork of state laws, and it recommends against creating any new federal AI rulemaking body, instead directing that AI be governed through existing agencies with subject-matter expertise and industry-led standards.

The DOJ established its AI Litigation Task Force in January 2026 with sole responsibility to challenge state AI laws that unconstitutionally regulate interstate commerce, are preempted by existing federal regulations, or are "otherwise unlawful in the Attorney General's judgment." Since mid-March, 19 new AI laws have passed at the state level in 2026, with Utah and Washington now requiring latent disclosures in generative AI systems and Washington adding mandatory provenance-detection tools. The EU AI Act reaches full application on August 2, 2026 — simultaneous with US federal-state fragmentation entering its most active phase.

📎 Historical Precedent: The CAN-SPAM Act (2003) federally preempted state spam laws; its single-standard approach was industry-favoured but was criticised by consumer groups for setting a lower floor than some state laws had provided.
⚖️ PRACTITIONER ANALYSIS

The Framework's preemption recommendation is not self-executing — it requires Congressional action that has been consistently refused in the 119th Congress (the One Big Beautiful Bill Act and NDAA both rejected preemption language). But the DOJ AI Litigation Task Force does not need Congress: it can challenge already-enacted state laws through federal court injunctions. Practitioners should map clients' AI deployments against the 19 newly enacted state laws and the Washington provenance-disclosure requirement — the disclosure obligations and latent-disclosure mandates have compliance timelines running into late 2026 and are enforceable now, regardless of any federal preemption outcome. The EU AI Act's August 2 full-application date compounds the compliance calendar.

🔄 CONTRARIAN TAKE

Federal preemption of state AI laws plays directly into the hands of the largest AI incumbents — OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, Meta — who can absorb a single federal compliance standard more easily than a patchwork of 50. Smaller AI developers actually benefit from state-level enforcement because it fragments the compliance landscape in ways that create differentiated legal positions; federal preemption homogenises the market and favours the incumbents the Framework's supporters are nominally trying to compete against.

🏗️ INDUSTRY IMPLICATIONS

OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, and Meta are the primary beneficiaries of federal preemption — each has the legal infrastructure to manage one federal standard and lobbied for this outcome. For AI-integrated fintech products (trading bots, risk-scoring models, KYC automation), the Washington state provenance-disclosure requirement is the most immediately consequential obligation: any GenAI system deployed to Washington residents needs disclosure infrastructure in place now. The EU AI Act's August 2 full-application date means multi-jurisdictional AI compliance — EU plus fragmented US state law — is the live problem until Congress acts, which may not occur in 2026.

📊 PORTFOLIO IMPACT

Bullish [Large-cap AI — GOOGL, MSFT, META]: Federal preemption favours incumbents with deep compliance infrastructure. Bearish [EU-dependent AI companies]: August 2 full EU AI Act application creates a hard compliance cost for any AI business serving EU customers without a dedicated compliance programme. Neutral [Crypto protocols]: No direct market-structure link to AI governance framework.

MAS Expands Crypto Payment Sandbox — Real-Time Stablecoin Settlement for Cross-Border Trade Finance, $4.5B Target Volume

"MAS has added three new participants to its live regulatory sandbox enabling real-time stablecoin settlement for cross-border trade finance transactions, targeting up to $4.5 billion in annual transaction volume by end-2026."
— MAS regulatory sandbox update, April 2026 [paraphrased; source: CoinReporter, MAS.gov.sg]

The Monetary Authority of Singapore expanded its regulatory sandbox to include three additional participants enabling real-time stablecoin settlement for cross-border trade finance, with the expanded sandbox targeting $4.5 billion in annual transaction volume by end-2026. MAS separately confirmed it is ready to begin drafting stablecoin legislation and will issue tokenised government bills settled in central bank digital currency at some point in 2026. The sandbox operates under MAS's 2023 single-currency stablecoin (SCS) framework, which requires reserve composition, valuation, custody, and audit standards for any SGD or G10-currency-pegged stablecoin issued from Singapore.

📎 Historical Precedent: MAS's Project Ubin (2016–2021) pioneered CBDC-for-settlement in Asia; this sandbox is the next operational iteration, now focused on commercial stablecoin rather than central bank digital currency as the settlement asset.
⚖️ PRACTITIONER ANALYSIS

Singapore's stablecoin sandbox is the most operationally advanced cross-border settlement pilot in Asia, and MAS's intention to draft stablecoin legislation signals the framework will harden into formal law — making Singapore the first APAC jurisdiction with a complete statutory stablecoin regime after the US GENIUS Act. Firms seeking a regional stablecoin-issuer domicile for Asian cross-border payments should engage MAS's FinTech Office now: sandbox participation creates regulatory familiarity that materially shortens the subsequent licensing process. The $4.5B volume target is modest but credible; if achieved, it provides the proof-of-concept that accelerates MAS's legislative timeline.

🏗️ INDUSTRY IMPLICATIONS

Circle (USDC) and Ripple (RLUSD) are the primary beneficiaries of expanded Singapore stablecoin infrastructure: both hold MAS licences and both have product roadmaps oriented toward cross-border trade settlement in Asia. Tether's USDT has not sought MAS SCS designation and faces increasing competitive pressure from regulated alternatives in the region. For tokenised RWA platforms — Ondo, Securitize, rwa.xyz — Singapore's sandbox creates a tested settlement rail that reduces the operational friction of cross-border tokenised-asset transactions.
