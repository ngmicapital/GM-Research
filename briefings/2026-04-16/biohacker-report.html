Section 01
Longevity & Biomarkers
GLP-1 + Bimagrumab: The Body Recomposition Drug Combination — BELIEVE Trial

[Phase 2 RCT] Published in Nature Medicine (March 2026), the BELIEVE trial compared three arms: semaglutide alone, bimagrumab alone, and the combination. The combination group lost an average of 22.1% body weight — with 92.8% of that loss as fat mass, while lean mass was largely preserved. By comparison, semaglutide alone lost 15.7% body weight but only 71.8% as fat. Bimagrumab alone lost 10.8% — entirely from fat, with lean mass increasing 2.5%.

⚡ In Plain English

Bimagrumab is an antibody that blocks activin receptor signalling — the pathway that limits muscle growth and promotes fat storage. Blocking it while on semaglutide (a GLP-1 drug that suppresses appetite and accelerates fat burning) creates a metabolic environment where the body preferentially burns fat while protecting muscle. CRP (C-reactive protein) is a blood marker for systemic inflammation — an 83% drop is cardiovascular-significant.

⚠ Context

This is a Phase 2 trial — it shows strong signal but needs Phase 3 replication. Bimagrumab is not commercially available. Side effects included mild acne and muscle spasms in some participants. For now, the free version remains the same: progressive resistance training + adequate protein. But this trial sets the benchmark that future body recomposition drugs will need to beat.

⚡ Implication

If you are managing weight on GLP-1 drugs (or know someone who is), the evidence is now unambiguous: resistance training + 1.6g+/kg protein is the non-drug version of bimagrumab — it is the primary lever for preserving lean mass during fat loss. Without it, 30–40% of GLP-1 weight loss comes from muscle.

Epigenetic Pace Clocks: DunedinPACE Measures How Fast You Are Aging

[Mechanistic][Observational] Third-generation epigenetic clocks like DunedinPACE (Duke University) do not just estimate biological age — they measure the pace at which you are aging right now. A DunedinPACE score of 0.8 means you are aging at 80% of the average rate; 1.2 means 20% faster. This distinction matters enormously: two people with the same biological age may be aging at vastly different speeds, with very different longevity trajectories.

⚡ In Plain English

DNA methylation refers to chemical tags that attach to DNA and regulate which genes are active — they change predictably with age, forming the basis of epigenetic clocks. DunedinPACE analyses methylation patterns at specific sites to calculate how fast those patterns are changing — essentially measuring your rate of biological degradation, not just your current biological age snapshot. Exercise, sleep quality, and not smoking are the top three lifestyle factors that reduce your DunedinPACE score.

⚡ Implication

Services like TruAge and Elysium Index offer DunedinPACE testing from blood samples (~$200–$400 AUD). If you are doing multiple protocol experiments (adding sauna, adjusting sleep, changing training), this is now the most precise biomarker to track whether they are actually slowing your aging rate — not just changing a single blood marker.

Rapamycin PEARL Trial: Results Expected 2026–2027

[Phase 2 RCT] The PEARL trial — a double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 2 study evaluating 5mg/week rapamycin vs. placebo in healthy adults aged 50–85 — is approaching completion with results expected late 2026 or 2027. A 2026 review in Aging journal summarised the current state: mechanistic animal data is compelling, but human evidence remains limited and mixed, with some participants showing elevated blood lipids and concerns about muscle protein synthesis suppression from chronic mTOR inhibition.

⚡ In Plain English

Rapamycin (sirolimus) is an immunosuppressant drug originally used in organ transplants. It inhibits mTOR (mechanistic target of rapamycin) — a master regulator of cell growth and metabolism. In animals, blocking mTOR extends lifespan significantly. The concern for athletes: mTOR is also the primary pathway that drives muscle protein synthesis after training. Suppressing it chronically may blunt gains and reduce muscle maintenance — the opposite of what most biohackers want.

⚠ Watch, Do Not Act

Off-label rapamycin use is growing in longevity clinics, but the evidence base for healthy adults is still insufficient to justify the immunosuppression risk. Wait for PEARL trial results. If you train seriously, chronic mTOR inhibition is mechanistically at odds with hypertrophy goals.

Section 02
Recovery & Sleep
Cold Water Immersion: Timing Is Everything for Lifters

[Network Meta-Analysis] A 2025 Frontiers in Physiology network meta-analysis across 55 RCTs settled the optimal CWI protocol for muscle recovery: 5–15°C water for 10–15 minutes is the most effective dose for reducing DOMS (delayed onset muscle soreness) and restoring performance markers. Beyond that sweet spot, colder or longer does not add benefit.

The critical nuance for resistance trainers: a separate 2024 systematic review (Wiley, European Journal of Sport Science) confirmed that CWI performed within 4 hours of a resistance training session blunts the acute inflammatory and mTOR signalling required for hypertrophy adaptation. The same inflammatory cascade that makes you sore is also the signal your muscle needs to grow. Killing it with cold water suppresses that signal.

⚡ In Plain English

DOMS (Delayed Onset Muscle Soreness) is the muscle soreness that peaks 24–72 hours after training — caused by microscopic muscle fibre damage and the subsequent inflammatory repair response. mTOR signalling is the molecular "grow now" instruction that triggers muscle protein synthesis after training. Cold water suppresses both — which is great for reducing soreness and useful for rapid recovery between sessions, but counterproductive if your goal is maximum hypertrophy from that session.

✓ When CWI Helps
  • After cardio / Zone 2 sessions
  • In-season sports with back-to-back training days
  • >6 hours after resistance training
  • Managing acute soft tissue inflammation
  • Mental clarity and mood — evidence is positive
✗ When CWI Hurts
  • Within 4h of resistance training (blunts hypertrophy)
  • During active strength peaking blocks
  • When primary goal is maximum muscle growth
  • Immediately post-workout on heavy leg days
⚡ Implication

On PPL training days: skip the ice bath immediately post-session. On rest days or after Zone 2 cardio: 10–12 minutes at 10–14°C is the evidence-backed sweet spot for recovery and mood benefits. The morning cold shower is fine — it is brief enough not to suppress the overnight repair signalling.

Red Light Therapy Umbrella Review: What Actually Works

[Umbrella Review of RCTs] A 2026 umbrella review in PMC (PMC12326686) examined photobiomodulation across multiple systematic reviews of randomised controlled trials. Findings: 660nm (red) and 850nm (near-infrared) wavelengths have the most robust evidence for reducing DOMS, attenuating CK (creatine kinase — a muscle damage marker) elevation, and improving next-session performance. The mechanism is mitochondrial: these wavelengths are absorbed by cytochrome c oxidase, boosting ATP production and reducing oxidative stress in muscle tissue.

⚡ In Plain English

Photobiomodulation (PBM) uses specific wavelengths of red and near-infrared light to stimulate cellular energy production. Cytochrome c oxidase is an enzyme in the mitochondrial membrane that absorbs these wavelengths and uses the energy to accelerate ATP (cellular fuel) production. CK (creatine kinase) leaks out of damaged muscle fibres into the bloodstream — measuring it tells researchers how much muscle damage training caused. Lower post-session CK means less damage and faster recovery.

Key practical constraint flagged in the review: the evidence quality is significantly weakened by lack of protocol standardisation — most commercial devices do not disclose fluence (energy density delivered to tissue), making it impossible to compare studies or calibrate consumer panels. If buying a red light device, look for published irradiance specs (mW/cm²) and target 30–60 mW/cm² at skin surface.

⚠ Hype Watch

Red light therapy is real — but the consumer market is saturated with underpowered devices. A panel that does not specify its irradiance output is a red flag. The clinical dose is typically 4–6 J/cm² delivered to each muscle group. Most budget panels ($100–200) deliver 30–50% of the therapeutic dose seen in studies. Mid-range panels ($400+) from companies that publish full spec sheets are more likely to match research protocols.

⚡ Implication

Post-training red light (660nm + 850nm, 10–20 min per muscle group) is evidence-supported for recovery — and unlike CWI, it does not suppress the inflammatory signalling needed for hypertrophy. It is the recovery modality that stacks cleanly with resistance training on the same day.

Section 03
Nutrition & Fuel
Early Time-Restricted Eating Beats Late TRE — Circadian Alignment Is the Variable

[Network Meta-Analysis] A 2026 preprint network meta-analysis (medRxiv, January 2026) across RCTs comparing early TRE, midday TRE, late TRE, and standard caloric restriction found consistent rankings favouring early TRE — eating windows aligned to the morning (e.g., 7am–3pm or 8am–4pm) — for fasting insulin reduction, fat mass, and blood pressure, even without calorie restriction. A second 2026 Frontiers in Nutrition meta-analysis corroborated: early TRE significantly reduces systolic and diastolic blood pressure vs. unrestricted eating.

⚡ In Plain English

Your body processes glucose (blood sugar) differently at different times of day because of circadian rhythms — internal 24-hour biological clocks that regulate hormone release. Insulin sensitivity (how well your cells respond to insulin to clear blood sugar) peaks in the morning and declines through the afternoon into the evening. Eating your largest meals in the morning when insulin sensitivity is highest means less insulin is required, less fat is stored, and blood sugar returns to baseline faster. Eating a big meal at 9pm does the opposite.

Important caveat: a 2026 Science Translational Medicine study found that nearly isocaloric TRE (same total calories, just different timing) without genuine caloric restriction did not improve cardiometabolic markers in women with overweight — suggesting that for some populations, calorie reduction matters more than timing alone. The benefits of early TRE may be partly mediated by naturally reducing late-night eating (and thus total intake).

⚡ Implication

For body recomposition: shift your eating window earlier if possible — aim for first meal by 8am and last meal by 6pm. For training: this aligns well with morning or midday sessions. The evidence is clearest when early TRE is combined with resistance training — not as a replacement for calorie management, but as a circadian amplifier of it.

EPA vs DHA: Different Omega-3s, Different Performance Effects

[RCT] A 2025 Frontiers in Nutrition study (PMC12417169) compared six weeks of either EPA-rich or DHA-rich omega-3 supplementation in endurance-trained males. Key finding: EPA supplementation altered submaximal exercise physiology — improving cardiovascular efficiency and reducing the oxygen cost of a given workload. DHA supplementation did not produce equivalent exercise physiology changes. Both reduced inflammatory markers post-exercise (IL-6, TNF-α, CK).

⚡ In Plain English

EPA (Eicosapentaenoic Acid) and DHA (Docosahexaenoic Acid) are both omega-3 fatty acids found in fish oil — but they have different biological roles. EPA is primarily anti-inflammatory and cardiovascular. DHA is primarily structural, concentrated in brain and retinal tissue. Most fish oil supplements contain both, but the EPA:DHA ratio varies. Submaximal exercise means sustained effort below maximum capacity — like Zone 2 cardio — where cardiovascular efficiency improvements translate directly to better endurance performance and faster recovery.

Practical dosing from 2026 research consensus: 2–3g combined EPA + DHA daily, with EPA-dominant formulations (2:1 EPA:DHA ratio or higher) preferred for athletes focused on performance and cardiovascular adaptation. Fish oil (sardines, mackerel, salmon sources) is preferable to algae-based for EPA content. Take with a fatty meal for 3× better absorption.

⚡ Implication

If choosing a fish oil supplement, check the EPA:DHA ratio on the label. For athletic performance, favour EPA-dominant products (>1.5× more EPA than DHA). Standard 1g capsules typically contain only 180mg EPA + 120mg DHA — you need 10–15 capsules to hit 2–3g combined. Liquid fish oil (e.g., Carlson Labs) is more cost-effective for hitting therapeutic dose.

Biohacking Protocols
CGM for Non-Diabetics: What the 2026 Evidence Actually Shows

[Systematic Review] A 2026 systematic review in European Journal of Medical Research (Springer) examined CGM use in non-diabetic populations across observational and interventional studies. Summary: CGM shows genuine value as a behavioural biofeedback tool — helping users personalise meal timing, exercise timing, and food selection based on real-time glucose response data. However, CGM metrics in non-diabetics do not reliably correlate with HbA1c (the standard 3-month blood sugar average) — meaning the device measures something real but different from long-term glycaemic control.

⚡ In Plain English

A CGM (Continuous Glucose Monitor) is a small sensor worn on the arm that measures glucose (blood sugar) in the fluid under your skin every few minutes, sending readings to your phone. HbA1c (glycated haemoglobin) is the standard blood test that shows your average blood sugar over the past 3 months — it is the gold standard for long-term glucose control. The issue: in healthy people, glucose swings are naturally small and brief, so CGM produces a lot of data about transient fluctuations that have limited long-term health significance. It is more useful for identifying which foods spike your glucose than for tracking disease risk.

Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health (2026): CGMs are most useful for non-diabetics as a short-term (4–8 week) dietary personalisation tool — not as a permanent wearable. Individual glucose responses to identical meals vary enormously based on gut microbiome, activity levels, and metabolic phenotype. Key practical insights users report: carbohydrates consumed post-training spike blood sugar far less than the same carbs at rest; morning glucose is typically highest (dawn phenomenon); alcohol suppresses overnight glucose then causes a rebound spike.

The Dexcom Stelo is now FDA-approved over the counter in the US (no prescription), priced around $89/month. In Australia, CGMs remain prescription-only for non-diabetic use (TGA), but some sports medicine clinicians will prescribe for metabolic optimisation purposes.

⚡ Implication

A 4–6 week CGM experiment is worthwhile if you want to understand your personal glucose response to foods, training timing, alcohol, and sleep quality. Use it as a data-gathering sprint, not a permanent fixture. Key questions to answer: which meals spike you most, how much does morning training blunt carbohydrate spikes, and how does alcohol affect overnight glucose and sleep quality.

Spermidine & Autophagy: The Fasting-Mimetic Compound with New Mechanistic Proof

[Mechanistic][First CV RCT Enrolling] A landmark 2024 study in Nature Cell Biology established that spermidine is not merely associated with autophagy — it is required for fasting-induced autophagy. Blocking spermidine synthesis genetically or pharmacologically prevented fasting from triggering autophagy in yeast, nematodes, and human cells, and abolished lifespan extension in model organisms. This positions spermidine as the molecular mechanism linking fasting to cellular rejuvenation — not just a correlate.

⚡ In Plain English

Autophagy (literally "self-eating") is the cellular recycling process where damaged proteins and organelles are broken down and their components reused. It is activated by fasting, exercise, and caloric restriction, and it is one of the primary reasons these interventions appear anti-aging. Spermidine is a polyamine compound (found naturally in wheat germ, soybeans, aged cheese, mushrooms) that the body also synthesises internally. The new finding: fasting triggers autophagy partly because fasting raises endogenous spermidine levels — it is the molecular messenger that tells cells to start recycling.

A 2023 observational cohort (1,770 participants, 13-year follow-up) found high dietary spermidine intake associated with significantly reduced all-cause mortality. The first cardiovascular RCT enrolled its first patient in January 2024 — results anticipated in 2026. Supplemental spermidine (as spermidine trihydrochloride) is available at doses of 1–6mg/day in Australia, though food-first sources are preferable until RCT data lands.

Top food sources of spermidine: wheat germ (~24mg/100g), soybeans (~20mg/100g), mature cheddar (~20mg/100g), mushrooms (~8mg/100g), green peas (~7mg/100g). A tablespoon of wheat germ provides approximately 1mg spermidine — the lower end of supplementation doses.

⚡ Implication

If you fast (intermittent or extended), spermidine is the downstream mechanism doing much of the cellular benefit work. Prioritising spermidine-rich foods — especially wheat germ, mushrooms, and aged cheese — on eating days amplifies the autophagy signal. Supplementation (1–2mg/day) appears safe and may be worth trialling while waiting for the cardiovascular RCT results.

Movement & Training
Grip Strength as a Longevity Biomarker: New 2026 Data

[Observational] Multiple 2026 cohort analyses have reinforced grip strength as one of the strongest single-variable predictors of all-cause mortality and hospitalisation risk — outperforming BMI and many blood markers in predictive power. A 2026 synthesis across major cohort databases (ELSA, HRS, SHARE) found that each 5kg decline in grip strength was associated with a 16% increase in all-cause mortality risk in adults over 45. This is a proxy for total body muscularity, neuromuscular function, and metabolic reserve.

⚡ In Plain English

Grip strength is measured with a handheld dynamometer — you squeeze it as hard as you can and it records the force in kilograms. It predicts longevity so well because it reflects total lean mass, neuromuscular efficiency, and the functional capacity of your entire musculoskeletal system — not just hand muscles. A person with strong grip has typically maintained muscle mass, trained consistently, and has the metabolic profile that correlates with long healthspan. You can test yourself at a physio clinic or buy a hand dynamometer (~$30 AUD) for home tracking.

Reference ranges for males (dominant hand): Age 20–29: 50–60kg normal; Age 40–49: 44–54kg normal; Age 50–59: 40–50kg normal. Below-average grip for age group is a meaningful signal to prioritise compound pulling movements (rows, deadlifts, pull-ups) and total muscle mass.

⚡ Implication

Track grip strength quarterly as a longevity biomarker — it is cheap, fast, and highly predictive. For PPL training: heavy rows, deadlifts, and pull-ups are the compound movements that most directly develop grip strength as a byproduct. Farmer carries (walking with heavy dumbbells) are the most direct grip strength training tool if you want to target it specifically.

VO2max Training: What the 2026 Scoping Review Says About Zone Distribution

[Scoping Review] A 2026 scoping review of 617 exercise training studies (PMC12922157) confirmed the hierarchy of cardiorespiratory adaptation: polarised training (80% Zone 1–2, 20% Zone 4–5) produces the largest VO2max improvements in trained individuals over 12+ weeks, outperforming threshold-heavy (tempo) approaches. However, for untrained individuals, almost any structured cardio improves VO2max — the distribution matters more as fitness increases.

⚡ In Plain English

VO2max is your body's maximum oxygen processing capacity during exercise — the single strongest predictor of longevity in the research literature (stronger than blood pressure, cholesterol, or BMI). Polarised training means most cardio is done at very easy intensity (conversational pace, nose breathing — Zone 2), with a small proportion at very high intensity (near-maximum effort intervals — Zone 4–5). Avoiding the "moderate" middle zone (Zone 3, sustained threshold pace) is the counterintuitive finding — moderate intensity is not as effective for VO2max development as the combination of easy base + hard intervals.

⚡ Implication

For someone doing PPL who wants to improve VO2max: add 2–3 Zone 2 sessions of 30–45 minutes per week, plus one weekly interval session (e.g., 5 × 3-minute efforts at near-max effort with 3-minute recovery). Avoid spending most cardio time at "comfortable but hard" paces — that moderate zone produces the least adaptation per unit of fatigue accumulated.
