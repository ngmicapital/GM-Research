TL;DR — Today's Thesis

The AI infrastructure race is splitting into two lanes: OpenAI is weeks from launching GPT-6 "Spud" (Polymarket: 78% by April 30) while Google's Gemini 3.1 Pro quietly leads 13 of 16 major benchmarks at a third of the API cost. Anthropic is playing a different game entirely — the Claude Code Routines launch signals a shift from models-as-products to agents-as-infrastructure, where the moat is the workflow, not the weights. That said, Anthropic has a credibility problem: widespread user complaints about degraded Claude quality surfaced the same week as the Routines launch, with reports of quietly reduced default compute to manage infrastructure costs. For crypto, the AI token sector is diverging: TAO's developer exodus is a stress test (network survived, price didn't), while RENDER's RenderCon catalyst has it on a 5-day tear. The most immediately actionable item: Claude Haiku 3 retires in 3 days.

Model Landscape

frontier models · benchmarks · launches · training signals
GPT-6 is pre-trained and sitting behind a launch button — the question is not "if" but which week in April–May OpenAI pulls the trigger, and whether Google's Gemini 3.1 Pro has already made the benchmarks irrelevant by winning on cost.
[Launch]

GPT-6 "Spud" — Pre-Training Complete, Launch Imminent

OpenAI's GPT-6, internally codenamed "Spud", finished pre-training on March 24 at the Stargate facility in Abilene, Texas. The model has not launched yet. Sam Altman said "a few weeks" in late March, and Polymarket currently prices the probability of a launch by April 30 at 78%.

The most likely launch window is April 21 through May 25. OpenAI's pattern is to announce 1–2 weeks before GA access, so a blog post any day now would be consistent with a late-April launch. The Stargate facility itself — a $500B joint venture with SoftBank — represents the largest single AI infrastructure investment in history.

What to expect: if GPT-6 follows the GPT-5 → GPT-5.4 trajectory, the initial release will be a base model with Pro/Max tiers rolling out weeks later. The benchmark gap vs. Gemini 3.1 Pro is the number to watch on day one.

The trade is not "buy OpenAI" — it's watch what happens to Gemini 3.1 Pro API pricing the week after GPT-6 drops. Google has room to cut further and may use the launch as a trigger for aggressive pricing moves.
[Benchmark]

Gemini 3.1 Pro — 13 of 16 Benchmarks, 1/3 the Cost

Google's Gemini 3.1 Pro now leads 13 of 16 major public benchmarks and ties GPT-5.4 Pro on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index — at roughly one-third the API cost. Real-time voice and image analysis are native, not bolted-on features.

This is the quiet story that the GPT-6 hype cycle is drowning out. For cost-sensitive production workloads, Gemini 3.1 Pro is the strongest dollar-for-dollar option available right now. The benchmark lead is broad — not concentrated in one category — which makes it the safest general-purpose API bet until GPT-6 ships and the leaderboard resets.

If you're running high-volume API workloads and haven't benchmarked Gemini 3.1 Pro against your current provider in the last two weeks, you're overpaying. The cost gap is large enough to justify migration even if GPT-6 reclaims the top spot on launch day.
[Rumour] [Unconfirmed — single source on parameters]

Claude Mythos 5 — 10T Parameters, Not for Public Release

Details of Claude Mythos 5, a 10 trillion parameter model, leaked on April 7. Anthropic confirmed the model exists but will not be publicly released — access is restricted to select partners under "Project Glasswing" due to cybersecurity risk concerns.

The parameter count — if accurate — would make Mythos 5 roughly 5x larger than the largest confirmed frontier model. The decision to gate it behind a partner program rather than ship it publicly is the most interesting signal: Anthropic is openly acknowledging that some models are too capable for general release. This is the first time a major lab has taken that position with an actual product rather than a hypothetical.

Watch the partner list. "Project Glasswing" participants will have a meaningful capability advantage in whatever domain Mythos 5 is deployed for. The cybersecurity framing suggests the model has offensive security capabilities that Anthropic considers genuinely dangerous at public scale.
[Launch] [Open Source]

Open Source Frontier: Gemma 4 + Llama 4 Maverick

Google Gemma 4 shipped under Apache 2.0 — no commercial restrictions, frontier-level quality, natively multimodal. This is the strongest fully-open model available for local deployment. Meanwhile, Meta's Llama 4 Maverick brings 400B params and a 10M context window to the open-source tier — the longest context available in any open model by a factor of 5x.

The 10M context window on Maverick is the headline, but the practical constraint is hardware: running 400B parameters with 10M context requires serious GPU infrastructure. For most users, Gemma 4 is the immediate-deployment option; Maverick is the "when you need it" option for organizations with GPU clusters.

Gemma 4 is the new default recommendation for local deployment. Zero licensing headaches, genuinely frontier-competitive quality. Maverick's 10M context is a research tool, not a consumer product — but it's the model to reach for when you need to process an entire codebase or document corpus in a single pass.

Claude Ecosystem

claude code · api · routines · platform updates
Anthropic shipped Routines and a full Claude Code desktop redesign in the same week it faced the loudest user backlash since launch — the feature velocity is real but the performance controversy is not going away.
[Launch] ⚡ delta

Claude Code Desktop Redesign — April 14

The Claude Code desktop got a ground-up redesign on April 14. The feature list is substantial: a new sidebar for session management (browse, search, and resume past sessions), drag-and-drop layout for panel arrangement, an integrated terminal that lives inside the app rather than requiring an external window, a built-in file editor with syntax highlighting, a rebuilt diff viewer with side-by-side comparison, and an expanded preview pane for web development workflows.

This is not a cosmetic refresh. The session sidebar alone changes the daily workflow — being able to search and resume previous Claude Code sessions without reloading context from scratch addresses one of the top user complaints from the last six months. The integrated terminal means one fewer window to manage during coding sessions.

If you're still using Claude Code in terminal mode exclusively, the desktop app is now worth switching to. The session management and diff viewer are genuine productivity upgrades, not feature bloat.
[Launch]

Routines — Scheduled Agents, Research Preview

Routines launched April 14 as a research preview. The concept: bundle a prompt, a repo, and a set of connectors into a single unit that runs on a schedule, via API call, or triggered by a GitHub event. Runs execute on Anthropic's cloud infrastructure with daily run caps based on your plan tier (Pro, Max, Team, Enterprise).

This is the clearest signal yet that Anthropic sees the moat in workflow orchestration, not model weights. Routines turn Claude from a tool you invoke into infrastructure that runs autonomously — daily code reviews, scheduled research synthesis, automated PR triage. The daily run caps and plan-based pricing suggest Anthropic is being cautious about infrastructure load during the research preview phase.

Test Routines on one low-stakes automated task this week — daily research digest or automated PR labeling are good starting points. Watch the capacity limits closely; research preview caps will tell you a lot about GA pricing.
[Alert]

Performance Controversy — Quality Complaints + Compute Reduction Reports

Fortune reported on April 14 that widespread user complaints about degraded Claude quality have been building for weeks. The specific allegation: Anthropic quietly reduced default compute effort to manage infrastructure costs, resulting in noticeably worse outputs for the same prompts that previously worked well.

Anthropic has not directly confirmed or denied the compute reduction. A confirmed service outage on April 15 (reported by TechRadar) added fuel to the narrative. The timing — same week as the Routines launch and desktop redesign — creates a credibility problem: Anthropic is shipping ambitious new features while the core model experience is degrading for existing users.

This matters because the value proposition of Claude has always been output quality. If the quality floor is dropping while the feature ceiling rises, power users will start routing sensitive work to Gemini 3.1 Pro or waiting for GPT-6.

If you've noticed degraded Claude output quality in the last 2–3 weeks, you're not imagining it. Consider explicit compute-level parameters where available, and benchmark critical workflows against Gemini 3.1 Pro as a fallback.
[Update]

Other Claude Updates — Caching, Recaps, Skills

Several smaller but practically useful updates shipped alongside the headline features: 1-hour prompt caching (up from the previous window) and forced 5-minute prompt caching for frequently-used context, session recap that generates a summary when resuming a previous session, and Skill tool access that lets Claude Code invoke slash commands programmatically — meaning skills like /commit and /review-pr can be chained into automated workflows.

The caching changes are the most immediately impactful for API cost management. If you're running repeated similar queries against large system prompts, the 1-hour cache window substantially reduces input token costs.

Workflow & Tools

agent frameworks · dev tools · mcp ecosystem · productivity
OpenAI's Agents SDK got sandbox execution and portable workspaces — the agent framework war is now a three-way race between OpenAI, Anthropic (Routines), and the MCP ecosystem's 4,000+ servers.
[Launch]

OpenAI Agents SDK — Sandbox Execution, Configurable Memory

OpenAI updated its Agents SDK on April 15 with the features that were missing at initial launch: model-native harness with sandbox execution, configurable memory that persists across agent runs, and portable workspaces that can be snapshot and redeployed. The infrastructure provider list is now serious: Blaxel, Cloudflare, Daytona, E2B, Modal, Runloop, Vercel.

Python SDK is shipping now; TypeScript SDK is confirmed incoming. The portable workspace feature is the differentiator — being able to freeze an agent's entire execution state, move it to a different provider, and resume is something neither Anthropic Routines nor LangChain offer cleanly. For multi-cloud agent deployments, this is the framework to watch.

If you're building agents that need to run across multiple cloud providers, the OpenAI Agents SDK's portable workspaces solve a real problem. For single-provider Anthropic-native workflows, Routines is the simpler path.
[Workflow]

MCP Ecosystem — 4,000+ Servers, 97M Monthly Downloads

The Model Context Protocol ecosystem has hit critical mass: 4,000+ servers, 97M monthly SDK downloads, and 70% of major SaaS vendors now offer remote MCP endpoints. Governance has moved to the Linux Foundation, which is the clearest signal that MCP has transitioned from Anthropic's protocol to an industry standard.

The practical implication: most integrations you'd want to build already exist. Before writing a custom MCP server, check the registry — the odds are good that someone has already built and maintained what you need. The 70% SaaS coverage means the "MCP or custom API wrapper" decision has tipped decisively toward MCP for new projects.

[Workflow]

Raycast — The Mac Productivity Tool That Replaced Four Paid Apps

Raycast has emerged as the most complete Mac productivity tool in 2026. The free tier now replaces 4+ paid applications (launcher, clipboard manager, window manager, snippet expander), with native MCP integration and Ollama support for local model access. The AI features are additive — the core launcher and automation capabilities stand on their own.

The MCP + Ollama integration is what makes Raycast relevant to this briefing: you can invoke local models and connect to remote MCP servers from a keyboard shortcut, turning Raycast into a lightweight agent interface without running a full IDE or terminal session.

[Workflow]

Hermes Agent v0.8.0 — 209 PRs, Dual Trending

Hermes Agent v0.8.0 shipped April 8 with 209 PRs merged and 82 issues resolved in a single release cycle. The framework is trending on GitHub both weekly and monthly — sustained momentum, not a launch spike. This is the lightweight agent framework that avoids LangChain's over-engineering and the enterprise lock-in of managed platforms.

Open Source Pulse

github trending · star velocity · community projects · local-first ai
OpenClaw went from 9K to 210K+ stars in three months — the fastest-growing open-source project in GitHub history — and the visual AI builder category (Langflow, Dify, Flowise) is now larger than the framework category.
[Launch]

OpenClaw — 210K+ Stars, Fastest-Growing Open Source Project Ever

OpenClaw is a personal AI assistant that runs locally and connects to 50+ integrations — WhatsApp, Telegram, Slack, Discord, Signal, iMessage, and more. The star count has exploded from 9K in January to 210K+ in April, making it the fastest-growing open-source project in GitHub history by star velocity.

The appeal is obvious: a single local-first AI assistant that unifies all your messaging platforms without sending data to a third-party cloud. The 50+ integration count means it covers the long tail of communication tools that no single vendor supports. The local execution model is the key differentiator — your messages stay on your machine.

Worth installing and testing if you use 3+ messaging platforms daily. The privacy model (local-only) solves the trust problem that kills most AI assistant adoption. Monitor for enterprise features — at this growth rate, a commercial offering is inevitable.
[Workflow]

Visual AI Builders — The Category Is Now Bigger Than Frameworks

The visual AI builder category has crossed a scale threshold where it's now collectively larger than traditional code-first frameworks. Langflow leads with 146K stars, followed by Dify at 136K and Flowise at 51K. Combined, these three projects have more stars than LangChain, LlamaIndex, and CrewAI put together.

The signal is clear: the market for AI automation tools is shifting from developers who write code to operators who build visually. This doesn't mean code-first frameworks die — it means the total addressable market for AI tooling is 10x larger than the developer population, and visual builders are capturing that expansion.

[Research]

MemPalace — 96.6% LongMemEval, Highest-Scoring Free Memory System

MemPalace scored 96.6% on the LongMemEval benchmark — and 100% in hybrid mode — making it the highest-scoring free AI memory system available. The design philosophy stores the reasoning context behind memories, not just conclusions, which is a direct response to the common complaint that AI assistants "decide what to remember for you" without preserving why.

This pairs naturally with the personal wiki trend that Andrej Karpathy and others have been advocating — long-term single-user knowledge systems that grow smarter over time rather than resetting every session. MemPalace is the most technically complete implementation of that idea.

Deals & Regulation

funding rounds · policy · legislation · compliance
The Trump AI Framework is the first federal attempt to preempt the 600+ state AI bills — and the AI companion chatbot regulation wave now includes potential felony charges for developers.
[Regulatory]

Trump National AI Policy Framework — March 20

The Trump administration released a National AI Policy Framework on March 20, prioritizing four pillars: child safety, free speech, innovation, and targeted federal preemption. The framework explicitly cautions against vague safety standards and fragmented state-level regulation — positioning the federal government as a deregulatory counterweight to the 600+ state AI bills currently active.

The "targeted federal preemption" language is the key signal. If enforced, it would give the federal government authority to override state AI laws that it deems overly restrictive or constitutionally questionable. The DOJ's AI Litigation Task Force has been given explicit authority to challenge such state laws.

For AI builders: the framework favors innovation over precaution. For AI investors: federal preemption reduces regulatory risk for companies operating nationally. Watch the DOJ Task Force actions — they'll show whether preemption has teeth or is aspirational.
[Regulatory]

AI Companion Chatbot Regulation — Felony Charges on the Table

The state-level regulatory wave targeting AI companion and mental health chatbots has intensified. Washington passed disclosure mandates requiring chatbots to identify as non-human. New York's S-3008C mandates suicide detection protocols in AI companions. The sharpest development: some states are now considering potential felony charges for developers whose AI companions cause demonstrable harm — a criminal liability standard that goes far beyond the civil penalties in existing tech regulation.

The EU AI Act adds a parallel pressure: 15 industry associations are requesting a 12-month extension for the generative AI labeling requirements (up from the current 6-month compliance window). This is the first coordinated industry pushback against EU AI Act timelines.

Criminal liability for AI developers is a new legal frontier. This is currently limited to companion/therapy bots, but the precedent — holding developers criminally responsible for AI-caused harm — will spill into other categories if any prosecution succeeds. Not directly relevant to coding or trading tools today, but the legal framework is being built right now.

Research Radar

papers · arxiv · conference submissions · technical breakthroughs
An AI system wrote a paper that got accepted at a major conference — this is no longer a hypothetical capability, and Google's PaperOrchestra is pushing simulated acceptance rates above 80% for CVPR and ICLR.
[Research]
The AI Scientist-v2: Workshop-Level Automated Scientific Discovery via Agentic Tree Search
SakanaAI · arXiv 2504.08066 · Code: github.com/SakanaAI/AI-Scientist-v2

One-line finding: End-to-end agentic system using progressive tree search autonomously produced a paper that cleared the acceptance threshold at a major conference — the first fully AI-generated paper to achieve this milestone.

Why it matters: v2 removes human-authored code templates entirely — the bottleneck that made v1 a demo. The VLM figure-refinement loop and dedicated experiment manager agent are the novel components. This compresses the hypothesis-experiment-write cycle for well-scoped ML research problems. The code is open-sourced and already spawning forks.

Published, peer-reviewed, code open-sourced. This one passes the "will become a product" test.

[Research]
PaperOrchestra: Multi-Agent Framework for Submission-Ready Paper Generation
Google Research · April 2026

One-line finding: Multi-agent framework converts pre-writing materials (notes, outlines, experimental results) into submission-ready papers with a simulated acceptance rate of 84% for CVPR and 81% for ICLR.

Why it matters: AI Scientist-v2 automates the entire research pipeline; PaperOrchestra automates only the writing phase but does it at a higher quality bar. The 84% CVPR acceptance rate (simulated) means the output is indistinguishable from human-written papers at the reviewer level. Combined, these two systems sketch a future where the full research-to-publication pipeline is automated.

Google's approach is complementary to AI Scientist-v2, not competitive. Watch for integration — a combined system would be transformative for labs with more ideas than writing bandwidth.

[Research]
MemPalace & Personal Wiki Architectures for Long-Term Single-User Knowledge
Multiple groups · Karpathy et al. cluster · Q1–Q2 2026

One-line finding: A cluster of memory architecture proposals for personal AI systems that retain context across sessions — MemPalace (96.6% LongMemEval), Karpathy's personal wiki concept, and several derivative projects — are converging on a shared design principle: store reasoning traces, not just conclusions.

Why it matters in 6 months: The session-reset problem (AI assistants forgetting everything between conversations) is the single largest friction point in daily AI usage. This research cluster is producing the architecture that will eventually ship inside every major AI assistant. MemPalace's 100% score in hybrid mode suggests the retrieval problem is essentially solved at the research level.

[Research]
TurboQuant: Online Vector Quantization with Reduced Memory Overhead
Google · ICLR 2026 (accepted)

One-line finding: Addresses the memory overhead problem in vector quantization — the compression technique used to make large models fit on smaller hardware. Accepted at ICLR 2026.

Why it matters: Better quantization directly translates to running larger models on consumer hardware. This is the kind of infrastructure paper that doesn't make headlines but shows up in the next generation of local deployment tools (llama.cpp, vLLM, etc.) within 3–6 months.
