📚 Learn Something: Quantum Computing
Quantum computers use qubits that can exist in multiple states simultaneously (superposition), enabling them to solve certain problems exponentially faster than classical computers. The catch: qubits are extremely fragile. They lose their quantum state (decohere) within microseconds, and every computation introduces errors. That's why NVIDIA's Ising model matters — it uses AI to calibrate quantum processors faster and correct errors more accurately, tackling the two biggest bottlenecks to practical quantum computing. Today's quantum machines have 50-1,000+ qubits, but useful applications (drug discovery, materials science, cryptography) likely require millions of error-corrected qubits — still 5-10 years away. The stocks rally on each incremental breakthrough, but commercial revenue remains near zero. Trade the hype cycles, don't marry the position.
ASML's memory demand surge + Tesla's AI5 tape-out + NVIDIA's quantum push = the AI capex supercycle has multiple vectors. The ASML dip on China headlines is the actionable opportunity today.
