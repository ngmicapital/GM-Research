📚 Learn Something: AI Inference vs Training

When people talk about AI compute, there are two very different workloads. Training is the upfront process of building a model — it requires massive GPU clusters running for weeks or months and costs tens of millions of dollars per run. Inference is what happens every time you ask ChatGPT a question — the trained model processes your input and generates a response. Training is a one-time cost; inference runs 24/7 at scale across millions of users. This is why inference is projected to be the larger long-term market — Goldman estimates inference could represent 60-70% of total AI compute spend by 2028. Companies optimising for inference efficiency (smaller models, quantisation, custom ASICs like Google's TPU) will capture disproportionate value as the market matures beyond the "build the biggest model" phase.
NVDA's streak has legs. The pullback in broader tech is a rotation, not a thesis change. Buy NVDA dips; the Vera Rubin catalyst in H2 gives a second leg.
