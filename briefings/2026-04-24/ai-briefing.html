Research Radar

Papers that become products in 6 months
A formal proof that transformers trained on MSE can't beat the noise floor in financial time series is the most actionable paper this week for anyone using LLMs for signal generation.
[Research]
Transformer Forecast Collapse Under MSE — Andreoletti (2026)
arXiv: 2604.00064 Unconfirmed: single source — verify on arxiv.org

Finding: For financial time series — where signal-to-noise ratio is low and conditional mean is approximately flat — increasing model expressivity leads to a higher prediction error, not lower. The root cause: MSE (Mean Squared Error) as a loss function creates an error floor that is double the irreducible minimum when training and test noise are independent and additive.

Why it matters: Every LLM-based financial forecasting system trained on MSE is provably unable to beat the noise floor. The paper suggests alternative loss functions calibrated to the actual signal properties of financial data.

⚡ Implication for dc: If you're using any LLM or transformer-based model for price prediction or signal generation, this paper is directly relevant. The conclusion is practical: MSE-trained transformers on financial data are not just sub-optimal — they're formally bounded below their maximum possible performance. Verify the arXiv preprint directly before changing anything in your pipeline.
[Research] [Launch]
AI Scientist-v2 — Automated Research at Conference Quality

AI Scientist-v2 introduces agentic tree search for automated scientific discovery: autonomous hypothesis generation, experiment execution, data analysis, and paper writing. A paper fully generated by this system was accepted by a major AI conference — the first confirmed instance of fully autonomous research passing peer review.

The pipeline uses PaperOrchestra (a companion multi-agent framework) to transform pre-writing materials into submission-ready LaTeX manuscripts with literature synthesis and generated figures.

⚡ Why it matters in 6 months: Automated research pipelines will begin generating novel ML results faster than human researchers can read them. The bottleneck shifts from research production to research validation and integration. Expect first commercial products (automated systematic literature reviews, hypothesis testing services) by Q4 2026.
