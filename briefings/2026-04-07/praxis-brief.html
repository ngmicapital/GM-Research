Quantum Computing
Google Cuts Bitcoin's Threat Threshold by 20x — and Nobody Can Ignore It Now

Google Quantum AI's March 30 paper, Securing Elliptic Curve Cryptocurrencies against Quantum Vulnerabilities, delivered a significant update to the timeline debate: breaking Bitcoin's ECDSA secp256k1 signatures now requires fewer than 500,000 physical qubits — down from prior estimates of 10 million. The circuits modelled achieve a 41% success rate against a 10-minute confirmation window, a 9-minute attack on a fault-tolerant machine.

No such machine exists today. The paper does not claim Bitcoin is currently at risk. But the 20x reduction forces the community to re-evaluate timelines — and highlights that 6.9 million BTC sitting in addresses with exposed public keys are already in the highest-risk category whenever that threshold is reached. Zcash rallied 20% on quantum-resistance positioning; Algorand gained 44%.

"We are not claiming that anyone can break Bitcoin today. But we are providing the community with the information it needs to prepare." — Google Research Blog

The practical implication: address hygiene matters now. Moving funds to fresh addresses with unexposed public keys is the simplest near-term hedge.

Agentic Economy
Stablecoins + AI Agents: The Internet Is Getting a Native Payment Layer

Two convergent moves this week crystallised what builders have been saying for months: the internet is being rewired for machine-to-machine commerce.

x402 Foundation launched on April 2 under the Linux Foundation with backing from Google, Microsoft, Stripe, Cloudflare, AWS, Visa, and Mastercard. The x402 protocol revives the HTTP 402 "Payment Required" status code, embedding stablecoin payments directly into web requests — an AI agent can hit a paywall, pay in USDC, and continue its task in the same interaction with no human required. x402 has already processed 162 million transactions at $45M in volume since October 2025.

Ant Group's Anvita platform enables AI agents to hold crypto assets, execute trades, and settle payments autonomously using stablecoin rails. McKinsey projects AI agents could mediate $3–5 trillion of global commerce by 2030. The two primitives converging everywhere: stablecoins for settlement, autonomous agents for execution.

Mindset
Scarcity Never Gets Solved — It Just Changes Form

Mark Manson's weekly newsletter (#200) offered a disarmingly clean reframe this week: scarcity is not a problem to be solved, but a mechanism through which things become meaningful.

"When you are alone, a relationship feels meaningful. When you're in a relationship, time to yourself feels meaningful. When you are overworked, time off feels meaningful. When you have plenty of downtime, work feels meaningful. The scarcity never gets solved, it simply changes form." — Mark Manson

The implication cuts in two directions. First, the chase for "enough" is structurally unwinnable — recognising this frees you from the treadmill. Second, what you perceive as scarce right now is a direct signal of what you currently value most. Use it diagnostically rather than fighting it.

His practical prompt: pick one thing you have right now that you once wanted badly, and treat it like it could disappear tomorrow. This is the cheapest available tool for resetting baseline appreciation.

Stoicism
It Has Always Been Worse — The Historical Antidote to Despair

Ryan Holiday's Daily Stoic this week pushed back against the ambient sense that civilisation is uniquely broken right now. The argument is historically grounded and worth internalising:

"You can look out at the news and despair about things. Or you can zoom out and see progress. You can focus on the bad people and miss that the bad people today are almost certainly better than the bad people back then." — Daily Stoic

Ancient Greece had earthquakes, theft, and systemic injustice. Rome had tyrants and cruelty we wouldn't recognise today. The adversaries you disagree with politically are not sending children into mines — a baseline common not long ago. The Stoic frame: see what's broken, work to fix it, but do so from gratitude rather than despair. You inherited a world already improved by those before you; your job is to leave it marginally better.

This is particularly relevant for processing the current market cycle (BTC's worst Q1 since 2018, Fear & Greed at 13/100) without catastrophising.

Philosophy

"Trust us, you look amazing. You look beautiful. And from up here, you know, you look like one thing. Homo sapiens is all of us. No matter where you're from or what you look like, we're all one people — and so, this mission shows you what we can do not just when we put our differences aside, but when we bring our differences together and use all the strengths to accomplish something great." — Victor Glover, Artemis astronaut

Crypto Security
Quantum-Ready Address Hygiene: What to Do Before the Debate Resolves

You don't need to know when fault-tolerant quantum machines arrive to take sensible precautions now. The Delphi community's most popular response to this week's poll: move funds to fresh addresses with unexposed public keys, and eliminate address reuse until an upgrade path is clear.

The logic: ECDSA vulnerability applies to addresses whose public keys have been revealed on-chain (i.e., after spending). Addresses where only the hash of the public key is visible are currently safe. Practically: consolidate holdings into newly generated addresses you haven't spent from. Avoid Taproot addresses if quantum-resistance is a priority concern, as they expose public keys natively. Monitor Bitcoin Core for any signals of urgency before making larger positioning moves.

Source: Delphi Digital · Google Research Blog
Agentic Positioning
Builders: x402 Is the Primitive to Integrate Now, Not Later

x402's backing coalition (Google, Microsoft, Stripe, Cloudflare, AWS, Visa, Mastercard) is not a crypto-native experiment — it is a bid to install native payment rails at the HTTP layer. For builders, the integration surface is straightforward: any API or content endpoint that could charge per-call has an immediate use case. For investors and strategists, the companies best positioned are those building on top of this layer (agent frameworks, API-first SaaS, content platforms) rather than competing with it.

The Machine Payments Protocol (MPP.dev), co-authored by Stripe and Tempo, is the open standard for machine-to-machine payment coordination. Worth bookmarking alongside x402 docs as the two canonical references for agentic payment infrastructure.

Practice
Apply the Scarcity Diagnostic: Inventory What Feels Rare Right Now

Manson's scarcity frame is most useful as a weekly diagnostic. The exercise:

1. Name what feels most scarce in your life right now — time, attention, connection, creative output, physical energy. This is what you currently value most.
2. Identify one thing you currently have that you once badly wanted. Write it down specifically.
3. Treat it as temporary for 24 hours. Don't manufacture anxiety — simply hold the fact of its impermanence lightly in mind.

This practice doesn't solve scarcity. It reorients you toward appreciation for the form abundance currently takes, rather than perpetual dissatisfaction at the form it doesn't.

Source: Mark Manson Newsletter #200

Delphi's newsletter title this week — "Markets Bled Through the Quarter. But the Infrastructure Got Stronger" — is a framing worth operationalising. BTC's worst Q1 since 2018, extreme fear, and sentiment at 13/100 are often the conditions that produce the clearest signal-to-noise ratio on structural developments.

None of these are price events. All of them are infrastructure events. Building a habit of tracking infrastructure developments separately from price prevents conflating fear with deterioration.
