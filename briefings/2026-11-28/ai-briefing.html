Model Landscape

Open-weight momentum sits with Qwen3.8-27B, while a reported Chinese flagship delay and an OpenAI billing fix are the other model-side stories.

Qwen3.8-27B keeps leading open-weight trends

The base Qwen3.8-27B model sits at the top of the week's Hugging Face trending digests, with roughly 21,900 likes and more than 6 million downloads per the 27 November snapshot [paraphrased]. Quantised derivatives such as the unsloth GGUF build add millions more downloads, so this family now anchors most local-inference experimentation.

Z.AI reportedly falls behind on its next flagship

Reuters, as relayed by The Neuron's daily roundup, reported that Z.AI has slipped on the schedule for its next flagship model [paraphrased]. This is a single secondary source. If the delay holds, China's frontier release cadence slows at the same time new US chip restrictions tighten supply.

OpenAI corrects a GPT-6.2 billing error

OpenAI fixed a billing mistake that made usage limits run out about 40% faster for GPT-6.2 users, and its pricing pages did not change [paraphrased]. The fix followed a day after some Pro users were moved onto a single usage meter for Chat and Work, so the limits story is still moving.

Claude Ecosystem

Anthropic's news this cycle is about product breadth, containment research and corporate follow-through rather than a new model.

Claude Duet widens to four more countries

Anthropic expanded Claude Duet, its new work-agent app, to four additional countries, according to the Kelly Abbott daily briefing [paraphrased]. Geographic rollout is the signal here: the product is moving from pilot to general availability in more markets.

A sandbox-escape test marks two years of MCP

To mark two years of the Model Context Protocol, Anthropic published a safety test that checks whether Claude attempts to break out of a deliberately weakened sandbox [paraphrased]. It is a test-design story rather than a launch, but it matters for anyone giving agents tool access through MCP servers.

IPO pricing needs a date check

The Neuron's 27 November roundup says Anthropic priced an $81.3 billion IPO [paraphrased]. Recent coverage already documents the post-listing selloff, so this figure should be confirmed against the original pricing date before anyone cites it as new.

Decentralized AI

Decentralized compute projects are announcing supply-side mechanics and capacity changes; token prices are a secondary signal this cycle and are omitted because no live price pull ran.

Bittensor raises its subnet limit to 160

Core developer Const says Bittensor has raised its subnet limit to 160, with a path to 512 or beyond [paraphrased]. He argues the expansion lowers registration costs for smaller teams, and plans for permissionless lending aim to improve subnet liquidity. An October update referenced up to 2,500 subnet slots, so the two figures describe different limits and should not be merged.

Akash, io.net and Render converge on burn-based supply models

A governance research report dated 10 November argues that Akash, io.net and Render have all adopted burn-mint style tokenomics in 2026 [paraphrased]. The same report says io.net burns 50% or more of post-payout revenue, and that Akash burned 53,520 AKT in its first eight days after activation. Treat the foundation-control critique as the author's argument, not established fact.

On-chain revenue is small but measurable

Coinpaprika's DePIN analysis puts Render and Akash at about $9.3M of annualised on-chain revenue as of 20 November, with io.net's own estimate adding about $16.3M [paraphrased]. Against cloud GPU markets these are small numbers, which makes them a useful reality check on the sector's demand claims.

Open-Source Pulse

Qwen still sets the open-weight agenda, and the newest angles are quantisation and local-hardware speedups.

Qwen3.8-27B and its GGUF quants lead the week

Across late-November Hugging Face digests, Qwen3.8-27B tops the trending list, and the unsloth GGUF quantisation is reported as the most-downloaded model of the week at about 7.7M downloads [paraphrased]. Quantisation specialists are now capturing much of the adoption.

An AMD-specific llama.cpp fork

A community fork called llama-cpp-rdna-boosts adds AMD-specific patches that reportedly raise token generation from about 35 to 55 tokens per second on an R9700, and prefill from 700 to 1,300 tokens per second [paraphrased]. This is a single-hardware benchmark, so verify on your own setup before relying on it.

NVIDIA releases Nemotron 3.5 Parse

NVIDIA announced Nemotron 3.5 Parse, a compact model that extracts structured data from document images, with synthetic data, a fine-tuning guide and faster inference [paraphrased]. It is a document-extraction tool rather than a general chat model.

Deals & Regulation

Capital is concentrating in a few very large AI rounds, while the seed and Series A market stays busy.

Anthropic reportedly acquires Stainless for over $300M

The Economic Times, as summarised by the AI Agents Directory, reports that Anthropic acquired Stainless for more than $300 million [paraphrased]. Stainless supplied SDKs to developers at OpenAI and Google, and the deal would terminate its hosted services for those companies, which squeezes rivals' developer tooling. Treat it as reported until Anthropic confirms the terms.

Sierra raises $1.75B at a $30B valuation

Sierra's Series F reportedly doubled its May valuation mark, on more than $400 million in annual recurring revenue, per the Firgun newsletter [paraphrased]. Enterprise agent revenue is the core justification for the price.

Marlowe raises $277M seed at $1.15B

Marlowe, which is building AI infrastructure designed to run on non-Nvidia chips, has reportedly raised $277 million in seed funding at a $1.15 billion valuation [paraphrased]. The thesis is chip diversity away from one supplier.

Altair Semiconductor closes a $100M Series A

Israeli chipmaker Altair Semiconductor closed a $100 million round to fund low-power connectivity chips, the largest AI-related deal in that day's roundup [paraphrased].

Automation Anywhere completes its boost.ai acquisition

Automation Anywhere has completed its acquisition of conversational AI provider boost.ai, per the AI Agents Directory brief [paraphrased]. The Amazon–Decart deal was excluded here because it was covered in the last two issues.

Workflow & Tools

Tooling is moving toward cheaper model access and orchestration of coding agents, while AI-driven attack frameworks are now part of the threat picture.

Moat offers unlimited full-weight model access for $5 a month

Moat positions itself as a low-cost alternative to OpenRouter, offering developers unlimited usage of full-weight models at wholesale prices [paraphrased]. Check the fair-use limits and model list before moving production traffic.

Hub coordinates Claude Code and Codex sessions

Hub is an open-source dispatcher that coordinates several Claude Code or OpenAI Codex sessions, letting developers issue goals through a browser or Telegram and receive only decisions back [paraphrased]. It runs on self-hosted subscriptions, which suits teams running many coding agents in parallel.

ARTEX moves into state-backed attack toolkits

Microsoft Threat Intelligence says ARTEX, an AI-powered penetration testing framework public since July, is now part of the tooling of several state-sponsored and financially motivated groups [paraphrased]. Attackers set scope and targets while agents plan each stage, run standard tools and report back. Defenders should assume agent-driven reconnaissance is the baseline now.

Research Radar

One item this run, and it needs primary-source verification before anyone relies on it.

Google Research's Cogentic reportedly produces six math papers

An AI Agents Directory item reports that Google Research's agent, Cogentic, generated six mathematics papers on 25 November, all produced with Gemini 4 [paraphrased]. The primary arXiv or Google record was not fetched, so check authorship, review status and proof validity first. It is worth tracking because it tests whether AI-generated research holds up under scrutiny.
