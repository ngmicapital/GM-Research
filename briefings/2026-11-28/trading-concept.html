Alpha — Tape Pace

Saturday, 28 November 2026 · Sydney, Australia · Issue 62 11 min read
TL;DR

Tape pace is the rate at which trades print, and it tells you how urgent the market is rather than which way it will go. Measure the current print rate against a rolling baseline, read the aggressor side inside any burst, and give the most weight to the moment speed falls back under normal while price holds a level. On BTC on 27 November, the rally toward 62,000 faded below the weekly open on thin holiday liquidity, which is the kind of tape where the speed read earns its keep.

§01
What It Is
Prints per minute, read against a baseline
Tape pace is how many trades print per minute compared with what is normal for that venue and hour.

Builds on Passive vs Aggressive Flow (27 Nov 2026 issue). That piece covered who rests and who crosses. This one measures how fast the crossing happens.

Tape pace is the rate at which trades print on the tape. In crypto that means the count of executed trades per minute on a single venue, read against what is normal for that venue at that hour. It measures urgency rather than direction. A fast tape tells you many participants are transacting right now; it does not tell you which side is winning. Read alone, a speed count is noise. Read against a rolling baseline and paired with the side of each print, it becomes one of the earliest tells that a level is being attacked or abandoned.

The reason it matters is that a candle is a lagging summary. A bar can close flat while a burst of sell-side prints hits the bid and gets absorbed, and the chart shows none of that until the bar finishes. The tape shows the burst as it happens. A Wyckoff-native reader will recognise the idea: effort against result, measured in time rather than in volume per bar.

PrintOne executed trade, with time, price, size and side.
SpeedPrints per minute, compared with a rolling baseline.
AggressorThe side that crossed the spread and forced the fill.
BaselineThe normal print rate for this venue, session and hour.
BurstA run of prints well above baseline, usually one-sided.
If you only remember one thing: tape pace tells you how urgent the market is, and only the aggressor side tells you who is in a hurry.
§02
Mechanics
From a match to a speed reading
It counts executed prints over a window against a rolling baseline, then tags each burst by the side that initiated it.

Every print is an executed trade, so the mechanic starts at the matching engine. A resting limit order sits in the book until a market order or a marketable limit order crosses the spread and matches it. Each match produces a print stamped with time, price, size and the side that initiated it. Count those prints over a fixed window and you have speed. The signal comes from comparing that count with what the venue normally does.

The sequence a reader runs is:

  1. Build the baseline. Take the rolling average of prints per minute over the last 30 to 60 minutes on the venue you trade, excluding the burst you are measuring.
  2. Flag the burst. When the live count runs at a multiple of baseline, note whether the prints are one-sided or two-sided.
  3. Check the aggressor mix. One-sided prints at the bid mean sellers are crossing hard. Two-sided prints at similar sizes mean the level is contested.
  4. Wait for the fade. When speed falls back under baseline while price still sits at the level, the initiating side has run out of fuel. That is the observation to act on.
Speed ratio = prints in last 60 s ÷ rolling 30-min average prints per minute
Above 3 reads as a burst. Below 1 after a one-sided burst is the fade flag. Thresholds are a starting point; set them per venue and per session.

§04
In Practice
BASELINE FIRST, SIDE SECOND
Speed alone is noise. Act only when a one-sided burst is followed by speed falling back under baseline while price holds.
1

Baseline first, always

A speed count means nothing until it is set against the same venue, the same session and the same hour. Build the baseline before you look at the burst, and exclude the burst from it.

2

Speed without side is noise

Fast prints are urgency, not direction. Before you act on a burst, confirm that one side is crossing the spread and the other is being hit.

3

Trust the fade more than the spike

The burst tells you something is happening. The drop back under baseline with price still holding the level tells you the initiator has stalled, which is the moment to act.

§07
Common Traps
WHERE SPEED GETS MISREAD
Reading raw print counts with no baseline, so that a normal Thanksgiving-thin session looks like a burst.
1
Reading raw speed with no baseline

A count of 80 prints in a minute means little on its own. Set it against the venue's own rolling average for that hour, or every quiet session will look like a breakout.

2
Mistaking speed for direction

A fast tape is urgency with no side attached. Confirm which side is crossing the spread before you read a burst as bullish or bearish.

3
Treating one venue as the whole tape

Crypto prints are fragmented across venues. One exchange's print rate is that venue's view. Read the venue you trade, and check one reference venue before you size up.

4
Calling a thin holiday session normal

Thin liquidity around Thanksgiving and expiry days exaggerates bursts. Tag these sessions and widen your baseline window rather than trusting one fast minute.

!
Overhyped: precise speed win-rates

Some tape-reading guides quote exact reversal hit-rates for speed spikes, drawn from small-cap stock day trading, without method, sample size or any crypto test. Treat such figures as hypotheses to test on your own prints, not as an edge.
