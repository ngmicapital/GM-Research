Adding three weekly resistance sessions to tirzepatide roughly quartered lean-mass loss and reversed strength decline in a 40-week Nature Medicine RCT, making it the strongest finding this cycle (sourced in §02 with context from §01 and §03). Driving Thesis

The strongest result this cycle is the T-REx trial in Nature Medicine: 120 adults with overweight or obesity, aged roughly 50 to 70, took tirzepatide for 40 weeks while either lifting three times a week or keeping their usual routine. Weight loss was about 22 kg in both arms and fat loss did not differ, yet the lifting arm lost roughly a quarter less lean soft tissue and gained strength while the drug-only arm got weaker.

The NAD+ thread went the other way. A mouse study reported that blocking CD38, a route to higher NAD+, reduced bone thickness in ageing animals, and a separate human sample found no sign that blood NAD+ falls with age. Together these weaken the case for buying precursors on the basis of a supposed age-related deficit, and they reinforce that outcome trials, not metabolite levels, should decide what earns a place in the stack.

Section 01
Longevity & Performance Science

NAD+ is being treated as a single dial, and this cycle’s data push it in two directions. Blocking CD38, an enzyme that breaks NAD+ down, is one way to raise NAD+ levels, and in ageing mice that route came with thinner bone. A separate human sample then found no sign that NAD+ in the blood falls with age, which undercuts the premise behind many precursor products. The mechanistic lesson is that a metabolite pool is not the same thing as the tissue function it is supposed to support, and only outcome trials in people can settle that.

Raising NAD+ by blocking CD38 thinned bone in ageing mice
Mechanistic

In a mouse study summarised by Lifespan.io, blocking CD38 increased NAD+ and was followed by reduced bone thickness as the animals got older. The summary also notes that NAD+ falls across several tissues with age, so the skeletal result is a warning that a metabolite-raising lever can carry a cost that a metabolite measurement alone would never reveal. Caveat: I did not open the primary paper, so the dose, sex mix and bone assay are unverified.

Source: Lifespan.io news digest, November 2026
Blood NAD+ shows no clear age decline in healthy adults
Observational

A new study of healthy adults reported no evidence that NAD+ in the blood drops as people age, which cuts against the common pitch for NMN and NR products. For a buyer, the sharper question is whether blood NAD+ is even the right readout for tissue function. Design, sample size and assay details are not verified from the summary I could access.

Source: The Morning, summarising Earth.com, 15 Nov 2026
⚡ Implication: Keep NAD+ precursors out of the “proven longevity” column for now. If you buy one, judge it on human outcome data rather than on a rise in a blood level.
Section 02
Training & Performance
Resistance training during tirzepatide: lean-mass loss cut by roughly a quarter
Human RCT

In the T-REx trial, 120 adults took tirzepatide for 40 weeks while either lifting three times weekly or keeping their usual activity. Weight loss was about 22 kg in both arms and fat loss did not differ, but total lean soft tissue fell by about 4.7 kg on the drug alone against roughly 3.6 kg with training. Strength diverged sharply: bench press, lat pulldown and knee extension dropped by about 4 kg on the drug alone but rose by roughly 5 to 9 kg with lifting. Adherence to the lifting was 91 per cent.

Source: Green et al., Nature Medicine 2026 · PubMed abstract
Integrate now
  • Start lifting the same week as the drug, not after weight loss, since the trial did exactly this.
  • Three sessions a week was the tested dose and it proved feasible at 91 per cent adherence.
  • Track strength next to body weight, because strength is where the two arms separated.
Caveats
  • DXA lean mass includes water and organ tissue, so it is not a pure muscle measure.
  • The population was adults aged roughly 50 to 70; applying the result to a younger lifter is an inference.
  • Fat loss did not differ, so the benefit is preservation of lean tissue, not extra fat loss.
⚡ Implication: If you ever run a cut on tirzepatide or another incretin drug, keep the lifting programme intact and measure strength and DXA lean mass at each block. Two registered trials, TUM RESIST (thigh muscle volume, primary completion targeted August 2027) and Exeter’s FLEX study, will test whether the effect holds in other designs before it is treated as settled.
Section 03
Supplements & Nutrition
Urolithin A and brain blood flow during mental work
Human RCT

A JAMA Network Open trial reported that urolithin A, a compound derived from plant ellagitannins, increased blood flow to the brain during mental activity, and participants tolerated it well. The perfusion signal is the finding; a cognitive benefit has not been shown, and the researchers themselves call for larger, longer trials. The pathway, dose and sample size were not in the summary I accessed, so treat this as a promising mechanism rather than a recommendation.

Source: ScienceDaily, 26 Nov 2026 (JAMA Netw Open 2026;9(10):e2638047)
Less protein, slower ageing? A 350-study review
Narrative Review

A large review of more than 350 studies argues that eating less protein could slow ageing, improve metabolic health and possibly extend lifespan, which runs against the common view that more protein is always better. The reported pathway runs through nutrient-sensing signalling, but I could not verify the review’s mechanistic analysis or whether its method was systematic. For a lifter the practical read is narrow: the T-REx data show that muscle retention is the outcome to protect during energy restriction, so protein should not drop below what training requires.

Source: ScienceDaily nutrition digest, 10 Nov 2026
Ketogenic diet beat Mediterranean and low-fat diets for liver fat
Human RCT

A new clinical trial found a ketogenic diet reduced liver fat more than Mediterranean or low-fat diets, even when participants lost similar amounts of weight. That separates a diet-composition effect from weight loss itself, which matters if you track liver markers. Trial size, duration and any lifting-performance data were not in the summary I could access, so the signal is useful but not yet detailed enough to act on.

Source: ScienceDaily nutrition digest, Nov 2026
