  • INTC: not retrieved this run. A headline notes Nvidia cut its Intel stake, a positioning detail rather than a fundamental read.

Broader AI Landscape

  • Micron's gain of more than 7% on the Friday session suggests the rebound is in memory, where AI demand is most direct.
  • Michael Burry's warning that debt insurance costs are rising for Nvidia, Oracle and Meta points to funding stress at the spenders, not just the chip supplier.
  • The AI trade is being judged on returns on investment, with rising rates and a possible post-midterm capex slowdown as the stated concerns.
Learn: why debt insurance costs matter for the AI trade. When investors pay more to insure a company's bonds against default, the market is pricing rising funding risk. For AI, the spend is financed by debt and circular partner deals, so a widening in those costs can precede an equity sell-off even when revenue is still growing. A lower equity price here does not mean the revenue has broken, only that the cost of funding the next leg has risen.
Stay selective. Favour the memory names where demand is direct over the single-name leader that carries governance risk. Nvidia needs to reclaim its key averages on volume before the bearish lean on the complex is reduced; a Micron-led follow-through with Nvidia lagging would confirm the broadening-but-fatiguing read.
