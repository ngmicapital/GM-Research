Learn Something: Why the "Build Your Own GPU" Trend Is Bullish for NVDA
Anthropic, Amazon, and Google all exploring custom chips might seem bearish for NVDA. It's not — yet. Custom silicon takes 2–3 years to reach production parity, and total AI compute demand is growing faster than any single vendor can supply. BofA's $300B upward revision in 4 months tells the story: the TAM is expanding faster than market share can shift. NVDA's 92% data centre GPU share will erode eventually, but the absolute dollar opportunity grows. The analogy is Intel in the 1990s — competitors arrived, but the market grew so fast it didn't matter for a decade.
NVDA at ATH is not a sell signal — the earnings growth ($62.3B single-quarter data centre revenue, +75% YoY) justifies the multiple at P/E 44.6. The competitive threat from custom chips is 2028+. Near-term catalyst: Rubin H2 ramp. Risk: Rubin memory supply constraints flagged in prior quarters. Stay long semis with NVDA as core, AVGO/MRVL as broadening plays.

7 🌐 Geopolitical Calendar
🚨 STRAIT OF HORMUZ CRISIS — ACTIVE / NEGOTIATIONS STALLED
Status: Conditional ceasefire in place but effectively non-functional. Shipping traffic through Hormuz down 90%+. US has turned back 48 Iranian ships in 20 days. A bulk carrier was attacked by "multiple small craft" 11 nautical miles west of Sirik, Iran on May 2. Iran submitted a 14-point response to US peace proposal on May 2, but Trump rejected it over the weekend, demanding nuclear program guarantees first.

Transmission Mechanism: Hormuz handles 20% of global oil. The effective closure has driven US gas to $4.45/gal (+49% since the Feb 28 war start). This flows directly into CPI via fuel and transport costs, constraining the Fed (70%+ hold), compressing airline/consumer margins, and supporting energy equities. Second-order: the political cost of $4.45 gas is feeding into Senate odds (50/50 from 60/40 GOP in January).

Reversal: A sustained Hormuz reopening would collapse crude $15–20 in a session. Iran's 14-point proposal includes a demand to resolve all issues within 30 days — ambitious but shows engagement. Watch for any US-Iran backchannel signals this week. The attack on the bulk carrier near Sirik suggests spoiler elements are active even during "ceasefire."
🚨 US TARIFF REGIME — REDUCED BUT PRESENT
Status: Supreme Court struck down IEEPA broad tariffs. Section 122 temporary 15% global import duty remains. New tariffs on EU autos/steel have disrupted supply chains. US-EU trade agreement has eased some tensions but situation evolving.

Transmission Mechanism: Import price elevation compounds the oil-driven inflation shock, keeping the Fed locked. Front-loading of imports ahead of potential escalation is inflating inventory and trade data artificially. China tensions remain elevated with expanded export controls and semiconductor restrictions.

Reversal: Congressional override of Section 122 (unlikely). Trade deal announcements would provide relief. Midterm election calculus may force de-escalation on tariffs as voter sensitivity to prices intensifies.
🚨 RBA RATE DECISION — TOMORROW 2:30PM AEST
Status: RBA Board meets today and tomorrow (May 4–5). CBA and NAB expect a hike to 4.35%. Markets are split — prediction markets at 55% hike. Oil-driven inflation persistence is the key variable.

Transmission Mechanism: A hike would strengthen AUD, weigh on ASX 200 (which just snapped a 7-day losing streak), and tighten financial conditions for Australian households already stretched by energy costs. The ASX has underperformed global peers YTD due to this domestic rate uncertainty.

Reversal: A surprise hold would rally ASX 200 and boost AUD/USD, but would raise questions about RBA credibility on inflation targeting.
Trump's weekend rejection of Iran's 14-point proposal is the single most important development since Friday's close. Prediction markets at 75% no-ceasefire-by-June are more bearish than equity markets, which rallied on Friday's oil pullback.
