CFTC Deploys Microsoft 365 Copilot Across Workforce — AI Now Reviews Crypto Registration Filings as Headcount Falls 23% From 708 to 543 FTEs

"AI tools can be used to review the applications, flag certain things for the staff, make their jobs easier, make it much faster for them to provide feedback and also reject certain things that aren't materially complete." — Chairman Michael Selig, interview with CoinDesk, 27 April 2026.

Chairman Mike Selig has confirmed the CFTC will use AI to triage incoming crypto registration applications — flagging incomplete or facially deficient filings, ranking the queue, and freeing the agency's shrunken human staff for harder cases. The CFTC's headcount has dropped from 708 full-time employees at the close of FY2024 to roughly 543 today, a ~23% cut in eighteen months, driven by Trump administration federal-staffing reductions. Selig has authorised Microsoft 365 Copilot agency-wide and is building bespoke surveillance models targeting fraud, market manipulation, and insider trading across crypto and prediction markets.

This is the first time a US financial regulator has formally tied its registration-review pipeline to a generative AI workflow. Applications for FCM, swap dealer, DCM, DCO, and SEF status — including those expected from Polymarket, Coinbase Derivatives, and the wave of crypto firms anticipated post-CLARITY — will now be filtered through Copilot before reaching a human reviewer.

The shift compounds an institutional reality: with four of five Commission seats vacant, Selig is the only sitting commissioner. Every contested approval already requires his signature; AI now stands between filers and his desk.

📎 Historical precedent: SEC EDGAR filing-quality screens (since 2016) automated form-type validation but never substantive merit review; the CFTC step is materially broader.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Per Sidley's "Project Crypto" alert series and Cleary Gottlieb's 2026 Digital Assets Update, registration timelines under the post-CLARITY CFTC perimeter were already projected to bottleneck at 543 staff. AI triage solves throughput, not legal judgment — every Section 5 finding for a DCM, every fitness-and-propriety call for a DCO, still requires Commission-level review. The Brief's read: the practical effect is a barbell — clean filings approve faster, marginal filings die in the queue with no clear due-process path to human review. Counsel should now over-document on first submission; "iterate with staff" is no longer a viable strategy.

🔄 Contrarian Take

The Brief's read: the workforce cut, not the AI tooling, is the binding constraint — Copilot is a face-saving narrative for a regulator that lost its ability to do the job. If Senate Democrats (see Story 3) succeed in imposing a prediction-market rulemaking on top of the CFTC's existing crypto, derivatives, and surveillance docket, no AI deployment closes that gap. Expect litigation challenging AI-driven rejections under APA §706(2)(A) "arbitrary and capricious" review within twelve months.

Polymarket Asks CFTC to Lift US Ban on Main Offshore Exchange — Single Sitting Commissioner Means Selig Decides Alone

"If prediction markets are pushed offshore, it would lead to a repeat of the FTX-style collapse." [paraphrased] — Chairman Selig, public remarks April 2026 via CoinDesk reporting.

Polymarket has formally engaged the CFTC about lifting the geo-block on its main, overseas exchange — the platform that has been off-limits to US traders since the 2022 settlement. Bloomberg first reported the discussions on 28 April; CoinDesk corroborated. The ask is distinct from the limited intermediated US sandbox the CFTC approved in late 2025 (which has yet to fully launch).

Procedurally, the request lands in front of an unprecedented single-commissioner CFTC: with four seats vacant, any approval falls to Chairman Selig alone — eliminating the deadlock risk that has historically frustrated event-contract approvals.

📎 Historical precedent: KalshiEX won single-judge prelim injunction against CFTC election-contract block (D.D.C. 2024); Polymarket avoided that fight by exiting US market under 2022 settlement.

The Brief's read: approving Polymarket's main exchange before that rulemaking is finalised would be procedurally aggressive — it preempts the very public process the Democrats are now leveraging. Timing matters: the comment-period close means the CFTC must now address every major commenter's argument before publishing a final rule, and Democrats have framed the docket so a refusal to ban sports/election contracts becomes APA-challengeable on "failure to consider relevant factors."

FinCEN-OFAC Joint NPRM Treats Permitted Payment Stablecoin Issuers as BSA Financial Institutions — Comments Due 9 June, Final Rule Required by 18 July GENIUS Statutory Clock

"PPSIs must have the technical capabilities, policies, and procedures to block, freeze, and reject specific or impermissible transactions that violate Federal or State laws, rules, or regulations as provided in the GENIUS Act." [paraphrased] — FinCEN/OFAC NPRM, 91 Fed. Reg. — (10 April 2026), via WilmerHale and Sullivan & Cromwell client alerts.

FinCEN and OFAC have jointly published a Notice of Proposed Rulemaking implementing the GENIUS Act's anti-money laundering, counter-terrorism financing, and sanctions-compliance regime for Permitted Payment Stablecoin Issuers (PPSIs). The NPRM treats every PPSI as a "financial institution" for Bank Secrecy Act purposes and imposes parallel obligations on programmatic transaction-blocking, sanctioned-address screening, and reserve-account compliance.

Critically, the proposed rule distinguishes primary from secondary market activity. The NPRM acknowledges that "the majority of illicit finance involving payment stablecoins occurs on the secondary market" — but caps its secondary-market reach at technical capability requirements (block/freeze/reject), expressly declining to impose customer due diligence, ongoing monitoring, or SAR obligations on secondary-market activity.

Public comment closes 9 June 2026. Treasury's statutory deadline to publish final regulations is 18 July 2026 (per GENIUS), with full enforcement no later than 18 January 2027. The window between comment close and final rule is six weeks — minimal by federal-rulemaking standards.

📎 Historical precedent: FinCEN's 2020 "Travel Rule" amendment for crypto VASPs imposed similar but less-prescriptive technical capability requirements; the GENIUS-Act PPSI rule is a material escalation in scope and specificity.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Per Sullivan & Cromwell's 11 April 2026 client memo and Covington & Burling's "Five Things to Know" alert, the secondary-market carve-out is the most consequential piece. PPSIs only need to maintain technical block/freeze capability over secondary-market transactions — they do not have to monitor, report, or KYC end-users. Practical effect: Circle (USDC), Paxos (USDP), Ripple (RLUSD) avoid a reporting nightmare but must build sanctioned-address blocklists into protocol-level redemption logic. Per Holland & Knight's 18 April 2026 alert, this is a near-perfect alignment with OFAC's existing sanctioned-address SDN List rails — but it imports OFAC strict-liability standards into stablecoin issuance for the first time.

🔄 Contrarian Take

Per Consensys's 2 May pushback letter (reported in The Coin Republic), the technical-block requirement is "operationally infeasible for protocol-issued stablecoins" — proof-of-reserve issuers (including non-custodial designs being explored by Frax, Lido-staked-USD variants) cannot block on-chain transactions without compromising the redemption guarantee that makes the asset a stablecoin. The first PPSI rejection on this ground will trigger litigation testing whether GENIUS preempts non-custodial token issuance entirely.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Compliance moat for incumbent custodial issuers: Circle (USDC), PayPal (PYUSD), Paxos (USDP), Ripple (RLUSD) already have OFAC blocklist tooling — incremental cost is modest. For decentralised projects: MakerDAO/Sky, Frax, Liquity face an existential question — file as PPSI under GENIUS or accept that their stablecoins cannot be marketed to US users by 18 January 2027. Sanctions-screening vendors win: Chainalysis, Elliptic, TRM Labs all have pre-built block-list APIs that map directly into the proposed rule's technical-capability requirement.

🟢 Bullish CRCL (Circle), PYPL (PYUSD), Chainalysis/Elliptic/TRM (private), legacy bank-rail BaaS providers — moat hardens.

SDNY Prosecutors Reject Roman Storm's Cox v. Sony Dismissal Bid — DOJ Files 7 April Letter, Targets October 2026 Retrial on Money-Laundering and Sanctions-Evasion Counts

"[The Cox Communications ruling] applies only to civil copyright law and has no bearing on the criminal charges in this case." [paraphrased from DOJ letter via The Block] — SDNY prosecutors, letter filed 7 April 2026.

Tornado Cash co-founder Roman Storm's effort to dismiss the unresolved counts of his 2025 prosecution has been rejected by SDNY prosecutors. Storm had relied on the Supreme Court's 2026 ruling in Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment — which limited contributory liability for ISPs in civil copyright cases — arguing the same logic should defeat his criminal money-laundering and sanctions-evasion exposure. DOJ disagreed in a 7 April court letter, calling the analogy "no bearing" on criminal-law contributory liability.

The August 2025 jury convicted Storm on conspiracy to operate an unlicensed money-transmitting business but deadlocked on money-laundering and sanctions counts. DOJ now seeks an October 2026 retrial — projected to last roughly three weeks and revisit intent and the Tornado Cash protocol's role.

📎 Historical precedent: The DOJ's March 2026 report to Congress acknowledged lawful uses of crypto mixers — yet aggressive prosecution of Storm continues, marking the limits of the new "softer mixer policy."

Per Mayer Brown's August 2025 trial-implications memo and Bankless's 7 April reporting, the DOJ's letter explicitly distinguishes civil from criminal contributory liability — preserving the Government's preferred theory that 250+ post-deployment Tornado Cash contract changes by Storm constitute affirmative criminal conduct. The Brief's read: the DOJ filing closes the most efficient developer-liability defence available under Cox; future DeFi-developer prosecutions should expect the same wall. FCA Opens CP26/13 Cryptoasset Perimeter Guidance — Final Boundary Question Before October 2027 Regime Commences, Comments Due 3 June 2026

"[CP26/13] invites comment on the regulatory boundary for the United Kingdom's incoming crypto regime." [paraphrased] — FCA, CP26/13 publication 15 April 2026.

The FCA has published Consultation Paper CP26/13 on cryptoasset perimeter guidance — the final substantive consultation before the October 2027 commencement of the new UK cryptoasset regime under the Financial Services and Markets Act perimeter. The paper is the companion piece to the closed CP25/40 (December 2025) and CP25/41 consultations on activities and admissions; CP26/13 specifically addresses which activities fall inside vs. outside the regulated perimeter.

Practical issues: how staking-as-a-service is classified, when DeFi front-end provision crosses into "operating a trading platform," and whether tokenised-fund administration is within the regulated activity definition. Final policy statements are scheduled for Summer 2026 (CP25/40 family) and Autumn 2026 (CP26/13).

Application window for the new regulated activities opens 30 September 2026 and closes 28 February 2027.

📎 Historical precedent: FCA's 2019 Cryptoasset Perimeter Guidance (PERG 19A) created the regulated/unregulated split that has governed UK crypto promotion since — CP26/13 is the first comprehensive update.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Per A&O Shearman's "UK Future Crypto Framework" alert and Hogan Lovells' CP25/40 takeaways, the perimeter guidance is the consultation that decides whether DeFi front-end providers, staking aggregators, and wallet operators sit inside or outside the FSMA framework. The Brief's read: firms that wait for the Autumn 2026 policy statement will miss the application window — the practical course is to file under CP25/40 assumptions now and amend if the perimeter narrows. The FCA has signalled it will treat early applicants favourably on supervisory engagement.

🏗️ Industry Implications

UK-domiciled crypto firms (Copper, Zodia Custody, Bitstamp, Revolut) accelerate licence-prep work; offshore-licensed firms (Crypto.com, Bybit, OKX) must now decide whether to passport via post-MiCA EU subs or apply directly.
