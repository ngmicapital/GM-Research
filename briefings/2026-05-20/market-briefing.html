The 30Y Yield Shock — Why It Matters More Than Iran: Tuesday’s session was defined by a bond market that has fully priced out any near-term Fed easing and is now testing hike territory. The 30Y briefly above 5.19% — levels last seen in 2007 — creates a direct transmission mechanism: higher mortgage rates crush the housing and renovation cycle (hence Home Depot’s soft guidance), higher discount rates compress equity multiples (hence the Nasdaq underperformance), and higher borrowing costs squeeze corporate margins (hence the broad Dow decline). The Iran de-escalation removed the geopolitical tail risk but did not address the structural bond market challenge: CPI at 3.8% annual (highest since 2023) means Warsh has no room to ease, and the market increasingly believes the next Fed move is a hike rather than a cut.
Three consecutive losing sessions ahead of the year’s single most important afternoon (FOMC minutes + NVDA) sets up a high-volatility event window. The S&P at 7,353 is now 0.6% below the 7,380 level flagged as key support. If FOMC minutes read hawkish AND NVDA disappoints, the 7,200 level becomes the next test. If both read constructively — minutes are hawkish-hold rather than hiking, NVDA beats with strong Q2 guidance — a sharp relief rally above 7,500 is plausible. The 30Y is the swing variable: any drop below 5.10% on the back of today’s data is the signal to cover shorts.

The FOMC Minutes — What the Market Is Looking For: The April meeting minutes (released today) are simultaneously stale and critical. Stale because they pre-date Monday’s Iran de-escalation announcement and the subsequent oil relief. Critical because they reveal for the first time the internal committee dynamics under Warsh’s shadow: did anyone float explicit rate hike language? How did the committee characterise the energy CPI pass-through? Was there any “looking through transitory inflation” language or has that framing been abandoned entirely? SocGen’s Rajappa has noted the structural contradiction at the heart of Warsh’s position: he campaigned on lower rates but the energy environment forces higher-for-longer. The minutes are the first public document that shows how the committee is resolving this contradiction. Explicit hike language pushes the 10Y to 4.65–4.70%. “Hawkish-hold” language (watching, not hiking yet) triggers a relief rally that could take 20–30bp off the 30Y.
The bond market has done more economic damage in 2026 than the Hormuz crisis alone. With the 30Y at 5.19%, the US housing market faces the highest mortgage rates in two decades, corporate refinancing risk is rising, and equity risk premiums are negative against bonds for the first time since 2007. If today’s FOMC minutes are hawkish-hold (base case: ~60% probability), markets can process the news as “no surprise” and shift focus to NVDA. If they are explicitly hawkish (rate hike discussed as a near-term option), the 30Y tests 5.30% and all risk assets face a leg lower. The wildcard is if the minutes show surprising dovish dissents — Warsh’s “regime change” rhetoric may not be as uniform as the bond market assumes.
