Senator Warren's 18 May Letter to Comptroller Gould Names Ripple, Circle, Paxos, Fidelity, BitGo, Coinbase, Stripe/Bridge, Crypto.com — Alleges OCC Approved "At Least Nine" National Trust Charters Since December 2025 As Crypto Bank-In-Drag Vehicles That "Evade The Fundamental Safeguards" of Bank Holding Company Act Supervision

"These companies are effectively crypto banks that want to evade the fundamental safeguards and obligations that come with being a bank. Your decision to facilitate this regulatory arbitrage not only conflicts with federal law, it also poses serious risks to consumers, the safety and soundness of the banking system, and the separation of banking and commerce."
— Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-MA), Ranking Member, Senate Banking Committee · Letter to Comptroller Jonathan Gould, 18 May 2026

Senator Elizabeth Warren on Monday delivered a six-page oversight letter to Comptroller of the Currency Jonathan Gould stating that the OCC has, since December 2025, approved "at least nine" national trust charters for crypto firms whose business plans go "far beyond the narrow set of activities permitted by law." Warren names Ripple, Circle, Paxos, Fidelity Digital Assets, BitGo, Coinbase, Stripe's Bridge subsidiary, and Crypto.com as approved or pending applicants; the still-pending World Liberty Financial application is treated separately because of the Trump family's ownership.

The substantive argument turns on a single point of bank-regulatory doctrine: national trust banks under 12 U.S.C. § 27(a) and 12 C.F.R. § 5.20(e)(2) must conduct fiduciary activities as their primary business. Warren's letter argues that the approved business plans contemplate non-fiduciary custody, payments, lending and stablecoin issuance — activities that, in her reading, require a full-service national bank charter under the Bank Holding Company Act, with Federal Reserve supervision, source-of-strength obligations, and CRA reporting that trust charters do not impose.

Warren wrote that "many of the business plans do not include specific fiduciary trust activities and none imply that bona fide fiduciary trust activities would be the primary business of the trust company." The letter requests records of every communication between the OCC and the White House (or Trump family members) on chartering matters, and frames the trust-charter pipeline as the Atkins-Gould Treasury's mechanism to deliver federal banking access to crypto firms without congressional action.

📎 Historical Precedent: Mirrors the 2020–2021 OCC fight over special purpose national bank charters for fintechs (CSBS v. OCC, S.D.N.Y. 2019; CSBS v. OCC II, 2021) — where state bank regulators successfully challenged the OCC's authority to charter non-depository institutions.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis Davis Polk's January 2026 client update on the OCC charter wave flagged exactly this risk vector: a trust charter is statutorily narrower than a full national bank charter, and OCC interpretive letters do not displace 12 C.F.R. § 5.20(e)(2)'s "primary purpose" test. Warren's letter is procedural setup for a CSBS-style state-AG or Conference of State Bank Supervisors lawsuit if Democrats retake either chamber in 2026 midterms. The Brief's read: the political win the OCC delivered to Coinbase, Circle, Ripple and Paxos is structurally fragile — none of these charters can be unwound without notice-and-comment, but they can be enjoined mid-stream, and the courts in CSBS II already held the OCC's chartering authority is justiciable. The risk-weighted move for chartered firms is to begin building out genuine fiduciary lines now to harden the "primary purpose" defense.
🔄 Contrarian Take Patomak Global Partners (Atkins' former firm) and Cooley's bank regulatory group have argued that the OCC's reading is well within Comptroller's interpretive authority — custodying digital assets is functionally identical to bailment-type fiduciary services that national trust banks have performed since 1864. The Brief's read: Warren needs litigation standing she does not have; without an injured state bank regulator or competitor plaintiff, the letter is rhetoric, and the OCC can run out the clock through the rest of Trump's term while charter holders compound their reliance interests.
🏗️ Industry Implications For Coinbase (COIN), Circle (CRCL), and Ripple (pre-IPO), the trust-charter pathway is the centerpiece of their institutional product roadmap — GENIUS Act stablecoin issuance, prime brokerage, and Wall Street custody all assume federal banking access. Warren's letter does not change the operating reality today, but it caps the political ceiling: every M&A advisor in the space (Goldman, Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan) will now stress-test charter-dependent revenue lines in 2027 base cases. For banks like JPMorgan (JPM), BNY (BK), and State Street (STT) facing crypto-firm competitive entry into custody, the letter is a tactical gift — it gives ABA and BPI air cover to lobby for tighter trust-charter interpretive standards. The Brief's read: the most underpriced beneficiary here is Anchorage Digital, the only crypto firm that already holds a full national trust charter (Jan 2021) — its earlier-vintage charter is grandfathered out of the Warren critique.

"Foreign-issued trust-type stablecoins will qualify as electronic payment instruments under the Payment Services Act, provided issuance and redemption mechanics meet the reserve and segregation standards set by Japanese trust law." [paraphrased from FSA Cabinet Office Ordinance summary, May 2026]
— Japan Financial Services Agency, Cabinet Office Ordinance on Electronic Payment Instruments, May 2026

Japan's Financial Services Agency has finalized an amendment to the Cabinet Office Ordinance under the Payment Services Act (PSA) that clarifies how foreign-issued stablecoins can operate inside the Japanese system. Under the revised ordinance, trust-type stablecoins issued abroad qualify as "electronic payment instruments" under the PSA, with full operational effect — including all Cabinet orders and related ordinances — scheduled for 13 June 2026. The Ministry of Justice gazette places the first-tier effective date at 1 June 2026.

The doctrinal mechanism matters: Japan has classified stablecoins under the PSA rather than the FIEA (financial instruments) regime, which means redemption-at-par, reserve, and AML obligations are administered through the PSA's electronic-payment-instrument architecture. Foreign-issued tokens (USDC, USDT, EURC, future GBP-pegged tokens) become Japan-distributable only if structured through a domestic trust intermediary that satisfies Japanese reserve and segregation rules.

Parallel commercial pipeline: SBI Holdings is targeting a Q2 2026 yen-pegged stablecoin launch; MUFG, SMBC, and Mizuho are co-developing trust-based yen stablecoins through the Progmat platform. The 13 June effective date is the binding constraint on go-live timing for all three.

📎 Historical Precedent: Builds on the 2022 PSA amendments that first created Japan's stablecoin regime — the first major economy to legislatively define stablecoins as a distinct asset class.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis Anderson Mōri & Tomotsune's May 2026 client alert noted that the FSA's trust-type mechanism makes Japan the first major jurisdiction to formally bridge foreign-issued stablecoins into a domestic regulated framework without requiring re-issuance — a structural advantage that MiCA and the proposed US GENIUS Act both lack. "Providers of AI systems will have to inform users when they are interacting with an AI system and implement machine-readable marks in generative AI systems to enable the detection of synthetic content."

The European Commission published draft guidelines on the implementation of Article 50 transparency obligations under the EU AI Act on 8 May 2026, opening a targeted consultation that closes 3 June 2026.
