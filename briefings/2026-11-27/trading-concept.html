Alpha — Passive vs Aggressive Flow

Friday, 27 November 2026 · Sydney, Australia · Issue 63 11 min read
TL;DR

Passive vs aggressive flow is the split under every other orderflow read: limit orders rest in the book and supply liquidity, while market orders cross the spread and take it. It matters because delta only registers the aggressive side, so a flat delta bar beside a heavy bid can say more than a large green print. Practical takeaway: before you act on any sweep, absorption or delta signal, ask which side initiated and whether the passive size is refreshing. Today BTC is +0.75% on the day but −2.3% on the week, with the broader market down 2.7%, which is exactly where that check earns its keep.

§01
What It Is
Who is resting and who is crossing
Passive orders are limit orders resting in the book; aggressive orders are market orders that cross the spread and take liquidity.

Every print on the tape has two sides, and the question that decides how you read it is which side paid to be there. A passive order is a limit order resting in the book; it supplies liquidity and waits. An aggressive order is a market order that crosses the spread and takes that liquidity immediately. Everything else in orderflow, from delta to sweeps to absorption, is a measurement of which of these two groups is winning at a given level.

The distinction matters because the two groups leave different footprints. Aggressive buying shows up as a trade at the ask and pushes delta positive. Passive size shows up only as a level in the book, and it does not register on delta until something hits it. That asymmetry is why a flat delta bar next to a heavy bid can be more informative than a big green bar on its own. This issue builds on Absorption (26 Nov) and Sweep Orders (24 Nov), which both assume this split without defining it.

Limit orderResting order that waits at a price and provides liquidity.
Market orderImmediate execution against the best available resting size; crosses the spread.
InitiatorThe side that sent the aggressive order in a given trade.
SpreadGap between best bid and best ask; the cost of being aggressive.
Remember If you only remember one thing: before you read a delta bar, a sweep or an absorption, ask which side initiated the trade and which side was resting.
§02
Mechanics
From the queue to the initiator
The side that initiates a trade shows on delta, while the resting side only shows once it is hit or refreshes.

The mechanic is a queue. A limit order joins the queue at its price and waits; a market order skips the queue and takes whatever is on the other side. When aggressive buying exceeds the resting size at the ask, price steps up through the book. When it does not, the resting sellers absorb the buying and price stalls.

Step 1. Identify the best bid and best ask, and the spread between them.
Step 2. Classify each trade as buyer-initiated (lifted the ask) or seller-initiated (hit the bid). This is the aggressive side.
Step 3. Measure how much aggressive size was needed to move price one tick. Thin resting size means a small aggressive push moves price; thick resting size means the push is absorbed.
Step 4. Watch whether resting size refreshes after it is consumed. Refresh at the same level means passive participants are defending it.

Aggression ratio = aggressive buy volume ÷ (aggressive buy volume + aggressive sell volume)
Above 0.5 means buyers are initiating more of the volume. It says nothing about passive size, so pair it with the book.
Resting absorption = aggressive volume at a level − price displacement in ticks × typical resting size
A rough screen, not a standard metric. Large aggressive volume with little displacement points to passive size absorbing.

§04
In Practice
Read the refresh, not the size
Identify the initiator on each print, then check whether resting size refreshes after it is consumed.
1

Name the initiator before the move

Check which side crossed the spread on the last several prints at the level. A push made of market buys into a thick ask is a test, not a breakout yet. Confirm with the next print.

2

Count the refresh, not the size

A single large resting bid tells you little. The same bid refilling after it is hit tells you a passive buyer is defending the level. That refresh is the signal you are trading.

3

Treat a widening spread as a warning

When the spread opens, aggressive orders are paying more to get filled and passive makers are pulling back. Wait for the spread to close before reading the next push as conviction.

Worked example (illustrative, not a live trade)
SetupPrice holds 61,290 bid; 4.0 BTC refreshes after two aggressive sell prints
EntryLimit buy 1.0 BTC at 61,290
Stop61,240 (−50 points, 1R)
Target61,400 (+110 points, 2.2R)
InvalidationBid size does not refresh on the next test; market sells take the level
Timeframe fit: read passive vs aggressive on 1–5m for the level; confirm the bias on 1h or 4h. The split is a microstructure read, so it only holds while the book is visible.

§07
Common Traps
Where the split misleads
Treating every market order as conviction, when it may be a stop, a liquidation or a rebalance.
1
Reading every market order as conviction

A market buy can be a stop, a liquidation or a rebalancing order. Check whether the aggressive flow keeps pushing price after the first fill, not just whether it happened.

2
Assuming the book is the position

Resting size can be pulled before it fills. Treat passive size as a claim you verify on refresh and fill, not a fact you bank on. See the spoofing issue of 23 Nov.

3
Using delta alone to read passive flow

Passive participants do not register on delta until they are hit. A flat delta bar beside a heavy resting bid is the case delta misses, so pair it with book refresh.

×
Overhyped: the passive/aggressive label as a standalone system

The split is a definition that every order flow vendor repackages with a new name. It is useful as a question to ask of each print, not as a signal you can trade on its own.
