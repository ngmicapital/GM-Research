Switzerland Approves 57 Partner Jurisdictions for Crypto Tax Data Exchange From 2028

“[paraphrased] Switzerland will exchange crypto tax data only with partners that want the exchange and meet CARF confidentiality and compliance standards.” Swiss Federal Council decision, as reported by MLQ.ai and CoinMarketCap, 25 Nov 2026

The Swiss Federal Council approved 57 countries and territories on 25 November for automatic exchange of crypto-asset tax information under the OECD's Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework, with first exchanges due in 2028. The list covers every EU member state, the UK and several G20 economies. CoinMarketCap notes the timetable is a slip from the 2026 start Switzerland had targeted in its 2024 proposal.

The approval is conditional, not automatic. Partners must want to exchange with Switzerland and meet OECD confidentiality and data-security standards, and Cryptobriefing warns the live partner list could end up shorter than 57. Swiss crypto providers must collect identity, tax residency and transaction data on customers who are residents of partner states and report it to Swiss tax authorities.

📎 Historical Precedent: Switzerland has exchanged bank-account data automatically since 2018, and CARF carries that same architecture over to crypto-asset holdings.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

Switzerland is extending a mature bank-data exchange to crypto, which places the operational burden on Swiss providers that already run KYC rather than on the tax authorities. Advisers should map which clients are tax residents of the 57 partner states, because that list, not the Swiss domestic rule, determines who is ultimately reported. The Brief's read: firms with clean KYC and tax-residency data gain; offshore-facing platforms serving Swiss residents with thin onboarding records lose.

🔄 Contrarian Take

The conditional partner list means holders in non-listed states remain opaque to Swiss tax authorities, so the reporting net has holes by design. The Brief's read: the conditionality is a feature, because it ties data flows to OECD confidentiality standards; the real limit is which jurisdictions sign up to CARF at all, not the Swiss list.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Swiss custodians and exchanges face new collection and reporting builds, which favours tax-data and compliance-tooling vendors in the near term. The Brief's read: offshore venues serving Swiss residents are the likely capital-flow losers, as clients with partner-state tax exposure migrate toward regulated venues.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟩 Bullish COIN — compliant venues with mature tax-reporting stacks gain share against unreported flows.

🔴 Bearish Offshore venues onboarding Swiss residents with thin KYC files.

⚪ Neutral BTC and ETH spot — the regime changes who sees flows, not supply.

ASIC's Digital Asset Platform Consultation Closes Today, Ahead of the 8 April 2027 Start

“for illustrative purposes only and the final form of the instruments may change considerably from the preliminary drafts” ASIC, Consultation Paper 392 (as quoted in secondary reporting), 15 Oct 2026

Submissions on ASIC's Consultation Paper 392 close at 5pm AEDT today. The paper sets out how digital asset platforms and tokenised custody platforms will be licensed and operated, with a draft regulatory guide and draft asset-holding, transactional and settlement, and financial requirement instruments. The sources describe the package as the standards and guidance flagged in ASIC's April 2026 roadmap.

Two proposals matter most for trading venues. The draft standards would restrict platform operators and their related companies from trading as principal, and operators would not have to hold client assets in Australia. A separate proposal for all AFS licensees would exclude “restricted stablecoins” from net tangible asset calculations. ASIC expects final instruments in March 2027, ahead of commencement on 8 April 2027.

📎 Historical Precedent: ASIC's INFO 225 no-action position ran until 30 September 2026, the interim period that the statutory regime now replaces.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

The regime pulls digital asset platforms into the Australian AFSL perimeter. Thomsons Lawyers says ASIC's aim is “same activity, same risk, same regulatory outcome” and identifies principal trading, net tangible assets, client money and wallet reporting scope as where evidence matters most. HWLE Lawyers points to 7 October 2027 as the licence-application deadline. The Brief's read: offshore exchanges serving Australian clients without a local licence face the sharpest decision.

🔄 Contrarian Take

The drafts already contain an internal mismatch: Bits of Blocks reports that CP 392 gives a neutral venue add-on of $750,000 while the draft instrument says $500,000. The Brief's read: that inconsistency is an argument for filing today rather than waiting. The counter-case is that ASIC treats the drafts as illustrative and can absorb feedback into the March rewrite without a further round.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Australian exchanges, custodians and tokenisation platforms face new capital requirements, limits on trading against clients and new best-execution obligations, per Bits of Blocks' summary. The Brief's read: licensed incumbents with existing AFSLs gain a moat, while thinly capitalised start-ups carry most of the cost.

📊 Portfolio Impact

🟩 Bullish Licensed AFSL holders with capital headroom for the new financial requirements.

🔴 Bearish Principal-trading desks run inside Australian platforms, which the draft restricts.

⚪ Neutral ASX-listed brokers with no digital-asset exposure; no listed ticker is identified in the sources.

📋 What To Do — Before 1 July
TraderLog every principal-trading desk your platform runs for Australian clients, since the draft would restrict it once the regime commences.
CounselFile a submission at fintech@asic.gov.au before 5pm AEDT today, raising the $500,000 versus $750,000 neutral-venue inconsistency.
ComplianceAudit where client assets are held and whether any custody sits outside Australia, since the draft drops that requirement.
FounderModel the proposed capital and financial requirements against your runway before the 8 April 2027 commencement.

Switzerland's Spot Bitcoin ETF Begins Trading on SIX After Regulatory Clearance in October

“[paraphrased] The first Swiss spot bitcoin ETF launched on SIX with 350 BTC and a 0.19% fee, widening regulated access for non-US investors.” Phemex Crypto Market Daily, 27 Nov 2026

A spot bitcoin ETF began trading in Switzerland around 26 November, which The Block describes as the only spot bitcoin ETF in Europe. Phemex reports the fund launched on SIX with 350 BTC and a 0.19% fee. [Unconfirmed — sources conflict: The Block names the product “BIT”, while Phemex names it “BBIT”.]

Bitcoin Magazine reports the Swiss financial regulator cleared the first bitcoin ETF in October, so the listing question was settled before the launch. The practical effect is distribution: a SIX-listed wrapper lets European and non-US investors hold bitcoin exposure inside an exchange-traded perimeter.

📎 Historical Precedent: Canada listed the first spot bitcoin ETF in 2021, ahead of US approval, and Switzerland's SIX listing follows the same non-US-first pattern.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

With the listing cleared in October, the live legal questions for distributors are suitability and disclosure rather than product approval. The Brief's read: European wealth managers gain a regulated bitcoin wrapper to offer clients who cannot buy spot directly, which shifts flows away from unregulated products.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Issuers, SIX and Swiss private banks share the fee and distribution economics, and a 0.19% fee will pressure higher-cost incumbents. The Brief's read: 350 BTC of seed capital is modest, so the signal here is regulatory, not a demand shock.

French Senate Rejects Register for Self-Custodied Crypto Worth €100,000 or More

“[paraphrased] Holders warned that a declared list of self-custodied wallets would give criminals targets.” The Crypto Desk, citing CoinDesk, 26 Nov 2026

French senators voted down, on Tuesday 24 November, a requirement to declare self-custodied crypto holdings worth €100,000 or more. Holders argued that such a list would mark them as targets for criminals.

The vote removes the most intrusive element of the proposal. The reporting does not say whether a narrower disclosure duty will return in later readings of the text.

📎 Historical Precedent: The EU Transfer of Funds Regulation (2023/1113), applicable to crypto transfers since late 2024, is the nearest analogue for counterparty identification, though it stops short of wallet registers.
⚖️ Practitioner Analysis

The rejection keeps France's self-custody position close to the status quo, a result that favours holders and privacy-led wallet providers. The Brief's read: the target-list argument will carry into any EU-level discussion of identifying self-hosted wallet holders.

🏗️ Industry Implications

Self-custody wallet and hardware-wallet makers are the direct beneficiaries, while French regulated custodians and exchanges keep the KYC burden alone. The Brief's read: that asymmetry raises the value of compliant on-ramps for French users who still need fiat access.
