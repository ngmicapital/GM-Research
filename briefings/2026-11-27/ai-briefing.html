Model Landscape

Chinese open-weight labs set the release pace this week, while the closed frontier was quieter than the deal flow.

Qwen4-Max lands as Alibaba's efficiency-led flagship

Alibaba's Qwen4-Max is dated 16 November, and it sits a generation above Qwen3.8-Max. The tracker listing gives it a 1M-token context window and roughly 2T total parameters with 71B active, with Alibaba pitching efficiency as the headline rather than raw scale.

Launch Qwen4-Max · 16 Nov · reasoning model, 1M context, listed at $1.41 input / $4.24 output per million tokens [tracker figures, not verified against Alibaba's pricing page]

Qwen3.8-27B keeps the open-weight crown

Hugging Face trending snapshots from 21 to 23 November put Qwen3.8-27B at about 20,900 likes and 6.3M downloads, and unsloth's GGUF build of it passed 7.6M downloads. The base model is now the foundation for much of the leaderboard, so its quantisations matter more than its headline benchmark.

Z.ai's GLM-5.4 and Ant's Ling-3.2-air

Z.ai released GLM-5.4 on 24 November as its first Loop Transformer model. Ant Group's Ling-3.2-air, released 26 November, is a multimodal model that takes text, images and video and targets professional tool-use tasks; its weights can be adapted commercially under the MIT licence.

Why it matters: Three separate labs shipping permissively licensed or open weights inside one week means the open tier now competes on agentic and multimodal features, not only on chat quality. Anyone choosing a default open model should re-test against these before the next trending cycle.

Claude Ecosystem

Anthropic's news this week is about turning Claude into a hub other tools plug into, not about a new model.

Skills over MCP enters public beta

Anthropic has put Skills over MCP into public beta in Claude and Claude Code. The extension lets an MCP server ship reusable workflow instructions alongside its tools, so the vendor can update tools and guidance together and the user installs nothing extra. Shopify, HubSpot and Atlassian are named as early server candidates. The extension spec itself is still labelled beta, and SDK and client support is still being built.

MCP passes 700 million monthly SDK downloads

Two years after launch, Anthropic says its MCP developer SDKs now see more than 700 million downloads a month, and Claude has more than 10,000 MCP connectors. The same expansion adds background tasks, follow-up questions for users and file uploads, and it is being extended so agents can coordinate with each other directly.

Claude artifacts move onto the desktop

Claude artifacts started rolling out in beta as Mac and Windows apps on 25 November. Claude Agent SDK v0.2.189 also shipped that week without release notes, which is worth knowing if you pin versions in production.

Claude agents migrate Anthropic's own API to Rust

Anthropic reports it used Claude agents to migrate its API codebase to Rust. It is an internal engineering claim with no independent benchmark attached, so treat it as a signal of agent maturity rather than a measured result.

Why it matters: Skills over MCP changes what a company has to approve when it connects a server. Procurement now reviews workflow instructions as well as tool permissions, and that is a different review surface from anything in the earlier Claude coverage.

Decentralized AI

Decentralised compute is starting to report revenue, but the numbers are small and mostly self-reported.

On-chain revenue is modest and the estimates disagree

DePIN Pulse data for 20 November put Render and Akash at about $9.3M of annualised on-chain revenue combined. io.net's own estimate adds roughly $16.3M, and Aethir's company dashboard claims about $47.7M a year. The gap between on-chain figures and company estimates is the core diligence question for this sector.

Bittensor keeps re-engineering its subnet economy

Bittensor's October upgrades added a null-consensus mode with up to 2,500 subnet slots and fee-free proof-of-work entry, which lets subnet owners run a larger network with a single reward-weight setter. A later upgrade lets subnet owners rebalance participant emissions while keeping root stakers' baseline share. Subnet 4 (Targon) has also opened a revenue dashboard as enterprise customers book long-term GPU capacity.

Free inference has a cost, and Chutes learned it

Chutes, the busiest inference service on Bittensor, switched off its free tier on 27 February 2026. The lesson for the sector is that free compute was not free for the GPU suppliers, so the networks now have to price usage that they used to subsidise.

Prices: token prices were not refreshed in this run. Read the narrative above as the signal; check current quotes before acting on any price move.

Open-Source Pulse

Qwen dominates the Hugging Face trending board, and derived builds are doing most of the community work.

Qwen3.8-27B's derivatives drive the leaderboard

The Qwen3.8-27B family accounts for most of the trending board, and its derivatives together represent tens of millions of downloads. The community is turning the base model into quantised GGUF builds that run on consumer hardware, which is where most of the download volume sits.

Uncensored and heavily tuned forks spread quickly

Several community forks of Qwen and DeepSeek builds, including DavidAU's NEO-IMATRIX and Heretic variants, show between 1.8M and 2.3M downloads on single listings. Several of these strip safety tuning on purpose, so downstream teams should treat them as unvetted artefacts rather than drop-in replacements.

LTX-2.5 keeps open video momentum going

Lightricks' LTX-2.5 sits near 12,900 likes and about 3M downloads on the 21 November snapshot, positioning it as a leading open image-to-video and text-to-video option.

Tracker-listed releases to verify

An aggregator lists PrimeIntellect's INTELLECT-3 as released on 27 November with a context window of 131K and token pricing of $0.20 input and $1.10 output. I could not confirm this against the lab's own page, so verify before adding it to any evaluation list.

Watch: OpenClaw 2026.11.10 rolled up 7,671 pull requests and added OpenHands projects in Canvas along with nested sub-agent conversations. Multi-agent open-source tooling is now shipping at a pace that makes weekly version pinning necessary.

Deals & Regulation

Consolidation is moving toward compute infrastructure and agent security, with large all-stock and strategic buys.

Marvell's reported $115B Lumentum deal

One AI roundup reports a $115 billion Marvell deal for Lumentum. Only a single secondary source carries the figure, so treat it as reported until a filing or primary announcement confirms it.

AMD agrees to buy Taalas for $2.9B

A tracker lists AMD's $2.9B acquisition of Taalas, an AI inference hardware company, on 23 November, with about $5M of prior funding disclosed. Inference silicon is the market AMD most needs to contest, so this is the more strategic of the week's hardware buys.

Palo Alto Networks to acquire Applied Compute

Palo Alto Networks agreed to acquire Applied Compute, a startup founded by former OpenAI researchers to help companies post-train and run open-weight models, in an all-stock deal valued at $4.5B. It is a sign that security vendors want to own the post-training layer, not only the network edge.

Sierra raises a $1.75B Series F at $30B

Sierra's Series F, led by Greenoaks and Lightspeed, values the agent-software company at $30B on 23 November. The round is one of the largest pure agent-application raises of the month.

Pipeline: Reported talks at Cognition ($2B or more at about $75B) and Databricks (several billion at about $225B) have not closed, and Nvidia's near-deal for Inception has not been announced. CrowdStrike's $43.9B all-stock agreement for Zscaler is framed around AI agent security. Regulatory items are left to the Legal Brief.

Workflow & Tools

The tooling story this week is agent access: vendors want agents to go through MCP servers and CLIs, not raw REST APIs.

Google makes Workspace MCP servers and CLI generally available

Google has made its Workspace MCP servers and Workspace CLI generally available, with a Google Cloud Agent Gateway for LLM, MCP and Agent2Agent traffic. The gateway checks agent identities against an agent registry, applies IAM policy, uses default-deny outbound rules and screens requests with Model Armour.

The addendum that changes the rules

Google's new Workspace AI Tools Addendum requires agents to use the Workspace MCP servers or CLI instead of the existing REST APIs. Any team with an agent that hits Gmail, Drive, Docs, Sheets or Calendar directly should plan a migration rather than wait for enforcement.

Payments reach the MCP layer

Versine launched an MCP that lets an agent send and request money from a self-custodial USDC wallet on Base. Sending needs approval through a passkey in its app unless the contact is marked trusted, which is the right default for agent-initiated payments.

Developer tooling updates

Chrome DevTools MCP 1.14.0 shipped on 24 November. OpenCode v2.4.0 added a built-in desktop browser, and agent-browser v0.44.0 was published on 23 November.

Watch: Each MCP approval step and each addendum becomes a policy surface. Teams that already route agents through a gateway will adapt quickly; teams with ad-hoc scripts will not.

Research Radar

Three benchmark papers from the last two weeks are worth tracking, all focused on how models handle incomplete or inconsistent inputs.

ClosureBench (arXiv 2611.26335, 19 Nov). The authors test whether LLM agents apply the stated rule for missing information, treating absent facts as unknown or as false depending on the source's completeness. They argue that evaluations should check which assumption an agent uses when information is absent, not only whether it reasons over the facts it has. Accepted at NeSy 2026.

Razavi-Bench (arXiv 2611.20935, 16 Nov). Built from 50 analog-circuit questions and graded against reference answers, the benchmark tracks 73 multimodal models released between May 2024 and November 2026. The top score rose from 28.83% to 98.33% over 30 months, mostly before reference answers were public, which raises a contamination question worth asking of every leaderboard.

The Logic Chain Breaking Point (arXiv 2611.20447, 16 Nov). Across sixteen open-weight models, instruction-tuned models score essentially zero on inconsistent premise sets, and reasoning-tuned models recover only partially. Inconsistent premises produce the sharpest degradation under the abstention-based scoring rule.
