Learn Something: Vera Rubin NVL72 — Why CoWoS-L Packaging Matters
NVIDIA's Vera Rubin platform uses TSMC's CoWoS-L (Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate, Large) advanced packaging, which enables wider die interconnects and more HBM4 stacks per chip than the prior CoWoS-S used in Blackwell. The practical result: bandwidth per GPU increases ~40%, and memory per module jumps from HBM3e to 8-layer HBM4. The constraint: CoWoS-L yield at TSMC is still in the 50–70% range vs. 80%+ for mature nodes. If TSMC can't scale packaging fast enough, NVL72 shipment volumes will disappoint in H2 2026, which is why the COMPUTEX supply chain meetings — not just the keynote stage announcement — are the real signals to watch.
COMPUTEX is a make-or-break week for the AI semi trade. If Vera Rubin NVL72 + supply chain roadmap delivers on bandwidth + shipping timelines, NVDA re-rates to $220–$230 and the complex follows. If the keynote is hype-heavy but light on delivery dates, the profit-taking accelerates. INTC's downgrade today is a warning that the market is done giving "trust us, the turnaround is coming" stories a free pass. 7 🌐 Geopolitical Calendar
🚨 IRAN CONFLICT — OIL NORMALISING BUT STRUCTURAL RISK UNRESOLVED
Status: WTI crude collapsed 16.2% in May to ~$88, down from $102 mid-month. The oil market is pricing in reduced disruption risk — either partial Strait of Hormuz reopening, OPEC+ spare capacity injection, or both. The Iran ceasefire remains technically in place but Oxford Economics characterises the conflict as the most significant energy crisis since the 1970s. US Operation Project Freedom for maritime escort has been intermittently active and paused.

Transmission Mechanism: The oil collapse is the most significant deflationary variable in the global macro picture right now. Every $10 fall in WTI reduces annual US CPI by ~25bps with a 2–3 month lag. WTI down from $102 to $88 = roughly 60bps of future CPI relief. This reopens the Fed's optionality but does NOT yet eliminate the inflation problem (core PCE at 4.4%). For equities: airlines (beneficiary), consumer discretionary (beneficiary), energy names (headwind on margins). For crypto: indirect positive via Fed flexibility, if disinflation narrative takes hold.

Reversal: Any confirmed Hormuz re-closure or ceasefire breakdown pushes WTI back through $100 within a session. Watch for OPEC+ emergency meeting announcements or US Navy incident reports in the Gulf. Probability markets give ~40% odds the ceasefire survives to July — tail risk is non-trivial.
🚨 TARIFF UNCERTAINTY — 150-DAY IEEPA WINDOW CREATES PARALYSIS
Status: The Supreme Court struck down broad IEEPA tariff authority earlier in 2026, but Section 122 temporary 15% global import duties continue in a 150-day transition window. Total tariffs on Chinese imports remain at 54% with China's 34% retaliatory rate in place. Firms cannot price multi-year capital investment while tariff levels are in legal limbo. The AI chip trade specifically is impacted: AI-driven semiconductor trade grew 40% in 2025, but export restrictions and tariff threats are creating supply chain disruption exactly when capex needs certainty.

Transmission Mechanism: Tariff uncertainty compresses business investment (CAPEX delayed, hiring frozen) which is a lagging drag on the economy that won't show up fully in Q1 data but could accelerate by Q3. Importers are absorbing 27% of tariff burden; consumers bear 55% in higher goods prices. The compounding of oil-driven inflation relief + tariff-driven goods inflation is the "two stories at once" problem for the Fed and for asset pricing.

Reversal: Any US-China trade deal or tariff sunset announcement would be the single largest positive macro catalyst available. A Trump-Xi bilateral on chip export easing specifically would re-rate NVDA, AMD, and TSMC simultaneously.
🚨 US CREDIT TRAJECTORY — MOODY'S Aa1 DOWNGRADE AFTERMATH
Status: Moody's downgraded US sovereign credit to Aa1 from AAA in May 2025 (the last major agency to do so, following S&P in 2011 and Fitch in 2023). US federal debt projected at 134% of GDP by 2035, with deficits running ~7% of GDP annually. The 30Y yield briefly touched 5% on the downgrade but has since settled. Banks (JPMorgan, BofA, Wells Fargo) were simultaneously downgraded, raising funding costs across the financial system.

Transmission Mechanism: The structural debt path keeps the 30Y yield elevated (currently ~4.85% est.), which limits how far growth stock multiples can expand. For crypto: persistent fiscal deficit + dollar debasement risk is the long-run structural Bitcoin bull thesis — but this plays out over years, not months. Near-term, the higher-for-longer yield environment keeps institutional crypto allocation constrained.

Reversal: A credible Congressional fiscal package (debt ceiling deal with spending constraints) would ease the 30Y yield pressure. Currently no such deal is in sight ahead of the next debt limit episode (~Q3 2026).
